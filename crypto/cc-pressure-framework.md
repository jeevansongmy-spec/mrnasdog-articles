# CC Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

**Canton Coin (CC) supply is growing.** Over the 90 days to Oct 1 2026 the Canton Network created **2.10B CC** — reward claims, foundation grants and voted releases from the unclaimed reward pool — while network fees burned **1.34B CC**. That leaves a net rise of **+1.91%** of the **39.73B CC** in circulation, and the next 90 days look the same. The burn is real and large, but under the current minting curve of about 10B CC a year it cannot catch the rewards; there is no supply cap.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.91%** net supply over the last 90 days and **+1.91%** for the next 90 days. Our supply monitor, which watches the market-wide supply figure, reads **+2.01%** for the same stretch, a gap of only **0.10 percentage points** — well inside our tolerance, so no warning is shown. The two readings agree because Canton Coin is simple to count: every new coin is minted by a reward round, a grant or a vote, and every fee destroys coins. Label for the record: **a burn-and-mint coin whose rewards still outrun its burn**.

## Sell pressure: where new CC comes from

**Protocol inflation is the big one: 1.94B CC.** A new mining round opens roughly every ten minutes, and each round lets apps, validators and super validators claim fresh Canton Coin for the work they did. The curve allows about 10B CC a year from December 2025 to June 2029. Claims in the window came to **1,939.8M CC**, about 21.6M a day, which is about 83% of what the curve allowed after the development-fund share; rewards nobody claims are simply never minted. The pace was flat — the first 30 days and the last 30 days each minted about 645M CC — so the forward figure keeps the same rate.

**Vesting unlocks are zero.** Canton Coin had no pre-sale, no team allocation and no investor pile. Every CC that exists was earned in a reward round since the network launched in mid-2024, so there is no vesting calendar and no cliff to wait for.

**Foundation grants added 65.77M CC.** Five percent of each round goes into a development fund as coupons that are not yet coins. When the foundation pays a grant, those coupons turn into new CC. Grants were paid on 15 days in the window, from **Jul 9 2026** to **Sep 28 2026**, and each payment shows up as a matching jump in total supply. With grants landing every month, the next 90 days carry the same amount.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy: zero.** No court estate or legal lock is paying CC out. Coins locked by super validators are already counted as circulating, so an unlock there moves coins inside the float and adds nothing new.

**Voted releases from the unclaimed reward pool added 97.12M CC.** Rewards that go unclaimed build up in a pool that only a super-validator vote can release. In the window, ten votes paid partners who hit agreed goals — the largest were **25.42M CC** to Zenith on Sep 5 2026, **19.62M** to LayerZero on Aug 13 2026, **19.23M** to Chainlink on Jul 31 2026 and **15.09M** to Wormhole on Sep 18 2026. Each landed as a step in total supply on its day. One earlier vote for Zenith pointed at a wrong account and never minted; it was voted again and counted once. Releases have come every month this year, so the forward column keeps the same pace.

## Buy pressure: where new CC goes

**There is no programmatic buyback.** No contract or treasury buys Canton Coin back on the market. The network does something more direct: it destroys the coins used to pay fees.

**The protocol fee burn removed 1.34B CC.** Traffic on the Global Synchronizer is priced in dollars and paid by burning CC, so every transaction, transfer and app action takes coins out of supply for good. Over 90 days the burn came to **1,344.7M CC**, about 14.9M a day, which equals **64%** of all new coins. It barely changed from the first month (440.5M) to the last (442.6M). Because fees are set in dollars, a lower CC price means more coins burned for the same activity, and a higher price means fewer.

**Foundation buy: zero.** No foundation, company treasury or fund was seen buying CC off the market for a reserve in the window.

**New long-term lock: zero, though locks are large.** Super validators keep about **18.31B CC** locked to hold their voting weight, and featured apps lock CC to keep their status. These coins still count as circulating, so a bigger lock takes nothing out of the float, and an unlock drips out over a year rather than all at once.

## Foundation and overhang

Three piles are worth watching. The first is the **development fund**: about **212.7M CC** of unminted coupons on Oct 1 2026, growing by about 1.3M a day and paid out as grants with no fixed dates. The second is the **unclaimed reward pool**, which is not published as a single balance; it only becomes coins through a dated vote, and 97.12M CC came out of it in this window. The third is the **super-validator lock wallets**, about 18.31B CC across 43 groups, led by Digital Asset, Cumberland and the Global Synchronizer Foundation. Those coins are already inside the circulating count, so they cannot add new supply, only move it. We re-read all three at every rebuild: if the fund or the pool falls between checks, the outflow lands in the sell rows at the next refresh.

## How CC compares to other fee-burning Layer 1s

Canton Coin sits in the same family as chains that burn fees and pay rewards in new coins, but its numbers lean the other way. On Ethereum the fee burn removes only a small slice of what validators are paid; on Canton the burn eats about two thirds of new coins, because every bit of network traffic must be paid in CC. That makes Canton Coin one of the few large coins where real usage visibly fights the mint every day.

The difference from capped, halving-style coins is the curve. Bitcoin issues a fixed reward that halves every four years toward a hard cap. Canton Coin has no cap: its curve steps down on dates — 10B CC a year now, 5B from June 2029, then 2.5B a year for good — and the burn decides how much of that becomes net supply. When activity grows faster than the reward pace, net supply can stall or even fall; when activity cools, it rises.

It also differs from tokens with big locked team piles. With no pre-mine and no vesting, Canton Coin has no unlock cliffs. Its discretionary supply comes instead from two governed sources — foundation grants and voted pool releases — which together added **162.9M CC** in 90 days, about 8% of all new coins.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Oct 6 2026:** super validators adopt a new version of the network's core contracts and vote on adding weight for LSEG; neither moves supply directly, but rule changes ride on these upgrades.

**Oct 7 2026 and Oct 9 2026:** a weight vote for Marex and a move of the network to a new consensus setup. Watch for any change to the reward split or the traffic price.

**New pool releases:** six new super validators were approved in September (Shinhan Asset Management, TreasurySpring, a16z crypto, LSEG, Paxos and Marex). Their goal-linked rewards are paid by votes from the unclaimed pool, the same way as row 5.

**Reward-rule proposals:** a proposal to base validator rewards on real traffic is still open, and traffic-based app rewards are still in a test phase. Either could cut how many reward coins get claimed.

**Monthly headlines about an "833M token emission":** that is the curve's monthly ceiling (10B ÷ 12). Actual claims have run near 650M a month, with about 450M a month burned.

## Summary

Canton Coin is inflationary on the current curve: **2.10B CC** created against a **1.34B CC** fee burn gives **+1.91%** net supply in 90 days, with the same expected next, and our monitor agrees within **0.10 percentage points**. The mechanism is burn-and-mint: rewards print new coins every ten minutes, and every fee destroys them. The key risk is the governed supply — foundation grants and voted pool releases — which can come in large lumps. The ceiling is the curve itself: about 10B CC a year until June 2029, with no hard cap after.
