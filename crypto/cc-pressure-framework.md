# CC Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Canton Coin is an **uncapped burn-and-mint coin** with no pre-mine and no vesting calendar. Over the 90 days to **Sep 3 2026** the Canton Network minted **1.93B CC** in activity rewards, super validators voted a further **60.8M CC** out of the unminted reward pool, and **1.29B CC** was destroyed paying for network traffic. Net of a **39.4M CC** rise in featured-app lock balances, that is **+1.68%** on a **39.50B CC** base. Canton's traffic burn is the largest of any chain in this catalog — trailing-year fees run **13.2%** of market value — and it still cancels only about **65%** of what the Canton Network mints.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 3 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.68% net** — a sell side of **1.99B CC** against a buy side of **1.32B CC**, leaving **663M CC** of new float on a circulating base of **39.50B CC**. Our independent supply monitor reads the same window at **+1.95%**, a gap of **0.28 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag ships. The Canton Coin figure is built from the Canton Network's own mining-round record and the core identity closes to a rounding artefact: mint minus burn over the window is **641,579,700.83 CC**, and the chain's published supply series moved by exactly **641,579,700.83 CC**. Canton Coin is **structurally inflationary while the mint leads the burn**, and the whole question is whether institutional traffic on the Canton Network can close a gap of roughly **660M CC** a quarter.

## Sell pressure: where new CC comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is almost all of the sell side at **1.93B CC** over 90 days. The Canton Network mints fresh Canton Coin in mining rounds and pays it to whoever did measured work. The split is the story: **1.41B CC** went to application providers, **259.9M CC** to Super Validators and **254.4M CC** to validators, so nearly three quarters of new Canton Coin lands with the apps generating transactions rather than with the infrastructure running them. The minting curve caps how fast this can run — **40B CC** a year at genesis, **20B** from six months, **10B** from eighteen months, **5B** from year five and **2.5B** from year ten, for **100B CC** across the first decade. The Canton Network sits in the **10B CC** tranche until roughly **Jun 26 2029**. That budget is paid per mining round rather than per calendar day, and the rounds run slow — measured at **610.30 seconds** against a **600 second** target, so about **141.6** rounds a day instead of **144** — which trims the real allowance by about **1.7%** before anything else happens. Of what remained, roughly **79%** was actually minted, because unearned budget is never created.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**, and it is zero for the strongest possible reason: Canton Coin was fair-launched. There is no pre-mine, no investor round and no team allocation, so there is no vesting calendar and no cliff to arrive. Every Canton Coin in existence was minted as a reward for measured work on the Canton Network. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **60.8M CC**, and it is a genuinely separate flow from the mining rewards. Reward entitlement that nobody claims expires into an unminted pool, and Super Validators vote slices of that pool into circulation when a party completes a milestone. Eleven such votes reached quorum and took effect inside this window, between **Jun 16 2026** and **Aug 18 2026**; the two largest were **19.6M CC** on **Aug 12 2026** and **19.2M CC** on **Jul 31 2026**. These do not appear in the mining-round series at all, which we verified rather than assumed: three votes totalling **25.7M CC** took effect on **Jul 31 2026**, yet the app-reward line was flat at **15,688,737 CC** that day and the day before. A twelfth vote for **25.4M CC** reached quorum on **Aug 22 2026** but never minted, on a bad party identifier, and was resubmitted for **Sep 5 2026** — a vote with quorum is not an executed mint, so it is not in this ledger. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Canton Coin has never had a bankruptcy estate or a trustee distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new CC goes

Buy #2 — the protocol fee burn — is the mechanism that defines Canton Coin, at **1.29B CC** destroyed over the window. On the Canton Network every transaction consumes synchronizer traffic, traffic is priced in dollars per megabyte, and the submitting party burns the equivalent Canton Coin at the on-chain conversion rate. Nobody collects it. There is no validator tip, no treasury cut and no fee switch to argue about: the fee is destruction, and destruction is the entire buy side of this coin. Traffic purchases accounted for all of it — the separate transfer-fee burn read **zero** on every one of the 90 days. Two independent readings agree on the size: the chain's own cumulative burn counter differences to **1,285,274,971.33 CC**, and taking the network's dollar fee take for the same window and dividing it day by day by that day's Canton Coin price gives **1,278.39M CC** — half a percent apart.

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, and cannot be otherwise: the Canton Network destroys fees rather than recycling them into open-market bids, so there is no buyback contract, no budget and no accumulation wallet to track. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero**; no open-market purchase by the Canton Foundation is disclosed anywhere in the governance record. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **39.4M CC**, and it is new this quarter. Featured App status on the Canton Network now requires a standing Canton Coin deposit: **5M CC** per app party and **25M CC** for asset issuers, unwinding at one sixtieth per day on sixty days' notice. Existing apps had thirty days from approval on **May 20 2026** to comply, and the locks duly landed inside the window — seven fresh **5M CC** deposits plus one **4.07M CC** deposit between **Jun 19 2026** and **Aug 27 2026**, with nothing withdrawn. Measured at both window ends across the whole party set, locked balances went from **2.37M CC** to **41.77M CC**.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest overhang on Canton Coin is not a foundation treasury at all. Under the Super Validator locking framework, **43** Super Validator organisations have **18.33B CC** sitting in disclosed wallets against **25.60B CC** of lifetime Super Validator rewards — a **71.6%** lock ratio, and roughly **46%** of all circulating Canton Coin. The critical detail is that this is a disclosure regime, not a smart-contract lock: every Super Validator party reads zero locked balance on chain, the coin is spendable today, and compliance is evaluated weekly off chain by docking governance weight. It is watched as an overhang, not booked as buy pressure. Behind it sits the unminted reward pool itself, which filled by roughly **497M CC** over this window while governance drew only **60.8M CC** out of it — filling about eight times faster than it drains. The Canton Foundation's own operating wallets are small by comparison at **27.3M CC** combined, and the on-chain development-fund wallet holds just **12.1K CC** and has not moved at all between readings. The featured-app lock pool, **41.77M CC** across **22** wallets, is tracked here too because it can unwind. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CC compares to other burn-and-mint chains

Canton Coin belongs to the small family of chains with no supply cap and no pre-mine, where issuance is a reward budget and the only counterweight is fee destruction. Measured chain against chain — not chain against application, which is a different kind of thing entirely — the Canton Network took **$48.84M** in fees over the trailing 30 days, the highest of the **169** chains with fee coverage. Tron is second at **$25.50M**, Solana third at **$22.24M**, Ethereum sixth at **$10.52M** and Bitcoin seventh at **$6.68M**. The Hyperliquid L1 — the chain, not the perps venue that runs on it — took **$1.67M**, so the Canton Network runs about **29 times** its fee volume.

Relative to size the gap is wider still. Trailing-year fees of **$580M** against a market value of **$4.39B** put Canton Coin at **13.2%** of market cap earned and destroyed in a year. The next chain on that measure is Tron at **1.15%**, then Solana at **0.41%**, BNB at **0.23%**, Ethereum at **0.07%** and Bitcoin at **0.01%** — Canton Coin is roughly **11 times** the next chain and more than a thousand times Bitcoin. That is the direction worth stating plainly, because it is easy to get backwards: those fees are not revenue accruing to anyone. Every dollar of Canton Network fees is a dollar of Canton Coin destroyed, so the ratio is a measure of buy-side force, and it is the single largest force on this ledger.

And it is still not enough. Ethereum's base-fee burn only turns the supply negative because Ethereum issues sparingly to stakers; the Canton Network is two years into a curve that permits **10B CC** a year, so a burn that would flip almost any other chain deflationary cancels about **65%** of the mint and leaves Canton Coin growing. Compared with a halving-model chain like Bitcoin, where issuance falls on a fixed calendar regardless of usage, Canton Coin's issuance falls on a fixed calendar too — **10B** to **5B** a year around **Jun 2029** — but its burn scales with institutional settlement volume, which is the variable actually in play.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Two reward-pool mints totalling **26.8M CC** take effect on **Sep 5 2026**, including the **25.4M CC** resubmission that failed on a bad party identifier in August. The Depository Trust and Clearing Corporation has signalled a broader rollout of tokenized Treasury settlement on the Canton Network in **October 2026** after a pilot with more than forty firms — the largest single potential burn catalyst on the calendar, because burn scales directly with settlement traffic. The traffic-based app-reward change remains unactivated: the activation vote failed on **Aug 18 2026** and the mechanism is still in dry run, so any successful re-vote would change how the mint is calculated for the first time since launch. A further proposal to put validator rewards on the same traffic basis was filed on **Jun 29 2026** and has not been approved. Finally, the featured-app lock pool can unwind as easily as it built: nineteen apps are past their compliance date, and a forfeit shows up as a lock balance falling rather than as any announcement.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.68%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Sep 3 2026**, and projects the same rate forward because no dated mechanism change fired inside the window. The structural mechanism is a burn-and-mint equilibrium that has not yet reached equilibrium: the Canton Network mints **1.93B CC** a quarter in activity rewards and destroys **1.29B CC** paying for traffic, so roughly **65%** of new supply is cancelled and the rest reaches the market. The key risk is that **18.33B CC** — about **46%** of circulating supply — sits with Super Validators under an off-chain disclosure regime rather than a contract lock, and is spendable today. The ceiling is the minting curve: **10B CC** a year until roughly **Jun 2029**, then **5B**, with no maximum supply at any point. Canton Coin turns deflationary only when institutional settlement volume grows faster than a fixed reward budget shrinks.
