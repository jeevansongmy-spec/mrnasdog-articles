# CC Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Canton Coin is an **uncapped burn-and-mint coin** with no pre-mine and no vesting calendar, and its supply is still growing. Over the 90 days to **Sep 13 2026** the Canton Network minted **1.93B CC** in rewards, the Canton development fund paid out **68.0M CC** in grants, and super-validator votes released **87.6M CC** from the unminted reward pool. Against that, **1.31B CC** was burned paying for network traffic and new featured-app locks took **53.4M CC**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.84% net** against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.93%**, a gap of **0.09 percentage points**, with **+1.97%** projected for the next 90 days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 13 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.84% net** — a sell side of **2.09B CC** against a buy side of **1.36B CC**, leaving **728M CC** of new float on a circulating base of **39.58B CC**. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+1.93%**. The gap is **0.09 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so Canton Coin ships with **no data-conflict flag**. The ledger also closes against the chain's own balance: every coin minted, granted and voted out, less every coin burned, lands within **2.6M CC** of the measured change in total Canton Coin supply. The forward column reads **+1.97%**, a little higher, only because no new lock is scheduled. The label for Canton Coin is **inflationary by design while the mint leads the burn**: every fee is destroyed, and the Canton Network still creates more CC than it destroys.

## Sell pressure: where new CC comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is almost all of the Canton Coin sell side at **1.93B CC**. The Canton Network mints fresh CC every ten-minute round and pays it to the parties that did measured work: **1.41B CC** to application providers, **261.7M CC** to super validators and **258.5M CC** to validators. A minting curve caps the pace — **10B CC** a year in the current tranche, stepping down to **5B** around mid-2029 and to **2.5B** a year after year ten. Only about **80%** of this window's budget was actually minted; unclaimed rewards expire into a pool instead of becoming coins. One proposed change was tested and ruled out: traffic-based app rewards ran only in a dry run from **Jul 7 2026**, and the vote to switch them on for real was rejected on **Aug 13 2026**. The Canton Coin mint ran near **21.5M CC** a day across the whole window, so the forward column holds that rate.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: Canton Coin had no pre-mine, no investor round and no team allocation, so there is no unlock schedule at all. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **68.0M CC**. Since the development fund went live, **5%** of every Canton Coin mint is set aside as unclaimed coupons, and the coins are only created when the foundation pays a grant. Eighty-nine grants landed inside the window, in monthly batches, the largest **13.77M CC** on **Sep 3 2026** and **13.71M CC** on **Jul 16 2026**. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: no estate or trustee has ever distributed Canton Coin. A fifth row carries the governance milestone mints, **87.6M CC**: ten unanimous super-validator votes moved coins out of the unminted reward pool to operators who met agreed milestones, the largest **25.4M CC** on **Aug 28 2026**. A matching **25.4M CC** vote on **Aug 14 2026** was never claimed and expired, so it is not counted.

## Buy pressure: where new CC goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: Canton has no buyback contract or budget, and fees are destroyed rather than recycled into bids. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **1.31B CC**. Every transaction on the Canton Global Synchronizer is paid for by buying network traffic with CC, and that Canton Coin is destroyed — no validator keeps a tip and no treasury takes a cut. The burn equals **3.3%** of circulating supply in 90 days and cancelled about **63%** of all new Canton Coin created in the same period. The pace rose from about **12.6M CC** a day in June to about **15.3M CC** since mid-August; with no dated change to the traffic price, the forward column holds the slower 90-day average. Canton has no burn address, so the burn was checked from both sides: two independent explorer series agree on the burned total within **0.02%**, and supply rose by only **779M CC** against **2.09B CC** of gross new coins, which proves the difference was destroyed rather than moved. Mint and burn are separate mechanisms — a round can mint with no traffic, and traffic can burn with no reward claimed — so they sit on two rows.

Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**; the funds and treasury companies that hold Canton Coin bought it on the market. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **53.4M CC**. To stay featured, a Canton app must lock **5M CC** per wallet, or **25M CC** for an asset issuer, released over 60 days if it leaves. Ten new **5M CC** locks and one **4.07M CC** lock landed inside the window, seven of them on the **Jun 19 2026** deadline, while a handful of small older locks were released; **62.7M CC** now sits locked across **127** wallets. Locked coins are not destroyed, so they never overlap with the burn, and with no lock scheduled the forward column carries none.

## Foundation and overhang

Three Canton Coin overhangs are watched. The first is the **unminted reward pool**: roughly **350M CC** of unclaimed rewards expired into it this window while **87.6M CC** was voted out, and its standing balance is not published, so every executed vote is read as it lands. The second is the **development fund**, which has earned about **157M CC** it has not yet paid out. The third is the **19.39B CC** held in wallets that super validators and featured apps have disclosed as committed to the network — about **49%** of circulating supply. Only **44.1M CC** of that is locked on chain; the rest is spendable today and checked weekly off chain, so a withdrawal would show up as ordinary coins moving. These are re-read on every refresh, and if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CC compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake chains

Canton Coin belongs to the class of uncapped chains that pay their infrastructure in newly minted coins and lean on a fee burn to push back. Many chains in that class burn only a small fraction of what they issue. Canton Coin does not: its traffic burn destroyed about **63%** of all new supply in this window, and in dollar terms the burn ran about **$50M** over the last 30 days, near **15%** of Canton Coin's market value over the trailing year. The difference is design. Canton charges every transaction in dollars and settles it by destroying CC at the spot price, so the burn scales with traffic rather than with a fixed per-transaction fee. The reward budget, by contrast, is fixed in coins, so a falling CC price raises the burn in coins while the mint stays where it is, and a rising price does the opposite.

Against capped proof-of-work chains, Canton Coin has no ceiling at all; its curve slows issuance in steps rather than stopping it. Against chains that pay validators purely from fees, Canton Coin still carries a large scheduled mint, which is why supply grows even with a burn this size. Two features are unusual. There was no pre-mine, so there is no investor or team overhang, and discretionary supply arrives instead through governance mints and development-fund grants, which together added **156M CC** this window. And the burn and the mint are measured in different units, so the balance between them moves with price, not only with activity.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, two milestone mints worth **16.0M CC** have already passed and take effect between **Sep 15 2026** and **Sep 18 2026**; they sit inside the projected run rate, and a larger batch of votes would push the sell side above it. Second, traffic-based app rewards: switching them on for real would change who earns the **1.41B CC** app share, and the last attempt failed on **Aug 13 2026**. Third, a proposal to pay validators by traffic as well is still only proposed, and would re-shape the validator share if approved. Fourth, the traffic burn itself: at about **15.3M CC** a day since mid-August it is running above the 90-day average the forward column uses, and a sustained rise would lower the forward reading. Fifth, the monthly development-fund grant batch, which has landed early in each of the last four months.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.84% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+1.97%** over the next 90, with a supply monitor at **+1.93%** and no data-conflict flag. The structural mechanism is an uncapped burn-and-mint design: **1.93B CC** of rewards, **68.0M CC** of development-fund grants and **87.6M CC** of governance mints, against **1.31B CC** destroyed paying for traffic and **53.4M CC** newly locked. The key risk is discretionary supply — governance votes and grant batches can add coins faster than the run rate, and **19.39B CC** of disclosed commitments are spendable today. The ceiling is the minting curve: **10B CC** a year until around mid-2029, then half that, so Canton Coin supply keeps rising unless the burn grows past the mint.
