# CC Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Canton Coin is an **uncapped burn-and-mint token** with no pre-mine and no vesting: over the 90 days to **Aug 16 2026** the Canton Network minted **1.91B CC** as activity rewards and burned **1.24B CC** paying Global Synchronizer traffic fees, for **+675M CC** of net new supply on a **39.4B** base. That is **+1.72% net**, and the same rate projects forward. Canton Coin runs the largest fee burn in crypto — and its mint still runs ahead of it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 16 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.72% net** — a **1.91B CC** mint against a **1.24B CC** burn, leaving **+675M CC** against a circulating base of **39.4B CC**. Our independent supply monitor reads the same window at **+1.86%**, a gap of **0.15 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag ships. The two reads are genuinely independent: the framework figure is built from the chain's own mining-round record of minting and burning, and the identity holds to the coin, because the round series' own supply total moved by exactly the same **+675M CC** over the window. Canton Coin is **structurally inflationary while the mint leads the burn**, and the whole investment question is whether institutional traffic on the Canton Network can close a gap of roughly **670M CC** a quarter.

## Sell pressure: where new CC comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the entire sell side of Canton Coin, at **1.91B CC** over 90 days. The Canton Network mints fresh CC in mining rounds roughly every ten minutes and pays it to the participants doing measured work. The split is the story: **1.41B CC** went to application providers, **255M CC** to Super Validators and **245M CC** to validators, so nearly three quarters of Canton Coin's new supply now lands with the apps generating transactions rather than with infrastructure. That is deliberate — the January 2026 issuance step-down halved the ceiling and pushed the app share up at the Super Validators' expense.

The issuance curve caps how fast this can run: **40B CC** a year at genesis, **20B** from the six-month mark, **10B** from the eighteen-month mark, **5B** from year five and a flat **2.5B** thereafter, for roughly **100B CC** across the first decade. The Canton Network sits in the **10B CC** tranche, which allows about **2.47B CC** in a 90-day window; only **1.91B** was actually created, about **78%**, because the budget mints only when someone earns it. There is no next step-down until **2029**, so the forward mint is projected at the trailing rate.

The other three sell rows are all **zero**, and each for a different reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because Canton Coin had a fair launch — no pre-mine, no venture allocation, no team or seed cliff — and public unlock trackers show the token fully unlocked with nothing scheduled. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because no token sale ever built a stockpile to release; the pools that do exist are watched rather than booked. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero because no bankruptcy estate or trustee distribution touches Canton Coin, and none can be created.

## Buy pressure: where new CC goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is the whole netted buy side, at **1.24B CC** over 90 days, every coin of it from traffic purchases on the Global Synchronizer. Canton Network fees are quoted in dollars but settled by destroying CC, and there is no fee recipient anywhere in the model: the coins simply cease to exist. Two properties make this burn unusual. It scales with institutional usage rather than speculation, and because the fee is dollar-denominated, a falling CC price burns **more** coins for the same activity — a burn that gets stronger exactly when the token is weak. It currently erases about **65%** of the mint.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: no treasury bids for Canton Coin, because the protocol destroys fees outright instead of recycling them into purchases. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — no discretionary open-market buying was disclosed or observed. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, and this is where the framework parts company with the headlines. Since **May 20 2026** a featured app must lock **5M CC** per identity — **25M CC** if it issues assets — to keep earning rewards, which across 119 apps and 13 issuers would take **855M CC** out of the float, and press coverage has reported more than **1B CC** locked with 85-plus apps compliant. Scanning the lock contracts directly on **Aug 17 2026** returns a different number: **25M CC** actually locked, **2** of 119 apps covered and 117 still pending, plus **30M CC** sitting in locks not yet attached to an app. Phase 1 of the rule still allows a lock wallet to be disclosed privately, so the shortfall may be real or merely unpublished — either way it is not evidence, and a lock that unwinds over 60 days is not permanent, so the framework books nothing and watches the pool instead.

## Foundation and overhang

Canton Coin has an unusually thin overhang for a token of its size, because there was never an allocation table to hang one on. Four pools are enumerated and watched. First, and now the most important, the **unclaimed-rewards pool** — budget the issuance curve allowed but nobody earned, which Super Validators can vote to mint to a partner on completing a milestone. Two such votes are live as of **Aug 17 2026**, one effective **Aug 18 2026** and one **Aug 22 2026**, and neither publishes its quantum, so the framework enumerates them and refuses to estimate. Second, the protocol development fund party, which holds just **12,120 CC** on chain and whose share of new emissions currently reads as switched off. Third, the Super Validator locked-reward pools created by the **Mar 2 2026** governance rule, under which each of the 13 Super Validators locks a large share of lifetime earned rewards to keep full voting weight and unwinds it over a year if it exits. Fourth, the featured-app lock pool described above. None of the four showed an outflow to market in the window, so each carries a value of zero. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CC compares to other uncapped burn-and-mint chains

Canton Coin belongs to the burn-and-mint family rather than the hard-cap family — the same structural class as an uncapped smart-contract L1 with a fee burn, and the opposite of a halving chain whose supply path is fixed in advance regardless of usage. In a hard-cap chain the schedule is the answer; in Canton Coin's design the schedule is only the ceiling, and usage decides. That is why the mint came in at **78%** of the allowance rather than 100%: unearned budget is never created.

Against the fee-burn L1s, Canton Coin's burn is far larger in relative terms — it destroys roughly **3.1%** of circulating supply every 90 days while returning about **4.9%** as rewards — but its mint is also far larger, because Canton Network rewards apps directly rather than only validators. Against exchange tokens that buy and burn from profits, the difference is that Canton Coin's burn requires no discretionary decision and no treasury: it is a byproduct of settlement. And against the fair-launch privacy coins with tail emission, the resemblance is the absence of investor unlocks — no cliff can ever hit this token, which is why its sell side is one row rather than four.

The structural verdict follows from the arithmetic. Canton Coin turns deflationary only when traffic burn exceeds the live mint: today that burn runs near **1.24B CC** a quarter, or **4.9B** annualised, against a **7.7B** annualised mint. Closing the gap needs roughly a **55%** increase in on-chain fee activity — or the next issuance step-down, which is years away. The long-run model settles at about **2.5B CC** a year minted from year ten, a level today's burn would already clear twice over.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The nearest event is **Aug 18 2026**, when a passed configuration vote switches app rewards to the traffic-based model — app rewards are nearly three quarters of the mint, so this is the one change that could move Sell #1 materially, and the framework will re-base the forward mint once a post-change run rate exists. The same day brings a partner milestone mint from the unclaimed-rewards pool, with a second on **Aug 22 2026**; if either publishes a quantum, it enters Sell #3. The tokenized settlement expansion targeted for **Oct 2026** is the largest potential burn catalyst, because settlement volume converts directly into destroyed CC. Watch the featured-app lock coverage too: it currently stands at 2 of 119 apps on chain, and a real move toward the **855M CC** requirement would be a genuine float reduction. Finally, the monthly mint has sat in a narrow band all year while the burn has swung wider — a burn month above **500M CC** would be the first real sign of the equilibrium arriving.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Canton Coin at **+1.72% net supply growth** over the last 90 days and the same rate over the next 90, against an independent monitor read of **+1.86%** — a **0.15 percentage point** gap, well inside tolerance. The structural mechanism is a usage-metered mint of **1.91B CC** a quarter set against the largest fee burn in crypto at **1.24B CC**, with no vesting, no foundation stockpile and no bankruptcy estate anywhere in the ledger. The key risk is simply that the burn depends on institutional traffic that has not yet scaled to match the reward schedule. The ceiling is the protocol's own issuance curve — **10B CC** a year until 2029, then **5B**, then **2.5B** — so Canton Coin's path to deflation runs through usage, not through scarcity.
