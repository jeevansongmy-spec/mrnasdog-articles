# OP Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

OP supply is growing at about **+1.09%** per 90 days, and the MrNasdog Pressure Framework projects about **+1.67%** for the next 90 days. No OP is minted: all **4.29B OP** were created in 2022. The growth comes from the Optimism Foundation handing out grants — **25.04M OP** in the last 90 days — plus about **13.32M OP** of early-team coins the Foundation expects to unlock next, while almost nothing is burned. The inflation monitor reads **+6.58%** because of a one-time recount on Jul 15 2026.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 1 to Sep 29 2026, **25.04M OP** entered circulation and about **0.26 OP** left it, a net change of **+1.09%** on a circulating supply of **2.30B OP**. The next 90 days add the early-team remainder, for a projected **38.36M OP** and **+1.67%**. The inflation monitor reads **+6.58%** for the same window, a gap of **5.49 points**, so the page carries a ⚠ monitor gap chip. The gap is not new OP: on Jul 15 2026 the Foundation's circulating count jumped **122.26M OP** in one update, and about **120.11M** of that were investor and early-team coins that had already unlocked by May 31 2026, before this window. That catch-up alone is **5.56 points**. OP is a fixed-supply governance token whose float grows by treasury distribution — a steady, Foundation-paced dilution with no buyer on the other side.

## Sell pressure: where new OP comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. OP has no block reward and no staking reward, because gas on OP Mainnet is paid in ETH. Total supply read 4,294,963,292 OP at both ends of the window, a hair under the original 2^32 because holders have burned about 4,004 OP by hand since launch. The token can still mint: its mint manager allows up to **2%** a year, about 85.9M OP, and the yearly clock that a zero-size mint restarted in October 2025 opens again on **Oct 28 2026**. The Collective's rate has stayed at 0% since launch, and no vote to raise it is open, so this row books 0 in both windows.

Vesting unlocks were **0** in the last 90 days. Investors hold 17% of OP and early core contributors 19%; both followed a four-year schedule that paid its last monthly slice in May 2026. Every investor coin — 735.86M OP — is now counted as circulating, and the early-team count did not move between Aug 6 and Sep 4 2026. The Foundation's own Year 5 outlook still expects about **35.2M OP** more for early contributors by Apr 30 2027, from later grants with no public dates. Spread evenly from the Foundation's last count, that is about **13.32M OP** in the next 90 days, which is why the forward figure is higher than the trailing one.

Foundation grants are the real engine: **25.04M OP** in the last 90 days. We read every Foundation wallet at both window ends and every transfer in between, and the two agree to a fraction of one OP. A grant wallet sends batches most weeks — about 8.2M OP in July, 4.5M in August and 12.1M in September. The Foundation's own circulating count rose 17.87M OP from Jul 15 to Sep 4 2026, against 17.05M OP paid out on-chain over the same dates, so the two views match. The forward figure holds the same pace.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**. There is no estate, no trustee schedule and no long lock releasing OP.

## Buy pressure: where new OP goes

The programmatic buyback books **0**. In January 2026 governance approved spending half of Superchain revenue on OP every month for a year, with the coins held in the Collective treasury. Three monthly buys settled between March and May 2026 — **9.45M OP** in all, for about 514 ETH — and that wallet held exactly the same balance at both ends of this window. Base, which paid most of the revenue, left the Superchain in February 2026, and no buy has been reported since the March-revenue purchase. Even when the buyback runs, the coins stay in a wallet that the circulating count already includes, so they do not shrink the float unless they are burned.

The protocol fee burn is effectively **0**: fees on OP Mainnet are paid in ETH, and holders burned only about 0.26 OP by hand in 90 days. There was no Foundation buy outside the buyback, and no new long-term lock: OP has no staking contract that takes coins out of circulation.

## Foundation and overhang

About **1.99B OP** — 46% of all OP — is not yet circulating, and almost all of it sits with the Optimism Foundation. The largest wallet is a reserve safe holding **1.29B OP**, which also controls the mint; it did not move this window. The main treasury holds **318.5M OP** after sending 142M OP on Aug 7 2026 into a new Foundation safe, which now holds **113.4M OP** and feeds the grant wallet. An older grants safe holds **21.4M OP** and has been idle. The buyback wallet holds **9.45M OP**. Early-team coins still to unlock total about 123.4M OP, held in custody off-chain.

The biggest change this quarter is on paper: in August 2026 governance turned the unused **546.9M OP** user-airdrop pool into a Strategic Ecosystem Fund for partnership and enterprise deals, with no release dates and yearly reporting only. The Foundation's outlook for May 2026 to April 2027 already plans about 200M OP from the ecosystem fund and 10M from the governance fund, well above the current pace. We read these wallets on-chain at every rebuild; if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How OP compares to other Layer-2 governance tokens

OP belongs to the class of pre-minted Layer-2 governance tokens, alongside ARB (Arbitrum) and similar rollup tokens. None of them pays validators or burns fees in its own token — the rollup collects gas in ETH — so their supply story is entirely about unlocks and treasury spending. OP differs in two ways: its investor and team vesting is already finished, while treasury grants keep flowing, and its token contract keeps a live 2% yearly mint that governance has held at zero. That makes OP's dilution discretionary rather than scheduled: it depends on how fast the Foundation spends, not on a fixed calendar.

Compared with Ethereum's ETH, which issues new coins to validators and burns part of every fee, OP has no issuance and no burn at all. Compared with exchange tokens or fee-sharing DeFi tokens that buy back and burn, OP's buyback holds coins in a treasury instead of destroying them, and it has paused after the revenue source shrank. The result is one-directional: supply can only grow, at the pace the treasury chooses.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Oct 11 2026** — an unlock tracker lists a Seed Fund release on this date, with no published size; it would show up as a faster grant pace. **Oct 28 2026** — the yearly mint window reopens; any mint above 0% would be new supply and would enter Sell #1. The Strategic Ecosystem Fund — the first deals from the 546.9M OP pool would appear as larger outflows from the Foundation wallets. The early-team remainder of about 35.2M OP — a lump release would land in Sell #2. The buyback — a restart, and above all any decision to burn the 9.45M OP held, would be the first real buy pressure OP has had.

## Summary

OP is inflationary on its circulating float: **+1.09%** in the last 90 days and about **+1.67%** projected for the next 90, with no minting at all. Every new coin comes from the Optimism Foundation's treasury — grants of **25.04M OP** a quarter plus the last early-team unlocks — and nothing is burned or locked on the other side. The key risk is size: about 1.99B OP, 46% of supply, still sits in Foundation wallets, including a 546.9M OP deal fund with no dates. The ceiling is the fixed 4.29B supply, unless governance ever turns on the 2% yearly mint.
