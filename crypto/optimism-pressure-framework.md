**TL;DR.** Optimism minted **no OP at all** in the last 90 days — the chain read of total supply is identical at both ends of the window at **4,294,963,292 OP** — and yet **136.5M OP** reached free hands, because the four-year lock-up on the investor and core-contributor allocations hit its final tranche. Against that, buy pressure was **zero**: the Superchain-revenue buyback has not bought a single OP since **May 1 2026**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+5.97% net** for the window and **+0.77%** for the next, against a monitor read of **+6.40%** — a gap of **0.43 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no conflict chip is attached. OP is a **fixed-cap token with an unlock problem, not an issuance problem**, and the unlock problem has just finished.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 17 2026**, the framework reads **OP at +5.97% net**, with **+0.77%** projected over the next 90 days. Our supply monitor reads **+6.40%** for the same trailing window; the gap is **0.43 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, and the residual is pure denominator convention — the same **136.5M OP** measured against the **2,151.5M** float of 90 days ago is **+6.35%**, and against today's **2,288.0M** it is **+5.97%**. Both numbers describe one event. Optimism is an **unlock-driven token with no issuance and no burn**: the OP contract issues nothing, gas settles in ETH so no OP is ever burned, and every move in the float is a vesting calendar emptying into private wallets.

## Sell pressure: where new OP comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and this build proves it from the chain rather than from the tokenomics page. The OP token's **totalSupply** was read at OP Mainnet block **151,812,926** and again at block **155,700,926**, the two ends of the window, and returns the same figure to four decimals: **4,294,963,292.4987 OP**. Optimism does hold a governance mint power, capped at **2%** of supply per year, and reading the MintManager contract directly shows the permission timer set to **Oct 28 2026**. Because that timer is only ever advanced by a mint call, it also proves a mint was executed on **Oct 28 2025** — and the transfer log for that block shows it issued exactly **0 OP**. Optimism governance has now touched the lever and declined to use it. The cap it could use is roughly **85.9M OP**, and this row books zero because the power to issue is not the same as issuing.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the entire sell ledger, at **136.5M OP**. The published unlock schedule's free-supply series moved from **2,151.5M** to **2,288.0M OP** across the window, almost all of it in a single step in mid-July, as the **36-month** linear vest covering Investors (**730.1M OP**) and Core Contributors (**816.0M OP**) reached its terminal tranche. The chain dates that release precisely. On **Jul 22 2026**, between 04:11 and 04:20 UTC, **119** separate transfers moved **542.8M OP** out of wallets that had been funded on **Aug 17 2022** and had not moved a token in four years, each into a freshly created address. That is a lock-up expiry followed by a custody rotation, six days after the schedule stepped. None of the receiving wallets has sold since — the coins are free, but they are sitting.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — books **zero**, and this is the row where reading balances rather than press releases changes the answer; the detail is in the overhang section below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** because no estate, trustee or court-administered pool holds OP, and Optimism has never deployed a long-dated lockup contract whose expiry could put a legacy position onto the market.

## Buy pressure: where new OP goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, and that is the most consequential correction on this page. Optimism governance approved a 12-month pilot on **Jan 28 2026** with **84.4%** support, committing **50%** of net Superchain sequencer revenue to monthly OP purchases held in the Collective treasury. The programme executed three times, buying **1,574,800 OP** on **Mar 2 2026**, **6,951,453 OP** on **Apr 1 2026** and **925,654 OP** on **May 1 2026** — a cumulative **9,451,924 OP**. Then it stopped. The destination wallet was read at both ends of this window and holds the identical **9,451,924.21 OP**, with no transfer in or out for 90 days. The rule carries its own off-switch: conversions pause when monthly revenue falls below **$200,000**. A cumulative purchase report is not a reserve balance, and dividing **9.45M OP** by the months since February would have invented roughly **4.7M OP** of buy pressure that the wallet says never happened.

The destination matters as much as the amount, and here it works against the token: repurchased OP is **held, not burned**. Those **9.45M OP** remain a live balance that governance can redeploy, so the buyback converts network revenue into treasury inventory rather than into permanent scarcity. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero** because OP Stack chains charge gas in ETH; transactions on Optimism burn ETH and never touch OP. That is verified from supply at both ends rather than asserted, and the burn address received **10.8 OP** across the entire quarter. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**: no open-market purchase programme exists outside the dormant pilot. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: OP has no staking contract and no vault, and delegating OP for governance leaves the token in the holder's own wallet, fully transferable.

## Foundation and overhang

Roughly **2.0B OP** — **46.7%** of the cap — sits outside the float, and every wallet holding it was read at both ends of the window. The governance-gated reserve holds **1,294.0M OP** and did not move. A second pair of safes under a single separate signer holds **149.1M OP** and did not move. The Foundation's grants distribution wallet holds **21.4M OP** and paid out **nothing at all** in 90 days, which is the quiet finding: an announced grant budget divided by four would have produced a number the wallet flatly contradicts. The buyback treasury holding **9.5M OP** is the fourth watched balance, unscheduled and governed later.

One movement did occur and it is not what it looks like. On **Aug 7 2026**, **142.0M OP** left the Optimism Foundation's operating treasury for a Safe created a week earlier. Reading the signer sets settles it: the new safe, the reserve, the operating treasury and the grants wallet all share the signer **0x3f3cd78e…**, so the transfer never left Foundation control and is not sell pressure. Aggregated across all six addresses, Foundation custody went from **1,925,051,625 OP** to **1,925,051,635 OP** — a change of ten tokens. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How OP compares to other layer-2 governance tokens

OP belongs to the class of **fixed-cap layer-2 governance tokens** whose chains do not charge gas in their own token — the same design as most OP Stack and rollup peers. That design decision is what separates Optimism from a layer 1 at the root. A proof-of-stake L1 pays validators in its own token, so it has an issuance rate to argue about; Optimism pays its sequencer in ETH, so OP has no issuance rate at all and no fee burn either. The entire supply question collapses into a distribution calendar. That is a genuine advantage over uncapped emission chains: once the calendar is spent, the sell side goes to zero permanently rather than settling at a floor.

The comparison that hurts is with exchange tokens running quarterly buyback-and-burn. Those programmes destroy what they buy, so every purchase is permanent and compounds; Optimism's buyback parks OP in a treasury that governance can spend again, which makes it an inventory transfer rather than a scarcity event. Worse, it is revenue-linked in a year when Superchain sequencer revenue fell far enough to trip the programme's own **$200,000** monthly floor, so the mechanism switched itself off precisely while **136.5M OP** was unlocking. Against hard-capped proof-of-work chains, OP shares the cap but not the credibility: Optimism's **4.29B** ceiling can be raised **2%** a year by a governance vote, where a halving schedule cannot be moved by anyone.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things. First, **Oct 11 2026** is the next dated release on the published schedule, a Seed Fund tranche whose size is not disclosed on any free surface; the arithmetic ceiling on everything still locked outside Foundation custody is **81.9M OP**, so that is the worst case the row could carry. Second, **Oct 28 2026** is the day the governance mint permission reopens, allowing up to **85.9M OP** of new issuance in a single vote; nothing has been proposed, and the last time the timer came up governance minted zero. Third, the buyback wallet — if a deposit lands there, Superchain revenue has recovered past the **$200,000** floor and Buy #1 turns positive again. Fourth, the **542.8M OP** that rotated custody on **Jul 22 2026** is now sellable and has not moved; the first transfer from those wallets to an exchange would be the signal that the unlock has become an actual sale.

## Summary

Optimism's OP is a **fixed-cap token that issues nothing and burns nothing**, so its supply story is entirely a distribution calendar. That calendar delivered its heaviest quarter in years: **136.5M OP** reached free hands as the four-year investor and core-contributor lock-up expired, giving **+5.97%** net for the window against a monitor read of **+6.40%** and a gap of just **0.43 percentage points**. The buy side offered nothing to absorb it — the revenue-funded buyback has been dormant since **May 1 2026**, and what it bought before that is held rather than burned. The key risk ahead is not issuance but decision: a **2.0B OP** Foundation overhang that has not moved a token in 90 days, plus a governance mint power capped at **85.9M OP** that unlocks on **Oct 28 2026**. With the vesting schedule spent, the projected next 90 days fall to **+0.77%**.
