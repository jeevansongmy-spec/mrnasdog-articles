**TL;DR.** Optimism is a fixed-cap token that is still inflationary by its vesting. OP can never exceed **4,294,967,296** and has **no protocol mint**, yet over the last 90 days about **136M OP** reached the market out of the original allocation and into the **2.29B** circulating float — through monthly cliffs and Foundation ecosystem deployment. A real revenue buyback absorbs only about **4.7M OP** a quarter, so the framework reads **+5.74% net** against a supply monitor at **+6.34%**. With a large **116M OP** unlock scheduled for Sep 21 2026, the next quarter looks even more inflationary at **+6.70%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending Aug 9 2026, the Pressure Framework reads **OP at +5.74% net**. Sell pressure totals about **136M OP** against buy pressure of about **4.7M OP**, on a circulating base of **2.29B OP**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change at **+6.34%**, a gap of **0.60 percentage points**, which clears the framework's half-point tolerance and ships a data-conflict flag on the OP overview. That gap is not hidden dilution: the float genuinely grew about **136M OP** — real vesting and ecosystem supply reaching the market — and most of the residual is a denominator convention, since the monitor divides growth by the supply **90 days ago** (about 2.15B) while the framework divides by today's **2.29B**. OP is best characterised as a **fixed-cap governance token that is still structurally inflationary on the active float**, because unlocks — not issuance — drive its supply.

## Sell pressure: where new OP comes from

None of it is fresh mint. Optimism governance can vote to mint up to about **2%** of supply a year, but that power has never been used — the on-chain total supply is flat at the **4,294,967,296 OP** genesis cap — so Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**. The real sell pressure is vesting. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is about **94M OP** last window and about **116M OP** next: the published schedule releases the locked allocation in monthly cliffs of roughly **31.3M OP** to investors, core contributors and the fund buckets, running for years toward **2029**, and the next window carries a notably large cliff of about **116M OP** around **Sep 21 2026**.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is about **42M OP**. On top of the scheduled cliffs, the Optimism Foundation deploys its Ecosystem Fund into grants and incentives, which reaches the open market outside the vesting tracker. The float grew about **136M OP** last quarter while the cliffs account for about **94M**, so roughly **42M** is this Foundation deployment. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: Optimism has no estate, trustee schedule or court order touching OP.

## Buy pressure: where new OP goes

Almost nowhere. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is the only non-zero row, at about **4.7M OP** a window. A governance vote that closed **Jan 28 2026** with **84.4%** approval committed **50%** of net Superchain sequencer revenue to monthly OP buybacks for a 12-month pilot, buying roughly **1.575M OP** a month. The catch is destination: the repurchased OP is **held in the Collective treasury, not burned**, so it parks supply rather than destroying it, and offsets only a small slice of the unlocks. On-chain, the buyback wallet had bought about **9.45M OP** by **Aug 6 2026**.

The other three buy rows are zero. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0** because gas on OP Mainnet and the other OP Stack chains is paid in **ETH**, so the base-fee burn destroys ETH, never OP. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** beyond that revenue buyback. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: there is no live OP staking or lock-up removing float. With the buy ledger nearly empty, the roughly **136M OP** of vesting and ecosystem supply reaches the market almost unopposed.

## Foundation and overhang

The standing overhang on OP is large and known. Of the **4,294,967,296 OP** fixed cap, about **2.29B** is circulating (~53%), leaving roughly **2.0B OP** still locked or undistributed — investor and core-contributor vesting plus the Foundation-managed ecosystem, governance, seed and partner funds. The Foundation's own Year-5 budget forecasts about **343M OP** entering circulation between **May 2026** and **April 2027** — Ecosystem Fund 200M, core contributors 47.6M, investors 15.3M, governance fund 10M — while Retro Funding and user airdrops are **paused** and the Collective cut new OP commitments about **35%** in Year 4.

Separately, the buyback treasury holds the roughly **9.45M OP** repurchased since February 2026 and is growing about **1.575M** a month. We re-read these buckets on every rebuild, so if the buyback treasury or any Foundation wallet starts sending OP to the market between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. Today the unlocks are the pressure and the buyback is a small, held-in-place offset.

## How OP compares to other L2 governance tokens

The right peer group for OP is not a hard-capped, halving chain — it is a large genesis allocation working through a multi-year vesting calendar. OP does have a fixed cap of **4.29B** and no block reward, which sounds deflationary, but the cap is not the same as a free float: only about half the supply is distributed, and the rest is still unlocking. Against a halving-model chain, whose new supply shrinks on schedule and whose float is largely already free, OP is the opposite shape — its float is climbing from roughly half toward full distribution, and the dilution comes from the vesting calendar, not from issuance.

The sharper comparison is with **Arbitrum**, the other large Ethereum-rollup governance token with a low initial float and years of scheduled unlocks. Both dilute through vesting rather than mint; the difference is the sink. Arbitrum has **no buyback and no ARB burn** at all, so its unlocks are fully unopposed. Optimism is one step further along: it has turned **50%** of Superchain sequencer revenue into an OP buyback, which ties token demand to network usage across Base, Unichain, Ink, World Chain, Soneium and OP Mainnet. But because the bought-back OP is **held, not burned**, the mechanism does not yet remove supply — it only parks it in the treasury, where a later governance vote could burn it, stake it, or redeploy it as incentives. Until that decision, OP's buyback is a demand signal, not a supply sink.

That distinction is what keeps OP inflationary despite a fixed cap and a real revenue buyback. A token whose fee revenue funded a **burn** could grow into deflation as usage rose; OP's revenue funds a buy that sits in custody. So the comparison that matters is not cap versus cap — it is **burn versus hold**. On that axis OP is a hold, and the unlock calendar wins.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the large scheduled cliff of about **116M OP** around **Sep 21 2026** — the single biggest release ahead and the reason the next-90-day read climbs to **+6.70%**, above the trailing one. Second, the monthly vesting cliffs of roughly **31.3M OP** on **Aug 31 2026** and **Oct 31 2026**, the steady baseline of new supply. Third, whether governance decides to **burn** the buyback treasury rather than hold it — a burn vote would turn the roughly **9.45M OP** already bought, and everything after it, into a genuine buy-side sink and materially change this reading. Fourth, whether the Foundation's ecosystem deployment slows as the pledged **35%** spending cut takes hold, which would ease Sell #3. Fifth, any move to activate the unused **2%** annual mint, which would push Sell #1 off zero for the first time.

## Summary

The Pressure Framework reads OP at **+5.74%** net new supply over the last 90 days and projects **+6.70%** over the next 90 — inflationary and rising into a large September cliff. The mechanism is **unlocks, not issuance**: the total supply is flat at the **4,294,967,296** cap, but about **136M OP** a quarter reaches the market as monthly vesting cliffs and Foundation ecosystem deployment, with a large **116M** cliff due around **Sep 21 2026**. The one offset — a Superchain-revenue buyback of about **4.7M OP** a window — is **held in treasury, not burned**, so it parks supply rather than removing it. Our monitor reads **+6.34%**, a **0.60 percentage-point** gap that is mostly a measurement-base convention on a genuinely growing float. The key risk is that the dilution has no real sink; the one thing that could flip the picture is a governance vote to burn the buyback treasury instead of holding it.
