Avalanche has a genuine hard cap of **720M AVAX**, and that cap is doing almost nothing for holders today, because the network is still **246.82M** tokens away from it. Over the 90 days to **Jul 27 2026** Avalanche minted roughly **2.81M AVAX** in staking rewards and released another **1.67M** from the Avalanche Foundation's quarterly vesting schedule, while burning only **34.2K AVAX** in transaction fees. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads AVAX at about **+1.03%** net on a circulating base of **431.77M AVAX**, against our supply monitor's **+0.08%** — a gap of **0.95 percentage points** that comes from a stale circulating figure, not from a disagreement about the chain. AVAX is **capped but structurally inflationary**, with a burn that no longer offsets the mint.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Jul 27 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **AVAX at about +1.03% net**: sell pressure of **4.48M AVAX** against buy pressure of **34.2K AVAX** on a circulating base of **431.77M AVAX**, with the forward window projected at **+1.04%**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.08%** for the same period, a gap of **0.95 percentage points**, which is well over the half-point tolerance, so a monitor-gap flag ships with this page. The deep walk closed it on mechanism rather than leaving it open. The classified circulating figure the monitor divides by has been pinned at exactly **431,771,961** every single day since **Feb 8 2026**, while the Avalanche P-Chain staking endpoint returned **473,178,882 AVAX** of minted supply on **Jul 27 2026** and the protocol kept issuing rewards throughout. The monitor's **+0.08%** is rounding noise around a frozen constant, not a measurement of flat supply. AVAX is best labelled **a hard-capped chain whose cap is decades away and whose float grows about 4% a year**.

## Sell pressure: where new AVAX comes from

New AVAX exists for exactly one reason on Avalanche, and it is worth stating plainly because it is unusual: validators are paid nothing except newly minted tokens. Avalanche burns **100%** of the transaction fees on its C-Chain, P-Chain and X-Chain, so there is no fee revenue to distribute and every unit of validator income is fresh issuance. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **2.81M AVAX** for the window. The Avalanche reward calculator pays out a slice of whatever is still unminted under the cap: with **473.18M** AVAX minted and a **720M** ceiling, **246.82M** remains available, and each staker earns in proportion to their share of total supply and the length of their commitment. An average of **207.33M AVAX** was staked across the window at a realised rate of about **5.5%** a year, which is what produces the **2.81M** figure. That rate is not a guess: Avalanche's own ACP-285 proposal states the live spread between a 14-day and a 365-day stake as **1.02 percentage points**, and the on-chain supply read reproduces that spread as **1.003** percentage points independently.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **1.67M AVAX**, and it is the one dated event on the Avalanche calendar. The Avalanche Foundation's own allocation releases **1,667,000 AVAX** on a quarterly cliff that runs to **2030**, with **28.34M** still to come. One release fell inside the trailing window and the next lands **Aug 10 2026**. These are genesis tokens sitting in P-Chain time-locks that simply open on their date, so nothing has to be claimed and the scheduled quantum is the released quantum. Every other Avalanche launch allocation — team, seed, private sale, the three public sale tranches, the airdrop, strategic partners, the community and development endowment, the incentivised testnet — is already **100%** released, which is why the Foundation cliff is the only vesting line left. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** in value, discussed below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: no estate, no trustee distribution and no court-ordered sale touches AVAX.

## Buy pressure: where new AVAX goes

Avalanche has a real burn and it is one of the cleanest in the industry, which makes what has happened to it more striking. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **34.2K AVAX** for the window. Unlike Ethereum, where the priority fee is paid to the block producer, Avalanche destroys the entire transaction fee, base and tip together, on all three of its built-in chains. In **2021** and **2022** that burn removed millions of AVAX; cumulatively it has taken roughly **9.7M** tokens out of existence, which is the whole difference between the **473.18M** the P-Chain has minted and the **463.44M** that still exists. But ACP-125 cut the C-Chain minimum base fee from **25** to **1** in the smallest denomination, and the burn collapsed with it. C-Chain fees ran **24.9K AVAX** in February **2026**, **16.3K** in March, **14.2K** in April and **9.4K** in May — about **380 AVAX** a day across the window. Against a mint of **2.81M**, the burn now offsets barely **1%**. Cheap transactions were the right call for adoption; they were not free for holders.

The other three buy rows are all **zero**, and each for a structural reason. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero** because Avalanche has no buyback of any kind and, unusually, no obvious way to build one: the fee revenue that a buyback would normally spend is already destroyed at source. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**; the Avalanche Foundation is a seller of its own allocation, not a buyer of AVAX. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**, and this is where AVAX differs sharply from the vote-escrow tokens it is often compared with. Staking on Avalanche is short and reversible, with a two-week minimum and a one-year maximum, and it shrank over this window: staked weight fell from **229.6M** to **210.89M AVAX**, so holders released **18.7M** more than they committed. There is no lock-up to book on the buy side, only an unwind.

## Foundation and overhang

Four pools of AVAX sit outside ordinary market hands. The first is the Avalanche Foundation's remaining vesting allocation: **28.34M AVAX** still on the quarterly schedule out to **2030**, released **1.67M** at a time and refreshed on every walk. The second is the broader non-circulating bucket — **31.67M AVAX**, the difference between the **463.44M** that exists and the **431.77M** classified as freely tradable — which has no single published release calendar beyond the Foundation cliff. The third is the Foundation's discretionary sales programme, which is real and has fired once: **7.32M AVAX** went to Avalanche Treasury Company in **October 2025** for **$50M** at a **60%** discount under a Token Sale Agreement, and that buyer holds an 18-month priority over further Avalanche Foundation token sales.

The fourth is that buyer itself, and it is the most interesting overhang on the AVAX ledger. Avalanche Treasury Company listed on Nasdaq on **Jun 11 2026** through a **$675M** merger and holds roughly **13.39M AVAX** and staked AVAX. Its first quarterly filing, made **Jun 29 2026**, disclosed a **$26.8M** net loss, a **$9.1M** working-capital deficit, nearly **$142M** of unrealised losses on its AVAX position and substantial doubt about its ability to continue as a going concern. That would normally read as an imminent sell risk, and the reason it is booked at **zero** rather than as sell pressure is specific: the AVAX delivered under the Token Sale Agreement carries transfer restrictions for **5 years** from the transaction date. The rule that governs all four pools is the same — if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How AVAX compares to other hard-capped proof-of-stake chains

AVAX belongs to a small class: chains that took Bitcoin's idea of a fixed maximum supply and grafted it onto proof-of-stake. The comparison that matters is not with Bitcoin itself, where the issuance schedule is halved on a fixed block count and the remaining supply is nearly exhausted, but with the other capped proof-of-stake designs, and with the uncapped continuous-emission chains that reject the cap entirely. On the mechanism questions the Pressure Framework asks, AVAX sits in an awkward middle. It does have a hard cap, and that cap is enforced in the reward calculator rather than promised in a blog post — every reward is explicitly a fraction of the unminted remainder, so issuance mathematically decays toward zero as supply approaches **720M**. But with **246.82M AVAX** still unminted, that decay is a story about the **2040s**, not about this quarter. A cap is a ceiling, not a brake.

Where AVAX genuinely differs from an uncapped emission chain is on the burn. Avalanche destroys every unit of transaction fee on all three of its chains, with no share paid to validators — stricter than Ethereum, which burns only the base fee, and stricter than most Cosmos-style chains, which burn nothing and pay fees to stakers outright. In a high-activity regime that burn is a serious counterweight, and it once was. What ACP-125 demonstrated is that a fee burn is a function of fee level, not of transaction count: Avalanche processes millions of transactions a day and burns almost nothing, because each one costs almost nothing. The chains that stay deflationary through cheap blockspace are the ones that burn a share of something other than gas — an exchange token burning a slice of trading revenue, or a protocol routing fee income into buybacks. Avalanche routes its fee income into the fire before anyone can spend it, which is elegant, and which currently amounts to **1%** of the mint.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First and most important, the Helicon upgrade and ACP-285. Avalanche shipped Helicon as a test-network pre-release on **Jul 21 2026** with activation there on **Jul 28 2026**, and it carries ACP-285, which cuts the minimum consumption rate in the reward calculator from **10%** to **7.5%**. Its own authors project that this takes **0.5** to **1** percentage point a year off AVAX issuance, phased in linearly over **90** days after activation. It is still marked as proposed and no mainnet date has been set, which is exactly why this page keeps the current rate — but a scheduled mainnet Helicon would be the single largest improvement to the AVAX inflation reading in years. Second, the Foundation cliff on **Aug 10 2026**, worth **1.67M AVAX**. Third, the staked balance, which fell **18.7M AVAX** over this window; because rewards scale with the staked share of supply, a continued unwind mechanically reduces the mint as well as adding float. Fourth, Avalanche Treasury Company's next filing, which will show whether its going-concern warning has been resolved and whether any of its **13.39M AVAX** has moved despite the five-year restriction. Fifth, the classified circulating figure itself: it has been frozen since **Feb 8 2026**, and the day it is corrected our monitor will print a large one-off jump that is bookkeeping, not issuance.

## Summary

AVAX is the clearest case on our catalogue of a hard cap that does not deliver low inflation. Avalanche mints roughly **2.81M AVAX** a quarter in staking rewards and releases another **1.67M** from the Avalanche Foundation's vesting schedule, while its fee burn — genuinely total, covering every fee on every chain — removed only **34.2K AVAX** over the same window after the base-fee cut. That leaves a net **+1.03%** increase in the tradable float for the last 90 days and a projected **+1.04%** for the next, roughly **4%** a year. The key risk is that nothing on the buy side is growing: there is no buyback, the burn scales with fee prices that were deliberately cut, and staking is unwinding rather than locking. The ceiling is real and enforced in code at **720M**, but **246.82M AVAX** of headroom remains, so the discipline it imposes is a matter of decades rather than quarters — unless ACP-285 reaches mainnet.
