Over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** Avalanche created **2.19M AVAX** to pay its stakers and released a further **1.67M AVAX** from the Avalanche Foundation's vesting cliff on **Jul 26 2026**, for sell pressure of **3.86M AVAX**. On the other side there is no buyback at all and the C-Chain fee burn destroyed just **34,955 AVAX** — one coin removed for every sixty-three created. On a circulating base of **431.77M AVAX** the Pressure Framework reads **+0.89%** net against our supply monitor's **+0.01%**, a gap of **0.88 percentage points** that ships with a flag. AVAX is **a hard-capped chain that is nowhere near its cap and is still growing its float at roughly 3.6% a year**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 19 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **AVAX at +0.89% net**: sell pressure of **3.86M AVAX** against buy pressure of **0.035M AVAX**, on a circulating base of **431.77M AVAX**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.01%** for the same window, a gap of **0.88 percentage points**, and because that is over the half-point tolerance a monitor-gap flag ships on this build. The gap has a single, checkable cause: the circulating-supply figure the monitor divides by has printed exactly **431,771,961 AVAX** every day since **Feb 6 2026** — 194 days without moving — while Avalanche's own supply counter went from **468,399,310** to **473,667,721 AVAX** over the same period and two Foundation cliffs of **1,667,000** each landed on top. A denominator that has not moved in six months cannot register six months of issuance. Avalanche is best labelled **structurally inflationary on the active float, with a hard cap that binds in the 2030s rather than now**.

## Sell pressure: where new AVAX comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **2.19M AVAX**, and the way it was measured matters more than the number. Avalanche keeps one supply counter for the whole network on its P-Chain, and every P-Chain block carries the running value, so the counter can be read at any past height rather than modelled. It read **471,473,075 AVAX** at the block closest to **May 21 2026** and **473,667,721 AVAX** at the block closest to **Aug 19 2026**. That is **2,194,646 AVAX** of genuinely new coins, minted to pay validators and delegators. Running the protocol's own reward rule instead predicts about **2.72M**, and Avalanche's published emission model predicts about **2.76M**. Both sit roughly a quarter above the measurement, in the direction you would expect from models that assume every staked coin earns continuously. The measured figure governs here. The pace is also violently uneven, because a stake only pays out when its term ends: across nine consecutive ten-day legs the chain minted **57K**, **26K**, **13K**, **11K**, **35K**, **20K**, **10K**, **34K** and **13K** AVAX a day. Any arithmetic built on a short sample would have been wrong by two or three times.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **1.67M AVAX**. Every original AVAX allocation is now fully released except one: the Avalanche Foundation's **66,680,000 AVAX** tranche, which pays out in quarterly cliffs of **1,667,000** and still has **28,339,000 AVAX** to give, running to 2030. Team, strategic partners, both public sales, the seed and private rounds, the airdrop, the community endowment and the testnet programme are all at 100%. One cliff fell inside this window, on **Jul 26 2026**, and one falls inside the next, on **Oct 25 2026**. Getting the date right took work: four public unlock trackers quoted four different dates for the same event — **Feb 11**, **May 12**, **Jul 25**, **Aug 10** and **Nov 8 2026** — while all agreeing on the size. Only the dated per-event release list produces the clean 91-day rhythm the schedule actually follows.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**, and Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** as well. The Avalanche Foundation demonstrably holds AVAX and demonstrably sells it — that is how it funds the ecosystem — but no dated release to market landed inside this window, so the framework books nothing and watches the balance instead. On the bankruptcy side, a large estate made its fifth creditor distribution on **Jul 31 2026**, inside the window, but it pays creditors in cash and liquidated its AVAX years ago.

## Buy pressure: where new AVAX goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **34,955 AVAX** and it is the only thing on this side of the ledger that moves at all. Avalanche burns the entire transaction fee on its C-Chain — not just the base fee but the priority tip as well — because there is no fee recipient to pay: every block on the chain reports the burn address as its reward address. That is worth stating plainly, because it is the single most common error in reading Avalanche's burn. Treating the tip as validator income, the way Ethereum works, understates the burn by a factor of roughly five. Even at full strength the burn is nearly irrelevant against the mint: **34,955 AVAX** destroyed against **2.19M** created is one coin in sixty-three, and the trend is downwards — Avalanche burned **24,898 AVAX** in February 2026 and **6,781** in the first nineteen days of August. Three independent readings agree on the quarter: the chain's own fee series gives **34,955**, gas used multiplied by average gas price gives **34,331**, and a chain-fee aggregator's dollar series implies the same volume at an average AVAX price of **$6.84**.

The other three buy rows are zero, each for a structural reason. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: Avalanche has no buyback contract, no revenue share and no treasury mandated to purchase AVAX. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**: the Avalanche Foundation is a net seller of AVAX by design and has never announced an open-market purchase. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** despite **216.24M AVAX** being staked, and the sign was checked before that call was made — the staked total actually rose over the window, from about **204.81M** to **212.64M**. It is still not a lock in the sense this row means, because staked AVAX sits inside the counted circulating figure the whole time and every Avalanche stake ends at its own chosen term and returns automatically.

## Foundation and overhang

Three overhangs are tracked on AVAX. The largest is the unminted staking-reward budget: **246.33M AVAX**, the difference between the **720,000,000** cap and the **473,667,721** ever created. It is not discretionary — nobody can release it by decision, and it drains only through Sell #1 — but it is more than half the current float and it is the reason the cap does not constrain anything today. Second is the Avalanche Foundation's undelivered genesis allocation, **28,339,000 AVAX** held in on-chain locktime outputs and released on the quarterly calendar already described; the Foundation's addresses are not published and the chain exposes no aggregate of locked outputs, so this one is walked from the dated schedule rather than read from an escrow, and that assumption is recorded. Third is a listed treasury company built to hold AVAX, which finished its merger on **Jun 11 2026** and holds roughly **15M AVAX** bought from the Foundation before **Mar 31 2026**; its exchange has given it until **Feb 2 2027** to cure listing deficiencies, which is the clearest route by which a block that size could be forced onto the market. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How AVAX compares to other hard-capped proof-of-stake chains

AVAX belongs to a small class: proof-of-stake chains with a genuine, protocol-encoded maximum supply. Most large proof-of-stake networks are uncapped and mint whatever their bonded ratio demands, so their supply has no ceiling at all. Avalanche cannot: **720,000,000** is the asymptote and the reward for every stake is calculated as a slice of what is left unminted, so the emission rate falls automatically as the budget drains. That is a real structural advantage over an uncapped peer. It is also, at this stage, mostly theoretical — with **246.33M AVAX** of the budget still unissued, the cap is a fact about the 2030s, and today's holder faces roughly **3.6%** annualised supply growth on the counted float, of which about **2.1%** is fresh issuance.

Against halving-model chains with hard caps, the comparison is less flattering. A halving chain's issuance steps down on a fixed calendar independent of participation. Avalanche's issuance instead depends on how much AVAX is staked, which moved between roughly **197M** and **213M** inside this window alone — the emission is capped but not scheduled, and it can rise if staking rises. Against fee-burn chains, the comparison turns on scale. A base-fee burn only offsets issuance when blockspace is genuinely contested; Avalanche burns its whole fee, tip included, which is more aggressive than the Ethereum design, and it still only removes **1.6%** of what it mints, because C-Chain fees have collapsed to a few hundred AVAX a day.

The genuinely distinctive feature is the three-chain structure, and it is a trap for anyone measuring supply. AVAX lives on the P-Chain, the X-Chain and the C-Chain, and a reader who sees three ledgers naturally reaches for three supply figures to add together. There is only one. The P-Chain counter is network-wide, equal to the **360,000,000** genesis allocation plus every staking reward ever paid; the X-Chain AVAX asset is fixed-cap with no mint function, and C-Chain AVAX is the native gas coin with no supply function at all. Coins move between the three ledgers by atomic transfer, which conserves the total. Summing anything here would double-count the entire cross-chain float.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things would move this reading. The Foundation's next vesting cliff of **1,667,000 AVAX** falls on **Oct 25 2026** and is already booked into the forward column. The Helicon upgrade — six Avalanche Community Proposals that between them rework staking economics, including a proposal to cut the minimum consumption rate that sets the reward floor — activated on the Fuji testnet on **Jul 28 2026** but has no announced mainnet date; if it activates, Sell #1 falls and this page changes shape. The staked total is the third: because Avalanche's reward is proportional to the staked share, issuance rises when staking rises, and it climbed roughly **8M AVAX** over this window. Fourth, the listed AVAX treasury company faces a **Feb 2 2027** deadline to restore its share price and market value; a forced unwind of its **15M AVAX** would be four times the size of a quarterly Foundation cliff.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads AVAX at **+0.89%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** and projects the same for the next 90. Avalanche minted **2.19M AVAX** to pay stakers and released **1.67M** more from the Avalanche Foundation's vesting cliff, while its C-Chain fee burn — which destroys the priority tip as well as the base fee — removed only **34,955 AVAX**, one coin in sixty-three. The key risk is not the cap, which is real but leaves **246.33M AVAX** still unissued and binds nothing this decade; it is that the only mechanism working against issuance depends entirely on C-Chain fee demand, and that demand has fallen by three quarters since February. The number to distrust is the headline one: the circulating-supply figure most of the market quotes for AVAX has been frozen at **431,771,961** since **Feb 6 2026**, and the real float is roughly **8.9M AVAX** higher.
