GT is the exchange token of Gate and the staking and gas asset of GateChain, and over the 90 days to **Aug 23 2026** the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **0.11M GT** of sell pressure against **zero** buy pressure on a circulating base of **106,601,940 GT**, for a net of **+0.10%**. Gate did burn **2,570,063 GT** on **Jul 4 2026**, lifting the lifetime burn to **189,947,220 GT** — about **63%** of the original 300,000,000 — and that burn is completely real. Every burned GT came out of a reserve wallet that has received nothing since 2019, and that reserve is precisely the slice of supply the circulating count already excludes, so the ceiling fell and the tradable GT float did not move by a single token. Meanwhile GateChain paid its consensus nodes in newly issued GT. GT is **slightly inflationary, not deflationary**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 23 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **GT at +0.10% net**: sell pressure of **0.11M GT**, buy pressure of **0**, on a circulating base of **106,601,940 GT**. The next 90 days read **+0.10%** as well, because the only mechanism that adds GT — GateChain consensus issuance — is continuous, and the only mechanism that removes it, the quarterly burn expected around **Oct 2026**, draws on the excluded reserve. Our supply monitor reads **-0.26%** for the same window, a gap of **0.36 percentage points**, inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag is raised on this build; the monitor's own reading scatters about a third of a point either side of zero on a float that barely moved, and its steadier seven-day figure for the same window is **+0.08%**. The right label for GT is **an exchange token whose burn shrinks the ceiling while its own chain slowly grows the float**.

## Sell pressure: where new GT comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0.11M GT**, and it is the row that decides the sign of this page. GT is almost always described as fixed at **300,000,000**, and on Ethereum that description is exactly right: the GateToken contract is a 2019 ERC-20 with no mint function anywhere in its verified source, no proxy and no upgrade path, so the supply read was **300,000,000.000000** at both ends of this window and always will be. GateChain is the part that gets missed. It is a proof-of-stake chain, and it pays its consensus nodes in newly issued GT drawn from a **30,000,000 GT** allocation of which Gate's own supply page shows **8,748,062 GT** mined and **21,251,938 GT** still unmined, at a current annualised yield of about **0.99%**. That issuance is measurable independently: the published total supply minus what survives on Ethereum leaves **8,800,392 GT** that exists only on the GateChain side, and that residual grew by **107,640 GT** across this window. Two angles, ten percent apart.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0**. GT finished its distribution in **2019** through the Gate Points sale and has never had a vesting contract, a cliff calendar or an escrow to read. The one unlock-tracker page that claims otherwise for GT prints a next release of **283.7 billion** tokens for a token whose entire genesis was **300 million**, which is not a schedule but a fabrication, so it is discarded and the question settled against the wallets themselves.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** — the ability to sell is not the same as a decision to sell — but the overhang is listed rather than waved away, and for GT it is unusually large. The frozen 2019 reserve holds **12,251,232 GT** and only ever pays the burn. The executor wallet that routes each burn holds **12,620,975 GT**, drawn out of that reserve in **Jan 2026** and not yet destroyed. Three further Gate wallets hold **52,399,955 GT**, **24,323,323 GT** and **7,038,973 GT**, and none of them has moved a GT since **Jan 20 2026**. Sell #4 is **0**: GT has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new GT goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**, and this single row is the whole GT story. The quarterly burn is real, it fired inside this window, and it settles on Ethereum: **2,570,063 GT** reached the burn address on **Jul 4 2026** in one transaction, announced two days later, taking the burn address from **187,377,156 GT** to **189,947,220 GT**. What decides the row is who sent the coins, because a treasury-funded burn and a market-funded burn look identical on a balance chart and are opposite in the ledger. Traced by sender, the path is two hops on the same day: the frozen 2019 reserve paid the executor wallet, and the executor paid the burn address. The reserve has received nothing since **2019**. There is no repurchase leg anywhere in its life.

The decisive test is the reserve measured against the non-circulating bucket. Total supply of **118,853,172 GT** minus circulating supply of **106,601,940 GT** leaves **12,251,232 GT**, which is, to nine decimal places, the balance of the reserve that paid for the burn. That reserve **is** the non-circulating bucket, so every burn reduces the ceiling and the reserve by the same number and leaves the tradable float untouched — which is exactly what the supply series shows, a flat band near **106.5M GT** for the whole window with no step across **Jul 4 2026**. Gate's published rule, that a share of quarterly profit funds the burn, is the formula that sizes it, not the wallet that pays for it. Booking the **2,570,063 GT** as absorbed float would have printed **-2.41%** of deflation that no GT holder experienced on any order book.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and here the mechanism exists but the volume does not. GateChain adopted a base-fee burn with its v1.1.6 upgrade in **2024**, so every transaction destroys a little GT. The chain runs under a tenth of a transaction per block, and sampling blocks across this window gives a measured base-fee burn of about **4.5 GT** over 90 days. Gate Layer, the newer layer-2 where GT is the exclusive gas token, produced roughly **91 GT** of base fee over the same period and routes it to a fee vault rather than destroying it. Together that is under a ten-thousandth of a percent of the float. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** — no open-market purchase of GT by Gate is disclosed or observed at any tracked wallet. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: no new lockup was created, and GT's only long-term reserve got smaller this window rather than larger.

## Foundation and overhang

The frozen 2019 reserve holds **12,251,232 GT** as of **Aug 23 2026** and is refreshed against the chain every day. It held exactly **30,000,000 GT** until **Jan 20 2026**, matching Gate's description of an insurance fund that can only be used for burns, and it sits outside the circulating count — it **is** the gap between total and circulating supply. At the current quantum it holds about four to five more quarterly burns. The executor wallet holds **12,620,975 GT**, identical at both ends of this window because it takes in and pays out on the same day; that balance left the reserve in **Jan 2026** and has only been drawn down by **2,570,063 GT** since, so roughly **12.6M GT** has already left the excluded bucket without reaching a market — a reserve-to-float reclassification waiting to happen rather than a sale. Three company wallets hold **52,399,955 GT**, **24,323,323 GT** and **7,038,973 GT**; all three sit inside the circulating count already, which makes the largest of them the more important risk, because it needs no unlock, vote or schedule to reach an order book. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh; and if a future burn is ever funded from an address other than the reserve, Buy #1 stops being zero on the float at the next refresh.

## How GT compares to other exchange tokens

Exchange tokens are usually ranked by how loud their burn is, and by that measure GT wins: **63%** of the original supply destroyed is among the deepest reductions in the sector. The mechanism-level question is narrower. Does the burn consume tokens that were tradable, and was the money that bought them raised outside the token? A revenue-funded market buyback — the exchange earns fees in other assets, buys its own token on an order book and destroys it — removes real float and is genuinely deflationary. A reserve-funded burn writes down an allocation the market never held; it lowers the fully-diluted ceiling and improves the long-run dilution picture, but on the day it fires nobody's GT became scarcer. GT is firmly in the second class, and the on-chain evidence is unusually clean because the funding wallet's balance matches the non-circulating bucket to nine decimals.

GT differs from most of its peers in a second way that cuts the other direction. An exchange token issued once as an ERC-20 has no issuance risk at all; GT does, because GateChain is a real proof-of-stake chain and pays its validators in new GT. That places GT somewhere between the pure fixed-supply exchange tokens, whose only inflation risk is release from a treasury, and continuous-emission layer-1s, whose supply grows every block by design. It also separates GT from fee-burn designs, where the destroyed tokens are paid by users out of circulating balances and a busy chain removes float continuously — GateChain's own base-fee burn is that design, but the chain is too quiet for it to matter. The net effect is that GT's headline deflation and its measured supply change point in opposite directions, and the measured one is **+0.10%**.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The Q3 2026 quarterly burn is expected around **Oct 2026**, on the schedule set by the previous settlements on **Jul 6 2025**, **Oct 15 2025**, **Jan 8 2026**, **Apr 26 2026** and **Jul 4 2026**; the number to read is not its size but the address that funds it. Second, the frozen reserve balance: at **12,251,232 GT** it holds four to five more burns, and the quarter it empties is the quarter the burn must either stop or start touching supply that counts. Third, the executor wallet's **12,620,975 GT** — an outflow from it that does not end at the burn address would be the first realised release in GT's history and lands in Sell #3. Fourth, the GateChain consensus allocation, **21,251,938 GT** still unmined, whose issuance rate is the only thing currently adding GT; a validator-set or yield change resizes Sell #1 directly. Fifth, Gate Layer adoption — GT is its exclusive gas token, and real traffic there would finally make the base-fee burn a number rather than a rounding error.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads GT at **+0.10% net** supply change over the 90 days to **Aug 23 2026**, and **+0.10%** again for the next 90 days, against a supply monitor reading **-0.26%** — a gap of **0.36 percentage points** and no data conflict to flag. The structural mechanism is a genuine quarterly burn, **2,570,063 GT** on **Jul 4 2026** and **189,947,220 GT** in total, funded entirely from a frozen 2019 reserve that equals the whole non-circulating bucket, set against GateChain consensus issuance of about **0.11M GT** a quarter that most descriptions of GT leave out. The key risk is not issuance but concentration: one Gate wallet already holds **52,399,955 GT** inside the float, and no unlock stands between it and an order book. The ceiling is **300,000,000 GT** on Ethereum and can never rise there, but GT is slightly inflationary rather than deflationary, and it stays that way until a burn is funded by buying rather than by writing down a shelf.
