Gate has burned about **189.9M GT** — roughly **63%** of the original 300M supply — and the token is marketed as deflationary, yet the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **GT at +0.15%** net supply over the last 90 days: slightly inflationary, not deflationary. Two things drive that. First, GateChain is a proof-of-stake chain that mints new GT as consensus rewards — about **0.16M GT** over 90 days — so GT is not fixed-supply. Second, the quarterly burn is real but float-neutral: the **2,570,063 GT** retired on **Jul 4 2026** came from a frozen 2019 reserve, not the open market, and the tradable count of **106.58M GT** never stepped down. Our supply monitor reads **−4.875%**, a gap of **5.03 percentage points** that traces to a cold-wallet reclassification, not a real burn. GT is a **heavily-burned exchange token that still mints faster than its burn touches the float**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **May 5 2026** to **Aug 3 2026**, the framework reads **GT at +0.15% net**: sell pressure of **0.16M GT** in GateChain PoS issuance against buy pressure of **zero** on the tradable float, over a base of **106.58M GT**. Our supply monitor reads **−4.875%** for the same period, a gap of **5.03 percentage points**, well past the framework's half-point tolerance, so the page ships with a data-conflict flag. That gap is not a disagreement about how much GT was burned — it is a classification artifact. The monitor's own supply reading fell in a single **6.1M** step around **May 12 2026** and then held flat near **106.5M** for the rest of the window, yet no burn hit the burn address in May — its only in-window deposit was the **2,570,063 GT** retired on **Jul 4 2026** — so that step is a wallet re-tag, not a real reduction. GT is best labelled a **burned-but-still-minting exchange token: deflationary in cumulative total supply, faintly inflationary on the tradable float**.

## Sell pressure: where new GT comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **0.16M GT**, and it is the row that quietly defines GT. GateChain is a proof-of-stake network, and it pays validators and stakers consensus rewards in newly issued GT: about **8.55M GT** over five and a half years by Gate's own account, running today at roughly a **0.99%** annualised staking yield, or about **0.16M GT** per 90 days and falling. It is small, but it is genuine new supply — which means GT is not the fixed-supply token it is usually described as. On a page where the whole question is how much new GT reaches the market, this is the only mechanism creating any.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**. GT launched in **2019** with no ICO, and the original 300M allocation is fully distributed; any team or advisor vesting expired years ago, and trackers show no scheduled unlock in the next 90 days. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** on observed behaviour, with the wallets enumerated below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Gate is a going concern, and no estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches GT. So the entire sell side of GT is a single small issuance row; there is no unlock cliff and no distribution overhang firing this window.

## Buy pressure: where new GT goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is the mechanism GT is famous for, and it reads **zero on the float**, which needs explaining because the burn is completely real. Gate runs a quarterly buyback-and-burn, marketed as **15%** of trading profits used to buy back and destroy GT, and the Q2 2026 burn retired **2,570,063 GT**, sent to the burn address on **Jul 4 2026**. The catch is where the GT came from: on-chain, it was drawn from a **frozen 2019 reserve wallet** that has had no market inflow since 2020, routed through an executor to the burn address — not bought off the open market. So total supply falls — the burn address now holds about **189.9M GT** — while the tradable count does not: it read about **106,483,500** on **Jul 3**, **Jul 4** and **Jul 6 2026** alike, with no step on the day the tokens were destroyed. Booking a 2.57M burn as 2.57M of absorbed float would have printed a **−2.4%** reading no GT holder experienced on the market.

The rest of the buy ledger is empty for related reasons. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**: GateChain does burn a share of gas as part of its dual-engine design, but that amount is folded into the quarterly on-chain figure and is not separately measurable this session, so it carries a placeholder zero. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**, with no disclosed Gate purchase of GT into a counted wallet beyond the burn path. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: Gate's earn and staking products pay out of existing pools, and no new escrow or lockup contract was created for GT in this window. Nothing on the buy side absorbed float; the only real removal was reserve GT Gate already held off-market.

## Foundation and overhang

GT carries three identified Gate-controlled overhangs, all read on-chain this session, and the distinctions between them are the whole story. The first is a frozen 2019 reserve holding **12,251,232 GT** — which, to the token, is the exact gap between the **118.83M** total supply and the **106.58M** tradable float, so this single wallet is the entire non-circulating bucket. It has had no market inflow since 2020 and only ever feeds the quarterly burn, so it is shrinking rather than selling — at roughly **2.6M GT** a quarter it holds on the order of five more quarters of burns. The second, and the one that matters more, is a consolidated Gate cold wallet holding about **52.4M GT** that sits **inside** the counted float already, consolidated in **January 2026**; it needs no unlock to reach an order book, and this window it showed no genuine distribution. The third is the burn-executor wallet, about **12.62M GT**, that routes reserve GT to the burn address. So the structure to watch on GT is not an unlock — there are none — but whether the reserve empties (forcing the burn onto supply that actually trades) or the cold wallet moves toward a market. If any of these balances falls toward an order book rather than the burn address, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How GT compares to other exchange tokens

The peer group is exchange tokens with quarterly burn programmes, and GT sits in an unusual spot within it. The textbook exchange-token burn is revenue-funded end to end — the venue takes a slice of profit, buys the token on the open market, and destroys what it bought — so the burn is simultaneously a bid and a supply cut, and both halves land on the tradable float. GT's realised burn this window behaved differently: the tokens were drawn from a frozen 2019 reserve Gate already held, so there is a total-supply cut but no verifiable market bid, which makes the burn genuine in accounting terms and inert in market terms. That is why a token that has destroyed **63%** of its supply still reads flat-to-slightly-up here rather than deflationary. It places GT alongside other exchange tokens whose burns eat an excluded or held allocation rather than the market, and away from the fee-burn chains where every burned unit comes out of a circulating balance and the float genuinely shrinks.

GT differs from both groups in one direction that works against it: it has its own layer-1, GateChain, and that chain **mints** new GT as proof-of-stake rewards. An uncapped fee-burn L1 dilutes holders through issuance and claws some back through burns; a classic exchange token neither mints nor issues. GT does both — it issues consensus rewards **and** burns reserve — and this window the issuance reached the float while the burn did not, which is exactly why the net is a small positive rather than a large negative. So GT is neither a clean deflation story nor a clean inflation risk. It is a heavily-burned token whose cumulative scarcity is real, whose quarterly burn is real, and whose float nonetheless drifts faintly upward because the mint is live and the burn, for now, is not on the market.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q3 2026 quarterly burn, expected inside this window around **early Oct 2026** on the quarterly rhythm — the number to check is not the size but the funding wallet, because a burn paid with GT bought off the open market rather than from the reserve would turn Buy #1 non-zero and flip the net negative. Second, the consolidated cold wallet holding about **52.4M GT** inside the float, and whether its balance falls toward an order book instead of sitting idle. Third, the frozen 2019 reserve of **12,251,232 GT**, which equals the entire non-circulating bucket and holds about five more quarters of burns before the burn must find supply elsewhere. Fourth, the GateChain PoS issuance rate — currently about **0.99%** annualised and declining — since it is the only live supply-add and sets the floor under the net. Fifth, any change to how the burn is sized away from a flat profit percentage, which would change the quarterly quantum.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Gate's GT at **+0.15%** net supply change over the last 90 days and projects the same for the next 90 — slightly inflationary, not deflationary. GT has burned about **63%** of its original supply and burned another **2,570,063 GT** on **Jul 4 2026**, but that burn was funded from a frozen 2019 reserve, so total supply fell while the tradable float held near **106.5M**. Against a flat float, the one live supply-add is GateChain's PoS issuance of about **0.16M GT** a quarter, which is why the net tilts faintly positive. The thing to watch is not an unlock — there are none — but the quarter Gate's burn reserve runs low, because that is when the burn must start absorbing supply that actually trades. For now the cumulative scarcity is real and the float simply drifts.
