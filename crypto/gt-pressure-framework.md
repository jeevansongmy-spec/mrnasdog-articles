# GT Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply shrinking · projected to keep shrinking

The MrNasDog Pressure Framework reads Gate's GT as a shrinking coin: net supply fell **1.80%** over the last 90 days and is projected to fall about **1.95%** over the next 90. Gate's quarterly buyback-and-burn removed **1,987,321 GT** on Oct 4 2026, while GateChain's block reward added only **102,987 GT**. The inflation monitor reads **−1.68%**, a gap of **0.12 points**, so the two readings agree. The one thing that could undo the burn is Gate's **12.25M GT** frozen reserve, which has twice refilled the burn wallet this year.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 17 to Oct 15 2026, GT supply moved by **−1.80%** of the **104.68M GT** in circulation: **102,987 GT** of new coins against **1,987,418 GT** destroyed. The inflation monitor, which measures the circulating count from the outside, shows **−1.68%** for nearly the same window. The gap is **+0.12 percentage points**, well inside our 0.5-point line, so no warning chip is shown. For the next 90 days we expect one more quarterly burn of about **2.15M GT** and the same small block reward, for a net of about **−1.95%**. GT is an exchange coin that shrinks by buyback-and-burn, with a slow chain reward on the other side.

## Sell pressure: where new GT comes from

Protocol inflation is the only real source of new GT. GateChain, the chain Gate built for GT, creates **0.4 GT** in every block to pay the validators who secure it. Blocks came about every 30.2 seconds in the window, which gave **102,987 GT**, about 1,144 GT a day. We checked the reward block by block: every sampled block minted exactly 0.4 GT, and the total created since launch, about **8.86M GT**, grew at the same pace since Gate last reported it in January 2026. The reward comes from a pool of 30M GT, so about 21.14M GT is still left to pay out, which at this rate lasts for decades.

Vesting unlocks book **0**. Unlock trackers still show **1.5M GT** opening on the 26th of every month until July 2030, carried over from GT's 2019 plan. We could not find a locked wallet behind that line. The only GT not counted as circulating is Gate's frozen reserve, and it did not move in these 90 days, while the GT still "locked" on the trackers is far larger than everything outside circulation. So any coins that open are already counted, and they add no new supply.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks book **0** for both windows, but this is the row to watch. Gate's frozen reserve held **12,251,232 GT** at both ends of the window. It sent 15.18M GT to Gate's burn wallet on Jan 20 2026 and 2.57M GT on Jul 4 2026, one hour after that quarter's burn and the same size, so that burn did not shrink the circulating count at all. Gate's January 2026 rules say the reserve is settled "in line with" future burns, but the Apr 26 and Oct 4 2026 burns were not matched, so there is no fixed date to project. Long-term locks and bankruptcy releases book **0**: there is no estate or trustee, and the 52.40M GT held in the bridge lock on Ethereum backs GT that already moves on GateChain.

## Buy pressure: where new GT goes

The programmatic buyback is the big number. Every quarter Gate buys GT back and sends it to a burn address that no one can spend from. The Q3 2026 burn on Oct 4 2026 sent **1,987,321 GT** there, worth about **$22.35M** at the quarter's average price, and the burn address now holds **191.93M GT**, 64% of the first 300M. The burns since early 2025 have come every 54 to 108 days, so we expect the Q4 2026 burn before Jan 13 2027. Because the burn is sized in dollars, we project the same $22.35M at today's price of $10.41, which is about **2.15M GT**.

The protocol fee burn is tiny: GateChain destroys a small part of each fee, about **97 GT** in these 90 days. Gate Layer, the newer network that also uses GT for fees, sends its fees to fee wallets instead of burning them. The Foundation buy row books **0**: apart from the quarterly burn, nothing on-chain shows Gate buying GT to keep, and a second buyback round mentioned in September 2026 came with no size and no wallet. New long-term locks book **0** as well. About 39.90M GT is staked with GateChain validators, but staked GT still counts as circulating and can be withdrawn.

## Foundation and overhang

Gate controls three balances we watch. The frozen reserve holds **12.25M GT** on Ethereum and is the whole non-circulating bucket; we read it every refresh. Gate's burn wallet holds **10.63M GT** and pays each quarterly burn; it is already counted as circulating, so its spending only matters when coins reach the burn address. The bridge lock holds **52.40M GT** on Ethereum and 62.78M GT on GateChain; each side backs coins that move on the other chain, so they are one supply seen twice, not a pile waiting to be sold. GateChain's own burn address is about 7.12M GT behind Ethereum's, because Gate has not yet copied the 2026 burns over; when it does, that is the same burn seen again, not a new one. If the frozen reserve's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How GT compares to other exchange tokens

GT belongs to the exchange-token family, where a trading platform uses part of its profit to shrink the coin. Most of these coins run a scheduled burn, and the real differences are how the burn is sized, whether new coins are still being created, and whether a reserve sits behind the burn. GT's burn is set in dollars each quarter, so a lower GT price burns more coins and a higher price burns fewer. BNB also burns each quarter, but by a published rule tied to price and blocks, and it adds a gas-fee burn on every block, so its buy side does not depend on one company's quarterly decision.

On the sell side GT is unusual for an exchange coin: it still creates new coins, because GateChain pays validators **0.4 GT** per block. At about **0.10%** of supply per quarter that is small next to the burn, so GT stays clearly deflationary. Exchange tokens that finished their supply in one large burn, such as OKB, no longer create anything, but they also no longer shrink. GT sits between the two: it still burns every quarter and still adds a little.

The bigger structural difference is the reserve. A burn only shrinks the market when the coins come from the float. Gate's frozen reserve has refilled the burn wallet after one of the three burns since its January 2026 rules, so part of GT's burn history may be a move from the reserve into the market and then out again. Exchange tokens that burn coins bought in the open market, with no reserve behind them, do not carry that risk.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q4 2026 burn, expected before Jan 13 2027: the framework already counts about **2.15M GT** for it, so a late, smaller or skipped burn would move the next-90-day reading toward flat. Second, the frozen reserve: if it refills the **1,987,321 GT** from the Oct 4 2026 burn, that adds the same amount back to circulation, and if it does the same for the Q4 burn, the next 90 days end close to flat. Third, the monthly tracker unlocks on Oct 26, Nov 26 and Dec 26 2026, **1.5M GT** each on paper; we expect them to book nothing unless coins leave the frozen reserve. Fourth, Gate's mirror burn on GateChain, about 7.12M GT, which changes nothing in the count. Fifth, any change to the 0.4 GT block reward or to how Gate sizes the burn.

## Summary

The MrNasDog Pressure Framework reads Gate's GT at **−1.80% net** over the last 90 days and about **−1.95%** over the next 90: a **1,987,321 GT** quarterly buyback-and-burn against **102,987 GT** of GateChain block rewards, with the monitor at **−1.68%**. The burn is sized in dollars from Gate's results, so it depends on the exchange keeping its profits and its promise. The key risk is the **12.25M GT** frozen reserve, which can refill the burn wallet and cancel a burn, as it did in July 2026. While Gate keeps burning about $20M of GT a quarter and the reserve stays still, GT supply keeps shrinking by close to 2% a quarter.
