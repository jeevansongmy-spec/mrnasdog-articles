Gate destroyed **2,570,063 GT** on **Jul 4 2026**, taking GateToken's lifetime burn past **189.95M** — roughly **63%** of every GT ever made — and yet the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads GT at **+0.15%** net supply over the last 90 days, very slightly inflationary. The reason is where the burned GT came from: a frozen 2019 reserve wallet holding **12.25M GT** that the tradable count already excludes. Sell pressure was **0.16M GT** of GateChain consensus rewards, buy pressure on the float was **zero**, and the tradable supply of **106.57M GT** did not move on the burn date. Our supply monitor reads **−31.32%**, a gap of **31.47 percentage points** caused entirely by two classification changes, not by any token flow. GT is a **token whose burn shrinks the ceiling rather than the float**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **Apr 28 2026** to **Jul 27 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **GT at +0.15% net**: sell pressure of **0.16M GT** against buy pressure of **zero** on a tradable base of **106.57M GT**. Our supply monitor reads **−31.32%** for the same period, a gap of **31.47 percentage points**, which is far past the framework's half-point tolerance and therefore ships with a data-conflict flag. That gap is not a disagreement about GateToken's supply — it is a disagreement about bookkeeping. The circulating series for GT stepped down **44.1M** on **May 1 2026** and another **6.1M** on **May 12 2026** with no on-chain movement on either date; the burn address did not change and no Gate wallet transacted. Those two steps sum to almost exactly the **52.40M GT** that Gate consolidated into a single cold wallet on **Jan 20 2026**, being re-tagged as company-held months after the fact. Strip the re-tagging out and nothing happened. GateToken is best labelled a **reserve-funded burn: permanently deflationary in total supply, marginally inflationary in tradable supply**.

## Sell pressure: where new GT comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **0.16M GT** per 90 days, and its existence is the first thing most GT coverage gets wrong. GateToken is widely described as a fixed-supply token, and the Ethereum contract behind it genuinely is: it minted **300,000,000 GT** in 2019 and has never issued another one. But GateChain, the network GT secures, pays its consensus nodes in newly created GT. Gate's own tokenomics page puts the running total at approximately **8.55M GT** of staking rewards over five and a half years at a current annualised yield near **0.99%**, and the live figure today works out around **8.77M**. That difference across four months is where the **0.16M** comes from. It is small — under two tenths of a percent a quarter — but it is the only mechanism in this ledger that creates GT, and on a page where every other row is zero it decides the sign.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**. GateToken was distributed in 2019 and no live vesting or escrow contract holds any of it. Unlock trackers still publish a linear schedule for GT running to **Jul 2030** with **290M GT** shown as locked; there are only **110.05M GT** left in existence, so those pages are modelling a retired cap table rather than anything that can fire. Reporting in mid-July flagged a GT unlock on **Jul 26 2026** on the strength of the same data; no such transfer exists on-chain and the reserve wallet did not move. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** on observed behaviour, with two pools enumerated below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Gate is a going concern and no estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches GT.

## Buy pressure: where new GT goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is the mechanism GT is famous for, and it reads **zero on the float**, which needs explaining because the burn is completely real. Gate runs a quarterly burn tied to platform revenue, and it has fired every quarter without a miss: **2,163,900 GT** on **Jan 8 2026**, **2,557,729 GT** on **Apr 26 2026**, and **2,570,063 GT** on **Jul 4 2026**. The burn address now holds **189,947,220 GT** out of the original **300,000,000** mint. Only the July firing falls inside this window; the April one landed two days before it opened, which is why this page books one burn rather than the two a quarterly quantum multiplied out would suggest.

The catch is the funding source. Following the July burn hop by hop, the **2,570,063 GT** left a wallet that the deployer funded with **40,000,000 GT** in 2019 and that has taken in no GT from the market since 2020, passed through an executor address, and reached the burn address the same day. That funding wallet is exactly the bucket the tradable count excludes: total supply of **118.82M GT** minus circulating supply of **106.57M GT** equals **12.25M**, which is the wallet's balance to seven decimal places. So when the burn fires, total supply falls and the excluded bucket falls by the identical amount, and the float does not change. The circulating count read the same figure on **Jul 3**, **Jul 4** and **Jul 6 2026**. Booking a **2.57M** burn as **2.57M** of absorbed float would have printed a **−2.4%** reading that no GT holder experienced.

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero** for a related reason. Gate states that around **20%** of quarterly platform profit repurchases GT on the open market before the burn. That purchase, if it happens, executes on Gate's own venue and leaves nothing an outsider can verify: no Gate address shows a position building, and the GT that actually reaches the burn address is 2019 reserve rather than anything acquired this quarter. One witness with no independent second angle is an opaque row under this framework, so it carries a placeholder zero rather than an estimate. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero** with no disclosed purchase beyond that programme. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: fee discounts, VIP tiers and airdrop eligibility give GT holders reasons to hold, but no contract escrows GT and none was created in this window.

## Foundation and overhang

GateToken carries two identified Gate-controlled pools. The first is the frozen 2019 reserve, holding **12,251,232 GT** at the last read. It sits outside the tradable count, it has never sent GT to a buyer, and it is being drawn down at roughly **2.57M** a quarter by the burn itself — about **4.8** more quarters at the current quantum before it is empty. That is the single most important date on GT's horizon, because the quarter after it runs dry is the first quarter the burn must come from somewhere that counts. The second pool is Gate's consolidated cold wallet, holding **52,399,955 GT**, funded on **Jan 20 2026** and untouched since. Unlike the reserve, this one sits **inside** the counted float already, so it needs no unlock, no vote and no announcement to reach the market. Gate's customer-deposit wallets are excluded from this enumeration because those tokens belong to depositors. Both pools are re-read on every rebuild: if either balance falls toward the open market between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and on the cold wallet that would be the largest single supply event this ledger could record.

## How GT compares to other exchange tokens

The peer group is exchange tokens with quarterly burn programmes, and on the headline statistic GateToken sits near the top of it: **63%** of all GT ever created has been destroyed, ahead of the roughly one-third retired by the largest exchange token in the sector. But the mechanism underneath decides what that number is worth. The classic exchange-token burn is revenue-funded end to end — the venue takes a slice of profit, buys the token on the open market, and destroys what it bought — so the burn is simultaneously a bid and a supply cut, and both halves land on the tradable float. Gate's realised burn is reserve-funded: the tokens are already in hand, so there is a supply cut and no verifiable bid.

That places GT alongside the other exchange tokens whose burns eat an excluded allocation rather than the market — a pattern this framework has now measured on more than one venue — and away from the fee-burn chains, where every burned unit comes out of a circulating balance and the float genuinely shrinks. GT also differs from a pure fixed-supply exchange token in one direction the marketing does not mention: it has a live issuance mechanism, because GateChain must pay for its own security. So GateToken is neither the hard-capped deflation story nor an uncapped emitter. It is a token with a small, real mint, a large, real burn, and an accounting gap between them — the burn retires supply that was never going to be sold this quarter, while the mint adds supply that already is.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q3 2026 quarterly burn, expected inside this window on the observed rhythm of **Jan 8 2026**, **Apr 26 2026** and **Jul 4 2026** — the number to check is not the size but the funding wallet, because a burn paid from anywhere other than the frozen reserve would turn Buy #2 non-zero immediately. Second, that reserve's remaining **12.25M GT**, which at the current quantum supports roughly five more quarters. Third, Gate's **52.40M GT** cold wallet, which is already inside the float and needs no unlock to sell. Fourth, GateChain's staking yield, quoted at **0.99%** — a rise there scales Sell #1 directly. Fifth, the Gate Layer L2, where GT is the exclusive gas token: heavier usage burns gas but also raises the consensus rewards funding it, and the framework will book whichever side actually moves.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Gate's GT at **+0.15%** net supply change over the last 90 days and projects the same for the next 90. GateToken burns more aggressively than almost any exchange token in the sector — **189.95M** of an original **300,000,000** destroyed, **2,570,063 GT** of it on **Jul 4 2026** — but the burned tokens are drawn from a frozen 2019 reserve that the tradable count already excludes, so total supply falls while the float stays put, and GateChain's **0.16M** of quarterly consensus rewards tips the net marginally positive. The key risk is not the mint, which is tiny, but Gate's **52.40M GT** cold wallet sitting inside the counted float with no lock on it. The ceiling keeps falling and the burn is genuine; for now its benefit accrues to GT's future rather than to today's market.
