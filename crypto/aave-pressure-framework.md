# AAVE Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Aave's AAVE is a **fixed 16,000,000** ERC-20 on Ethereum, fully minted in 2020, with no mint path fired and no burn of any kind — so the only supply that can reach the market comes out of one DAO ecosystem reserve, which released **19.25K AAVE** to safety module stakers over the last 90 days against **zero** buy pressure, a net of **+0.12%**. The AAVE buyback that coverage widely quotes at around 292 tokens a day has been switched off since **Apr 19 2026**, and a sweep of every AAVE transfer in the window found no wallet anywhere buying day after day. Our inflation monitor reads **+1.62%**, a gap of **1.50 percentage points**, because its counted supply was restated by **236,436 AAVE** in a single day on **Jul 19 2026** with no chain event to match it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads AAVE supply pressure at **+0.12%** of circulating, easing to **+0.09%** over the next 90 days as the Aave DAO's staking-reward rate settles at **150 AAVE per day**. Our inflation monitor reads **+1.62%** for the same window, a gap of **1.50 percentage points**, which is over the framework's half-point tolerance and ships with a monitor-gap warning on the AAVE overview page. The gap is not a disagreement about tokens: AAVE's on-chain total supply read exactly **16,000,000** at both ends of the window, and the monitor's counted supply jumped **236,436** in one day on **Jul 19 2026** — a supply-count restatement, not AAVE reaching the market. AAVE is best labelled a **fixed-cap governance token with a single reserve tap and a dormant buyback**.

## Sell pressure: where new AAVE comes from

Aave protocol inflation, the first sell row, is **19.25K AAVE** — and strictly speaking it is not inflation at all. Every AAVE that will ever exist was created in one step at the 2020 LEND-to-AAVE migration, and the AAVE contract's total supply read **16,000,000** at both window ends, so no token was minted. What moves is classification: the Aave DAO ecosystem reserve, which sits outside the tradable count, paid **19,252.54 AAVE** of safety module staking rewards into holders' hands across **3,082** separate claim transfers, dropping from **594,182.68** to **574,930.14**. That is the whole of AAVE's sell side.

Vesting unlocks, the second sell row, are **zero**, because Aave has no vesting. There is no cliff, no lockup contract and no release calendar to unlock from — the migration created the full 16,000,000 at once and every aggregator's residual "unlocked" figure is simply the ecosystem reserve already counted in the first row. The third row, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero**: no discretionary AAVE release was observed anywhere in the window, and the three team-controlled pools that could produce one are enumerated below. The fourth row, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the simplest reason — no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holds AAVE, so there is nothing in that category to release.

## Buy pressure: where new AAVE goes

The programmatic buyback, the first buy row, is **zero**, and this is the single most misreported fact about AAVE. Aave governance paused the buyback effective **Apr 19 2026** after the rsETH bridge incident of **Apr 18 2026**, and no restart has been voted. Coverage of Aavenomics 3.0, activated **Jun 27 2026**, widely reports the mechanism buying roughly **292 AAVE per day**; the Aave documentation says purchases are sent to the ecosystem reserve and held rather than burned. The chain says otherwise. Across the whole window the Aave ecosystem reserve received **zero** AAVE inflows, and a sweep of all **709,283** AAVE transfer events in the window, ranked by net flow, found no DAO-controlled wallet anywhere collecting AAVE day after day. An announced target is not a realised flow, and the Pressure Framework books only the second.

The protocol fee burn, the second buy row, is **zero** because AAVE has no burn of any kind. Both burn surfaces were checked: total supply held at **16,000,000** at both ends, and the dead address held the identical **0.27 AAVE** throughout. Neither moved, so nothing was destroyed. Foundation buy, the third row, is **zero** — the Aave DAO treasury's own AAVE position fell to nothing over the window as it was transferred to the finance committee, which is an internal move between DAO pockets rather than a purchase from the market.

The fourth buy row, new long-term lock, is the interesting **zero**. The Aave safety module genuinely absorbed AAVE: it took in a net **365.1K AAVE** over the window and now holds **2.48M**, rising at every sample point we measured. The quantum is real, so the row turns on whether staking is a lock. It is not. The safety module's cooldown period and unstake window both read **172,800 seconds** on-chain — two days each — at both ends of the window, and staked AAVE is already inside the counted circulating figure. A two-day exit notice is custody, not a lock. Booking it would have manufactured roughly **2.36%** of deflation that no supply figure anywhere reflects, so the safety module is watched on the AAVE overview page rather than counted.

## Foundation and overhang

AAVE's overhang is unusually clean, because a single arithmetic identity settles it. Total supply of **16,000,000** minus the circulating figure of **15,425,069.86** leaves **574,930.14** — and that is exactly the on-chain balance of the Aave DAO ecosystem reserve, to the unit. The reserve is therefore the one and only AAVE pool outside the tradable count. It funds safety module rewards at 150 AAVE a day under the allowance top-up governance passed on **Aug 28 2026**, with the remainder unscheduled, and it is re-read from chain every day.

Two further team-controlled pools are tracked but sit inside the counted float. The Aave finance committee safe holds the buyback's accumulated stack, **240.5K AAVE**-equivalent held as an interest-bearing aToken rather than raw AAVE — its raw balance reads zero at both window ends, which is the trap that catches a naive read. That stack rose **20.2K** over the window entirely by internal transfer from the Aave DAO treasury, not by buying. The Aave DAO collector holds a further **6.6K**. Because both are already inside the circulating count, a sale from either would hit the market without moving the headline supply figure at all — which is precisely why the buyback, even running at full pace, could not reduce AAVE's counted supply while it points at wallets on this side of the line. If any of these three balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How AAVE compares to other fixed-cap governance tokens

AAVE belongs to the small class of DeFi governance tokens whose supply is genuinely finished: fully minted, no emission curve, no vesting tail. That puts it much closer to a fixed-supply exchange token than to the uncapped continuous-emission layer-1s that dominate the Pressure Framework's coverage. Where a proof-of-stake layer-1 mints new units every block and needs a fee burn to offset them, AAVE mints nothing at all — its entire sell side is the reclassification of already-created tokens out of one reserve, which is a bounded quantity. That reserve holds **574.9K**; at the current 150 AAVE a day it would take roughly ten years to empty. There is no scenario in which AAVE's supply grows past 16,000,000 without a governance vote to reopen the mint path, which is technically possible because the AAVE token is an upgradeable proxy — the reason no row on the overview page carries a permanent tag.

The comparison that matters most is with the exchange tokens that run quarterly buyback-and-burn programmes. Those chains send purchased tokens to an address that destroys them, so the buy pressure shows up directly in total supply. Aave's design routes purchased AAVE into the ecosystem reserve to fund staking rewards and contributors instead of burning it — a redistribution, not a retirement. That distinction decides how the Pressure Framework scores AAVE even when the buyback is running: tokens bought into a wallet inside the circulating count remove nothing from the float, whereas tokens bought into the reserve or a burn address do. Right now the question is academic, because the programme has been off since April, but it is the single design change that would move AAVE from a neutral reading to a genuinely deflationary one.

Against fee-burn chains, AAVE has no offset mechanism whatsoever. Aave the protocol earns substantial revenue, but none of it currently reaches AAVE the token as a burn or a locked buy. The token's supply story is therefore entirely one-directional and entirely small: a slow, capped, governance-set drip out of one reserve, with nothing on the other side of the ledger.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, a governance vote to restart the AAVE buyback. The pause has stood since **Apr 19 2026** and the restart discussion is open without a vote; a restart alone would not change the framework reading unless it also changes the destination. Second, whether purchased AAVE is ever routed into the ecosystem reserve address or a burn address rather than the finance committee safe — that single routing change is what would flip the buy row from zero to its full quantum. Third, the next safety module allowance top-up: the **Aug 28 2026** update held the rate at 150 AAVE per day and funded 13,500 for 90 days forward, so any change to that rate re-bases the sell row directly. Fourth, the ecosystem reserve balance itself, currently **574.9K**, which is the only pool that can move AAVE's counted supply. Fifth, whether the safety module cooldown is lengthened from its current two days, which would turn a large custody balance into a genuine lock and give AAVE a buy row for the first time.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads AAVE at **+0.12%** supply pressure over the last 90 days and **+0.09%** over the next, a mixed and roughly steady reading on a token whose supply is already finished. The mechanism is simple and bounded: a fixed **16,000,000** AAVE, no mint and no burn, with the entire sell side being **19.25K** of safety module staking rewards released from a single Aave DAO ecosystem reserve that still holds **574.9K**. The key risk is not dilution but the absent offset — the AAVE buyback has been switched off since **Apr 19 2026**, and even when running it points at a wallet already inside the circulating count, so it removes nothing from the float. The ceiling is hard at 16,000,000 and cannot move without a governance vote to reopen the token proxy's mint path.
