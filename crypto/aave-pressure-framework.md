Aave's AAVE token has a finished supply: all **16,000,000 AAVE** were minted in 2020 and the number has not moved since. What counts as tradable is **16M** minus one DAO-owned ecosystem reserve, so that reserve's balance is the entire inflation question — and over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** it released **20.6K AAVE** of Safety Module staking rewards, falling from **597.6K** to **577.0K**. On the other side there is nothing at all: Aave has no burn, and the revenue-funded AAVE buyback has been switched off since **Apr 19 2026**. On a circulating base of **15.42M AAVE** the Pressure Framework reads **+0.13%** net, easing to **+0.09%** next quarter after a governance cut to the reward rate. AAVE is **a capped token whose only live supply mechanism is a reward stream nothing is offsetting**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 19 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **AAVE at +0.13% net**: sell pressure of **20.6K AAVE** released from the Aave DAO ecosystem reserve, against buy pressure of **zero**, on a circulating base of **15.42M AAVE**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.59%** for the same window, a gap of **1.46 percentage points**, well past the half-point tolerance, so a monitor-gap flag ships on this build. The deep walk found the cause and it is not a flow: that feed's implied AAVE supply sat flat near **15.18M** from early May to **Jul 18 2026**, stepped to **15.42M** on **Jul 19 2026** in a single day, and has been flat ever since — a **235K** supply-count restatement with no matching chain event, since the AAVE token read exactly **16,000,000** throughout and the ecosystem reserve moved only **364 AAVE** across **Jul 17 2026** to **Jul 21 2026**. Aave is best labelled **capped by design, mildly inflationary on the active float, with the offsetting mechanism currently switched off**.

## Sell pressure: where new AAVE comes from

Nowhere, in the literal sense. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **20.6K AAVE**, and not one of those coins is new. The Aave DAO ecosystem reserve at **0x25F2226B597E8F9514B3F68F00f494cF4f286491** is the only pool of AAVE that the market does not count as circulating, and the arithmetic proves it exactly: **16,000,000** total minus **15,422,972** circulating leaves **577,028**, which is the reserve's on-chain balance to the unit. Every AAVE the reserve pays out therefore crosses from outside the float to inside it, and that is the only way AAVE's tradable supply can grow. Over the window the reserve went from **597,642** to **577,028**, and a full sweep of its transfer log agrees to the decimal: zero inflows, **2,926** outflows totalling **20,614 AAVE**, all of them Safety Module reward claims.

The forward number is smaller because the reward rate was cut inside the window. The Safety Module's emission parameter on the staking contract read **220 AAVE** a day when the window opened and reads **150 AAVE** a day now; bisecting the change puts it at **Jun 13 2026**, the on-chain execution of the governance request to retune stkAAVE emissions toward a **2.75%** staking APR. Projecting the blended trailing rate forward would forecast an emission that no longer exists, so the next-90-day figure is re-based on the live parameter alone: **150** a day for **90** days is **13.5K AAVE**. Note that claims ran ahead of accrual this quarter — **20.6K** claimed against **15.1K** accrued — because stakers were drawing down a backlog of rewards earned earlier.

The other three sell rows are genuinely zero. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because AAVE has no vesting: the full 16M supply was minted in one event during the 2020 LEND-to-AAVE migration, and there is no lock contract, no cliff and no release calendar for any party. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** — nothing was released by any DAO wallet in the window, though the overhangs are real and enumerated below. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: there is no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered AAVE distribution anywhere in the picture.

## Buy pressure: where new AAVE goes

This is the section most readings of AAVE get wrong. Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **zero** — despite a widely repeated figure of about **292 AAVE** a day bought on the open market since the Aavenomics 3.0 upgrade went live on **Jun 27 2026**. AAVE buybacks were formally paused effective **Apr 19 2026**, after the **Apr 18 2026** rsETH incident, so the DAO could hold its balance sheet in reserve; the restart discussion is still open and no restart has been voted. The chain agrees with the pause, not with the headline. The Aave Finance Committee's multisig at **0x22740deBa78d5a0c24C58C740e3715ec29de1bFa** holds the buyback stack, and its AAVE balance reads **0.00** at both ends of the window because it forwards everything straight into an Aave v3 deposit — a wallet that looks empty while carrying real flow. Reading the deposit instead shows the stack rising from **220.3K** to **240.5K**, up **20.2K AAVE**. But tracing each of those five inflows by hash shows where they came from: **11,240 AAVE** from the DAO's own collector contract, **1,565 AAVE** from a vesting escrow, and **7,388 AAVE** from two ordinary accounts. The protocol's own swapper contract sent nothing. The stack grew by internal transfer, not by buying, and nothing has reached the multisig at all since **Jun 24 2026**.

Even a genuine purchase would not have counted here, and this is the structural point about AAVE. Because the excluded bucket is the ecosystem reserve and only the ecosystem reserve, the finance committee's stack is already inside the counted float. Moving AAVE into it removes nothing from circulating supply; it changes which wallet holds a coin the market still counts. The project's own documentation says acquired AAVE is sent to the ecosystem reserve, which would remove it — but the reserve has received zero AAVE inflows across the entire window, so on the evidence that route is not in use. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **zero** for the simplest reason available: Aave has no burn address and no destruction path, and the token supply read exactly **16,000,000** at both ends. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**; there is no separate treasury bid, and the DAO collector's own AAVE position fell over the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** even though Safety Module staking grew from **2.09M** to **2.45M AAVE**, a **364.2K** increase: staked AAVE stays inside the circulating count and unstakes after a two-day cooldown, which makes it custody rather than a lock. Booking it would have invented **2.4%** of deflation that no supply figure reflects.

## Foundation and overhang

Four AAVE overhangs are tracked, and only the first sits outside the market. The Aave DAO ecosystem reserve holds **577.0K AAVE** and is refreshed on-chain each rebuild; it pays out at **150 AAVE** a day today, and the remainder has no published release plan. The Aave Finance Committee's buyback stack holds **240.5K AAVE** as an Aave v3 deposit — already counted as circulating, which means a decision to sell it would hit the market without moving the headline supply figure at all, and is checked on-chain each rebuild. The DAO collector treasury holds roughly **6.6K AAVE** of deposit position after moving its raw balance to the committee in **Jun 2026**. Fourth is a reported exchange allocation of **250.0K AAVE** attached to a stake purchase discussed in **Jun 2026**, which has no proposal, no vote and no execution behind it and is tracked at zero on a bi-weekly web walk. The rule is the same for all four: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How AAVE compares to other capped DeFi governance tokens

AAVE belongs to the small class of DeFi governance tokens with a hard, fully-minted cap — the opposite pole from continuous-emission lending and DEX tokens that still mint liquidity incentives every block. Against those, AAVE's **+0.13%** quarterly drift is trivial; there is no emission curve to outrun, and the worst case is bounded forever by the **577.0K AAVE** left in the ecosystem reserve, which is **3.6%** of supply. A capped, fully-minted token cannot surprise a holder with new issuance, and that is the single most valuable property in this comparison.

Against fee-burn tokens, though, AAVE looks weaker by construction right now. A protocol with a base-fee burn converts usage directly into supply destruction, so heavy quarters shrink supply mechanically. Aave has no burn at all, so its only counterweight is discretionary: a buyback the DAO votes on, funds from revenue, and can switch off — and has switched off. That is the difference between a mechanism and a policy. Compared with exchange tokens that run mandatory quarterly buy-and-burn, AAVE's programme also differs in destination: bought AAVE is held for governance-approved uses rather than destroyed, so even at full pace it never reduces total supply, and while the destination sits inside the counted float it does not reduce circulating supply either. The upgrade that would change this class comparison is not a bigger budget — it is routing purchased AAVE into the ecosystem reserve or a burn address, which is the one edit that would turn Aave's revenue into genuine deflation.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the buyback restart. Buybacks have been paused since **Apr 19 2026** and are due to resume when protocol cashflow allows; a restart vote, and specifically the destination it names, is the single largest swing factor on this page. Second, the destination itself: if bought AAVE is routed into the ecosystem reserve at **0x25F2226B597E8F9514B3F68F00f494cF4f286491** rather than the committee multisig, the same purchase quantum flips from having no effect on circulating supply to reducing it one-for-one. Third, the Safety Module emission parameter, currently **150 AAVE** a day: it is retuned toward a target staking APR, and with the module having grown **364.2K AAVE** this quarter, holding that target implies a higher per-day rate, not a lower one. Fourth, the ecosystem reserve balance itself, which is the whole denominator — every rebuild reads it directly. Fifth, whether the supply-count restatement of **Jul 19 2026** stays in place; if it does, the monitor gap will roll out of the trailing window by **Oct 17 2026** on its own.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads AAVE at **+0.13%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026**, easing to **+0.09%** over the next 90 days. The structural mechanism is unusually simple: AAVE's **16,000,000** supply is permanently minted and unburnable, the tradable float is that cap minus the Aave DAO ecosystem reserve, and the only live flow is **150 AAVE** a day of Safety Module staking rewards leaving that reserve. The key risk is that the offsetting mechanism is a policy rather than a property — the revenue-funded buyback has been switched off since **Apr 19 2026**, and even when running it deposits into a wallet the market already counts as circulating, so it has never reduced the float. The ceiling is firm: with **577.0K AAVE** left in the reserve, total future supply growth is capped at **3.6%** no matter what the DAO decides.
