# LUNC Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Terra Classic destroyed **7.60B LUNC** in the last 90 days and created none — the chain's mint is switched off entirely, so its issuance setting and its yearly provision both read zero. That is the half of the story everyone quotes. The other half is that a chain-owned oracle reward reserve released **5.06B LUNC** of already-minted coins to validators and stakers over the same window, which cancels two thirds of the burn and never shows up in the supply figure. The Pressure Framework therefore reads a net of **-0.05%** rather than the **-0.14%** the burn alone would imply. Our inflation monitor reads **-0.25%** for the same window, a gap of **0.20 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no data-conflict flag is raised. The burn tax tripled to **1.5%** on **Aug 2 2026**, which pushes the next 90 days to roughly **-0.07%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026** the LUNC ledger reads **5,070.0M** of sell pressure against **7,596.4M** of buy pressure — a net of **2,526.4M LUNC** removed, or **-0.05%** on a circulating base of **5.53T LUNC**. Our monitor reads **-0.25%**. The two differ by **0.20 percentage points**, which is inside the framework's tolerance and needs no warning chip, and the direction of the difference is worth naming: the monitor watches a classified circulating figure that fell further than the chain's own supply did, so it sees more shrinkage than the flows justify. Terra Luna Classic is best labelled a **zero-issuance chain with a self-cancelling burn** — a network that cannot create a coin, destroys a real quantity every day, and pays for its own security by draining a reserve at almost the same speed.

## Sell pressure: where new LUNC comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the first thing to settle about Terra Classic. **Protocol inflation books 0.** The mint module on Terra Classic returns an inflation rate of zero and an annual provision of zero, with the minimum and maximum bounds both pinned to zero as well — there is no block reward, no emission curve and no staking mint on this chain. Total supply was sampled at 66 heights across the window and fell at every one of them; it rose at none. Terra Classic pays validators out of gas fees, the on-chain tax split, and a pre-funded reward reserve, so every coin a staker receives on Terra Classic is a coin that already existed.

**Vesting unlocks are 0**, and by history rather than by design. Terra Classic is the abandoned original chain: when its developer moved to a successor token in 2022, the team tranches, the investor tranches and the foundation cliffs went with it. No unlock tracker publishes a LUNC vesting calendar because there is no calendar to publish. **Foundation and unscheduled unlocks book 9.9M LUNC**, and there is no foundation involved — Terra Classic has no company and no entity behind it. The figure is a single community-pool spend that coin holders approved by vote on **Jul 1 2026**, paying **9,873,590 LUNC** to fund a cross-chain deployment. That is the only discretionary release of the quarter.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy books 0.** The failed developer's estate is wound down, and its Terra Classic wallet is inert: it held **293,211,784 LUNC** at the start of the window and **293,211,885** at the end, a movement of **101** coins in three months. No trustee schedule and no creditor distribution has put LUNC into the market.

A fifth sell row exists on Terra Classic because the largest supply-side flow on this chain fits none of the canonical four, and it is the reading most write-ups miss entirely. The **oracle reward reserve books 5.06B LUNC**. Because Terra Classic cannot mint, it pays for its own security out of a chain-owned module account stocked with coins that already exist. That reserve fell from **42,993.35M** to **37,933.19M** LUNC across the window — **5,060.2M** coins moved out of a wallet nobody could trade and into hands that can. Total supply does not move by a single unit when this happens, so a build that reads only the supply call books the entire Terra Classic sell side at zero and reports a chain three times more deflationary than it is. The drain is not a fixed quantum either: it is a constant share of whatever is left in the reserve, about **0.139%** a day across the window, which means it shrinks as the reserve shrinks.

## Buy pressure: where new LUNC goes

**Protocol fee burn books 4.52B LUNC**, and it is the mechanism Terra Classic is famous for. Every on-chain transfer pays a tax, and most of that tax is destroyed outright. On **Aug 2 2026** a governance vote tripled the rate from **0.5%** to **1.5%** — of which **1.2%** is burned and **0.3%** is routed to the community and reward pools. Reading the tax parameter either side of the switch pins the activation to a single block on that date. One trap belongs on the record because it silently zeroes this row: the chain's legacy treasury tax endpoint still returns **0**, and the live rate lives in a different module entirely. The burn run rate went from about **36M** a day before the vote to **81.6M** after it — a tripled tax that only roughly doubled the burn, because taxable volume did not follow the rate up.

**Exchange fee burns book 3.07B LUNC** in a fifth buy row, because a third party voluntarily destroying its own revenue is neither a protocol burn nor a buyback. One large exchange sends its LUNC trading-fee income to the chain's burn address on the first of each month, and three firings landed inside this window: **2,193.7M** on **Jun 1 2026**, **604.3M** on **Jul 1 2026** and **275.6M** on **Aug 1 2026**. Each was read on-chain as a single transfer to the burn address, and the published reports for the same three dates agree to within a tenth of a percent. The trend inside those three numbers matters more than their sum: the exchange burn has fallen to roughly an eighth of its June size in two months.

**The programmatic buyback is 0** and **foundation buy is 0.** Terra Classic has no treasury bidding for LUNC, no revenue routed into open-market purchases, and no entity with a mandate to accumulate — the community pool is a recipient of tax revenue, not a market participant. **New long-term lock is 0** as well, and that zero deserves a footnote: about **902,106.2M LUNC** is bonded to Terra Classic validators, but those are coins the market already counts as tradable and the position can be unbonded, so it reads as a demand signal rather than supply leaving the board.

One structural point decides how the whole buy side is counted. Terra Classic's burn address is not a wallet that piles up dead coins — it is a module account holding the burn permission, and its balance queries empty at every height, because it destroys what it receives in the same block. So the dead-address surface on this chain is pinned at zero and cannot move independently of total supply. There is exactly one observable surface, the chain's supply figure, and booking a burn against both would double the entire buy side of the page. The two burn mechanisms were separated instead by slicing supply around the three identified transfer transactions: what those transfers removed is the exchange leg, and everything else the window removed is the tax leg.

## Foundation and overhang

Terra Classic has no foundation to enumerate, which makes its overhang unusually clean. The largest is the **oracle reward reserve**, holding **37,933.2M LUNC** today and draining as described above — it is not a dormant capacity but an active flow, which is why it is booked as a sell row rather than left as a watch item. Second is the **community pool**, at **8,976.1M LUNC**, spendable only by a passed governance proposal. It is worth being precise about its direction: the pool **grew** by **814.4M LUNC** over the window, taking in more tax revenue than the single approved spend paid out. Third is the failed developer's wallet at **293.2M LUNC**, effectively frozen. The chain's largest single holder, at **1,705,881.2M LUNC**, is an exchange custody wallet and is deliberately excluded from the overhang — those coins belong to depositors, not to a team — but it is named here because it is the source of the monthly burns counted on the buy side.

All three overhangs are read on-chain at every rebuild. If the reward reserve, the community pool or the developer wallet falls between refreshes, that outflow enters the Terra Classic sell side at the next refresh. One item is genuinely unresolved and is flagged rather than guessed: the gap between the classified circulating figure of **5.53T** and the chain's **6.45T** total is **926.1B LUNC**, and it could not be enumerated account by account from primary sources. The chain's own published circulating figure is **5.54T**, so the two classifiers do not agree with each other and neither publishes its account list. This does not move any row on the page — every figure above is an absolute quantum, not a share of that denominator.

## How LUNC compares to other zero-issuance chains

The obvious comparison is to a hard-capped proof-of-work chain, and it is the wrong one. A capped chain still mints — it simply mints on a schedule that ends. Terra Classic does not mint at all: its issuance parameter is zero today, so the supply can only travel in one direction. In that respect it sits closer to a fully-issued token with a fee burn, like an exchange chain that destroys gas, than to any chain with a block reward. Where it differs from those is the funding problem. A fee-burning smart-contract chain pays validators out of newly issued coins or out of the fees themselves; Terra Classic pays them out of a finite reserve of pre-existing coins, and that reserve is the sell row.

That makes Terra Classic the clearest example in the catalogue of why a supply-only reading fails. On a chain with an active mint, supply growth and sell pressure move together and either measurement finds the answer. On Terra Classic they are decoupled completely: supply fell **7,596.4M** while **5,060.2M** of tradable pressure was created inside the same window, entirely invisible to the supply call. A comparison to an exchange token with a quarterly buyback-and-burn is instructive in the other direction — those tokens shrink supply and add nothing back, so their burn translates one-for-one into the reading. Terra Classic's does not.

The last structural comparison is governance. Most chains with a burn have a company or a foundation that can change the rate. Terra Classic's rate is set by coin-holder vote and has been moved repeatedly — down to **0.5%** and back up to **1.5%** inside this year. That is a genuine two-sided risk that a foundation-run chain does not carry: the same electorate that tripled the burn can halve it, and the reward reserve keeps draining either way.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

A community-pool spending proposal for **281.9M LUNC** closes voting on **Sep 4 2026**; it is not counted in the forward column because it has not passed, and if it does it lands in the Terra Classic sell side at roughly thirty times the size of the last approved spend. The monthly exchange fee burn fires again on **Sep 1 2026**, **Oct 1 2026** and **Nov 1 2026**, and the forward column assumes three firings at the most recent **275.6M** size rather than the June figure — if the decline continues, the buy side is over-stated; if trading volume recovers, it is under-stated. Watch whether the tripled burn tax holds its **81.6M**-a-day run rate now that the initial surge has passed, since a fall in taxable volume cuts the burn without any vote. And watch the reward reserve: at **37,933.2M LUNC** and draining about **0.13%** a day, it is the only sell pressure on this chain, and its exhaustion would be the single biggest structural change Terra Classic could undergo.

## Summary

Terra Luna Classic creates no new LUNC, destroyed **7.60B** coins in 90 days through a **1.5%** on-chain burn tax and monthly exchange fee burns, and still reads only **-0.05%** net — because a chain-owned reward reserve released **5.06B** already-minted LUNC into tradable hands over the same window, and that flow is invisible in the supply figure. The structural mechanism is a zero-issuance chain funding its own security from a finite reserve, so the burn and the payout very nearly cancel. The key risk is two-sided and governance-shaped: the burn rate is a coin-holder vote that has moved twice this year, and the exchange leg of the burn has fallen to an eighth of its June size. There is no supply cap on Terra Classic, but there is a harder constraint — with the mint switched off, the ceiling is the **6.45T** already in existence, and it can only go down.
