# LUNC Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

Terra Luna Classic (LUNC) supply is shrinking slowly: the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **−0.12% net** over the last 90 days and **−0.17%** for the next 90. The chain mints no new LUNC, while a **1.5% transfer tax** burned **7.13B LUNC**, Binance's monthly fee burns destroyed **966.42M** and the community pool took in **1.29B**. Against that, holders unstaked **2.30B LUNC** net and governance paid **534.92M** out of the pool, so the float fell by about 6.55B LUNC out of 5.52T.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Oct 19 2026, Terra Classic's circulating LUNC fell by **0.12%**: **2.84B LUNC** came back to the market and **9.39B LUNC** left it. The market-data monitor reads **−0.13%** for the same 90 days, a gap of only **0.01 points**, so the two readings agree and no warning chip is shown. For the next 90 days the reading deepens to **−0.17%**, because the higher transfer tax voted in on Aug 2 2026 now runs for the whole window and three more exchange burns are due. Terra Classic is a **slow-burn, zero-mint chain**: supply only goes down, but by a fraction of a percent per quarter.

## Sell pressure: where new LUNC comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. The Terra Classic mint rate is set to zero with zero upper and lower limits, and the old swap between LUNC and the Terra stablecoins, which once printed LUNC without limit, stays shut. Total LUNC supply fell from **6,454.08B** to **6,445.98B** over the window and dropped in every stretch we read.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. LUNC has traded since 2019, the early grants ended long ago, and no unlock calendar exists for it.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks come to **534.92M LUNC**. Terra Classic has no foundation; its treasury is the community pool, which is not counted as circulating and pays out only when stakers vote for it. Two payouts passed in the window: **281.85M LUNC** on Sep 4 2026 to keep the chain's IBC connections running, and **253.07M LUNC** on Oct 15 2026 for the finished Hyperlane bridge that moves LUNC to Ethereum, BNB Chain and Solana. No payout is in voting today, but six passed in the last twelve months for **2.22B LUNC**, so we carry about **548.5M** into the next 90 days at that pace.

Long-term locks released **2.30B LUNC**. Staked LUNC is not counted as circulating, so unstaking adds coins to the market. The staked total fell from **903.68B** to **901.38B LUNC**, after peaking near 914B in late September, and 54.24B more sits in the 21-day unbonding queue. Because staking moves both ways at each holder's choice, nothing is projected for it in the next 90 days.

## Buy pressure: where new LUNC goes

The protocol fee burn is the main force: **7.13B LUNC** destroyed in 90 days. Every on-chain transfer pays a tax, and 80% of it is burned on the spot. Governance raised the tax from 0.5% to **1.5%** on Aug 2 2026, so 1.2% of each transfer now burns. The burn ran about **38.9M LUNC a day** before the change and **85.4M a day** after it — more than double, though short of the full triple the new rate allows. At the new pace the next 90 days burn about **7.69B LUNC**.

Binance's exchange fee burn removed **966.42M LUNC**. On the first day of each month the exchange destroys half of the LUNC trading fees it collected: **275.65M** on Aug 1, **334.88M** on Sep 1, and **334.88M** plus a second 21.01M on Oct 1 2026. Three more burns fall in the next 90 days, on Nov 1, Dec 1 and Jan 1, which we project at the recent average of about 322M each.

The community pool kept **1.29B LUNC** off the market. A tenth of every transfer tax flows into the pool, and coins there are not counted as circulating until a vote spends them. The pool grew from **8.40B** to **9.16B LUNC** even after paying out 534.92M, and at the higher tax it should take in about **1.42B** next.

A programmatic buyback is **0** — the protocol never buys LUNC back. A foundation buy is **0**, because there is no foundation and no team wallet bought. A new long-term lock is **0**, because more LUNC left staking than joined it in this window; that net exit is counted on the sell side.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest pool that could reach the market is the staked LUNC itself: **901.38B** coins, about 14% of all supply, that holders can unstake with 21 days' notice. We read the staked total on-chain at every rebuild. The second is the **community pool** at **9.16B LUNC**, which pays out only by vote; every spend proposal is read from the chain's governance record.

Two more groups are already counted as circulating and so add nothing when they move: the oracle reward pool, **35.63B LUNC** paid out slowly to stakers, and the eight Terraform Labs wind-down wallets, **302.23M LUNC**, which moved only 0.44M this window. A further ~14.7B LUNC is left out of the circulating count without a named owner and did not move. If the staked total or the community pool falls between our checks, the coins that come out enter the sell side at the next rebuild.

## How LUNC compares to other burn-driven Layer-1 chains

LUNC sits in a small group of chains whose supply can only fall. Most proof-of-stake chains, including Cosmos Hub (ATOM) and Ethereum (ETH), pay stakers in newly minted coins, so supply grows unless a fee burn outruns issuance. Terra Classic switched minting off, and pays stakers from the oracle reward pool and fees instead, so every burn is a straight cut to supply with nothing printed against it.

The burn itself works differently from Ethereum's EIP-1559 base-fee burn or BNB's quarterly auto-burn. Terra Classic taxes the value moved, not the gas used, so the burn grows with transfer volume rather than with network congestion, and a high tax can push activity off the chain. The exchange burn is closer to a buyback-and-burn, but it is run by a third party out of its own trading fees, not by the protocol.

The scale is the catch. With about **5.52T LUNC** in circulation, even a 7B burn moves supply by little more than a tenth of a percent per quarter. A capped coin like Bitcoin shrinks its new supply by halving; LUNC shrinks its existing supply, but so slowly that the bigger short-term swing in its float comes from staking.
