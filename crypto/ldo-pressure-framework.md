# LDO Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

Lido DAO issues no new LDO and destroys none, so the entire Pressure Framework reading for LDO comes from one side of the ledger. Over the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026** the LDO token on Ethereum added **zero** new supply — the contract read exactly **1,000,000,000** at both ends of the window — while a holder-approved Lido DAO treasury buyback moved **12.75M LDO** off the open market and into the DAO treasury, a wallet that sits outside the circulating classification. On a float of **836.3M LDO** that is a net of **−1.52%** against our supply monitor's **−1.56%**, a gap of **0.03 percentage points**. LDO is **deflationary purely by discretionary buyback, on a supply that cannot move by itself**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 18 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **LDO at −1.52% net**: sell pressure of **zero** against buy pressure of **12.75M LDO**, on a circulating base of **836.3M LDO**. Our supply monitor reads **−1.56%** for the same window, a gap of **0.03 percentage points** — far inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag ships on this build. The two agree because they are measuring the same physical event from opposite ends. The Lido DAO treasury wallet at **0x3e40D73EB977Dc6a537aF587D48316feE66E9C8c** went from **102,013,354** to **114,765,849 LDO** across the window, an inflow of **12,752,496**; the monitor's classified circulating series fell from **849,525,859** to **836,302,736**, an outflow of **13,223,123**. One wallet filled up and the float emptied out by almost exactly the same amount. LDO is best labelled **a fixed-supply governance token whose only supply lever is a treasury with a spending mandate**.

## Sell pressure: where new LDO comes from

It does not come from anywhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and this is a measurement rather than a claim: the LDO contract at **0x5A98FcBEA516Cf06857215779Fd812CA3beF1B32** returned a total supply of exactly **1,000,000,000** at the block opening the window and exactly **1,000,000,000** at the block closing it, and the same figure at three sampled historical blocks stretching back over two years. Not one unit has been created since genesis in **Dec 2020**. The structural reason is that LDO is not a reward token. Lido is a liquid staking protocol; the yield it produces and the fee it charges are both denominated in staked ETH, and node operators and the treasury are paid in that asset. There is no validator issuance schedule, no emission curve, and no staking programme paying LDO to anybody. One caveat is worth stating plainly, because it is why this row is refreshed from the chain rather than closed permanently: the token's controller is still the Lido DAO's Aragon token manager at **0xf73a1260d222f447210581DDf212D915c09a3249**, which retains the ability to mint behind a governance vote. The fixed billion is a five-and-a-half-year governance convention, not a hard cap written into consensus.

The remaining sell rows are zero for equally concrete reasons. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the LDO vesting calendar physically expired: team and early-backer allocations ran a one-year lock from **Dec 2020** followed by a one-year linear release that completed on **Dec 16 2022**. That is an aggregator claim, so it was tested as a balance read rather than accepted — every identified LDO lock wallet, including the **50M**, **50M**, **45.83M**, **45M**, **31.12M** and **10M** multisigs and three separate **5M** safes, held an identical balance at both ends of the window. Not one token left an escrow. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: the Lido DAO treasury did release LDO through Easy Track grant motions as recently as **Mar 2 2026**, but it has released nothing in the five and a half months since, and with no published calendar the releases are sporadic rather than predictable. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** — Lido is a live protocol with no estate, no trustee and no court-supervised distribution naming LDO.

## Buy pressure: where new LDO goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is the entire buy side at **12.75M LDO**, and the mechanism is worth setting out precisely because a buyback can very easily be worth nothing. In **Mar 2026**, with LDO trading near an all-time low, Lido contributors proposed spending up to **10,000** staked ETH of idle DAO treasury assets on accumulating LDO; the Snapshot vote ran **Apr 6 2026** to **Apr 13 2026** and passed. Execution is deliberately slow and rule-bound: the budget is drawn in **1,000** staked-ETH batches through Easy Track motions, each carrying a published price cap and a **3%** maximum slippage tolerance, and the published rule is that acquired LDO must be delivered to the Lido DAO treasury before the next batch may be pulled. That is exactly what the chain shows. Three batches have been drawn — on **Apr 21 2026**, **Jun 19 2026** and **Jul 16 2026** — and the execution wallet at **0xf6F0732c1e9971497342C295141566E6F1A31e96** holds **zero** LDO today, having forwarded every purchase onward.

The decisive question for any buyback is whether the bought tokens actually leave the tradable float, and for LDO the answer is yes. Total supply of **1,000,000,000** minus a classified circulating supply of **836,299,461** leaves **163,700,539 LDO** outside the float, and the Lido DAO treasury alone accounts for **114,765,849** of that. The treasury is inside the non-circulating bucket, so coins arriving there are removed from the counted market — which is why our own supply monitor independently registered a **13.22M** drop in circulating LDO across the same window. A buyback that parked its purchases in a wallet the market already counts as circulating would be worth nothing at all; this one is not that. The remaining buy rows are **zero**. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** because LDO has no burn of any kind, confirmed by the flat total supply at both window ends — a burn can cut supply directly without ever touching a burn address, and neither happened here. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** because the treasury is the only buyer and its purchases are already counted in full under Buy #1. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** because LDO has no staking contract and no vote-escrow — holding is the only thing you can do with it, and holding is not a lock.

The forward number needs one more piece of care. The framework never divides an announced authorisation by four; it projects the realised spend. Across the window the execution wallet actually deployed **1,794.31** staked ETH into the market, which at today's **$1,906** per unit is roughly **$3.42M**, and at today's **$0.3042** per LDO buys about **11.24M LDO**. That is the next-90-day figure. It sits below the trailing **12.75M** not because the programme is slowing but because the same dollars buy fewer coins at a higher price than the roughly **$0.27** average realised over the window. There is ample runway: only **3,000** of the authorised **10,000** staked ETH have been drawn and **681.77** of those are still sitting undeployed, leaving about **7,682** units — more than four quarters at the current pace — so the budget cap does not bind on this projection.

## Foundation and overhang

LDO's team-controlled overhang is unusually concentrated and unusually visible. The largest item is the Lido DAO treasury itself at **114.77M LDO**, which is simultaneously the DAO's general reserve and the destination of every bought-back coin — the buyback does not burn anything, it moves supply from the market into a wallet the DAO can vote to spend again. That dual role is the single most important risk on this page, and it is refreshed from the chain on every rebuild. Second is the buyback execution wallet at **0xf6F0732c1e9971497342C295141566E6F1A31e96**, which holds no LDO but still carries **681.77** staked ETH of undeployed purchase budget. Third are the pre-DAO founder and early-backer multisigs, roughly **247M LDO** across nine identified wallets, every one of which held an unchanged balance from the first day of the window to the last.

The watch rule is the same for all three. If the Lido DAO treasury's balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, at whatever size the chain shows — and the treasury has a demonstrated history of doing exactly that, having released **1.50M**, **1.50M**, **1.68M**, **1.50M** and **1.10M LDO** in successive Easy Track grant motions between **Nov 2025** and **Feb 2026** before stopping in **Mar 2026**. The same applies to the founder multisigs. The honest framing of LDO's deflation is therefore conditional rather than structural: a buy-and-hold treasury takes coins off the market for as long as it chooses to keep holding them, whereas a burn takes them off permanently. Lido has chosen the reversible version.

## How LDO compares to other fixed-supply governance tokens

LDO belongs to the DeFi governance-token class — fixed-supply, non-inflationary voting tokens issued once at launch, with no block reward and no emission curve. Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap the contrast is instructive. A proof-of-work chain still mints, just on a decaying schedule, and its cap is enforced by consensus rules no vote can change; LDO mints nothing at all, but its billion is protected by a governance convention rather than by code, since the Aragon token manager retains a mint path. In practice the convention has held perfectly for five and a half years and through every market condition, which is meaningful evidence — but it is evidence about behaviour, not a guarantee about mechanism, and the framework tags the row accordingly.

The sharper comparison is against exchange tokens that run quarterly buy-and-burn programmes. Those destroy the tokens they buy, so the supply reduction is permanent and the total supply figure falls visibly quarter after quarter. Lido's buyback does not burn: **12.75M LDO** left the float and total supply stayed at exactly **1,000,000,000**. The reduction is real for as long as the treasury holds, and reversible the moment a vote deploys those coins into grants, contributor compensation or liquidity. It is also worth noting how LDO differs from vote-escrow exchange tokens, whose supply brake is thousands of individual holders choosing to lock: that brake is behavioural and fragile but decentralised, whereas Lido's brake is a single treasury with a mandate — stronger and more predictable while the mandate lasts, and entirely dependent on one governance process. Finally, unlike protocols that fund buybacks from live protocol revenue, this programme spends an accumulated balance-sheet asset. That is a finite resource with roughly **7,682** staked ETH left, not a perpetual pipe. The automatic successor programme is the one designed to be revenue-funded, and it is not firing.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Dynamic Buyback Program proposal, which entered forum discussion in **Aug 2026** with a Snapshot vote targeted for **Aug 25 2026**. It would replace the one-off authorisation with a standing tiered rule routing up to **70%** of incoming liquid treasury assets into periodic LDO buybacks while the liquid treasury sits above **$85M** — a materially larger and ongoing version of the mechanism this page measures. Second, the next **1,000** staked-ETH Easy Track batch, expected around **Sep 2026** on the roughly monthly pattern seen so far, worth about **6.3M LDO** at current prices. Third, the automatic buyback programme known as NEST, which is deployed on mainnet with a cap of **$50K** a day and **$10M** a year but only fires when Lido's staking revenue for the day exceeds roughly **$109K**; the protocol has run near **$75K** a day through **Aug 2026** and last cleared the trigger in **Apr 2026**, so a recovery in Ethereum staking revenue would switch on a second buy stream. Fourth, the Lido DAO treasury balance itself, which has released no LDO since **Mar 2 2026** — the first grant motion to resume would put a real figure into Sell #3 and could offset a full quarter of buyback. Fifth, any governance proposal touching the token manager's mint authority, which is the only path by which LDO's Sell #1 could ever stop being zero.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads LDO at **−1.52%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026** and projects **−1.34%** for the next 90, against a supply monitor reading of **−1.56%**. The structural mechanism is unusually simple: Lido DAO mints no LDO, burns no LDO, and finished its vesting calendar in **Dec 2022**, so the sell side of the ledger is zero on all four rows and a treasury buyback of **12.75M LDO** is the only thing moving the **836.3M** float. The key risk is that this deflation is discretionary and reversible — the bought coins are held in the Lido DAO treasury rather than destroyed, alongside a total team-controlled overhang above **360M LDO**, and a single governance vote could send them back to the market. The ceiling is equally concrete: the supply cannot exceed **1,000,000,000** without a mint vote, and the buyback cannot continue beyond the roughly **7,682** staked ETH of authorised budget still unspent unless holders approve more.
