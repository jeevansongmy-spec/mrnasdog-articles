# PEPE Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Pepe is the rarest thing the MrNasdog Pressure Framework measures: a ledger with a zero in all eight rows. PEPE has no mint function, no vesting calendar, no protocol fee burn and no buyback, so the framework books **0** sell pressure against **0** buy pressure on a fixed **420.69 trillion PEPE** supply, for a net of exactly **0.00%** over 90 days and **0.00%** forward. Reading the Pepe ERC-20 contract on Ethereum directly at both ends of the window confirms it: total supply moved by **327 PEPE** in 90 days, and the contract's owner slot reads the zero address at both blocks. The constraint that matters is the flip side of the same fact — a memecoin that cannot inflate also has no engine to shrink itself, which is why PEPE scores flat rather than perfect.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The Pressure Framework books **0 PEPE** of sell pressure against **0 PEPE** of buy pressure over the last 90 days, a net of **0.00%** of the **420.69 trillion** PEPE circulating float, and **0.00%** read forward. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+0.04%**, a gap of **0.04 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so no **⚠ monitor-gap chip** ships on this build. That residual is not a disagreement about Pepe at all: the monitor derives supply from market cap divided by price, so it wobbles by a few hundredths of a percent on rounding noise around a number the Ethereum chain says is fixed to the coin. The chain is the tiebreaker, and it is unambiguous — Pepe's ERC-20 totalSupply read **420,689,899,646,442** PEPE on **May 16 2026** and **420,689,899,646,115** PEPE on **Aug 14 2026**. Pepe is best labelled **a frozen supply**: a memecoin whose supply question was settled permanently on its launch day and has not moved since.

## Sell pressure: where new PEPE comes from

Nowhere, and that is a verifiable claim rather than a marketing one. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0** because the Pepe contract exposes no mint function to any wallet, contract or vote, and because ownership was renounced — the **owner()** slot returns the zero address, checked on Ethereum at both ends of this 90-day window rather than taken from the project's own site. That double read is the point. A renounce claimed in a launch tweet is a claim; a renounce that the chain still reports at two dated blocks three months apart is a mechanism. There is no upgrade proxy, no admin key and no governance surface through which a mint could be reintroduced, so Sell #1 is not merely zero today — it cannot become anything else.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** for a different reason: no vesting calendar exists to read. Pepe launched in **Apr 2023** as a stealth fair launch with no presale, no team allocation and no investor tranche. All **420.69 trillion** PEPE were created in the deployment transaction and distributed immediately — roughly **93.1%** into a Uniswap liquidity pool whose LP tokens were then burned, and **6.9%** into a launch multisig earmarked for exchange listings, bridges and additional pools. Three years later there is no cliff pending, no monthly tranche and no unlock schedule anywhere, which removes the single largest source of measured sell pressure across the rest of this catalog. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because Pepe has no foundation, no labs entity, no DAO and no operating treasury; the enumerated overhangs are covered below, and none of them moved. Sell #4 is **0** because no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches PEPE.

## Buy pressure: where new PEPE goes

The buy side is empty for the same structural reason the sell side is, which is the part most readers miss. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0** because Pepe generates no revenue with which to buy anything. A zero-tax token takes no cut of its own trading volume, so there is no fee stream, no treasury balance and no buyback contract to read. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0** for the identical reason — with no fee, there is nothing to destroy, and the Pepe contract runs no scheduled or automatic burn of its own. The measured removal across this window was **327 PEPE** of total supply, worth well under a dollar, sent by individual holders with no programme behind it. Under the framework's discretionary rules a sporadic dust burn with no schedule is booked at zero with a note; it is never annualised into a projected rate.

The Pepe burn most people remember is real but historical. Roughly **6.9 trillion** PEPE — about **1.64%** of supply — sits in the burn address from a single community event in **Oct 2023**, executed after the multisig incident that August. Across this 90-day window that address received a further **140,701** PEPE, which is dust and, more importantly, is not a supply reduction at all: coins sent to a dead address still count in the circulating classification. Meanwhile a community "cumulative burn" roadmap continues to circulate in price-prediction coverage with a mid-2026 target; the chain shows it has not executed, which is exactly the pattern the framework treats as announcement-only. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** because no entity exists to buy. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** because the one lock Pepe has is permanent and predates the window by three years — the launch liquidity position was burned in **Apr 2023**, so that pool can never be pulled, but it removed nothing from the float during these 90 days.

## Foundation and overhang

Pepe has no foundation to enumerate, so the overhang question becomes a holder question. The original **6.9%** launch multisig — the one that paid for exchange listings, that rogue signers drained of roughly **16 trillion** PEPE in **Aug 2023**, and that executed the **6.9 trillion** community burn that October — reads empty on-chain at both ends of this window. That allocation is spent, and this build confirms it by balance read rather than by inference.

Two items remain worth watching, and this build corrects the prior reading that the launch allocation had fully dispersed. Two multisig contracts still hold **2.95 trillion** and **2.12 trillion** PEPE respectively, together about **1.2%** of supply. Both are Safe-style contracts with multiple signers, and both held a balance identical to the coin on **May 16 2026** and **Aug 14 2026** — not one token moved in 90 days. Neither is claimed by the Pepe project in any public disclosure, so neither is booked as team-controlled sell pressure; they are surfaced at value zero and refreshed on every rebuild. The remaining large holders are exchange custodial wallets, which belong to depositors and are excluded from overhang accounting by construction. The trigger is plain: if either multisig's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How PEPE compares to other fixed-supply tokens

Against the halving-model chains, PEPE is stricter and duller. Bitcoin has a hard cap too, but it is still issuing — new coins arrive every block until the subsidy rounds to zero, so its framework reading is positive and decays on a schedule. Pepe skipped the schedule entirely: it minted its full **420.69 trillion** in one transaction and closed the door. There is no emission curve to model and no halving date to watch, which makes PEPE's supply the most predictable input in this catalog, in a framework built specifically to reward predictability.

Against its closest structural peers — the large fixed-supply memecoins on Ethereum and Solana — the differences are mechanical rather than cultural. Shiba Inu shares the fixed-supply, renounced-contract, burned-liquidity shape but has built an ecosystem around itself, and its layer-2 routes a share of fees into a burn portal, which gives it a genuine, if small, deflationary row that PEPE simply does not have. Bonk runs recurring community burn events with real quanta. PEPE has neither an ecosystem revenue line nor an executing burn programme, so its buy side stays empty by design, not by accident.

That is precisely why PEPE reads flat and not deflationary, and it is the single most misread thing about this asset. The Pressure Framework's inflation metric awards its top marks only to supply that is actively shrinking — real burns or buybacks larger than new issuance. A permanently capped, permanently frozen supply is a **3** on a scale to **5**, not a top score, because nothing is being removed. Exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks and fee-burning layer 1s can and do outscore PEPE on this metric while carrying supply risks PEPE does not have. The honest summary is that Pepe removes inflation as a variable rather than turning it into an advantage.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things, none of them scheduled. First, the two silent multisigs holding **2.95 trillion** and **2.12 trillion** PEPE: any outflow from either is the only realistic path to a non-zero Sell #3, and both are re-read on every rebuild. Second, the community burn roadmap — if a burn of meaningful size actually executes on-chain rather than being announced, Buy #2 turns non-zero and PEPE's inflation score moves off flat for the first time. Third, the spot PEPE ETF application filed with the SEC in **Apr 2026** and still under review, which would change custody and demand without touching supply, and would move coins from exchange wallets into a custodian rather than creating or destroying any. Fourth, the burn address balance itself, currently **6.92 trillion** PEPE: a step change there rather than the current dust drip would signal a coordinated event worth booking.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Pepe at **0.00%** net supply change over the last 90 days and **0.00%** forward, against a supply monitor at **+0.04%** — a gap of **0.04 percentage points** that is pure derivation noise, not a conflict. The mechanism behind that reading is permanence rather than management: **420.69 trillion** PEPE were minted in **Apr 2023**, ownership of the Ethereum contract was renounced to the zero address, and total supply has moved by **327 PEPE** across this window. The key risk is not dilution but the two unattributed multisigs holding about **1.2%** of supply between them, which have not moved and would enter the ledger the moment they do. The ceiling is the same fact as the floor — with no mint, no vesting, no fee burn and no buyback, PEPE's supply cannot grow, but it cannot shrink either, which caps this metric at flat.
