Pepe is a fixed-supply memecoin where every sell row is a real zero, and this build proved each one rather than assuming it. The PEPE contract on Ethereum has no mint anyone can call, its owner slot is the zero address, nothing is left to vest, and the launch team wallet is empty — a complete sweep of every transfer touching it across the whole window found none in and none out. Over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** the only movement was destruction: **130.31M PEPE** burned, against **420,690,000,000,000 PEPE** in circulation. That is **0.000031%** — the framework reads **−0.00%** net supply pressure and projects **−0.00%** forward. The widely repeated **$500 million** PEPE burn roadmap has not executed: the burn address took in about **$499** worth over the quarter.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** the framework reads PEPE at **−0.000031%** net supply pressure — **0 PEPE** on the sell side against **130,309,068 PEPE** destroyed on the buy side — and projects the same **−0.000031%** forward, because the burn is voluntary and continuous and nothing exists that could turn a sell row on. Our supply monitor reads **−0.6430%** for the same window, a gap of **0.64 percentage points**, and that gap is an artefact rather than a conflict, so no data-conflict flag ships on the overview. PEPE trades near **$0.00000383**, and the price feed behind that monitor carries three significant figures; one tick at that magnitude moves an implied supply figure by **0.26%** to **0.43%**, which is roughly ten thousand times the size of the real 90-day change. Worse, the base date this reading uses landed on the single highest print in the whole series — a day earlier the same calculation returned **+0.32%**, and a day before that **+0.08%**, a swing of nearly a full percentage point with no supply change on the chain at all. Recovering the supply series at full precision settles it: **420,690,000,000,000** on every one of the 90 days, a perfect flat line. PEPE is best labelled a **frozen supply with a decorative burn**: nothing can be added, and what is removed is a rounding error.

## Sell pressure: where new PEPE comes from

Nowhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and for PEPE that zero is provable from the published source rather than inferred from a flat chart. The Pepe ERC-20 at **0x6982508145454ce325ddbe47a25d4ec3d2311933** is a plain, non-upgradeable contract — no proxy, no implementation behind it — and everything the project added on top of a standard token amounts to four things: a constructor, two owner-only switches, and a public burn. The internal creation call appears exactly once, inside that constructor, and ran in **April 2023**. There is no external mint, no minter role and no cap parameter that could later be raised. On top of that the owner slot reads as the zero address, so ownership has been given up and even the two owner-only switches are permanently uncallable. That distinction matters: this is not a mint nobody happens to have called, it is a mint that does not exist and cannot be added.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because the whole supply was placed on day one — **93.10%** straight into a liquidity pool whose pool tokens were burnt, **6.90%** to a team wallet — with no investor round, no cliff and no release calendar. The unlock trackers record the Pepe schedule as finished in **2023** with no future events, in 2026 or after. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: PEPE has never been part of a bankruptcy estate and there is no trustee distributing it into this market.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is the row that had to be proved rather than asserted, because the launch allocation implies a team wallet somewhere. It is empty. The multisig associated with the 2023 team incident, **0x5BdEA820293689B77FA8e83b1E3D58B27B5ce9BA**, holds **0 PEPE** at both ends of the window — and a wallet reading zero at both ends can be hiding a pass-through, so we swept every PEPE transfer in and out of it across all **645,548** blocks of the window and found none of either. A control sweep on the same feed returned **112** PEPE transfers in the last 200 blocks, so the empty result is a real empty rather than a broken query. Two unlabelled whales sit close to the old **6.90%** share — **28.87T PEPE** and **28.41T PEPE** — and a balance match is exactly the trap here, so we checked what else they hold: one carries **800** different tokens and the other **41**, portfolios that belong to whales and airdrop magnets, not to a project safe. Neither is claimed by the project in any disclosure. They are watched, not counted, and so are two multisig contracts holding **2.95T PEPE** and **2.12T PEPE** that did not move a single token all window. Separately an exchange wallet released **17.74T PEPE** over the window; that is depositor-owned coin moving between custody accounts, not project supply, so it is excluded.

## Buy pressure: where new PEPE goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**, and it is a zero by construction: PEPE charges nothing, earns nothing and holds nothing, so there is no revenue to buy with and no treasury to buy from — and with ownership renounced, no buyback contract could be wired in now. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0** for the same reason. The project states plainly that there are no taxes, and the published source agrees: the transfer path carries no fee split at all. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0**, because there is no entity to do the buying and no accumulation wallet appears on the chain. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** — PEPE has no staking, no vault and no lockup contract of any kind, so a holder's only three options are hold, sell, or destroy.

That leaves Buy #5, community burns, the only non-zero number anywhere in the PEPE ledger, at **130.31M PEPE**. It sits outside the four canonical rows deliberately: this is not a fee burn, because there is no fee — it is holders choosing to destroy their own coins. Measuring it needs two meters, because PEPE can be destroyed in two different shapes. Most of it is transfers into the burn address **0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD**, which the contract itself never notices: that balance rose from **6,917,414,214,146 PEPE** to **6,917,544,522,308 PEPE** across the window, an increase of **130,308,162 PEPE**. The rest is real destruction through the contract's public burn call, which lowered the reported total supply by **906.53 PEPE** — a tiny number, but the one that proves the burn entrypoint is live rather than dormant. Nine evenly spaced readings across the window show the burn address rising at every single step and the reported supply stepping down twice, so nothing here is a frozen field being mistaken for a flat one. Together the two meters give **130,309,068 PEPE** destroyed, worth about **$499**.

That number deserves to be put next to the headline it contradicts. A widely repeated 2026 roadmap says PEPE is heading for **$500 million** in cumulative token burns by mid-2026. Differenced against the burn address itself, the entire cumulative destruction since launch is **6.92 trillion PEPE**, worth **$26.49 million** at today's price — about nineteen times below the claim — and almost all of it is a single community burn executed in **October 2023**. The quarter just measured added **$499** to that total. A separate headline claiming **36%** of supply burned belongs to Pepecoin, a different proof-of-work asset, and was not imported here. One popular burn tracker was checked and discarded: it publishes no burn figure at all and displays a PEPE price roughly fourteen thousand times too high.

## Foundation and overhang

PEPE has no foundation, no labs entity, no DAO treasury and no governance surface at all — with ownership renounced there is nothing for a vote to control, so there is no forum and no vote portal to watch. The launch multisig is empty and provably inert, and so is the address that executed the 2023 community burn. What remains is two multisig contracts that hold real balances and never move them: **0x593f9a120Cd21ef38d4b0E761fA5385fc7D66C96** with **2.95T PEPE** and **0x310039f661e9fbD3518D2820221Ed135FC99cBDD** with **2.12T PEPE**, together **1.21%** of supply and byte-identical at both ends of the window. Neither is claimed by the project in any disclosure, so both are watched rather than counted — capacity to sell is not the same thing as a habit of selling.

One of those two is a lesson in how identification goes wrong. It first looked like a spam wallet, because the great majority of the tokens it holds are counterfeit stablecoin lookalikes with letters swapped for identical-looking characters. But unsolicited airdrops land in any address that has ever done anything, and they say nothing about who controls it — checking what else a wallet holds can rule an identification in, and it can equally rule one out for the wrong reason. Both of these are multisig contracts holding roughly half a percent of a major token each, motionless for a full quarter. That is the shape of a residual allocation, and it belongs on the watch list. The two large unlabelled wallets, **28.87T PEPE** and **28.41T PEPE**, go the other way — they are ordinary individual accounts on the evidence, and their movements are noise rather than project supply. If any of these four balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh; the same trigger applies to the launch multisig the moment it stops reading zero.

One more classification matters. PEPE also exists on BNB Chain, Arbitrum and Avalanche, and those legs are mirrors rather than additional supply. The test is the residual: the published float minus the Ethereum supply is **100.35M PEPE** at the start of the window and **100.35M PEPE** at the end, a constant. If the sidechain legs were additive that residual would have to be around **445 billion**, four thousand times larger. They are bridge-minted representations of PEPE already counted on Ethereum, and they are not summed.

## How PEPE compares to other fixed-supply memecoins

PEPE belongs to the strictest structural class in this framework: a fixed-supply ERC-20 with a renounced, non-upgradeable contract and no fee mechanism. Compare that to an exchange token with a quarterly buyback, where a company earns revenue and spends part of it destroying coins — that token has a live buy-side engine whose size scales with business performance, and a treasury that can also release coins. PEPE has neither engine and neither risk. Compare it instead to an uncapped continuous-emission layer-1, where validators are paid in new coins every block and the sell side is a permanent structural headwind measured in whole percentage points a quarter. PEPE's sell side is not small; it is absent.

The closer comparison is to other renounced memecoins with a burn culture. There the difference is where the burn comes from. A memecoin attached to its own layer-2 or fee-bearing app has a genuine revenue stream feeding destruction, so its burn grows when usage grows. PEPE's burn is purely voluntary — holders sending their own coins to the dead address because they want to — so it does not scale with anything, it has no schedule, and it can stop entirely without breaching any commitment. And compare it to a fee-burning smart-contract chain, where the burn is mechanical and can genuinely outrun issuance in a busy quarter, producing real deflation. PEPE cannot reach that outcome from this structure: a burn worth **$499** a quarter against a **$1.6 billion** market is not a deflationary mechanism, it is a gesture. What PEPE does have, and most tokens in any of those classes do not, is a genuinely closed sell side — no mint, no vesting, no treasury, no estate.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

There is no dated supply event on the PEPE calendar between **Aug 26 2026** and **Nov 24 2026** — no cliff, no unlock, no scheduled burn — so the watch list is made of conditions rather than dates. First, the community burn roadmap: it remains a discussion with no contract, no funding source and no dated tranche behind it, and it would need a visible, funded execution before it could change any row here. Second, the two unlabelled whale wallets holding roughly **13.6%** of supply between them; if either starts distributing, Sell #3 stops being zero. Third, the two static multisig contracts holding **1.21%** of supply between them, and the launch multisig at **0x5BdEA820293689B77FA8e83b1E3D58B27B5ce9BA**, which has read zero all window and would become a live overhang the moment it does not. Fourth, the bridged legs on BNB Chain, Arbitrum and Avalanche — the Avalanche leg shrank by **3.05B PEPE** this window as tokens were redeemed back, and a sustained change in the residual would mean the mirror assumption needs re-testing. Fifth, and most likely to matter to anyone reading a supply chart elsewhere: the price feeds behind third-party supply figures still carry three significant figures at this magnitude, so any PEPE inflation number that swings by half a percentage point in a day is measuring rounding, not coins.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads PEPE at **−0.000031%** net supply pressure over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** and projects the same figure forward — flat, not deflationary. The structural mechanism is a renounced, non-upgradeable Ethereum contract with no callable mint, no vesting schedule, no treasury and no fee, which closes the sell side completely, set against voluntary community burns of **130,309,068 PEPE** that amount to **0.000031%** of a **420.69 trillion** supply. The key risk is not new issuance — none is possible — but concentration: two motionless multisig contracts hold **1.21%** of the float with no disclosure behind them, two unlabelled wallets hold roughly **13.6%** more on no schedule, and an exchange account released **17.74T PEPE** of depositor coin this window. The ceiling is absolute and already reached: **420,690,000,000,000 PEPE** was created once in **2023** and that number can only ever fall, but at the observed pace it would take longer than recorded history for the burn to matter.
