Audiera released **63.75M BEAT** from its vesting wallets over the last 90 days and destroyed **10.07M** through its weekly revenue burn, a net **+16.24%** of the **330.5M** circulating supply — while our own monitor reads **+24.20%**, a gap of **7.96** percentage points that carries a warning chip. BEAT is a fixed **1,000,000,000** BEP-20 token on BNB Chain whose owner slot is the empty address, so the mint function in its code can never be called again: every token that will ever exist already exists, and the entire inflation story is a release calendar. The burn is genuine and unusually well evidenced, but it is roughly a sixth of the calendar — and on **Nov 1 2026** a 12-month cliff makes the calendar bigger, not smaller.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** the Audiera sell ledger totals **63.75M BEAT** and the buy ledger **10.07M BEAT**, for a net of **+16.24%** against a circulating supply of **330,516,666 BEAT**. Our inflation monitor reads **+24.20%** over the same window, so the gap is **+7.96** percentage points — well past the half-point tolerance, and the page carries a warning chip for it. That gap is not a mystery and it is not hand-waved: **4.71** points of it are a base convention, because the monitor divides the same release by the supply as it stood 90 days ago rather than by today's, and **3.05** points are burn invisibility, because the supply figure it reads is the vesting calendar exactly — **139,266,666** at launch plus nine tranches of **21,250,000** — with no deduction whatsoever for BEAT sent to a dead address. The remaining **0.20** points are its own sampling noise. Audiera is **structurally inflationary on a fixed supply**: nothing is being created, and it is still diluting fast.

## Sell pressure: where new BEAT comes from

Nowhere, in the literal sense — and that is the point worth understanding about BEAT. Protocol inflation is **zero** and it is permanent. The BEAT contract on BNB Chain does contain a mint function, and it is gated to the contract owner, but the owner slot returns the empty address at the start of the window, at the end of the window, and back at launch on **Nov 1 2025**. Total supply read exactly **1,000,000,000** at six separate checkpoints across ten months. No Audiera token can ever be created again. What the ledger measures instead is vesting: **63.75M BEAT** released from a monthly calendar, in three tranches of **21.25M** on **Jun 1**, **Jul 1** and **Aug 1 2026**.

That number is a measurement, not a calendar entitlement. Audiera's allocations sit in eight identifiable wallets, and every one of them was read on-chain at both ends of the 90 days: together they fell from **733,234,730 BEAT** to **669,486,368**, a realised outflow of **63,748,362** against a scheduled **63,750,000**. The two agree to **1,638** tokens. Vesting on Audiera does what its documentation says it does, which is not something to assume — plenty of projects vest on paper while the tokens never leave the lock. The single most useful thing in that read is the marketing wallet: it held **30,000,000** on **May 21 2026** and **zero** on **Aug 19 2026**. The marketing and operations allocation is spent, which is why the monthly tranche drops from **21.25M** to **11.25M** from **Sep 1 2026**. That is an observed empty wallet, not a forecast.

The other two sell rows are zero for structural reasons. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero because every locked BEAT sits on a published release date — there is no undated discretionary pool at all, which is rarer than it sounds. Long-term locked or bankruptcy is zero because Audiera launched in **Nov 2025** and has never been through an insolvency, so there is no estate and no trustee distributing tokens into the market.

## Buy pressure: where new BEAT goes

Audiera runs no buyback, and the distinction is the most commonly mis-stated fact about this token. A number of write-ups describe the project converting revenue into stablecoins and repurchasing BEAT on the open market. It does not. Audiera prices its subscriptions in BEAT — **100 BEAT** a month for the standard creative plan and **300 BEAT** a month for the pro plan — so revenue arrives already denominated in the token, and the token is destroyed as it stands. That makes the flow a protocol fee burn, not a programmatic buyback, and the difference matters to a holder: a burn removes supply, but it puts no bid under the price and leaves no accumulation wallet anyone needs to watch.

The burn itself is **10.07M BEAT** over 90 days and it is unusually well evidenced. Audiera's total supply cannot fall — the contract has no burn function at all — so destruction happens by transfer to a dead address, and that address is the ledger. It held **10,150,369** BEAT on **May 21 2026** and **20,224,177** on **Aug 19 2026**. Sampled day by day the balance is a step function, flat for six days and then jumping about **800,000** at once, most recently on **Aug 16 2026**. Audiera's own weekly reports land on the same running totals from the opposite direction, and they show the project destroying roughly **99.8%** of the BEAT it takes in. Foundation buying is zero — the foundation wallet only pays out, from **122.5M** down to **113.7M** across the window — and new long-term locks are zero, because Audiera has no staking product and no lock contract live; governance is a later phase of its roadmap rather than something running today.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang behind BEAT is **669.5M** tokens, which is **two thirds** of everything that will ever exist, and it was enumerated wallet by wallet rather than taken from a tokenomics page. The community rewards wallet holds **325.0M BEAT** and pays out **8.33M** a month to **Nov 2029**. The foundation holds **113.7M** and pays **2.92M** a month on the same horizon. Three advisor and angel wallets hold **130.7M** between them — a sum that matches the published **13.07%** allocation to the token, which is how they were identified — and have never moved. The team wallet holds **80.0M** and has never moved. A second airdrop wallet holds exactly **20.0M** and has never moved. The marketing wallet now holds nothing. One smaller pot sits outside that queue and is worth naming because it is easy to miss: the single project wallet that collects revenue and sends it to the dead address held **801,150 BEAT** on **Aug 19 2026**, up from **628,300** at the start of the window — next week's burn, resting. Every one of these is refreshed by reading the chain, and the trigger is simple: if any of these balances falls faster than the calendar between refreshes, the extra outflow enters the sell ledger at the next refresh.

## How BEAT compares to other fixed-supply app tokens

BEAT belongs to a class that looks safe on the tin and often is not: fixed-supply application tokens whose contract cannot mint, but whose float is a small fraction of the cap. On the mint question Audiera is genuinely in the strongest tier — the owner slot is renounced, so it sits alongside tokens whose supply is a compiled constant rather than alongside chains that issue continuously to validators. An uncapped proof-of-stake network dilutes its holders a few percent a year and can never stop; Audiera dilutes them **16.24%** in a quarter and will stop entirely, on a known date, in **Nov 2029**. Those are opposite shapes of the same risk, and the second one is at least finite and fully scheduled.

Where BEAT separates from most of its class is the burn. The typical fixed-supply game or app token has a vesting calendar on the sell side and literally nothing on the buy side, so the reading is simply the calendar divided by the float. Audiera has a revenue burn that removed **10.07M BEAT** in 90 days, and unlike the quarterly buyback programmes run by exchange tokens, it is not a discretionary treasury decision that can be quietly skipped — it is the token the users paid, destroyed weekly, with the dead-address balance available to anyone who wants to check. What it is not is a bid. An exchange token that buys back on the open market both removes supply and spends real money buying the asset; Audiera removes supply and spends nothing. On a **90%** drawdown that difference is the whole difference.

The comparison that matters most, though, is against the cliff structure. Tokens with a 12-month insider cliff all share one property: the reading gets worse before it gets better, because the cliff opens while the earlier buckets are still draining. Audiera hits that point on **Nov 1 2026**, when team, advisor and second-airdrop supply all start releasing at once and the monthly tranche jumps from **11.25M** to **22.1M**.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

On **Sep 1 2026** the first tranche without a marketing component releases **11.25M BEAT** — the confirmation that the marketing wallet really is finished. On **Oct 1 2026** another **11.25M** follows. On **Nov 1 2026** the 12-month cliff opens and the tranche jumps to **22.1M**, made up of the existing **11.25M** plus **2.22M** of team supply, **3.63M** of advisor and angel supply and **5.0M** from the second airdrop; the team and advisor wallets have never moved a token, so that date is the first test of whether they sell. Every Sunday, Audiera publishes a revenue and burn report, and the number to watch is the BEAT count rather than the dollar figure: subscriptions are priced in BEAT, so a **95%** price fall makes the pro plan far cheaper in real terms, and whether that grows or shrinks the weekly burn is the single largest uncertainty on the buy side. Finally, watch the dead address itself — a week without a step would mean the burn has stopped, whatever any report says.

## Summary

Audiera's BEAT cannot inflate and is inflating anyway: the supply is fixed at **1,000,000,000** with the mint path renounced, yet the vesting calendar pushed **63.75M BEAT** to market in 90 days against a **10.07M** revenue burn, for a net **+16.24%**. The forward reading eases to **+10.45%** as the marketing allocation exhausts, and the escrow read confirms that allocation is genuinely empty rather than merely due to end. The key risk is the shape of what follows: a **669.5M** overhang, two thirds of the entire supply, still sits in seven wallets on a schedule that runs to **Nov 2029**, and the **Nov 1 2026** cliff makes the monthly release larger before the calendar starts to shrink. The ceiling is real and the burn is real; the floor is a calendar with three more years to run.
