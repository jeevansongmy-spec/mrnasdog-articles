# CRV Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

Supply growing · projected to keep growing. Curve DAO added **34.56M CRV** to circulation and removed **11.37K CRV** during the last 90 days, producing **+2.21% net** against the monitor’s **+2.30%**. Scheduled issuance supports **+1.53%** next quarter; old escrow claims and future vote-lock withdrawals could add more, but have no committed net payment schedule.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The Pressure Framework reads CRV at **+2.21%** over the window ending **Sep 20 2026**, with **+1.53%** scheduled through **Dec 19 2026**. The supply monitor reads **+2.30%**, a difference of **0.09 percentage points**, below the warning threshold, so no monitor-gap chip is needed. Curve remains structurally inflationary on its active float. The improvement in the forward figure comes from the smaller emission rate and from leaving undated future claims unquantified. It does not promise that those claims will stop. The circulating base is **1,561.36M CRV**, and every quantity measures movement across that base rather than token trading volume.

## Sell pressure: where new CRV comes from

Protocol inflation supplied **26.96M CRV** during the measured quarter. Curve’s token contract releases minting capacity per second; gauges create the tokens when rewards are claimed. The annual reduction took effect on **Aug 12 2026**, bringing the rate to approximately **3.081 CRV per second**. That rate supports **23.96M CRV** over the next 90 days. The historical window combines time before and after the reduction, so simply repeating its larger mint would overstate the currently scheduled rate. Changes in Ethereum’s block interval do not alter this time-based calculation.

Vesting contributed **0.93M CRV** of realised claims. The original liquidity-provider escrow completed its vesting clock years ago, but completion of the clock did not empty its wallet. Beneficiaries still withdraw previously vested coins. The framework therefore counts the actual decline in the excluded escrow balance, while leaving its next-quarter amount at **0 quantified** because the remaining claims have no reliable payment dates. This is an uncertainty in timing, not a conclusion that every old allocation has disappeared.

Foundation and unscheduled releases contribute **0 CRV** separately: the community escrow was unchanged, and adding its standing balance would invent a flow. Long-term locks supplied another **6.67M CRV** through the net decline in the veCRV contract. Withdrawals outweighed new deposits across the window. Future net withdrawals depend on expiries, claims and replacement locks, so the forward ledger does not repeat that behavioural total automatically. No separate bankruptcy distribution was identified. The difference between measured releases and forecast commitments is material to this reading.

## Buy pressure: where new CRV goes

Programmatic buyback is **0 CRV**. Curve’s fee-distribution system transfers settlement assets to eligible lockers; it does not establish a recurring purchase and destruction of CRV. Protocol fee burn is also **0 CRV**: the CRV dead-address balance was unchanged. Contracts called fee burners convert assets along a distribution path, and their names alone do not make CRV deflationary. The token’s issuance counter and its sink balances were checked separately so that a transfer-based removal would not be overlooked.

Foundation buy contributes **0 CRV**, with no verified purchase-and-removal programme booked. New long-term lock is **0 CRV** on a net basis because the vote-escrow balance declined; counting the same movement on both sides would duplicate it. An additional **11.32K CRV** entered the token contract itself, an address excluded from the circulating calculation. That small removal is shown separately from fee burns. No payment cadence supports repeating it forward. Holders also destroyed **55.49 CRV** through direct burn calls, a separate removal with no committed repeat. Gross minting minus those burns matches the supply counter exactly; the full ledger matches the official circulation helper’s change.

## Foundation and overhang

The community escrow holds **845.14K CRV**, unchanged through the quarter. The original liquidity-provider escrow retains **10.30M CRV** that beneficiaries can still claim. Gauges also have approximately **10.30M CRV** of mintable but unclaimed capacity, distinct from already minted escrow holdings. The veCRV contract holds **846.78M CRV**; this is user-owned locked principal, not a foundation treasury. Each balance is re-read on chain at a rebuild, with its movement assigned to the mechanism that actually released it.

The newer treasury wallet holds **5.73M CRV**, but it is already inside the circulating classification. Its spending can affect the market without creating another circulation addition. If a verified excluded reserve releases coins between refreshes, that release enters the relevant sell row at the next refresh; existing gauge claims and liquidity-escrow withdrawals are counted once in their own rows. A decrease in a wallet already circulating does not receive a second entry merely because the wallet is associated with governance.

## How CRV compares to other vote-escrow DeFi tokens

CRV combines a declining emission schedule with a renewable locking system. A governance token with no ongoing mint may still expand its float through old allocations, but Curve has both issuance and unlock exposure. A halving-style schedule reduces future issuance on a clock; Curve’s annual reduction serves a similar mathematical purpose without making the token a proof-of-work asset. In either case, slower issuance remains positive issuance until removals or a complete end of rewards offset it.

Vote escrow also differs from irreversible destruction. A holder can commit CRV for voting power and fees, then reclaim the principal when the commitment permits. New locks and expired locks must therefore be considered together. Revenue-sharing tokens that purchase and burn their own asset have a direct removal channel that Curve’s stable-asset distributions do not supply. Protocol activity, fee income and circulating supply can all move differently; none substitutes for the others in this comparison.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Through **Dec 19 2026**, watch gauge minting against the post-August rate and its existing unclaimed backlog. Recheck the original liquidity-provider escrow for actual claims rather than a newly invented vesting cliff. Track the veCRV balance for a reversal from net withdrawals to net deposits. Follow Curve governance for an executed allocation or CRV purchase mandate, and verify whether any recipient is outside or already inside circulation. No additional annual emission reduction falls inside this forecast window.

## Summary

CRV’s verdict is **Supply growing · projected to keep growing**, at **+2.21%** measured and **+1.53%** from currently quantified forward issuance. The mint is smaller, but old escrow claims and vote-lock exits remain additional sources of tradable coins. The principal forecast risk is undated claim activity, not an assumed repeat of every historical transfer. Curve’s declining issuance schedule constrains the rate; it does not eliminate the remaining locked and unclaimed supply.
