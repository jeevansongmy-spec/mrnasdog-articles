Curve DAO's CRV is still inflating, but by less every year. The Curve Finance gauge emission minted about **28.5M CRV** over the trailing 90 days and stepped down roughly **16%** on **Aug 12 2026**, taking annual issuance under **100M CRV** for the first time since launch. Add **~4.1M CRV** returned by expiring veCRV locks and **~0.9M** of unclaimed 2020 vesting finally collected, and the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+2.16%** net new supply — with a buy side of exactly **zero**, because Curve runs no buyback and no burn by design. Our supply monitor agrees at **+2.13%**, a gap of **0.03 percentage points**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window to **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **CRV at +2.16% net** for the trailing window and **+1.87%** for the forward window, the improvement coming entirely from the Epoch 6 emission cut rather than from any new demand. Our supply monitor reads **+2.13%** for the trailing window, a gap of just **0.03 percentage points** — far inside the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip appears on the CRV overview. The two agree because the ledger reconstructs the monitor almost exactly: **28.5M CRV** of gauge emission plus **4.1M CRV** of net veCRV lock expiry equals the **32.3M** of circulating growth the monitor measured. Curve DAO is best characterised as **a hard-capped DeFi token on a permanently shrinking emission schedule with no buy-side mechanism at all**.

## Sell pressure: where new CRV comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **28.5M CRV** over 90 days, and it is the only source of genuinely new CRV in existence. The Curve DAO token contract mints to liquidity-provider gauges at a rate hard-coded at deployment in August 2020, which divides itself down by a fixed factor once a year on the anniversary. That reduction is permissionless: no multisig, no admin and no governance vote can accelerate, delay or reverse it. The **Epoch 6** step landed inside this window on **Aug 12 2026**, cutting the mint from **316,563 CRV** a day to **266,197 CRV** a day, or roughly **115.5M** to **97.2M CRV** a year. Because the change is dated and documented, the framework keeps the trailing column as measured but re-bases the forward column onto the post-cut rate, giving **24.0M CRV** for the next 90 days.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **0.9M CRV**, and it is a claim tail rather than a schedule. Every CRV vesting programme from the 2020 launch has finished: the shareholder and employee allocations completed by **Aug 13 2024**, and the early-user community allocation a year after launch. What survives is the original community vesting escrow, which still holds CRV that vested years ago but was never claimed. Read on-chain at both ends of the window, that escrow paid out **0.9M CRV** and still holds **10.3M**. The framework books what actually left the contract, not what the calendar says vested. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**: the Curve DAO community-fund contract held **845,144 CRV** at both ends of the window without moving a coin, and the one large discretionary request of 2026, a **17.45M CRV** multi-year development grant, was voted down by the DAO. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: no estate or trustee schedule touches CRV.

Sell #5 — net veCRV lock expiry — is **4.1M CRV**, and it is the row that makes the CRV ledger reconcile. Curve's vote-escrow contract has no early exit: locked CRV can only be withdrawn once the lock expires, so a lock is a genuine removal from the tradable float and an expiry is a genuine return. Netting new locks against expiries, the escrow shrank from **854.9M** to **850.8M CRV** across the window, so **4.1M** came back to the market. This is a slow drift rather than an event — the same balance read **857.7M** in February 2026 — so the framework carries the observed rate forward unchanged.

## Buy pressure: where new CRV goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, and for Curve Finance that is a decision rather than an omission. The project has published its own argument that vote-escrow locking beats buy-and-burn, on the reasoning that veCRV has consistently locked around **three times** more supply than a comparable burn would have removed while also producing governance participation. Trading fees, admin fees and crvUSD interest are paid to veCRV lockers in a stablecoin, so protocol revenue never becomes a CRV bid. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero** as well. Curve has no CRV burn mechanism of any kind, and reading the destruction side directly confirms it rather than inferring it from a consumption metric: the null address holds no CRV, and the dead address held **170 CRV** unchanged across the entire window.

Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero**: no open-market CRV purchase by the Curve DAO, its treasury or any associated entity was disclosed or observed. The DAO's July 2026 treasury work went the other way, routing Arbitrum and Base fee revenue to the Ethereum treasury and authorising conversion into yield-bearing stablecoins — none of which touches CRV. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** too, and this is the trap worth naming: vote-locking is real supply removal, but only when the net change is positive. This window it was negative, so there is nothing to book on the buy side and the **4.1M** sits on the sell ledger instead. With every buy row at zero, CRV's net reading is simply its gross issuance divided by its float.

## Foundation and overhang

Curve DAO's team-controlled overhang is unusually small for a token of its age. The first item is the DAO community-fund contract, holding **845,144 CRV**, which did not move at either window end and can only be distributed through a passing DAO vote and a vesting contract of at least one year. The second is the residual inside the 2020 community vesting escrow, **10.3M CRV** of vested-but-unclaimed supply whose realised drip is already booked in Sell #2. The DAO's ownership, parameter and emergency agents hold no CRV at all. Two large balance moves inside the window — roughly **116.6M CRV** leaving one address and **101.0M** arriving at another — are exchange custodial wallets reshuffling depositor coins, not a team release, and are correctly excluded. The veCRV escrow itself, at **850.8M CRV**, is user-locked rather than team-controlled, so it is tracked as its own row instead of as an overhang. If the community-fund balance or the vesting-escrow residual falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CRV compares to other DeFi governance tokens

CRV belongs to the **emission-driven DeFi governance token** class — tokens whose supply grows because the protocol pays liquidity or usage incentives, rather than because of a validator mint or a vesting cliff. Within that class Curve Finance is the strictest case: the emission schedule is not a governance parameter at all, it is arithmetic embedded in the deployed contract, so CRV's issuance falls about **16%** every August whether the market is up or down and whether the DAO wants it or not. That is a fundamentally different property from a protocol whose emissions are set by a vote each quarter, where a bad market invites a bigger incentive budget.

The sharper contrast is with the buyback cohort. A growing number of DeFi and exchange tokens now convert revenue into open-market purchases, and some burn what they buy, which can push net supply flat or negative regardless of issuance. Curve rejected that path explicitly in favour of veCRV: instead of destroying supply, it pays lockers in crvUSD and lets them remove supply voluntarily. The mechanism has worked at scale — roughly **850.8M CRV**, about **35%** of total supply, currently sits locked — but it has one structural weakness a burn does not share. A burn is permanent; a lock expires. When locking momentum stalls, as it did this window, the same mechanism that removed supply starts returning it, which is exactly the **4.1M CRV** in Sell #5.

Against a hard-capped, halving-model asset the comparison is closer than it first looks. CRV does have a real cap, **3,030.3M**, and roughly **80%** of it is already minted, so remaining issuance is bounded and shrinking on a fixed curve — the same shape as a halving schedule, just on a yearly step and a shallower factor. Where the two diverge is the buy side: a fee-burning chain claws supply back with usage, while Curve's buy side is zero by construction, so CRV's net reading can only improve as fast as its own emission curve falls.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the veCRV locked balance near **850.8M CRV**: it is the only line in the CRV ledger that can move in either direction, and a return to net locking would flip **4.1M** of sell pressure into buy pressure without any protocol change. Watch the Curve DAO governance queue for any proposal to convert protocol revenue into CRV purchases or burns, which would create the first buy-side row CRV has ever carried — the risk-provider mandate under discussion in August 2026 includes at least one bid asking to be paid in CRV locked for four years, a small but real precedent. Watch the DAO community fund near **845,144 CRV** and the 2020 vesting escrow near **10.3M CRV** for any acceleration in outflow. And note the date that is not in this window: the next automatic emission cut is not due until **Aug 13 2027**, so the forward reading of **24.0M CRV** a quarter holds for a full year.

## Summary

Curve DAO is a hard-capped DeFi governance token that inflates on a schedule nobody can change, and that schedule is now the smallest it has ever been. The Curve Finance gauge emission minted **28.5M CRV** over the trailing 90 days before stepping down about **16%** on **Aug 12 2026** to under **100M CRV** a year, and expiring veCRV locks plus unclaimed 2020 vesting added another **5.0M** to the float. The framework reads **+2.16%** net and our monitor **+2.13%**, a **0.03-point** gap. The key risk is that the buy side is zero by design — Curve has chosen vote-locking over buybacks and burns, so the only thing reducing CRV's dilution is time, and the only thing that could make it worse is lockers deciding not to relock.
