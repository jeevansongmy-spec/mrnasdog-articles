# CRV Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->

**CRV** supply grew **+2.51%** in the 90 days to Oct 27 2026 and is on course for about **+1.99%** in the next 90, so the verdict is supply growing. Curve pays liquidity providers in brand-new CRV every second (**24.92M** CRV in the window), and more CRV left voting locks (**11.53M**) than went in (**2.25M**). Nothing buys or burns CRV in size, and the hard cap of about **3.03B** CRV is still years away.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Between Jul 29 and Oct 27 2026, about **42.02M** CRV entered the circulating supply and about **2.26M** CRV left it, a net rise of **+2.51%** on a circulating supply of **1.58B** CRV. Our independent supply tracker reads **+2.53%** for the same 90 days, a gap of just **0.01** percentage points, so the CRV coin page carries no warning note. For the next 90 days, to Jan 25 2027, the Curve DAO ledger points to about **+1.99%**: lower, because the yearly emission cut on Aug 12 2026 is now in force for the whole window, but still clearly positive. The plain label for CRV today is a liquidity-reward token that is inflationary by design, with the voting-lock pile slowly giving coins back.

## Sell pressure: where new CRV comes from

The first and largest source is protocol inflation. Curve releases a fixed number of CRV per second to the liquidity pools that CRV lockers vote for, and the coins are created when providers claim them. In the window providers claimed **24.92M** new CRV. On Aug 12 2026 Curve DAO entered its sixth yearly epoch and the release rate fell by about 16%, from about 3.66 CRV per second to about 3.08 CRV per second, or about **97.2M** CRV a year. At the new rate the next 90 days bring about **23.96M** CRV.

The second source is vesting. The original team and investor allocations finished vesting in 2024, and the reward for Curve's earliest users, about **151.5M** CRV, finished vesting on Aug 13 2021. Part of that early-user reward was never claimed, and unclaimed CRV does not count as circulating. In the window **5.57M** CRV left that pot, most of it, **5.29M**, on Oct 19 and Oct 20 2026, when a single wallet claimed on behalf of 200 early users at once. That push is not expected to repeat on a schedule, so the next 90 days carry only the normal pace of claims, about **0.28M** CRV.

The third row, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, books zero. The Curve DAO Treasury and the grant streams it pays already count as circulating, so when the DAO spends CRV no new supply appears. The fourth row is the long-term lock. CRV locked for voting power, called veCRV, is held out of the circulating count until the lock ends and the owner withdraws it. In the window **11.53M** CRV came out of ended locks. At the window end about **14.05M** CRV sat in locks that had already ended, and **7.09M** more ends by Jan 21 2027, including a group of **5.41M** CRV on Jan 7 2027. At the pace owners withdrew in the last 90 days, about **9.57M** CRV is expected out of locks next.

## Buy pressure: where new CRV goes

Curve does not run a programmatic buyback. Its trading and lending fees go to veCRV lockers in crvUSD, Curve's own stablecoin, and since June 2025 a 10% share goes to the DAO Treasury; neither part is used to buy CRV on the market. There is also no protocol fee burn: no share of any fee destroys CRV. The Foundation-buy row is zero too: no team or DAO purchase of CRV took place in the window, and the CRV that reached the Treasury came from a multisig, not from the market.

The only real buyer in the ledger is the new long-term lock. Holders locked **2.25M** CRV into new or larger veCRV locks in the window, which takes those coins out of circulation for up to four years. That is about a fifth of what came out of ended locks, so the locked pile shrank from about 850.82M to about **841.54M** CRV. A small extra row covers coins that are simply gone: holders sent **12,678** CRV by mistake to the CRV token's own address, where nothing can move it again, and burned another **77** CRV, for about **12.75K** CRV in total. Together the buy side removed about **2.26M** CRV, against about **42.02M** CRV added.

## Foundation and overhang

Curve has no foundation reserve waiting to unlock on a schedule. The piles worth watching are these. The early-user reward pot still holds about **4.92M** CRV that its owners can claim at any time; every claim enters the float. The DAO's community fund holds about **0.85M** CRV and did not move in the window; it can only pay out through a vote and a vesting of at least one year. The DAO Treasury went from zero to about **5.73M** CRV: it received 6.3M CRV from a multisig on Aug 26 and Sep 3 2026 and, on Sep 2 2026, sent **568,181** CRV into a one-year grant stream for a new risk manager, approved by vote that week and paying out until Sep 1 2027. The multisig that funded it still holds about **1.47M** CRV. Because the Treasury, the multisig and the grant stream already count as circulating, none of them adds new supply when it spends.

The biggest pile is the veCRV lock contract itself, at about **841.54M** CRV, a little over a third of all CRV created. Most of it sits with large lockers that keep relocking for the full four years, so their coins are far from release. We read every one of these balances again at each rebuild. If the early-user pot, the community fund or the lock contract falls between rebuilds, the outflow enters the sell side at the next rebuild, and if the Treasury moves its CRV into a lock or a burn, the buy side picks it up.

## How CRV compares to other vote-escrow DeFi tokens

CRV is the original vote-escrow token: lock it for up to four years, get voting power over where rewards go, and earn a share of fees. Many exchange tokens copied the design, but they differ on the one point that decides supply, which is who receives the fees. Curve pays lockers in crvUSD, so its fees never touch the CRV market. Exchange tokens that burn part of their fees, or that spend fees to buy their own coin back, have a steady buyer that CRV lacks. On those tokens, new supply from rewards is at least partly taken back each week; on CRV it is not.

On emissions, CRV sits between two models. Some reward tokens print a flat amount forever; others cut rewards every week. Curve cuts its rate once a year, by about 16%, toward a fixed cap of about 3.03B CRV, which keeps the yearly rate falling but still about 6% of today's circulating supply each year. The lock side is CRV's main defence: about a third of all CRV created is locked. The risk is the direction of that pile. When locks run down faster than new ones are made, as they did this quarter, the lock contract turns from a sink into a second source of supply.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Jan 7 2027:** the largest weekly group of veCRV locks in the window, **5.41M** CRV, reaches its end date; if most of it is withdrawn, the sell side runs above this forecast.

**The early-user reward pot:** about **4.92M** CRV is still unclaimed. Another claim push like the one on Oct 19 2026 would add most of it to the circulating supply at once.

**New locks versus withdrawals:** Curve DAO votes on a new propAMM setup for the YieldBasis ETH pool close on Oct 29 2026. If fee income keeps rising, more holders may lock CRV again; the lock side is the only part of the ledger that can turn quickly.

**Treasury CRV:** the DAO Treasury holds about **5.73M** CRV. Any vote to lock or burn it would move the buy side; grants paid from it do not change the count.

**Aug 12 2027:** the next yearly emission cut, about 16% again, falls just outside the next window; until then the release rate stays at about 3.08 CRV per second.

## Summary

CRV supply grew **+2.51%** in the 90 days to Oct 27 2026 and is projected to grow about **+1.99%** in the next 90, in line with our independent tracker's +2.53%. The growth comes from Curve's liquidity rewards, about **24.92M** CRV a quarter after the Aug 12 2026 cut, plus **11.53M** CRV released from ended voting locks and a one-off claim of **5.29M** CRV of the old early-user reward. Curve has no buyback and no fee burn, and its fees are paid in crvUSD, so new locks of about **2.25M** CRV a quarter are the only real buyer. The key risk is the lock pile of about 841.54M CRV giving back more coins than it takes in; the ceiling is the fixed cap of about 3.03B CRV, which the falling yearly rate approaches only slowly.
