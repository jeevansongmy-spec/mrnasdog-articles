# CRV Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Curve DAO's CRV emission is not a policy, it is arithmetic compiled into the token in 2020 — and on **Aug 12 2026** it stepped down again, cutting scheduled issuance from **115.55M CRV** a year to **97.16M**, under one hundred million for the first time since Curve Finance launched. Over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** the gauge emission created **28.17M CRV**, expiring four-year veCRV vote-locks returned **3.42M** to the float, and a 2020 vesting escrow that every unlock tracker calls finished paid out **0.93M** more. Against a buy side of exactly **zero** — Curve runs no buyback and no burn by design — the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CRV at **+2.10%** net on a circulating base of **1.55B CRV**, against our supply monitor's **+1.96%**, a gap of **0.14 percentage points**. CRV is best read as **a hard-capped token on a decelerating mint with no offsetting mechanism at all**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days ending **Aug 26 2026**, the framework reads **CRV at +2.10% net**: sell pressure of **32.52M CRV** against buy pressure of **0**, on a circulating base of **1,551.09M CRV**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.96%** over the same window, a gap of **0.14 percentage points**, so the overview card carries no data-conflict flag. The agreement is not luck. Curve DAO's non-circulating supply decomposes cleanly on-chain: total supply of **2,412,957,642 CRV** minus the veCRV vote-escrow's **850,638,954**, minus the 2020 pre-CRV liquidity-provider escrow's **10,302,478**, minus the community funds contract's **845,144**, lands within **85,372 CRV** of the tracked float — six thousandths of one percent. Every ledger row below is a movement in one of those buckets, which is why the framework and the monitor arrive at nearly the same place from opposite directions. The label the framework gives Curve DAO is **structurally inflationary on a schedule that shrinks, with nothing on the buy side to shrink it faster**.

## Sell pressure: where new CRV comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **28.17M CRV**, and it is the only row on this page that creates coins that did not exist before. Curve Finance pays liquidity providers with newly minted CRV through the Minter contract, weighted across pools by veCRV gauge votes. The emission rate is a value stored on the CRV token itself and it steps down once a year on the launch anniversary, so it can be read rather than looked up — and this build read it at both ends of the window rather than multiplying a published figure. At the window start the rate returned **115.55M CRV** a year in epoch five; at the window end it returned **97.16M CRV** a year in epoch six, with the epoch boundary timestamp stepping to **Aug 12 2026**. That boundary falls **14 days** inside the trailing window and **351 days** outside the forward one, which is why the forward column below is re-based onto the post-step rate while the trailing column stays exactly as measured. Checking that boundary against the window edge in both directions is not optional on Curve DAO — a fortnight either way flips the row.

The measurement itself is the difference in the token's own supply: **2,384,792,197.26** at the start against **2,412,957,642.51** at the end. Set that against the contract's scheduled ceiling — Curve DAO exposes a running total of what the emission schedule has authorised, and it moved by **27.89M CRV** over the same blocks — and CRV realised **100.99%** of its own schedule. Most emission tokens realise less; Curve DAO realised slightly more, and the mechanism explains why. CRV is minted on **claim**, not on a per-block update, and claims are capped by the authorised ceiling rather than by the clock, so an unclaimed backlog rides permanently in front of the schedule. That backlog fell from **10,474,914 CRV** to **10,197,450** across the window, and that **277,464 CRV** drawdown is the entire one-percent overshoot. Multiplying the published **115.55M** annual figure by a quarter would have produced **28.9M** — close by accident, and wrong by mechanism, because it ignores both the mid-window step and the claim backlog.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0.93M CRV**, and this is the row the trackers get wrong. The 2020 vesting escrow for Curve's pre-CRV liquidity providers reports itself fully vested on every measure the contract exposes: **151,515,152 CRV** allocated, **151,515,152** vested, nothing still locked, with a schedule that ran from **Aug 13 2020** to **Aug 13 2021**. That is why unlock trackers publish "fully unlocked" for Curve DAO. The escrow still held **10,302,478 CRV** of unclaimed backlog at the window end, down from **11,235,459** at the start. Sweeping every transfer out of it across all **647,047** blocks of the window found **49** separate claims to **49** distinct addresses totalling **932,981 CRV** — the largest a single **706,795 CRV** claim on **Jul 27 2026**, a six-year-old position finally collected. Vested is not the same as released, and released is what reaches the market.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** for this window, but not because there is nothing there. Curve DAO's community funds contract held **845,144 CRV** at both ends of the quarter — an identical read, which is exactly the pattern a wrong address produces. It is not one: the same contract at the same call returned **24,545,144 CRV** a year earlier, so **23.70M CRV** genuinely left it in the twelve months before this window and genuinely nothing left it inside. The DAO's ownership and emergency agent contracts hold no CRV at either end, each confirmed as deployed code before the balance was believed. And the largest proposed outflow of the year did not happen: a **17.45M CRV** development grant to Curve's core development company was put to a vote and rejected, **54.46%** against. Sell #4, long-term locked, is **3.42M CRV** — the veCRV vote-escrow, covered in full below.

## Buy pressure: where new CRV goes

Every buy row on Curve DAO reads **0**, and that is the single most important structural fact on this page. Buy #1, a programmatic buyback, is **0** because Curve Finance deliberately does not buy CRV. The protocol earns real admin fees on every stablecoin swap, and it pays them to veCRV vote-lockers in **crvUSD**, its own stablecoin — not by purchasing CRV on the open market. The fee distributor contract held no CRV whatsoever at either end of the window, which is what that design looks like on-chain. Buy-and-burn has been raised in Curve governance more than once and has never been enacted; the DAO's stated position is that vote-locking already does the job a buyback would do, by giving holders a reason to remove CRV from the market themselves.

Buy #2, a protocol fee burn, is **0** because CRV has no burn path at all. The zero address holds nothing, and the dead address holds **169.86 CRV** that has not changed — dust somebody sent by hand, not a mechanism. Buy #3, a foundation buy, is **0**: no dated open-market purchase of CRV by Curve or any entity acting for it landed inside the window. Buy #4, a new long-term lock, is **0**, and the reasoning matters. Vote-locking is the only lock CRV has, and over this window it ran the wrong way — more CRV left expiring locks than entered new ones. The net outflow is already carried at Sell #4, so booking gross new locks here while booking gross exits there would count the same coins twice in both directions. On a coin where the lock is draining, Buy #4 is not merely empty; it is the wrong side of the ledger.

## Foundation and overhang

Curve DAO's tracked overhang is three contracts, and all three are readable on-chain. The largest is the **veCRV** vote-escrow holding **850,638,954 CRV**, refreshed by direct chain read at every rebuild. It is a genuine term lock rather than a staking receipt: CRV goes in for up to four years, the veCRV balance it mints is non-transferable, there is no liquid receipt token to sell, and positions cannot be exited before expiry. Its balance sits outside the counted float, so it is the one contract on Curve DAO where a fall is unambiguously new supply — and it has fallen at every checkpoint for a year: **871.71M** on **Aug 26 2025**, **861.97M** on **Nov 24 2025**, **857.81M** on **Feb 22 2026**, **854.06M** at this window's start and **850.64M** at its end. A decaying lock is not absorption; on Curve DAO it is the second-largest source of supply on the page.

Behind it sits the 2020 pre-CRV liquidity-provider escrow with **10,302,478 CRV** of vested-but-unclaimed backlog, also refreshed by chain read, whose releases are carried in Sell #2 rather than double-counted here. Third is the community funds contract at **845,144 CRV**, which paid out **23.70M CRV** in the year before this window and nothing inside it — the smallest balance and the most recent firing history, which is why it stays on the watch list rather than being written off. Curve DAO has never had a bankruptcy estate, a trustee or a court-ordered distribution, so there is no sixth category to enumerate. If the balance of the veCRV escrow, the vesting escrow or the community funds contract falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and in the case of the two escrows already firing, it lands in the rows that measure them.

## How CRV compares to other hard-capped DeFi governance tokens

CRV belongs to the hard-capped governance-token class, and within it Curve DAO sits at an unusual point. It shares the cap — **3,030,303,031 CRV** written into the protocol, with **2.41B** minted so far — with tokens whose issuance stopped years ago, but unlike those it is still minting, and unlike an uncapped emission chain it is minting on a decaying schedule that converges. Compared with a governance token whose supply is simply fixed, CRV trades a lower ceiling for a higher near-term rate: the mint is **97.16M CRV** a year now and falls roughly **16%** every August, so a holder can price the exact issuance of every future year today. Compared with a halving-model chain, the mechanism is the same idea on a different clock — annual rather than four-yearly, and by a smaller step each time.

The comparison that separates CRV most sharply from its peers is the buy side. Governance tokens with revenue increasingly route it into a buyback, and some route it into a burn, which makes their net reading a contest between two live mechanisms. Curve DAO has revenue and routes none of it into CRV: fees pay vote-lockers in a stablecoin instead. That means the framework's buy total for CRV is not small, it is structurally **zero**, and the net reading is simply the gross mint. A protocol whose fee burn offsets even a fifth of issuance would read materially better on this metric with an identical emission schedule. It also means CRV's net cannot turn negative without a governance decision — there is no mechanism sitting idle that a rise in usage would switch on.

The third comparison is with tokens that use a vote-escrow model, which CRV invented and many have copied. In most copies the escrow is presented as an absorption mechanism — supply locked away, therefore removed. Curve DAO shows the other half of that trade. A term lock with a hard expiry is a supply **deferral**, not a supply **sink**: after four years the coins come back, and once the cohort of locks entering is smaller than the cohort expiring, the escrow becomes a net source. Curve DAO's escrow crossed that line some time ago and has drained for at least a year straight. Any vote-escrow token should be read at both window ends and in both directions before its lock is scored as buy pressure.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the emission rate is fixed for the whole forward window — the next annual step is not until **Aug 12 2027**, so Curve DAO will mint at the epoch-six rate for every day of it, roughly **24.20M CRV** once the claim backlog is accounted for. Second, watch the veCRV escrow's balance: the drain has been decelerating, from **9.74M** in the quarter to **Nov 24 2025** to **3.42M** in this one, and if new locks start outpacing expiries the row flips sign and the net reading falls by roughly a fifth. Third, watch the 2020 vesting escrow's remaining **10.30M CRV** — this window contained one **706,795 CRV** claim that was three quarters of its whole flow, so a second dormant whale collecting would move the row far more than the trickle suggests. Fourth, watch Curve governance for any buyback or burn proposal reaching an executed vote; the buy side is zero today and only a DAO decision can change that. Fifth, watch the community funds contract's **845,144 CRV** and any successor grant proposal, after the **17.45M CRV** development grant was voted down.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CRV at **+2.10%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026**: **32.52M CRV** of sell pressure against **0** of buy pressure on a **1.55B CRV** circulating base, and **+1.80%** projected for the next 90 days on the post-step rate. The structural mechanism is a gauge emission compiled into the token in 2020 that shrinks about **16%** each August and delivered **100.99%** of its own schedule this quarter, alongside a four-year vote-escrow that now returns more CRV than it absorbs and a 2020 vesting escrow that every tracker records as finished. The key risk is that all three sources are live and none of them has an offset: Curve Finance runs no buyback, has no burn path, and pays its fees to lockers in a stablecoin rather than in CRV, so the net reading is the gross mint with nothing subtracted. The ceiling is real — **3,030,303,031 CRV**, with **2.41B** already created — and the schedule converges on it, but Curve DAO reaches that ceiling by continuing to inflate for years, not by stopping.
