# SOL Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Solana (SOL) mints new coins to stakers at every epoch, and **5.24M SOL** of that Solana staking issuance reached circulating wallets over the last 90 days, at a yearly rate that fell from **3.79%** to **3.65%**. On top of the mint, **2.84M SOL** left locks and reached spendable wallets: monthly SOL unlock ladders, the tranches of a bankruptcy estate, and one Solana Foundation transfer. The Solana fee burn removed only **73.3K SOL**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads SOL at **+1.36% net** over 90 days against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.29%** — a gap of **0.08 percentage points**, which is agreement, not conflict. The Solana inflation rate keeps falling toward a **1.5%** floor, but **2.43M SOL** of estate tranches alone are still to unlock.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 13 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **SOL at +1.36% net**: **8.08M SOL** of new or newly released supply against **73.3K SOL** burned, on a circulating base of **586.73M SOL**. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+1.29%**. The gap is **0.08 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so SOL ships with **no data-conflict flag**. The forward column reads **+1.22%**: slightly less SOL issuance as the rate steps down, the same unlock ladders firing on the same days of the month, and no Foundation transfer projected because those have no schedule. The label for SOL is **structurally inflationary on issuance plus unlocks**: a proof-of-stake chain whose mint is shrinking slowly while a tail of locked supply from its early years is still arriving.

## Sell pressure: where new SOL comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the largest line at **5.24M SOL**. Solana pays staking rewards at each epoch boundary, and the window held **47** of them, minting **5.66M SOL** in all; the **5.24M** share that landed in circulating wallets is what counts here, because the rest accrued inside locked and Foundation accounts and is counted when those accounts unlock. The number was read from the chain's own reward record rather than a dashboard: the reward paid at the latest boundary, **95,007 SOL**, matches the protocol's published inflation schedule to the digit and reconciles with total SOL supply once the fee burn since that boundary is counted. Solana cut its slot time twice inside this window, to **350 milliseconds** on **Aug 21 2026** and to **300 milliseconds** on **Aug 28 2026**. That did not speed up the SOL mint, because the protocol raised its own count of slots per year by the same ratio; a naive reading that more epochs means more SOL would have overstated issuance. Blocks in fact run about **5%** slower than their target, so Solana mints a little less per day than the headline rate suggests.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **1.65M SOL**, and it was measured as coins that actually left the lock. Every Solana stake account on the chain was read, **1,435,939** of them. Monthly unlock ladders released tranches on the 7th of July, August and September, and their owners withdrew each tranche within three days. More SOL came due than moved: **2.62M SOL** whose locks also expired in the window is still sitting staked and untouched, so it is not counted as sell pressure. **17.93M SOL** remains locked. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0.59M SOL**: on **Aug 26 2026** the Solana Foundation's reserve split **587,871 SOL** into a new stake account and handed full control of it to an outside wallet, taking it out of the locked count. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **0.61M SOL**: the FTX bankruptcy estate still receives a locked SOL tranche on the 11th of each month and withdrew all three in this window, **605,576 SOL** in total. The estate has not finished — twelve tranches worth **2.43M SOL** remain.

## Buy pressure: where new SOL goes

Almost nowhere. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: nothing buys SOL off the market, and Solana staking rewards are newly minted SOL rather than purchases. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **73.3K SOL**. Solana destroys half of the flat **5,000-lamport** fee charged per signature, and the burn was checked against the runtime itself: across **184** sampled blocks, fees minus the block producer's reward equal exactly half the signature fee, while priority tips go entirely to the producer. That comes to about **814 SOL** a day across the window and **949** a day since the faster blocks arrived, and two readings of total SOL supply minutes apart showed it falling at a similar pace. A proposal to add a burned resource-based fee, which would have multiplied the Solana burn many times over, was rejected by validators on **Aug 28 2026**. Buy #3, Foundation buying, is **zero**: the reserve only sent SOL out this window, and the listed companies holding SOL treasuries have kept their combined holdings flat. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**: **438.74M SOL** is actively staked, but staked SOL stays in the circulating count and can be withdrawn after an epoch, so staking is not a lock.

## Foundation and overhang

The locked side of SOL supply was reconciled to the coin: stake accounts with a lock still in force hold **17.93M SOL**, the Solana Foundation's reserve holds **27.17M SOL** across **713** stake accounts, and a separate set of listed accounts holds **2.19M SOL**. Together they equal the chain's published non-circulating SOL supply of **47.29M** exactly. That reserve is the largest overhang and it moves on no schedule — nine releases in the past year, from **0.33M** to **4.5M SOL** — so it is re-read on every refresh rather than projected. Behind it sit the **2.62M SOL** of expired but untouched stake accounts, including four large Foundation-custodied grants, and the **2.43M SOL** the bankruptcy estate has left to receive. Exchange custody wallets and unlabelled holders are excluded, because those coins belong to depositors or to no identified group. If the Foundation reserve or any of those expired accounts falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SOL compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

SOL sits in the large class of uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1s that pay validators in freshly minted coins, and the structural comparison is with the chains that do not. A capped proof-of-work coin issues a fixed subsidy that halves on a timetable and eventually stops; Solana has no cap and never stops, falling instead to a permanent **1.5%** terminal rate. An exchange-linked chain that pays validators from fees and burns on a schedule shrinks its supply; Solana pays validators from issuance and burns only a sliver of fees, so its float grows. Against other uncapped staking chains, Solana's distinguishing features are a disinflation curve fixed in protocol code rather than set by a foundation each year, and a validator vote on **Aug 28 2026** that approved doubling the speed of that curve, from a **15%** yearly cut to **30%**, which is not yet switched on.

The second difference is the fee burn. Since priority fees were redirected to block producers, Solana burns only the base fee half, so a busy chain destroys little: **73.3K SOL** against **5.66M SOL** minted, about **1.3%** of issuance. A chain that burns a floating base fee lets heavy use eat into its issuance; Solana's flat fee cannot do that, and its validators have just declined the change that would have tied the burn to usage. The third difference is the tail of supply from Solana's early years — monthly unlock ladders and a bankruptcy estate still receiving tranches. SOL still has **17.93M** locked, releasing month by month.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the unlock calendar, which decides the forward reading: the monthly ladders release about **0.56M SOL** each on **Oct 7 2026**, **Nov 7 2026** and **Dec 7 2026**, and the bankruptcy estate receives about **0.20M SOL** on **Oct 11 2026**, **Nov 11 2026** and **Dec 11 2026**. Without those two schedules the forward net would sit below one percent. Second, the Solana double-disinflation gate: it is approved but waits on a change to how rewards are calculated, and once active it bends the curve down faster from the rate in force that day. Third, Alpenglow, Solana's new consensus, whose feature activation is scheduled to begin from **Sep 28 2026**; it retires vote transactions, which removes their share of the fee burn, and starts a small per-epoch validator admission fee that is burned. Fourth, the Solana Foundation reserve at **27.17M SOL**: any release lands in Sell #3 the refresh after it happens. Fifth, the **2.62M SOL** of expired stake accounts that did not move this window; if their owners withdraw, the unlock row rises.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SOL at **+1.36% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+1.22%** over the next 90. The structural mechanism is Solana staking issuance of **5.24M SOL** per quarter reaching circulating wallets on a curve falling 15% a year, plus **2.84M SOL** of unlocks from monthly ladders, a bankruptcy estate and the Solana Foundation reserve, against a fee burn of just **73.3K SOL**. The key risk is the unscheduled Foundation reserve of **27.17M SOL**, which released between **0.33M** and **4.5M SOL** at a time over the past year. There is no cap: the Solana inflation rate settles at **1.5%** forever, and an approved but inactive vote would bring that floor years closer.
