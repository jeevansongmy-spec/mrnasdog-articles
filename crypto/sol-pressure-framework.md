# SOL Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/sol/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sol/inflation).*

<!-- main-page -->
This is the long supply read. For the signal, the price drivers and the questions people ask about Solana, see [mrnasdog.com/research/sol](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sol).

**SOL** is inflationary by design: circulating supply grew about **+1.74%** in the 90 days to Oct 30 2026 and is projected to grow about **+1.26%** in the next 90. Solana pays stakers **5.34M** new SOL a quarter at a **3.58%** yearly rate that falls by 15% a year toward a 1.5% floor, and locked coins reaching the market added **5.08M** more. Against that, the fee burn removed only **82.2K** SOL and new locks **63.8K**; there is no supply cap and no buyback, and other trackers read **+1.55%** for the same stretch.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days, from Aug 1 to Oct 30 2026, SOL in the market grew by a net **+1.74%**, or about **10.28M SOL**. Other public trackers show **+1.55%** over a nearly identical stretch, a difference of **0.19 points**, which is small enough that the coin page carries no warning note. For the next 90 days, to Jan 28 2027, we expect about **+1.26%**: staking pay stays near the same size, while the locks that end each month are smaller than the batch that ended over the summer. The label that fits Solana today is **inflationary by design, with a falling issuance rate and a shrinking pile of locked coins**.

## Sell pressure: where new SOL comes from

**Protocol inflation** is the largest source. At every epoch the network mints new SOL for stakers and validators, and between Aug 1 and Oct 30 2026 that came to **5.34M SOL**, about 59,400 a day. The yearly rate stood at **3.58%** on Oct 30 2026 and steps down by 15% each year until it reaches 1.5%. Solana cut its block time from 400 to 200 milliseconds in four steps between Aug 21 and Oct 9 2026; each epoch got shorter, but the pay per epoch was cut in the same proportion, so new SOL per day stayed on the same path. We expect about **5.18M** in the next 90 days.

**Vesting unlocks** are stake accounts with a lock date. While the date is in the future the coins are not counted as circulating; once it passes, they can be sold. Locks that ended between Aug 1 and Oct 30 2026 released **4.39M SOL**, after the staking pay those locked accounts earned along the way. About **17.27M SOL** is still locked, with dates running into early 2028, and about **2.89M** of it unlocks by Jan 28 2027, mostly on the 7th (about 640K) and the 11th (about 204K) of each month. Much of this series appears to be the locked SOL the FTX estate sold to buyers in 2024. Net of the staking pay the remaining locked accounts keep earning, we expect about **2.40M** to reach the market.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks** added **692K SOL**. On Aug 26 2026 a staked reserve linked to the Solana Foundation handed **587,871 SOL** to a new owner, and three other reserve accounts on the protocol's not-circulating list paid out **104,448 SOL** on Sep 25 and Oct 4 2026. These releases come with no public schedule, so the next 90 days book zero for this row until the next one is seen on-chain.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy** is zero. No court or bankruptcy estate holds an uncounted pile of SOL waiting to be released; the SOL the FTX estate sold under lock is already counted in the vesting row, date by date.

## Buy pressure: where new SOL goes

**Programmatic buyback** is zero. Solana has no buyback contract and no treasury that buys SOL back for the network.

**The protocol fee burn** destroyed about **82,200 SOL** in the window. Half of every base transaction fee is burned, and the burn rose from about 820 SOL a day in August to about 1,000 a day in October. On Oct 21 2026 the Alpenglow upgrade moved validator voting off the chain, so validators stopped paying vote fees; each now pays a **0.8 SOL** admission ticket per epoch, and that ticket is burned. We expect about **94.6K** to be burned in the next 90 days, which is still under 2% of new staking pay. A plan to burn far more of each fee lost its validator vote on Aug 28 2026.

**Foundation buy** is zero. The Foundation does not buy SOL, and spot funds and listed companies that hold SOL buy coins that already circulate, so they take nothing out of the count.

**New long-term lock** took **63,830 SOL** out of the market. Between Aug 17 and Oct 27 2026, 82 new stake accounts were opened with long lock dates and funded from ordinary wallets. New lots open almost every week, so we expect about **63.8K** again in the next 90 days. Normal staking does not count here, because staked SOL without a lock stays circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest overhang is the **27.29M SOL** staked reserve linked to the Solana Foundation. The protocol lists its withdraw key as not circulating, so these coins sit outside the market count until the key lets them go. It did so on Aug 26 2026, and on Oct 8 2026 it moved **500,000 SOL** into a new account locked until Sep 13 2028, which keeps that amount off the market. A further **2.10M SOL** sits in other reserve accounts on the same list; two of them emptied in October 2026.

The dated locks hold **17.27M SOL**, and their release dates are written on-chain, so they enter the ledger month by month as each date passes. If the reserve's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How SOL compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Solana pays its stakers on a fixed, falling schedule with no cap on total supply. Ethereum also has no cap, but it pays validators according to how much is staked and burns the whole base fee of every transaction, so in busy periods its burn can cancel a large share of new issuance. Solana burns only half of a small base fee, plus the validator tickets, so its burn stays a rounding error next to staking pay. The gap between the two designs is the main reason SOL's net supply growth runs several times higher.

Chains with a hard cap work differently again. Avalanche has a maximum supply and burns all transaction fees, and Cardano pays staking rewards out of a reserve that was created at launch and shrinks over time, so neither prints coins beyond a fixed ceiling. Solana instead relies on its falling rate: issuance shrinks every year until it settles at a 1.5% floor, and a vote passed on Aug 28 2026 to make that fall twice as fast once it is switched on.

Solana also carries something many newer chains do not: a large, visible pile of locked SOL whose release dates are written on-chain. That makes its unlock pressure easy to see ahead of time, month by month, rather than a surprise.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Nov 7 2026, Dec 7 2026 and Jan 7 2027**: the largest monthly lock endings, about 640K SOL each, with about 204K more on the 11th of each month.

**The double-disinflation switch**: the change approved on Aug 28 2026 still has no start date. It needs another change to ship first and is not part of the client update planned for November 2026; until it goes live, the yearly rate keeps falling by 15% a year.

**The Foundation-linked reserve**: any new release from its 27.29M SOL would add to sell pressure; the last ones came on Aug 26 2026 and, from other listed accounts, on Oct 4 2026.

**The burn after Alpenglow**: the first full quarter with validator tickets instead of vote fees ends around Jan 21 2027, and will show whether the burn holds near 1,000 SOL a day.

## Summary

SOL's circulating supply grew **+1.74%** in the 90 days to Oct 30 2026 and is projected to grow about **+1.26%** in the next 90 days, so the coin stays inflationary. The growth comes from **5.34M SOL** of new staking pay and from locked SOL reaching the market on dates written on-chain, while the fee burn removes less than 2% of what is minted. The main risk is the **27.29M SOL** Foundation-linked reserve, which can release coins without notice. Solana has no supply cap; its ceiling is the falling issuance rate, which drops 15% a year toward a 1.5% floor.
