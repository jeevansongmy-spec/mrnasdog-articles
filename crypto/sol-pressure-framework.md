## Part 1 · Supply: SOL Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/sol/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sol/inflation).*

SOL supply is growing. Over the last 90 days, **9.86M SOL** reached the market against **284K SOL** that left it, a net rise of **+1.63%** of the **588.0M** circulating. Two things drive it: Solana pays stakers in newly created SOL at the end of every epoch, and stake accounts that were locked in old private sales keep opening on fixed dates. The independent monitor reads **+1.24%**, and the next 90 days project **+1.27%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Solana's circulating supply rose **+1.63%** in the 90 days to Sep 30 2026. The independent supply monitor reads **+1.24%** for the same window, a gap of **0.39 percentage points**, which is inside our 0.5-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown. About half of the new SOL came from staking rewards and the other half from lockups that ended, while the fee burn removed less than 1% of what was added. For the next 90 days the ledger projects **+1.27%**: staking keeps paying, and about **2.85M SOL** of locks are dated to open. The label that fits: **inflationary by design, with a steady unlock tail**.

## Sell pressure: where new SOL comes from

Staking inflation is the largest source. Solana's yearly inflation rate is **3.62%** of total supply today, and the protocol cuts it by 15% each year until it reaches a floor of 1.5%. In this window the chain paid 50 epoch rewards that created **5.43M SOL**. About 518K of that went to stake accounts that are still locked, so they stay off the market until their locks end; the rest, **4.91M SOL**, reached the market. We checked this number against the chain's own reward records for three different epochs, and they agree to within 0.03%.

One change inside the window matters for how to read this row. Solana cut its slot time three times, to 350 milliseconds on Aug 19 2026, to 300 on Aug 26 and to 250 on Sep 8. Each cut makes epochs shorter, but the protocol shrinks the reward per epoch by the same ratio, so the speed-up does not print more SOL per day. In practice the chain issues a little under its headline rate, because slots run slightly slower than their target.

Vesting unlocks added **3.74M SOL**. About 15.1M SOL still sits in stake accounts with a lockup date, most of it from long private sales that run to early 2028. Solana counts a locked stake account as non-circulating, and the moment its lock ends the whole balance counts as circulating. This window saw three monthly batches of about 632K on the 7th of July, August and September, two single accounts of 625K on Aug 1 and 875K on Aug 30, and many smaller locks.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks added **594.3K SOL**. On Aug 26 2026 one of the Foundation's withdraw keys split 587,871 SOL out of a reserve stake account and handed it to a new owner, which moved it onto the market. About 6.4K more SOL left accounts the chain lists as never circulating.

The bankruptcy row added **606.2K SOL**. The FTX and Alameda estate still holds locked stake accounts, and one lock of about 203K SOL opens on the 11th of every month. Three opened in this window, and public reports in September showed the estate moving about 202,710 SOL out of staking soon after one of them.

## Buy pressure: where new SOL goes

There is no programmatic buyback: nothing in the protocol buys SOL on the market. The fee burn removed **74.1K SOL**. Every signature pays a flat fee of 5,000 lamports, and half of it is destroyed; priority fees go in full to the block leader and are not burned. That is about 824 SOL a day, and about half of it comes from validator votes, which are ordinary transactions on Solana today. Because faster slots mean more votes each day, the burn now runs a bit higher, about **90.2K SOL** for the next 90 days. A governance vote on Aug 28 2026 rejected a plan to burn a much larger share of fees.

The Foundation does not buy SOL, so that row is zero. New long-term locks removed **209.7K SOL**: 75 new stake accounts were opened with a lockup and filled from ordinary wallets, the largest with 160,000 SOL on Jul 7 2026. Plain staking removes nothing, because staked SOL without a lockup still counts as circulating, even though about 440.5M SOL is staked. Buyers of SOL funds and SOL treasury companies also remove nothing here: they buy coins that were already on the market.

## Foundation and overhang

Four pools of SOL sit outside the circulating count, and we read each one directly from the chain. The largest is about **27.23M SOL** in 2,270 stake accounts whose withdraw keys belong to the Solana Foundation and early backers; it has no release schedule, and its only release this window was the Aug 26 handover. Next is about **15.1M SOL** in dated lockups from private sales, opening in monthly steps to early 2028. Then about **2.44M SOL** of the FTX and Alameda estate's locked stake, at about 203K a month to Sep 2027. Last, about **2.19M SOL** sits in accounts the Solana software names as never circulating.

All four are checked on-chain at every rebuild. If any of these balances falls between checks without a dated lock ending, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How SOL compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1s

Solana sits in the family of proof-of-stake chains that pay stakers with a declining inflation schedule and burn only a small slice of fees. Its 3.62% headline rate, falling 15% a year to a 1.5% floor, is higher than Ethereum's issuance, which depends on how much ETH is staked, and Ethereum burns the whole base fee rather than half of a flat signature fee. On Ethereum the burn can offset a real share of new supply in busy periods; on Solana the fee burn removed less than 1% of what staking created this window.

What sets Solana apart is the unlock tail. Most large proof-of-stake chains either unlocked their early sales long ago or track them through a token vesting contract. Solana's sold coins sit in ordinary stake accounts with lock dates, so the unlocks show up only when you read the accounts themselves. That tail adds roughly as much new supply as staking does in a busy quarter, and it keeps doing so until early 2028.

Chains with a hard cap and no staking inflation, such as Bitcoin, sit at the other end: new supply falls on a fixed halving schedule and there is no locked pool to open. Solana has no cap on total supply; the 1.5% floor means SOL keeps growing forever, slowly.

The faster disinflation vote. Validators approved doubling the yearly cut in the inflation rate, from 15% to 30%, on Aug 28 2026, but the switch is not yet queued for the live network. When it activates, new SOL from staking starts shrinking twice as fast.

Alpenglow. The new consensus upgrade went live on test networks on Sep 24 and Sep 25 2026 and has no main-network date. It moves validator votes out of blocks and replaces their fees with a fixed burn per validator, which would change the size of the fee burn. A further cut of slot time to 200 milliseconds is also queued; like the earlier cuts, it should not change how much new SOL is created per day.
