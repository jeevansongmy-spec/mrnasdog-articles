# SOL Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

*Originally published at [https://mrnasdog.com/research/sol/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sol/inflation)*

<!-- main-page -->
This is the long supply read. For the signal, the price drivers and the questions people ask about Solana, see [mrnasdog.com/research/sol](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sol).

SOL supply is growing and should keep growing at about the same pace. Between Aug 20 and Nov 18 2026 Solana paid its stakers **5.11M SOL** that reached the market, ending stake locks freed **1.99M SOL** and the reserve released **697K SOL**, while burns and new locks took back **179K SOL**. Net circulating supply rose **+1.29%** in 90 days and is set to rise **+1.31%** in the next 90, with no supply cap and a yearly issuance rate of **3.55%** that keeps easing.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 18 2026, about **7.61M SOL** joined Solana's circulating supply, a net rise of **+1.29%**, and the next 90 days project **+1.31%**. Other trackers print **+1.52%**, a gap of **0.23 points**, which is small enough that the coin page carries no warning note. The other trackers' figure sits a little higher, a sign that slightly more locked SOL reached the market than the dated locks show. SOL is an uncapped proof-of-stake coin whose supply grows on two engines at once, steady staking pay and a monthly calendar of old locks running out: inflationary by design, with unlocks on top.

## Sell pressure: where new SOL comes from

The main source is staking pay. At the start of every epoch, which now lasts about a day, Solana creates new SOL and pays it to the people who stake with validators. From Aug 20 to Nov 18 2026 the network created **5.31M SOL** this way. About **203K SOL** of that went into locked accounts that do not yet count as circulating, so **5.11M SOL** reached the free market. The yearly rate stood at **3.55%** on Nov 18 2026 and falls by 15% a year on its own schedule toward a floor of 1.5%.

Solana's blocks got faster four times inside the window: from 400 to 350 milliseconds on Aug 21 2026, to 300 at the end of August, to 250 on Sep 18 and to 200 on Oct 9 2026. Each time the network cut the pay per block in the same proportion, so faster blocks did not print more SOL per year. Real blocks also run a little slower than the 200-millisecond target, which keeps actual issuance slightly below the headline rate.

Vesting unlocks added **1.99M SOL**. About 16.5M SOL sits in stake accounts locked until a set date, much of it coins sold years ago that open month by month into 2028. The largest ladder ends a tranche on the 7th of each month: on Sep 7, Oct 7 and Nov 7 2026 it freed about **1.87M SOL**, and smaller locks that ran out in the window added 118K. The next 90 days hold about **2.91M SOL** of lock endings, the biggest of them about 643K SOL on Dec 7 2026, Jan 7 2027 and Feb 7 2027, and about 204K on the 11th of each of those months.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks added **697K SOL**. On Aug 26 2026 the Foundation-linked reserve handed **587,871 SOL** to an outside holder with no lock, and older reserve accounts paid out another 109K SOL on Sep 25, Oct 4 and Nov 3 2026. None of these releases follows a public schedule, so the next 90 days book zero here.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy holdings add **0**. No court or failed firm holds SOL for a later payout; the locked SOL that past estates sold already sits in the dated locks counted above.

## Buy pressure: where new SOL goes

There is no programmatic buyback. No contract or treasury buys SOL back, and the funds and listed companies that buy SOL take coins already on the market, so they do not change the count.

The protocol fee burn took **70,543 SOL**. Half of every basic transaction fee is destroyed, while the extra priority fees go to the block producer. Most of the burn came before Oct 21 2026, when validators still paid a fee on every vote; since the Alpenglow upgrade moved voting off the chain, the burn runs near 360 SOL a day. A proposal to burn far more, through a new fee on heavy computing, won only 53.9% of the vote on Aug 28 2026 against the two-thirds it needed.

The Foundation does not buy SOL back, so that row is zero. New long-term locks held back **94,530 SOL**: between Aug 31 and Nov 12 2026 holders opened 99 new stake accounts locked to future dates, funded with SOL taken from the market, and coins under a dated lock stop counting as circulating until the lock ends.

A new burn arrived with Alpenglow. Every validator that wants to vote now pays a **0.8 SOL** ticket each epoch, and the network destroys it. About 667 validators paid it 26 times by Nov 17 2026, burning **13,870 SOL**, a pace of about 45K SOL every 90 days.

## Foundation and overhang

The biggest overhang is the reserve stake under the Foundation-linked key that Solana's own software lists as not circulating: about **27.36M SOL** in 677 stake accounts, with no lock date and no published plan. On Oct 8 2026 it moved 500K SOL into a new lock that runs to Sep 2028, which keeps those coins off the market until then.

The second is the set of dated locks: about **16.5M SOL** that will reach the market lock by lock until 2028, roughly 0.6M to 1.9M SOL a month through 2027. The largest ladder alone holds about 9M SOL in monthly tranches through Jan 2028. Older reserve accounts named in the software hold about 2.2M SOL more. If the reserve's balance falls between checks, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next check.

## How SOL compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Solana pays stakers on a falling schedule: it started at 8% a year, drops 15% each year and stops at 1.5%. Ethereum works the other way round, paying validators by how much ETH is staked rather than by the calendar, and burning a larger share of fees when the chain is busy. Cardano pays stakers from a fixed reserve that shrinks every few days, so its new supply falls without a vote. Solana's path only changes by vote: a faster 30% yearly cut passed on Aug 28 2026 but is not switched on yet.

On the burn side, Solana destroys half of each basic fee and, since Oct 21 2026, a flat ticket from every voting validator. Both are small next to new issuance, well under 2% of it. Chains with a large fee burn can shrink in busy months; Solana cannot at today's fee level. What sets Solana apart is the unlock calendar on top of issuance: most large proof-of-stake chains finished their launch unlocks years ago, while Solana still releases several hundred thousand locked SOL every month into 2028.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**The 7th and 11th of each month.** About 643K SOL on Dec 7 2026, Jan 7 2027 and Feb 7 2027, and about 204K SOL on Dec 11, Jan 11 and Feb 11, are the largest dated additions to the market.

**The faster disinflation switch.** The 30% yearly cut voted through on Aug 28 2026 still has no activation date; it first needs a change to how rewards are calculated. Once it is on, issuance starts falling twice as fast, though the effect inside any single 90 days is small.

**The Foundation reserve.** The 27.36M SOL reserve released 587,871 SOL on Aug 26 2026 with no notice. Another release of that size would add about 0.1% to supply.

**Fee activity after Breakpoint.** The burn has run near 360 SOL a day since Oct 21 2026. Busier days after the Nov 15–17 2026 conference would lift it, but it would need to grow many times over to offset staking pay.

## Summary

Solana's circulating supply grew **+1.29%** in the 90 days to Nov 18 2026 and is set to grow **+1.31%** in the next 90. Staking pay, now **3.55%** a year and falling, adds about 5M SOL every quarter, and dated locks from old sales add about 2.91M SOL more by Feb 16 2027. The fee burn and the new validator ticket remove under 90K SOL a quarter, so they barely dent it. Solana has no supply cap; the main risks to holders are the monthly lock calendar and the 27.36M SOL reserve that can move without notice.
