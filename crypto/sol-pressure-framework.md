# SOL Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Solana supply is growing, and it is growing on schedule. SOL, the native coin of the Solana blockchain, pays its stakers with newly minted SOL at a protocol inflation rate of **3.63%** a year, and over the last 90 days that staking issuance added **5.05M SOL** to the circulating float. A second source added **4.95M SOL** more: locked stake accounts, the FTX and Alameda estate's among them, that reached their unlock dates and joined the float. Against that, half of every Solana base fee is burned, which removed only **about 75,400 SOL**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads SOL at **+1.79% net** over the last 90 days against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.17%** — a gap of **0.62 percentage points**, which is wide enough to flag and traces to a single one-day step in the monitor's own series. The forward reading is **+1.29%**: SOL inflation keeps falling by **15%** a year toward a **1.5%** floor, but the burn is far too small to offset it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 25 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **SOL at +1.79% net**: **10,586,360 SOL** entered the circulating float from staking issuance and stake-account unlocks, while the Solana base-fee burn removed **75,366 SOL**. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+1.17%**. The gap is **0.62 percentage points**, above the framework's half-point tolerance, so SOL ships with a **data-conflict flag**: the monitor's own series steps down by **3.62M SOL** on a single day, **Jul 29 2026**, and no lock, transfer or authority change into the non-circulating accounts matches it on-chain, so the primary reading is kept. The forward column reads **+1.29%** because the scheduled unlocks for the next 90 days are a little smaller than the ones just past and the SOL inflation rate keeps stepping down each epoch. The label for SOL is **structurally inflationary by staking issuance**: a proof-of-stake Layer-1 whose float grows every epoch because stakers are paid in new coins and the fee burn is a rounding error beside them.

## Sell pressure: where new SOL comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **5.05M SOL** over the last 90 days and the largest line in the ledger. Solana mints new SOL at the end of every epoch and pays it to stake accounts and validators; the rate started at 8% in 2021 and falls by 15% of itself each year, reading **3.63%** today. That figure was reproduced from the chain rather than taken from a document: the protocol's own rate for the current epoch matched the published curve to the sixteenth decimal, and the epoch reward it implies matched the reward the chain actually paid to within a fraction of a coin. Across the window, **49** reward payouts minted about **5.55M SOL** in total. Rewards paid into accounts that are still locked or held by the Solana Foundation stay outside the float until those coins move, so they are not counted here; about **9.1%** of each payout lands in those accounts.

This window also contained a rare change to Solana's clock. Solana cut its slot time from **400** to **350** milliseconds from **Aug 21 2026**, to **300** from **Aug 28 2026** and to **250** from **Sep 18 2026**, so an epoch now lasts about 32 hours instead of two days. The upgrade raised the number of slots the protocol counts per year by the same ratio, which keeps Solana issuance the same per year of real time — each epoch simply pays a smaller reward, more often. The forward column therefore uses the current per-epoch reward and the measured 32-hour epoch, rather than stretching the old two-day epochs forward.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **4.35M SOL**. Solana has no project vesting calendar left from its early rounds, but the chain holds hundreds of stake accounts with an on-chain lockup date, and the market does not count them as circulating until that date passes. The largest group unlocks on the **7th of every month** until **Jan 2028**, about **0.64M SOL** a tranche; the **Jul 7**, **Aug 7** and **Sep 7 2026** tranches were each unstaked and withdrawn within days of unlocking. One-off lockups added **2.46M SOL** more, led by **1.25M SOL** on **Aug 1** and **0.88M SOL** on **Aug 30 2026**; most of those coins have not moved since, but the market counts them as circulating from the day the lockup ends, so that is when they are booked. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0.59M SOL**: on **Aug 26 2026** the Solana Foundation split **587,871 SOL** out of its own stake and handed the new stake account to an outside key with no lockup, which moved those coins into the float. That is the only Foundation release this window, and the forward column books zero because releases like this follow no published schedule. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0.60M SOL**: the FTX and Alameda bankruptcy estate still holds locked Solana stake that unlocks on the **11th** of every month, and the **Jul 11**, **Aug 11** and **Sep 11 2026** tranches of about **0.2M SOL** each were unstaked and withdrawn within days of unlocking. Twelve tranches, about **2.43M SOL**, remain locked through **Sep 2027**.

## Buy pressure: where new SOL goes

Solana has one mechanism that removes SOL, and it is small. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, destroyed **75,366 SOL** over the window. Every Solana transaction pays a base fee of 5,000 lamports per signature, and half of it is burned; priority fees go to the block producer in full and burn nothing. That split was checked block by block across the window: the burned amount came out at **2,500 lamports** per signature, to within a fraction of a percent, on every block sampled. A large share of what gets burned today comes from validator vote transactions. The Alpenglow consensus upgrade, due on **Sep 28 2026**, removes vote transactions and replaces their fees with a fixed per-epoch admission fee that is burned in full, about the same amount as the vote burn it replaces, so the forward burn of **89,279 SOL** barely moves. A validator vote in August to raise the burn on fees (SGP-0003) failed.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: the Solana protocol buys nothing back, and listed companies that hold SOL on their balance sheets bought it inside the float. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**: the Solana Foundation sells and grants SOL; it does not buy it back. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**: staked SOL still counts as circulating, so a bigger stake removes nothing from the float, and no new locked stake account with a published size appeared this window.

## Foundation and overhang

Solana has **47.04M SOL** outside the circulating float, and the framework reads that whole bucket from the chain rather than trusting a label. The largest part is the Solana Foundation's own stake, about **27.21M SOL** across more than seven hundred stake accounts under one withdrawal key, which the protocol's own supply code marks as non-circulating whatever its lockup says. A further **2.20M SOL** sits in a short list of named accounts the same code excludes. The rest, about **17.63M SOL**, is ordinary stake accounts with a lockup date still ahead, and those are the coins that feed Sell #2 and Sell #4 month by month through **Jan 2028**. The Foundation stake earns staking rewards that stay outside the float; if its balance falls between refreshes and the SOL lands in a wallet the float counts, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SOL compares to other proof-of-stake Layer-1 chains

SOL sits in the large class of proof-of-stake Layer-1 coins that pay for security with new issuance and hope usage catches up. Against a burn-heavy design such as Ethereum's, the difference is where the fee goes: Ethereum burns the whole base fee, so heavy use can cancel issuance, while Solana burns only half of a fixed per-signature fee and sends every priority fee to validators, so even at high activity Solana removes only a sliver of what it mints. Against chains with no burn at all, Solana at least has a burn — but at about **1.4%** of issuance it does not change the direction.

The Solana schedule is also unusually predictable. The rate falls by a fixed 15% a year toward a 1.5% floor, the protocol keeps issuance steady per year of real time even as slots get shorter, and the one approved change — a validator vote (SGP-0002) to double the disinflation rate to 30% a year — only bends the curve once it activates, without any jump. Compared with chains whose supply moves on discretionary treasury decisions, the Solana risk sits elsewhere: in the lockup calendar, which adds a known amount of float every month, and in the Foundation stake, which is large and readable but not scheduled.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the monthly unlocks: about **0.88M SOL** in October, **0.99M SOL** in November and **0.99M SOL** by Dec 24, led by the stake-account tranches on **Oct 7**, **Nov 7** and **Dec 7 2026** and the FTX estate tranches four days after each. Second, Alpenglow on **Sep 28 2026**: it changes how the burn is collected, not how much SOL is minted. Third, the doubled disinflation rate approved in August: it needs a client release and a feature-gate activation that had not happened by **Sep 25 2026**, and even if it activates inside the window it would trim only a few percent from the forward issuance. Fourth, the Solana Foundation stake at **27.21M SOL**: any move of those coins into the float would open a Sell #3 line.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SOL at **+1.79% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+1.29%** over the next 90. The structural mechanism is Solana staking issuance at **3.63%** a year, paid every epoch, plus a fixed calendar of stake-account unlocks that adds roughly **0.9–1.0M SOL** of float a month, against a base-fee burn that removes less than two percent of what is minted. The key risk is the unlock calendar and the Solana Foundation stake, both large, both readable on-chain, and only the first of them on a schedule. The ceiling is the **1.5%** terminal inflation rate: SOL supply never stops growing under the current rules, it only grows more slowly each year.
