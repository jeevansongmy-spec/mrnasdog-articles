Solana mints new SOL for stakers at about **3.70% a year** — roughly **5.41M SOL** reaching the float this window — while its base-fee burn removes only **0.06M SOL**. The much-quoted August unlock "cliff" did not land on the market: expired estate stake keeps re-accumulating in non-circulating reserves rather than reaching creditors, so the on-chain non-circulating pool actually grew. The framework reads **+0.92%** net for the 90-day window against the inflation monitor's **+0.65%**, a gap of **0.27 percentage points**. Forward it eases to **+0.89%** as the mint rate keeps stepping down.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 9 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **SOL at +0.92% net** on a circulating base of **582.2M SOL** — **5.41M SOL** of sell pressure against just **0.06M SOL** of buy pressure. The independent inflation monitor reads **+0.65%** over the same window, a gap of **0.27 percentage points**, comfortably inside the 0.5pp tolerance, so no data-conflict chip is raised. Forward, the framework projects **+0.89%** for the next 90 days: the Solana emission curve keeps stepping down, and the estate unlock calendar — despite firing on schedule — has stopped reaching the market. Solana is **inflationary by design on a falling curve, but only mildly on the tradable float** — supply is growing under 1% a quarter and projected to grow a touch slower next quarter.

## Sell pressure: where new SOL comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is now almost the entire story. Solana is a delegated proof-of-stake Layer 1 that mints new SOL every epoch and pays it entirely to validators and delegators — the foundation share of issuance is zero. The annual rate read directly from the Solana chain is **3.70%**, sitting exactly on the as-launched disinflation curve that began at 8% and tapers about 15% a year toward a **1.5%** terminal floor. Against Solana's **631.9M SOL** total supply that is a gross mint of roughly 5.87M SOL a quarter, but issuance is paid pro-rata to stake and the share landing in non-circulating stake stays off the float. Sized on the circulating base, Sell #1 is **5.41M SOL** for the window just ended and **5.23M SOL** projected for the next one — the curve doing its work, quietly, quarter after quarter.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is where this analysis overturns the earlier reading. Solana's original team, seed and validator allocations vested out years ago; the one live vesting stream is the **bankruptcy-estate locked stake** — originally the Alameda and FTX allocations, largely auctioned to institutional buyers who inherited the original lockups. Reading the lockup timestamp on every non-circulating Solana account shows the calendar clearly: about **3.97M SOL** of stake lockups expire over the next 90 days, including an **875K cliff on Aug 30 2026**. But scheduled is not the same as realised. Over the window just ended — which already contained the largest August unlock — the on-chain non-circulating pool did not shrink; it **grew by about 1.8M SOL**. Expired stake is not being distributed to the market: it drains out of the locked bucket and re-accumulates in **no-lockup reserve accounts** that are still classified non-circulating. The framework measures supply that actually reaches the market, so under the released-beats-scheduled rule Sell #2 books **0** — scheduled 3.97M, realised 0 — and the monitor confirms it, reading only +0.65% over a window that contained the cliff.

The other two sell rows are booked zero for related reasons. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero: **29.81M SOL** sits outside the float carrying no lockup at all — Solana Foundation and reserve accounts held back by classification rather than by code — but this pool is currently **growing, not releasing**, and no discretionary distribution was observed. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero by classification: the bankruptcy estate's SOL is exactly the on-chain escrow already measured in Sell #2, and booking it twice would double-count the same coins.

## Buy pressure: where new SOL goes

Solana's only structural buy-side mechanism is the fee burn, and the split is what limits it. A Solana base fee is **5,000 lamports** per signature, split 50/50: half burned, half to the block leader. Priority fees go **100%** to the leader and are not burned at all — that changed with SIMD-0096, live since **Feb 12 2025** — and out-of-protocol validator tips never touch the burn either. So the burnable slice is half of base fees only. At the current pace of roughly **640 to 815 SOL a day**, Buy #2 is about **0.06M SOL** over 90 days: real, permanent, and only about one percent of the issuance it offsets. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero — Solana staking rewards are minted fresh, never bought on the market. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero, with no open-market accumulation programme disclosed. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero as a programmatic line: about two-thirds of SOL supply is staked, but Solana staking is holder-driven yield seeking with an epoch-length exit, not an announced lock with a fixed quantum.

## Foundation and overhang

The whole non-circulating pool of **49.72M SOL** is tracked as team-controlled or estate-controlled overhang, split two ways. The first is the **bankruptcy-estate locked stake**: about **19.91M SOL** still carries a future lockup on a dated calendar running through early **2028**. The second, and the one that is now moving, is the **no-lockup Foundation and reserve bucket**: about **29.81M SOL** with no lockup on it at all — nothing on-chain stops it moving, only policy — and it grew over the window as expired estate stake rotated into it. The aggregate non-circulating balance is read from a public node each rebuild as the tripwire. If any of these balances falls between refreshes faster than re-accumulation absorbs it — that is, if the estate actually starts distributing to the market — the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SOL compares to other uncapped Layer 1 chains

Solana belongs to the **continuous-emission, uncapped Layer 1** family, and the sharpest mechanism contrast is with Ethereum. Both mint for stakers; both burn fees. But Ethereum burns the entire base fee, which at busy times has pushed its net issuance to zero or below, while Solana burns only half of a base fee deliberately kept near-free — so essentially all Solana issuance reaches the float. That is a design choice, not a flaw: Solana optimises for cheap blockspace, which means the burn can never be a meaningful supply sink at current fee levels. Against a capped, halving chain such as Bitcoin, the difference is shape: a halving chain steps down in discrete jumps toward a hard cap, whereas Solana glides down a percentage-of-supply curve toward a permanent 1.5% floor that never reaches zero.

The second differentiator is one most uncapped Layer 1s do not carry: an **exogenous vesting overhang from a bankruptcy estate**. On paper that calendar keeps firing into 2028, and an unlock tracker would read it as a fresh wave of sell pressure every month. On-chain the story is different — for now the expired stake re-locks into reserves rather than reaching the market, which is exactly why the framework's float-based reading (**+0.89%** forward) sits well below what a headline unlock schedule would imply. The overhang has not gone away; it has simply not been distributed yet, and the day it starts to be, Sell #3 will move off zero.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The single most important date is **Aug 18 2026**, the signaling deadline for **SIMD-0550** and **SIMD-0553**. SIMD-0550 would double the disinflation taper from 15% to 30% a year and pull the terminal 1.5% floor forward to roughly 2029; SIMD-0553 is a resource-based fee-burn that could lift the Solana burn from a few hundred SOL a day toward **7,500 to 9,000**. As of Aug 9 2026 both sit at about **14.4%** validator support against a 15% threshold, with no filed stake-weighted vote and no activated feature gate — but if either clears, every future Sell #1 or Buy #2 figure resets sharply. Watch the **875K SOL scheduled unlock on Aug 30 2026** and the recurring **Sep 7 2026** and **Oct 7 2026** estate tranches — not because they have been reaching the market, but for any sign that they start to. And watch the **29.81M SOL** no-lockup reserve balance for a net decline, which would be the first evidence of real estate distribution and would lift Sell #3 off zero.

## Summary

Solana is mildly inflationary, and for now the protocol is essentially the only source. Staking issuance of **5.41M SOL** at about **3.70% a year**, against a **0.06M SOL** base-fee burn and estate unlocks that keep re-locking into reserves instead of reaching the market, gives a framework reading of **+0.92%** net for the window against the monitor's **+0.65%** — a **0.27pp** gap, well inside tolerance. Forward the framework reads **+0.89%**, as the mint rate falls toward its 1.5% floor. The key risk is that the large estate and Foundation overhang begins to distribute; the key relief is that the mint is contractually falling and, so far, the unlock cliffs are landing in reserves, not on the tape. SOL has no supply cap, but its net supply growth on the tradable float is now under 1% a quarter.
