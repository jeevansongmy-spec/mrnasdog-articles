# LINK Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
Looking for the buy-or-sell answer? It is on the LINK coin page, with demand and price drivers: [mrnasdog.com/research/link](https://mrnasdog.com/research/link)

**Chainlink (LINK) supply is growing, and it grows in steps.** No new LINK can be minted, but on **Oct 9 2026** the Chainlink team released **18.75M LINK** from its locked wallets, which lifted the circulating supply by **+2.45%** in 90 days. Nothing was burned or locked away to offset it. The next step, about **11.25M LINK** expected around Dec 18 2026, points to **+1.47%** in the next 90 days, and the team still holds **233.15M LINK** that can come out the same way.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 23 to Oct 21 2026, LINK's circulating supply grew by **+2.45%**: **18.75M LINK** entered the market and **0** left it, against **766.85M LINK** in circulation. Other supply trackers show **+2.49%** for the same 90 days, a gap of only **0.04 points**, so there is no data conflict to flag on this page. For the next 90 days the Chainlink ledger expects **+1.47%**, all of it from one December release. In short, LINK is a **fixed-cap token with a team-paced release**: the 1 billion cap can never grow, but the share that trades keeps rising by about 7% of the total each year.

## Sell pressure: where new LINK comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. The LINK token contract on Ethereum set the total at **1,000,000,000 LINK** when it launched in September 2017, and it has no mint function, no owner and no upgrade path, so Chainlink never pays node operators or stakers in brand-new LINK. That is the main difference from proof-of-stake chains, where validator pay is printed every day.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. There is no vesting contract and no dated unlock calendar for LINK: no investor cliffs, no team cliffs, no monthly drip. The original token sale coins have been liquid since 2017.

All of the new LINK comes from row 3, the team's own releases. The Chainlink team keeps the unreleased LINK in 33 locked wallets and moves part of it to the market a few times a year. On **Oct 9 2026**, eight of those wallets sent out **18.75M LINK** within about two hours. Of that, **16.88M LINK** went to a large exchange the same evening and **1.88M LINK** went to the team wallet that pays staking rewards. Chainlink says its release rate is currently **7% of the total supply per year**, which is **70M LINK**, and since 2024 it has released that in four steps a year: about 19M in spring, 21M in June, 18.75M in the autumn and **11.25M** in December. The December step came on Dec 20 2024 and Dec 19 2025, both the third Friday of December, so the next one should land around **Dec 18 2026**. That single step is the whole **11.25M LINK** we expect over the next 90 days.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**. No court, trustee or failed lender holds LINK that is waiting to be paid out.

## Buy pressure: where new LINK goes

The programmatic buyback books **0**, even though the buying is real. Every week the Chainlink Reserve turns fees it earns from its customers into LINK and holds it. In these 90 days it took in **1.35M LINK** over 14 weekly buys, and it now holds **6.29M LINK**; Chainlink's own quarterly review put it at 6,047,498 LINK on Sep 30 2026. The Reserve says it does not expect to withdraw for years. But those coins were already part of the circulating supply, and the Reserve is still counted as circulating, so its buying moves LINK from one holder to another and takes nothing out of the count. The Oct 9 release alone was about **14 times** what the Reserve bought in the whole 90 days.

The protocol fee burn is **0**. LINK has no burn: fees for Chainlink data, messaging and other services go to node operators and to the Reserve. The dead address holds only about 110 LINK from stray transfers.

A foundation buy is **0**. Outside the Reserve, the team sells rather than buys. The two US funds that hold LINK held 16.65M LINK together at the end of September, but they buy coins that already circulate.

A new long-term lock is **0**. The community staking pool holds **40.88M LINK** and has been full and unchanged for the whole window. Staked LINK still counts as circulating, so staking takes nothing out of the supply.

## Foundation and overhang

The big overhang is the team's locked supply: **233.15M LINK** across the 33 locked wallets, nine of which still hold almost all of it (five with 30M each, then 29.4M, 28.75M, 22M and 3M). They have no public calendar, but they have followed the same yearly rhythm since at least 2024, and we read their balances on-chain at every check. At 70M LINK a year, the locked supply would last a little over three more years.

Three more team-linked wallets are already counted as circulating, so they cannot add supply, but we watch them because they hold coins the team controls: the Chainlink Reserve with **6.29M LINK**, the staking-rewards wallet with **10.32M LINK**, and the staking reward vault with **3.23M LINK**, which pays stakers a little every day. If the locked wallets' balance falls between our checks, that outflow goes into row 3 at the next check; if the Reserve ever sends LINK out, that outflow goes into row 3 as well.

## How LINK compares to other oracle and data-network tokens

LINK sits at one end of the oracle and data-network group: a **hard cap with zero issuance**. The Graph (GRT) works the other way: it has no cap, issues about 3% new GRT a year to indexers and burns 1% of the query fees people pay, so its supply grows from new coins rather than from released ones. Pyth Network (PYTH) also has a fixed supply, but it releases locked tokens in large yearly cliffs on a published calendar each May, so its supply jumps once a year, while LINK's comes out in four smaller steps that the team times itself.

The second difference is what the fees do. Some tokens burn part of their fees, which takes coins out of the supply for good. Chainlink instead buys LINK for the Reserve and keeps it. That builds a growing pile the team says it will not touch for years, but it does not shrink the supply, so on our ledger it never offsets the releases. A coin that burns can show a net fall in supply; LINK can only slow its growth if the releases slow.

Compared with proof-of-stake chains such as Ethereum, LINK pays its stakers from a prefunded reward vault rather than from new coins, so staking does not add supply. The only thing that does is the team's release schedule, which makes LINK's supply easy to read: watch the 33 wallets, and you see every new coin before it trades.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Around Dec 18 2026:** the December release, expected at **11.25M LINK** from the locked wallets; if it slips past Jan 19 2027, the next 90 days would show no new supply.

**Every week:** the Reserve's buys, which ran about 75K–97K LINK a week from late August to mid-October 2026, down from about 128K–140K a week in late July and early August.

**Oct 29 2026:** Chainlink's Link:NYC event for banks and asset managers, a place where any change to staking, the Reserve or the release rate would likely be announced.

**Any day:** a change to the stated 7% yearly release rate, or a new staking version that changes the 40.88M LINK pool or how rewards are paid.

## Summary

Chainlink's LINK has a hard cap of 1 billion and no minting, yet its circulating supply grew **+2.45%** in the 90 days to Oct 21 2026 because the team released **18.75M LINK** from its locked wallets on Oct 9 2026. The next 90 days should add about **+1.47%** from an **11.25M LINK** December release. The Reserve bought 1.35M LINK with fees, but it keeps coins that already circulate, so it does not reduce supply. The key risk is the **233.15M LINK** the team still holds, released at about 70M a year, which would empty the locked wallets around early 2030 at the current pace.
