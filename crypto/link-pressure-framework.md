Chainlink has a hard **1 billion LINK** cap and mints nothing, yet the circulating supply of LINK still grows, because a non-circulating reserve is released into the market roughly once a quarter. The last 90 days caught a single release — about **21M LINK on Jun 20 2026** — against about **1.9M LINK** absorbed by the Chainlink Reserve. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+2.55% net** over that window; the supply monitor reads **+2.95%**, a gap of only **0.40 percentage points**, inside tolerance and with no data-conflict flag.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 10 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **LINK at +2.55% net** on the realised view and **+2.39% net** on the forward view, driven entirely by reserve release rather than by any protocol mint. The supply monitor reads the realised change at **+2.95%** for the same window, so the gap is about **0.40 percentage points** — inside tolerance, and this build therefore ships **no monitor-gap flag**. The residual is essentially the Chainlink Reserve's **1.9M LINK** absorption, which the upstream classifier still counts as circulating while the framework treats it as locked. Both the realised and the forward reading sit **below the +3% line**, so the inflation score stays at **1** on the 0–5 scale, not 0. The label for Chainlink: **hard-capped, zero-mint, but structurally inflationary on the active float** for as long as the reserve unwinds.

## Sell pressure: where new LINK comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and that is the defining fact about Chainlink tokenomics. LINK is a fixed-supply token on Ethereum: all **1 billion LINK** were minted at launch, there is no mint function, no block reward and no staking emission. An on-chain total-supply read at both ends of this window returned the same **1,000,000,000 LINK**, so not a single new token was created. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is also zero: Chainlink's original contractual vesting schedule physically finished in 2024, leaving no dated cliff calendar behind. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero as well, because no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution applies to LINK.

That leaves Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, as the entire sell story, at about **21M LINK** over the last 90 days. Chainlink Labs releases LINK from a large non-circulating reserve roughly once a quarter, in batches of about **10M to 21M**, to pay node operators, fund staking rewards and finance ecosystem development. This window caught exactly one firing: the **Jun 20 2026** release of about **21M LINK** out of four non-circulating addresses, of which about **18.375M** went to an exchange and **2.625M** to a staking-rewards multisig. There was **no release in July or August** — the supply monitor's 30-day reading is essentially flat, confirming the reserve stayed put. The prior firing, about **19M LINK** in early **Apr 2026**, fell before the window opened and is not counted. Because the release is discretionary rather than scheduled, the framework projects it by date and not by average: one firing lands inside the forward window, around **mid-Sep 2026**, sized at the mid-point of the last two at about **20M LINK**.

## Buy pressure: where new LINK goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the only active offset in the Chainlink ledger, at about **1.9M LINK** over 90 days, and it takes the form of the Chainlink Reserve. Through Payment Abstraction, on-chain service fees and off-chain enterprise revenue are automatically converted into LINK and deposited into a reserve contract that accumulates behind a multi-day withdrawal timelock; Chainlink states it expects no withdrawals for years. An on-chain balance read on that contract shows about **3.5M LINK** at the start of this window and about **5.4M LINK** today — it crossed **5.21M on Jul 31 2026**, having added **706,809 LINK** in July alone — so roughly **1.9M LINK** was taken off the open market and held. The accumulation rate is not static: monthly intake has climbed past **700,000 LINK** as network revenue compounds.

The remaining buy rows are zero. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero because LINK has no burn mechanism at all: fees for oracle, data-feed and cross-chain services are routed to node operators and to the Reserve, never destroyed, so the Reserve is an accumulation mechanism rather than a burn. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero, with no discretionary open-market buying disclosed beyond the Reserve accumulation already counted. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero as well: an on-chain read shows the Chainlink community staking pool sitting at its **40.88M LINK** capacity at both ends of the window, and the node-operator pool roughly flat, so no new LINK was locked. A staking cap increase would be the event that puts a number in this row.

## Foundation and overhang

The defining structural feature of LINK is its overhang. Roughly **252M LINK** — about a quarter of the entire 1 billion cap — still sits outside circulation in team-managed non-circulating allocations controlled by Chainlink Labs, with no published cliff calendar and no committed release date. That pool is what the quarterly releases draw from, and it is by far the largest supply variable for the token. A second, smaller overhang is the staking-rewards multisig that receives a slice of every quarterly release before those tokens are distributed to stakers. On the other side of the ledger sits the Chainlink Reserve, the buyback destination, an on-chain accumulation contract holding about **5.4M LINK** today behind a multi-day timelock; it is fully trackable on-chain and re-read on every rebuild. If the balance of the non-circulating reserve, the staking multisig or the Chainlink Reserve falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How LINK compares to other capped-supply utility tokens

Chainlink belongs to the family of hard-capped, pre-minted utility tokens rather than to the continuous-emission layer-1 family. Against an uncapped proof-of-stake chain, which pays validators out of fresh issuance every block and therefore prints supply forever, LINK looks structurally superior: the cap is absolute, the mint function does not exist, and the ceiling on lifetime dilution was fixed at launch. Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, the comparison inverts. A halving chain's remaining issuance is transparent, mechanical and decelerating on a published schedule; Chainlink's remaining **252M LINK** is released at the discretion of the issuer, in tranches whose size and timing are announced only by the on-chain transfer itself.

The closest structural analogue is an exchange token or an enterprise token with a large foundation float and a revenue-funded buyback. Both accumulate value by routing fees back into the token, and both carry a treasury overhang that dwarfs the buyback. The critical mechanical difference is destination: an exchange token typically **burns** what it buys, permanently shrinking supply, while the Chainlink Reserve **holds** what it accumulates. Held tokens can, in principle, come back; burned tokens cannot. That single design choice is why the Chainlink Reserve, at under **2M LINK** a quarter, offsets rather than reverses a **21M LINK** release, and why the framework reads Chainlink as inflationary on the float despite a genuinely fixed cap.

The offsetting argument, which the mechanism supports, is direction of travel. Reserve release is a finite process — the non-circulating pool only shrinks — while Reserve accumulation is tied to network revenue and has been accelerating, from roughly **2.2M LINK** held in **Feb 2026** to about **5.4M** by **Aug 2026**, with monthly intake stepping up past 700K. If revenue keeps compounding while the quarterly release stays near 20M, the two lines converge. That crossover has not happened yet, and the framework scores what is measured, not what is projected beyond one window.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The first watch item is the next quarterly reserve release, projected around **mid-Sep 2026** at roughly **20M LINK**; a release materially larger than 21M, or an unscheduled second release inside the window, would push the forward reading above the **+3%** line and tip the inflation score from 1 to 0. The second is the split inside that release between the exchange destination and the staking-rewards multisig, since a larger multisig share reaches the open market more slowly. The third is the Chainlink Reserve accumulation rate, which ran above **700,000 LINK** in **July 2026**; a sustained move higher is the only mechanism on this page that can compress the net figure. The fourth is Chainlink staking v0.3, still not live — a cap increase above the current **40.88M LINK** community pool would be the first non-zero Buy #4 in this ledger. The fifth is any first-ever burn or a change to the Reserve withdrawal policy; Chainlink has never burned LINK, and either announcement would restructure the buy side entirely.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Chainlink at **+2.55% net supply growth** over the 90 days to **Aug 10 2026** and **+2.39%** projected for the next 90 days — supply growing, and projected to keep growing, but both readings under 3%. The structural mechanism is unusual: LINK mints nothing and is capped at **1 billion** forever, so every unit of dilution comes from a discretionary quarterly release out of a **252M LINK** non-circulating reserve, most recently **21M on Jun 20 2026**. The key risk is precisely that discretion — the release calendar is unpublished, so size and timing are known only after the fact, and the overhang is large enough to fund years of releases. The offsetting force, the revenue-funded Chainlink Reserve, absorbed **1.9M LINK** in the window and is accelerating, but it holds rather than burns, so at current rates it softens the dilution instead of ending it.
