Chainlink has a hard **1 billion LINK** cap and mints nothing, yet the circulating supply of LINK keeps growing, because Chainlink releases LINK out of a non-circulating reserve at a published rate. Reading all **33** of those reserve wallets directly at both ends of the last 90 days shows them falling from **272.9M** to **251.9M** — exactly **21M LINK** reached the float, all of it in a single release on **Jun 19 2026** — against **1.71M LINK** bought on the open market and parked by the Chainlink Reserve. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+2.58% net** over that window; the supply monitor reads **+3.03%**, a gap of **0.46 percentage points**, inside tolerance and with no data-conflict flag.


## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 5 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **LINK at +2.58% net** on the realised view and **+2.08% net** on the forward view, driven entirely by reserve release rather than by any protocol mint. The supply monitor reads the realised change at **+3.03%** for the same window, so the gap is **0.46 percentage points** — inside tolerance, and this build therefore ships **no monitor-gap flag**. Most of that residual is the Chainlink Reserve's **1.71M LINK** absorption, which the upstream classifier still counts as circulating while the framework treats it as taken off the market. Both the realised and the forward reading sit **inside the 1% to 3% growth band**, so the LINK inflation score stays at **1** on the 0–5 scale. The label for Chainlink: **hard-capped, zero-mint, zero-burn, and still inflationary on the active float** for as long as the reserve unwinds.


## Sell pressure: where new LINK comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and that is the defining fact about Chainlink tokenomics. LINK is a fixed-supply token on Ethereum: all **1 billion LINK** were created at launch in 2017. This build did not settle that by watching the supply number sit still, which would prove nothing — it read the deployed token contract itself. The Chainlink token exposes **13** callable functions and not one of them mints, burns, upgrades or transfers ownership; every storage write in the contract lands in the balance or allowance tables; and the supply figure the contract reports is a fixed number compiled into the code rather than a value anything can rewrite. There is no write path, so no LINK can ever be issued. Chainlink staking rewards are not an exception: the reward vault took in exactly one top-up in the window, **607,500 LINK**, and that LINK came out of the same project reserve, so it belongs in the reserve-release row rather than in fresh issuance. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero as well — Chainlink has no vesting contract and no cliff calendar. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero, because no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holds LINK.

That leaves Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, as the entire sell story, at **21M LINK** over the last 90 days. Because Chainlink publishes the full list of its non-circulating wallets, this row is measured rather than estimated, and it is measured by counting every wallet rather than sampling the big ones: the aggregate balance of those **33** addresses fell from **272,900,030 LINK** to **251,900,030 LINK**, a realised outflow of exactly **21,000,000 LINK**. Subtracting that end balance from the **1 billion** cap reproduces the published circulating-supply figure for LINK to the sixth decimal place, so the numerator and the denominator on this page are measured against one and the same boundary. It all moved in one cluster on **Jun 19 2026**, out of four reserve wallets, each of which sent a single test token before the bulk transfer: **18,375,000 LINK** went to a deposit address that held nothing at either end of the window and forwarded every unit onward to an exchange hot wallet, and **2,625,000 LINK** went to an operational multisig that pays Chainlink node operators and funded the one staking-reward top-up. That multisig holds **9,555,000 LINK** today.

The forward projection for Chainlink is not an average of past flow and not the midpoint of recent firings — it is the published rule. Chainlink states that its token release schedule is currently **7% of the total supply per year**, which on a **1 billion** cap is **70,000,000 LINK** a year. A twelve-month on-chain read of the same 33 addresses confirms it almost exactly: **321,900,030 LINK** on **Sep 5 2025** against **251,900,030 LINK** today, a drawdown of **70,000,000 LINK** to within a fraction of a token, or precisely **7.000%** of the cap. The individual firings are lumpy and undated — the on-chain series places bulk releases of **18.75M**, **11.25M**, **19M** and **21M** between **Oct 2025** and **Jun 2026**, and no release calendar is published — so the rate is scaled to the framework's window rather than assumed to be one calendar quarter: **17.26M LINK** for the next 90 days.


## Buy pressure: where new LINK goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the only real offset in the Chainlink ledger, at **1.71M LINK** over 90 days, and it takes the form of the Chainlink Reserve. Chainlink Payment Abstraction lets customers pay for oracle, data-feed and cross-chain services in whatever asset they hold; those fees are then converted into LINK on decentralised exchanges and deposited into a reserve contract that holds rather than burns. Reading the Reserve contract directly gives **4,059,338 LINK** on **Jun 7 2026** and **5,770,818 LINK** today, a realised gain of **1,711,480 LINK** across **13** weekly deposits, with **zero** outgoing transfers of any size in the entire window. The important question is where that LINK was bought, because a buyback funded out of a project's own untraded reserve removes nothing from the market. Tracing the deposits back by sender settles it: **792,939 LINK** came off a Uniswap V3 LINK-to-ether pool across **127** separate swaps, and a further **745,423 LINK** came through an on-chain trade settlement contract. At minimum **1,538,362 LINK** of the Reserve's intake is traceable to two named trading venues, so this LINK genuinely leaves the tradable float. One caution for the next reading: the weekly deposit is shrinking, from **147,198 LINK** in June to **97,111 LINK** on **Sep 4 2026**.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** for Chainlink, and this build checked both of the surfaces a burn can show up on. The token contract has no burn function at all, so the supply figure cannot fall. The unspendable address, which is the only other way tokens can be destroyed in practice, went from **109.57 LINK** to **110.26 LINK** across the window — **0.69 LINK** of stray user dust, not a mechanism. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero: Chainlink's only market buying runs through the Reserve and is already counted above, and there is no separate discretionary purchase programme. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **1,744 LINK** — near enough to nothing, and for a specific reason. The Chainlink community staking pool is **100.000%** full at its **40,875,000 LINK** cap and did not move a single token in 90 days; the operator pool sits at **1,729,947 LINK** of a **4,125,000** cap. Together that is **42.6M LINK** locked, or **5.69%** of circulating supply, but the pool is full, so staking can absorb no more LINK until the cap is raised.


## Foundation and overhang

The headline Chainlink overhang is the reserve itself: **251,900,030 LINK** across the 33 published non-circulating addresses, which is **25.19%** of the entire 1 billion cap and roughly **3.6 years** of releases at the published 7% rate. It is fully trackable on-chain and re-read at every rebuild. Three smaller Chainlink-controlled pools sit outside that published list and are tracked separately: the operational payout multisig at **9,555,000 LINK**, the staking reward vault at **2,845,960 LINK**, and two routing buffers holding **912,624 LINK** between them. The Chainlink Reserve is the fourth, at **5,770,818 LINK** and rising, and it is the buyback destination: bought LINK goes there, stays there, and is guarded by a multi-day delay before any withdrawal can settle. Chainlink has no token-governance DAO treasury and no bankruptcy estate. Exchange custodial wallets and unidentified large holders are excluded from this count by design. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.


## How LINK compares to other hard-capped utility tokens

Chainlink belongs to a small class: hard-capped tokens with no protocol issuance at all, where every unit of supply growth is a reserve being unwound rather than a validator being paid. That makes LINK unlike an uncapped continuous-emission chain, where dilution is a permanent property of the consensus, and unlike a halving-model chain, where the issuance rate falls on a fixed schedule nobody controls. Chainlink's dilution has an end date — the reserve runs down in about **3.6 years** at the current rate, after which supply is genuinely fixed — but until then the release rate is a company decision, not a protocol constant, and that is the honest difference between a hard cap and a hard schedule.

Against exchange tokens that run quarterly buy-and-burn, the contrast is sharper still. Those tokens destroy supply outright, so the buy side lands on the total supply figure. Chainlink buys and parks: the LINK is off the float but not gone, and it can in principle come back. What Chainlink does have, and most oracle and middleware tokens do not, is a real fee economy funding that buying. Measured on **Sep 5 2026**, Chainlink's annual fees run at **0.72%** of its market capitalisation and its annual revenue at **0.67%** — a ratio in which a bigger number means more fee income per dollar of valuation. On that measure Chainlink sits **1.72×** Solana's **0.42%** and roughly ten times Ethereum's **0.07%**, all three measured on the same day. The leg doing the work there is the numerator: Chainlink's fee take is large relative to its capitalisation, not merely divided by a small one.


## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the next reserve release. Chainlink publishes a rate but no calendar, and the last firing was **Jun 19 2026** at **21M LINK**; on the observed gaps of roughly 75 to 172 days, another release is plausible inside this window and would land in Sell #3 the moment it clears. Second, the weekly Reserve deposit, which has fallen from **147,198 LINK** to **97,111 LINK** since June — if that decline continues, the only offset in the Chainlink ledger keeps shrinking and the net reading drifts up. Third, the staking cap. The community pool has been **100.000%** full at **40,875,000 LINK** for the whole window, and Chainlink's roadmap discusses a far larger pool; a cap raise would be the first credible new lock LINK has had in a long time. Fourth, any change to the published **7%** release rate, which is the single number the forward projection rests on. Fifth, any move by the Chainlink Reserve in the other direction — it has never sent a token out, and the first withdrawal would change the character of the buy side entirely.


## Summary

Chainlink is a hard-capped token that still dilutes. No LINK can be minted — the contract has no write path to its supply figure — and no LINK is burned, so the entire supply story is a **251.9M LINK** project reserve being released into the market at a published **7% of total supply per year**. Over the last 90 days that meant **21M LINK** reaching the float in one release on **Jun 19 2026**, against **1.71M LINK** that the Chainlink Reserve bought on decentralised exchanges and has never sold, for a net of **+2.58%** and a forward reading of **+2.08%**. The key risk is that the offset is shrinking while the release is not: Reserve deposits fell by a third across the window and the staking pool is full at its cap, so nothing else is absorbing LINK. The ceiling is genuine and roughly **3.6 years** away — the reserve is finite, and when it empties Chainlink's supply becomes fixed for good.
