Kaspa (KAS) mined **199.6M KAS** over the last 90 days, and nothing anywhere in the protocol offsets it — no buyback, no burn, no treasury, no staking lock. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.73% net** on a **27.5B** circulating base against a **28.7B** hard cap, with about **96%** of all KAS already mined. The distinguishing feature of Kaspa is the shape of that emission: the block reward steps down **5.6%** on the same date every month, so the next 90 days project lower at **+0.61%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 11 2026**, the framework reads **KAS at +0.73% net inflation** — proof-of-work mining alone, against a completely empty buy ledger. The independent supply monitor reads **+0.59%** over the same window, a gap of **0.14 percentage points**, comfortably inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag is raised. The residual is measurement noise in the monitor's own supply series rather than a disagreement about mechanism: Kaspa has exactly one supply input and it is published on-chain to eight decimal places. This is a quiet chain with a loud arithmetic — every KAS that will ever exist arrives through mining on a fixed curve, and that curve is now in its shallow tail.

## Sell pressure: where new KAS comes from

Kaspa has one mint and only one. Sell #1, protocol inflation, booked **199.6M KAS**. The chain pays **2.31 KAS** per block at ten blocks a second — about **23.1 KAS** every second, or close to **2.0M KAS** a day — to whichever miner wins each block in the BlockDAG. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** and always will be: Kaspa launched in **Nov 2021** with no premine, no presale, no ICO and no allocation to founders, developers or investors, so there is no vesting contract in existence to unlock. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**: there is no foundation treasury, no DAO wallet and no reserve pool holding KAS. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: no estate, no trustee, no escrow anywhere in the coin's history.

What makes the Kaspa emission worth walking carefully is that it decays continuously rather than sitting flat between cliffs. Kaspa halves once a year, but it delivers that halving as twelve monthly steps, each multiplying the reward by the twelfth root of one half — a fall of about **5.6%** per step, borrowed from the semitone spacing of a chromatic musical scale. A 90-day figure therefore has to be integrated across the steps that fall inside the window, not taken from a single point rate. Inside this window the reward ran at 27.5 KAS a second, then 25.96, then 24.50, then 23.12 — four monthly steps, which sum to the **199.6M KAS** above. Run the same integration forward across the steps due **Sep 4 2026**, **Oct 5 2026** and **Nov 4 2026** and the next 90 days come to **168.2M KAS**, or **+0.61%**.

The model was verified against the chain rather than assumed. The live block reward reads **2.31246515 KAS** per block; multiplied by ten blocks a second that is **23.1246515 KAS/s**, which is exactly 440 × 2^(−51/12) — the fifty-first step of the published curve, to the last decimal. Two live supply-delta measurements taken minutes apart bracket that rate from either side: **+4,317.37 KAS over 180 seconds** works out to 23.99 KAS/s, and **+2,280.09 KAS over 100 seconds** to 22.80 KAS/s, the normal short-run block-timing wobble around a schedule rate of 23.1247. The next step lands **Sep 4 2026**. The emission rate is published per second rather than per block, which is why the 2025 upgrade that raised the block rate from one to ten blocks a second changed nothing about issuance: the per-block reward was divided by ten at the same moment.

## Buy pressure: where new KAS goes

The Kaspa buy ledger is empty by construction. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: the protocol takes no revenue cut and holds no treasury, so there is nothing to fund a buyback and no contract that could run one — pages advertising a KAS buyback belong to unaffiliated projects, not to Kaspa. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**: transaction fees are paid straight to the merging miner inside the coinbase output, and the hard fork of **Jun 30 2026** — which added covenants, zero-knowledge verification opcodes and native L1 tokens — priced its new scripts in fees to miners rather than adding a sink. Token launches on the chain pay their deploy and mint fees the same way, to miners, and none of it is burned. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**: the donation-funded ecosystem foundation issues grants and has never run an open-market accumulation programme. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: proof-of-work Kaspa has no staking, no bonding and no vault, so a KAS is either held or spent. New KAS enters; nothing at the protocol level ever removes it.

## Foundation and overhang

Kaspa is one of the few coins in coverage where the team-controlled overhang is genuinely zero rather than merely small. The **Nov 2021** fair launch created no insider allocation of any kind, and the research company that built the original protocol dissolved its claim before mainnet went live, so no wallet anywhere is identifiable as team-controlled. The ecosystem foundation that exists today is funded by donations and grants and holds no protocol allocation it could distribute. The only forward supply is the roughly **1,071M KAS** still unmined beneath the **28.7B** cap, and that reaches the market solely through mining, at a pace the protocol fixes rather than a person decides. The trigger line is therefore about the schedule, not a wallet: if the chain's reward ever stops sitting exactly on the published curve between refreshes, the difference enters Sell #1 at the next refresh — and because Kaspa has no on-chain governance, moving that curve would require a hard fork adopted by node operators and miners in the open.

## How KAS compares to other hard-cap proof-of-work chains

Against Bitcoin and Litecoin, Kaspa runs the same destination on a different road. All three are fair-launch, hard-capped proof-of-work coins with no burn, no buyback and no treasury, so all three send mining emission to market in full. But Bitcoin and Litecoin halve in one step every four years, which means their issuance is flat for years and then falls off a cliff; Kaspa halves every single year and delivers it in twelve monthly steps, so its issuance is never flat and never cliff-edged. Practically, that makes the Kaspa forward reading strictly lower than its trailing reading in every window — **+0.73%** behind, **+0.61%** ahead — where a halving chain only sees that drop once every 1,460 days.

Against tail-emission coins such as Monero, the contrast is the opposite one. A tail emission holds a small permanent issuance forever to pay for security; Kaspa's curve decays toward zero, with roughly **96%** of the cap already mined and the remaining **1,071M KAS** spread thinly over the decades left in the schedule. That buys Kaspa a cleaner scarcity story and hands it the same long-run question Bitcoin faces — whether transaction fees can pay for proof-of-work security once the subsidy is negligible. Kaspa's answer is throughput: ten blocks a second, with the programmability upgrade of **Jun 30 2026** designed to give the chain fee-generating activity beyond simple payments.

Against uncapped continuous-emission proof-of-stake L1s, the comparison is structural rather than a matter of degree. Those chains mint on a governed inflation rate paid to stakers, and much of what they mint is immediately re-locked, so their headline issuance overstates what reaches the market while a governance vote can change the rate at any time. Kaspa has neither property: nothing re-locks, so all **199.6M KAS** is genuinely tradable float, and no vote exists that could raise the rate.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Three of the four watch items are already on the calendar. The emission steps down to **2.18 KAS** per block on **Sep 4 2026**, to **2.06 KAS** on **Oct 5 2026**, and to **1.94 KAS** on **Nov 4 2026** — each one cutting the mint by a further 5.6% and each one already priced into the **+0.61%** forward reading. The fourth is post-fork activity: native L1 tokens and covenant scripts raise the fee load on the chain, and while fees currently go to miners rather than to a burn, a future fork that redirected any part of them would be the first buy-side entry Kaspa has ever had. Beyond those, the only thing that could move this reading is a hard-fork proposal touching the emission curve itself, and no such proposal exists — the mid-window fork was programmability-only, with no monetary change of any kind.

## Summary

Kaspa is a fair-launch, hard-capped proof-of-work BlockDAG that minted **199.6M KAS** in the last 90 days into an empty buy ledger, for a framework reading of **+0.73% net** against a monitor read of **+0.59%** — a 0.14-point gap that is monitor noise, not a mechanism dispute. The structural mechanism is the chromatic emission curve: an annual halving paid out as a **5.6%** step down every month, which makes the forward reading of **+0.61%** lower than the trailing one by design and will keep doing so every window from here. The key risk is not the schedule but the absence of any counterweight — with no burn, no buyback and no lock, every mined KAS reaches the market in full, so demand alone has to absorb it. With about **96%** of the **28.7B** cap already mined and no discretionary actor able to move the curve, KAS has one of the most predictable and steadily cooling supply profiles in coverage.
