## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Kaspa at **+0.69% net**: **192.08M KAS** of newly mined supply reaching the market against **0** removed, on a circulating base of **27,672.14M KAS**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.93%**, a gap of **0.23 percentage points** — inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so this build ships no data-conflict flag, and the residual is smaller than the monitor's own endpoint noise on a signal this size. Projected forward the framework reads **+0.59%**, and every unit of that improvement comes from the Kaspa protocol itself rather than from any decision anyone will make. KAS is a **single-tap chain on a self-closing emission curve** — the cleanest label for the reading, and the reason its inflation number moves down a little every month without a governance vote, a burn programme or a buyback.

## Sell pressure: where new KAS comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is not merely the largest row on the Kaspa ledger — it is the only one. Kaspa is a proof-of-work BlockDAG running the GHOSTDAG consensus at roughly ten blocks per second, and every block pays its miner a subsidy set by a fixed schedule. After the pre-deflationary phase closed in May 2022 that subsidy entered what the Kaspa community calls its chromatic phase: instead of a halving every four years, the reward is multiplied by 2^(-1/12) — a cut of **5.6126%** — once per subsidy month, so twelve steps make one halving and the curve never jumps. This build derived the emission rather than quoting it. The subsidy is indexed by Kaspa's DAA score, and a subsidy month is a fixed width in that score of **26,298,000** units, so 90 days of Kaspa issuance is an integral across whichever steps the window happens to cross. It crossed three. The subsidy ran at **2.75** KAS a block at the window open and **2.31** KAS at the close, and integrating across those steps gives **192.08M KAS**. Two live reads confirmed the curve to eight decimal places, and the realised block rate was measured at **9.99854** per second over a 61-day baseline against a ten-per-second target — a chain running within a hundredth of a percent of its own parameter.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each is zero for a structural reason rather than a quiet quarter. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because Kaspa was fair-launched on **Nov 7 2021** with a genesis supply of zero: no ICO, no presale, no premine, no team or investor allocation was ever created, so no vesting schedule can exist to release one. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because there is no protocol allocation for any foundation, lab or DAO to hold; the only identified group-controlled wallet on this chain is a donor-funded community multisig, elected by community vote, holding about **1,831 KAS** — donated coins that were already circulating. Sell #4 is zero because Kaspa has no bankruptcy estate, no escrow contract and no lockup. The only KAS not yet in the market is the **1,031.89M** still unmined beneath the cap, and mining is the sole door it can come through.

## Buy pressure: where new KAS goes

Nowhere, and this is the honest reading rather than a missing measurement. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero because the Kaspa protocol takes no revenue and holds no treasury — there is no fee split to fund a buyback and no entity with standing to run one. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero because Kaspa transaction fees are paid straight to miners inside the coinbase; nothing is destroyed. This build checked the burn question on both surfaces rather than one, because a burn is often not a supply-reducing call. Kaspa does have a well-known keyless address holding **11.23M KAS** sent there voluntarily since 2021, most of it a single fair-distribution burn from the launch period — but across this window it received only **328 KAS** in eight transfers, which is roughly one millionth of one percent of supply. The published supply figure, meanwhile, totals the unspent output set, so coins parked at a keyless address stay counted forever and the supply series can never fall. The two surfaces are genuinely independent on Kaspa, and neither moved enough to book. Buy #3 is zero for the same reason as Sell #3 — no foundation treasury exists to buy with. Buy #4 is zero because proof of work has no staking, no bonding and no vault to lock coins into.

## Foundation and overhang

Kaspa is one of the few coins in this catalogue with no team-controlled overhang to enumerate, and the justification is the launch itself: with a genesis supply of zero and no allocation to founders, developers or investors, there is no wallet that could hold a protocol claim. The Kaspa Ecosystem Foundation is donation and grant funded and holds no protocol allocation. Community development funds sit in multisigs run by community-elected treasurers, but those hold donated KAS that was mined and already circulating — the same class as an exchange's custodial balance, not an overhang — and the identified treasurers' multisig read about **1,831 KAS** on **Aug 31 2026** on a chain read taken for this build. The keyless burn address, holding **11.23M KAS**, is watched the same way but is the opposite of an overhang: coins there can never move. If either balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The genuine forward supply on Kaspa is not a wallet at all — it is the **1,031.89M KAS** still unmined, and the chromatic curve alone decides when it arrives.

## How KAS compares to other hard-capped proof-of-work chains

The obvious structural analogue is Bitcoin, and the difference is entirely in the shape of the reduction. Bitcoin cuts its subsidy by half in one step every four years, so its inflation reading sits flat for years and then drops by half overnight; a Bitcoin holder's supply picture is a staircase. Kaspa cuts by **5.6%** every subsidy month, so the same total decay arrives as a ramp. The practical consequence for a framework like this one is that a 90-day Kaspa window always crosses two or three step-downs, which is why holding the current point rate flat would over-forecast the forward column by roughly nine percent, and why the forward reading **+0.59%** is meaningfully below the trailing **+0.69%** without anything having changed.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains the comparison is starker. A delegated proof-of-stake layer 1 that mints staking rewards into a reward pool has no ceiling and a rate set by governance, so its supply number is a policy variable that a vote can move. Kaspa has no on-chain governance and no vote portal, so its emission cannot be changed without a hard fork that every miner and node would have to adopt. Against exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks the comparison inverts: those coins fund a real buy side out of business revenue and can print a negative net, while Kaspa has no revenue and therefore cannot. And against privacy coins with tail emission, Kaspa is the stricter design — the chromatic curve runs to zero rather than settling at a permanent floor, which is a cleaner supply promise and an open question about how the chain pays for security once fees are all that remains.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Three dated events sit inside the forward window, and all three are the same event repeating. The Kaspa block subsidy steps down to **2.18** KAS a block on **Sep 4 2026**, to **2.06** on **Oct 5 2026**, and to **1.94** on **Nov 4 2026**. Beyond the curve, three watch lines matter. First, the Toccata hard fork that activated on **Jun 30 2026** brought covenants, transaction introspection and zero-knowledge verification to Kaspa layer 1 without touching the monetary code path — this build verified that by checking the live subsidy still sits exactly on the original curve — and any future fork should be re-checked the same way rather than assumed neutral. Second, tail emission and demurrage remain informal community discussion with no proposal and no schedule; if that ever becomes a real fork proposal it would be the first change to Sell #1 in Kaspa's history. Third, the keyless burn address is worth a periodic read: it is the only surface on this chain where a discretionary burn could ever appear.

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Kaspa at **+0.69% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.59%** forward, driven entirely by a proof-of-work block subsidy that issued **192.08M KAS** against a buy side of zero.
