Over the last 90 days Conflux minted **22.53M CFX** — mining **6.24M** and staking rewards **16.28M** — and a project wallet released a further **2.27M** before emptying itself, against a fee and storage burn of only **0.019M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CFX at **+0.47%** net for the window and **+0.43%** forward; our independent supply monitor reads **+0.84%**, a gap of **0.37 percentage points**. Conflux has **no maximum supply** — the mint is small, but nothing structural stops it, and the burn is far too thin to catch it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 28 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads Conflux at **+0.47%** net new supply — total sell pressure of **24.8M CFX** against buy pressure of **0.019M CFX**, divided by a circulating supply of **5,235M CFX**. Our independent supply monitor reads **+0.84%** for the same window, a gap of **0.37 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no data-conflict flag is raised on the CFX overview. The gap sits in the older end of the monitor's own derived series, which puts Conflux about 21.6M CFX lighter at the end of May 2026 than the chain itself does, while agreeing with the chain to four decimal places today. Forward the framework reads **+0.43%**, and the difference is entirely the project wallet: it emptied in June 2026 and cannot repeat. CFX in August 2026 is an **uncapped chain with a small, steady, twin-stream mint** — mild in rate, but with no ceiling and no meaningful offset.

## Sell pressure: where new CFX comes from

Conflux runs proof-of-work and proof-of-stake inside one protocol and pays both out of the same supply, so the Pressure Framework's Sell #1 protocol-inflation row carries two mints at once, worth **22.53M CFX** over the window. Mining is the smaller half: the block subsidy sat at **0.4013 CFX** per block at every reading across the quarter, and at roughly half a second per block Conflux produced **15,555,636** blocks in 90 days, minting **6.24M CFX**. Staking is the larger half at **16.28M CFX**, or **72%** of the mint — Conflux pays proof-of-stake rewards in newly created coins rather than from a pre-funded reserve, and the reward rate rises as the staked share of supply falls, so a shrinking stake makes the mint more expensive rather than less. The two figures were checked against the chain's own issued counter, which rose from **5,785,507,983 CFX** to **5,807,997,288 CFX** over the identical window; the two independent readings agree to **0.08%**.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and Conflux can prove it on chain rather than by press release: the network's own supply record reports both genesis lock buckets — the two-year tranche and the four-year tranche — at exactly zero CFX. The genesis release calendar finished in 2024 and there is no cliff, no queue and no locked tranche anywhere ahead of this window or the next one.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is the one row that moved this quarter, at **2.27M CFX**. The wallet that carried out the Conflux Foundation's announced **76,000,000 CFX** burn in 2025 held **2,268,657 CFX** on **May 30 2026** and **0.69 CFX** on **Aug 28 2026**. It released **300,000 CFX** on **Jun 2 2026** and **1,967,657 CFX** on **Jun 4 2026** into addresses that could not be tied back to the project, so the framework ships the conservative reading and books the outflow as released rather than as an internal move. Forward the row is **zero** — the wallet is bare, so it has nothing left to send. Sell #4 is **zero** too: there is no bankruptcy estate and no trustee distributing CFX on a schedule.

## Buy pressure: where new CFX goes

Buy #1, a programmatic buyback, is **zero**. Conflux has no buyback contract and routes no fee revenue into purchasing CFX. The nearest precedent runs the other way and is a destruction rather than a purchase: a community vote in 2025 burned **76,000,000 CFX** the Conflux Foundation already held, visible as an exact step in the burn address's balance. That was one firing, with no schedule behind it, so it projects nothing forward.

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the only live buy mechanism on Conflux, and it is starved: **18,531 CFX** over 90 days — a storage-point burn of **15,958 CFX** and a base-fee burn of **2,573 CFX**. That is **0.08%** of the 22.53M minted alongside it. This row is also where Conflux hides a trap worth naming: the burn never touches the burn address, which held **572,964,410.53 CFX** unchanged to the decimal at both ends of the window. A reading that watched the dead address alone would have reported no burn at all. Conflux destroys the coins inside its own supply counter instead, so both surfaces have to be read to see the mechanism. Buy #3, a Foundation buy, is **zero** — nothing is disclosed and no identifiable wallet has been accumulating.

Buy #4, a new long-term lock, is **zero**, and this is a deliberate call rather than an absence. Conflux staking grew substantially over the window — the proof-of-stake pool went from **851.7M CFX** to **892.8M CFX** — but unstaking clears in about two weeks, and the pool visibly reversed mid-quarter, falling to **747.1M CFX** in early July 2026 before recovering. A balance that can swing by more than a hundred million coins in five weeks is not a lock, and the circulating figure this page divides by already counts every staked coin. Booking it would have manufactured roughly **0.8%** of deflation that no CFX holder actually experienced.

## Foundation and overhang

Two team-controlled overhangs are tracked on Conflux. The first is the executing wallet already described: the address that sent the 2025 burn, refreshed from the chain every day, which now reads **0.69 CFX** against **2,268,657 CFX** three months ago. The second is the wider Conflux ecosystem-fund allocation, which is opaque — the Conflux Foundation has not published a wallet for it. What is public is a 2025 governance authorisation to place ecosystem-fund CFX into the digital-asset treasuries of publicly listed companies under a lock of no less than four years; re-checked on **Aug 28 2026**, that authorisation has still never executed, with no counterparty named and no wallet disclosed. It is walked by hand every fortnight. The burn address is deliberately excluded from the overhang list: its **572,964,410.53 CFX** is gone, not held. So is staked CFX, which belongs to its owners rather than to the Conflux Foundation. If either overhang's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — which is exactly what happened to the executing wallet this quarter.

## How CFX compares to other uncapped smart-contract Layer-1 chains

Conflux sits in the same structural family as the uncapped continuous-emission Layer-1 chains — the proof-of-stake networks that pay validators in new coins with no maximum supply written into the protocol. Against that class CFX is unusually mild in rate: an annualised mint near **1.7%** is at the low end, well under the staking-reward emissions many uncapped Layer-1 chains run. What it lacks is the offset. The uncapped chains that have turned net deflationary did it with a fee burn large enough to outrun issuance, and Conflux's burn removes eight hundredths of one percent of its own mint. Rate and offset are separate questions, and Conflux answers the first well and the second not at all.

Against hard-capped chains the difference is structural rather than numerical. A capped chain's issuance is a decaying schedule nobody can vote back up; Conflux's block subsidy and staking rate are both governance parameters, adjustable by on-chain vote without a hardfork. That cuts both ways — the same mechanism that could raise the mint has been used to cut it — but it means the forward number here is a policy setting, not a protocol guarantee. The one place Conflux is unambiguously ahead of most of its class is vesting: with both genesis lock buckets reading zero on chain, Conflux carries no unlock cliff at all, which is rarer than it sounds among Layer-1 chains of this age.

The comparison that matters most is with the hybrid consensus chains, because Conflux is nearly alone in paying proof-of-work miners and proof-of-stake validators from one supply. Most chains carry one reward stream; Conflux carries two, and the staking stream is nearly three times the mining stream. The practical consequence is that its inflation is driven by the staked share of supply rather than by hashrate, and the reward rate rises as staking participation falls — so a fall in staking would raise, not lower, the mint.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the on-chain parameter vote. Conflux adjusts its block subsidy, its staking interest rate and both burn ratios by community vote in fixed rounds, and every one of those values read identically at seven points across this window. A round that raises the block subsidy or the staking rate would move Sell #1 directly, and it is the single largest forward risk on this page. Second, the Conflux ecosystem-fund authorisation — the four-year listed-company treasury placement approved in 2025 and still unexecuted as of **Aug 28 2026**. Execution would surface a Sell #3 quantum and, if the four-year lock is real, a Buy #4 lock at the same time.

Third, the staked pool. It swung from **851.7M CFX** down to **747.1M CFX** and back to **892.8M CFX** inside this window; because the Conflux reward rate rises as the staked share falls, a sustained fall in staking would raise the mint rather than lower it. Fourth, the burn. Conflux's storage-point and base-fee burns scale with network traffic, and at current throughput they remove almost nothing; a genuine step-change in Conflux eSpace activity is the only path to the burn mattering. Fifth, watch the executing wallet that emptied in June 2026 — a refill would restore a Sell #3 that this build has projected forward at zero.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Conflux (CFX) at **+0.47%** net new supply over the 90 days to **Aug 28 2026** and **+0.43%** forward, against an independent monitor reading of **+0.84%**. The structural mechanism is a twin mint — **6.24M CFX** of proof-of-work block subsidy and **16.28M CFX** of proof-of-stake rewards, both newly created, out of an uncapped supply — with a fee and storage burn of **0.019M CFX** that is far too small to offset it. The key risk is governance: the block subsidy and the staking rate are on-chain parameters adjustable by vote, so Conflux's forward inflation is a policy setting rather than a protocol guarantee. There is no ceiling — Conflux has no maximum supply — but there is also no cliff: both genesis lock buckets read zero on chain, so nothing is queued to unlock, and the only wallet that released CFX this quarter has emptied itself.
