# CFX Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Conflux grew its CFX supply by **+0.43%** over the last 90 days and the Pressure Framework projects **+0.43%** for the next 90. Conflux is an uncapped Tree-Graph Layer-1 that pays miners and stakers out of the same new supply: **22.47M CFX** was minted over the window, **6.24M** to proof-of-work block rewards and **16.23M** to proof-of-stake rewards, against a combined storage-collateral and base-fee burn of only **16.2K CFX**. Genesis vesting for every CFX allocation ended in **2024**, so no unlock cliff exists at any future date. Our monitor reads **+0.78%** for the same window, a gap of **0.35 percentage points**, inside tolerance — no warning chip ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026** the Pressure Framework reads CFX at **+0.43%** net supply growth on a **5.23B** circulating float, and projects the same **+0.43%** forward because the Conflux emission mechanism did not change inside the window and nothing is scheduled to change it. Our monitor reads **+0.78%**, a gap of **0.35 percentage points**, which sits under the half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict chip is raised. The gap is entirely monitor-side resolution: the monitor infers supply from market cap divided by price on a **$219M** asset, and its 90-days-ago figure sits about **19M CFX** below what the Conflux chain itself recorded on that date, while its figure for today matches the chain to **0.04%**. The chain's own issued counter is the harder number and it is what the framework ships. CFX is best labelled a **fully unlocked, uncapped dual-consensus Layer-1 with a token burn too small to matter** — mildly inflationary, but with no cliff risk left anywhere in its schedule.

## Sell pressure: where new CFX comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the only live sell row on Conflux and it reads **22.47M CFX** for both the trailing and the forward window. Conflux has no maximum supply and runs proof-of-work and proof-of-stake inside one protocol, so it mints twice: a block subsidy to whoever mines each Tree-Graph block, and a staking yield to CFX locked in the PoS system. We measured both over the full 90 days rather than taking the documented rate. The Conflux chain produced **15,555,302** blocks in the window at a measured **0.4999** seconds each — a distinction that matters on a Tree-Graph, where blocks and epochs are different counters and the block subsidy is paid per block, not per epoch. Those blocks paid **6.24M CFX** to miners. Staking rewards paid **16.23M CFX** over the same period, which makes proof-of-stake roughly three quarters of all new CFX. We verified that the PoS reward is genuinely minted rather than paid from a pre-funded pool: it appears in the chain's own issued counter, which rose from **5,783.05M** to **5,805.49M** across the window.

The one recent change to Conflux monetary policy sits just outside this window. A DAO parameter vote halved the proof-of-work block reward from **0.8** to **0.4** CFX per block effective **Apr 7 2026**, five weeks before the window opened, so the entire quarter already runs at the reduced rate and there is no blended pre- and post-change figure hiding in the average. A fourteen-point walk of the supply counter across the window confirms it: issuance per day holds flat with no step.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** and permanently so. Conflux launched in **Oct 2020** with a 5B genesis supply split into an Ecosystem Fund (**40%**), Genesis Team (**36%**), Private Equity (**12%**), Community Fund (**8%**) and Foundation Holdings (**4%**), on two-year and four-year release schedules that all expired in 2024. We did not take that from a calendar — we read the release contracts, and both the two-year and four-year unlock balances return **zero**. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: no release was observed. Sell #4 is **zero** and permanent — there is no Conflux estate and no trustee.

## Buy pressure: where new CFX goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the only live counter-flow and it reads **16.2K CFX** in both columns. Conflux burns in two separate places, and reading only one of them returns almost nothing. A share of every transaction base fee is destroyed rather than paid to miners, which removed about **2.5K CFX** over the window; separately, a DAO-set proportion of the collateral posted when a contract reserves storage is burned and replaced with non-transferable storage points, which removed about **13.6K CFX**. Both mechanisms cut the supply counter directly rather than sending CFX to a dead address, which is why the Conflux burn address can sit completely frozen while a burn is running. Together they offset roughly **0.07%** of the mint. It is a real mechanism starved of the transaction volume it needs.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** — Conflux has no buyback contract and routes no fee revenue into CFX purchases, so there is no accumulation destination to track. The nearest precedent is a destruction rather than a purchase: a community vote in **May 2025** approved burning **76M CFX** the Foundation already held, and the Conflux burn address stepped up by exactly that amount and has not moved a single unit since. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**; the Conflux Foundation has never disclosed open-market accumulation. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** despite staked CFX rising about **58.4M** over the window, because Conflux unstaking clears in **14 days** and staked CFX is already counted inside the tradable float. Booking it would have manufactured over a percent of deflation that no CFX holder actually experienced.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs are watched on Conflux, and all three are opaque at the wallet level — the Conflux Foundation has never published an address for any of them, so they are tracked through disclosure rather than on-chain balance. The largest is the Ecosystem Fund, **40%** of the original genesis allocation, with no published release schedule; in **Sep 2025** the Foundation opened a governance vote authorising it to place CFX into publicly listed companies' digital-asset treasuries under a lock of at least four years, and re-checking on **Aug 18 2026** finds no execution, no disclosed counterparty and no wallet. The second is Foundation Holdings, **4%** of genesis plus any unsold private allocation, whose monthly release finished in 2024 and which is now purely discretionary. The third is the residual still classified outside the float, now only **32.6K CFX** after the classifier folded the previous 16.59M into circulating supply. The Conflux burn address is deliberately excluded — its **572,964,410.53 CFX** is destroyed, not held. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CFX compares to other Layer-1 chains

Conflux sits in an unusual structural slot: it is one of the very few Layer-1 chains that pays a proof-of-work block subsidy and a proof-of-stake yield out of the same uncapped supply. Against a hard-capped halving chain, CFX has no terminal supply and no automatic decay — its emission falls only when the Conflux DAO votes it down, as it did in **Apr 2026**, which makes monetary policy a governance risk rather than a protocol guarantee. Against Ethereum, the burn mechanism rhymes but the scale does not: Ethereum's base-fee burn regularly outruns its issuance, while Conflux's burn removes **0.07%** of what it mints because the chain's transaction volume is too thin to feed it.

Against most young uncapped Layer-1 chains, though, CFX is in the far better position on the axis that usually decides these pages. Those chains typically carry a small float and a large cliff calendar, and their emission line is a footnote next to the next unlock. Conflux has no cliff calendar at all: every genesis allocation finished vesting in **2024**, and the entire supply is unlocked. The whole CFX supply story is therefore one number, the **22.47M** quarterly mint, and that number is fully visible and fully measured. A dual-consensus chain with a bounded, governance-adjustable mint and no unlock overhang is a much simpler object to hold than one with a thin float and a cliff ahead.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Conflux v3.1.0 hardfork, which activates at epoch **155,140,000** around **Aug 25 2026**, with CIP-173 following at a PoS block height around **Aug 26 2026**. We read the proposals: they cover EVM compatibility, security patches and a PoS dispute-evidence and stake-locking fix, and none of them touches CFX issuance, block rewards or burning. Second, the next Conflux DAO parameter round. Rounds run 60 days and the last change to the block reward took effect **Apr 7 2026**, so the next opportunity to move the subsidy, the staking interest rate, the storage-point burn ratio or the base-fee share falls inside this window — it is the single mechanism that can change the sell row. Third, execution of the authorised listed-company treasury placement; the first confirmed deal would move both Sell #3 and Buy #4 off zero in the same quarter. Fourth, Conflux transaction volume, which is the only thing that can grow the burn on its own: the storage-point and base-fee burns scale directly with usage, and integrations such as the stablecoin rail added in **Jul 2026** are the route by which that could happen. Fifth, any repeat of the **May 2025** Foundation burn vote, which is the only precedent for CFX being destroyed at scale.

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CFX at **+0.43%** net supply growth for the last 90 days and projects **+0.43%** for the next.
