Over the 90 days to **Sep 5 2026** the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Rain at **+12.20% net** — sell pressure of **94,008M RAIN** against buy pressure of **7,458M RAIN** on a circulating base of **709,200M RAIN** — against our supply monitor's **+13.90%**, a gap of **1.69 percentage points**. Rain's Sablier vesting escrow on Arbitrum paid out **93,706M RAIN** in the window — **13%** of everything circulating — and every coin of it landed in four multisig wallets the Rain project signs for, where it still sits unsold.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the window **Jun 7 2026** to **Sep 5 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **RAIN at +12.20% net**. Our supply monitor reads **+13.90%** for the same window, a gap of **1.69 percentage points**, outside the half-point tolerance, so the monitor-gap flag ships on the overview card. That gap is not a disagreement about the flow — it is the base each side divides by. Take this build's own numerator and put it over the supply as it stood 90 days ago, the way the monitor measures, and it reads **+13.90%**, sitting **0.002** percentage points from the monitor. Put it over today's larger supply, as the framework does, and it reads **+12.20%**. Every number underneath both readings closes on chain: the Rain vesting escrow released **93,706,465,842 RAIN** in exactly thirteen transfers and the four receiving multisigs gained the same amount between them, with a residual of **0.01 RAIN** on ninety-three billion. RAIN is best labelled **a hard-capped token with no issuance at all, whose entire inflation is a vesting escrow draining into project custody**.

## Sell pressure: where new RAIN comes from

It does not come from issuance. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and it is measured rather than assumed. Rain minted its whole **1,150,000M RAIN** supply at launch, and the project's own regulated white paper records that Rain has no supply-adjustment protocol and no supply-adjustment mechanism. This build tested that rather than trusting it: sweeping every coin-creating event on the RAIN token across the window returns zero, and the mint-minus-burn identity closes against the measured total-supply change to the seventh decimal. The row is tagged as watched rather than closed for one reason — the token contract still has a live owner wallet, so supply finality is a policy, not yet a certainty.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **93,706M RAIN**, and it is the whole page. Rain's allocations vest through a Sablier lockup escrow on Arbitrum, and across the window that escrow fell from **518,444M RAIN** to **424,738M RAIN**. The framework books the realised outflow rather than the calendar entitlement, and the two differ here: the published schedule streamed **105,188M RAIN** over the same days, so **11,482M RAIN** vested on paper and was never withdrawn, sitting inside the escrow as an undrawn backlog now worth **24,836M RAIN**. What did leave went to four destinations and only four, and this is the finding the whole reading rests on. Every one of the thirteen withdrawal transfers went to a Rain multisig, and the sum of what those four multisigs gained is **93,706M RAIN** — the same number as the escrow lost, matched to a hundredth of a coin. So the unlocked RAIN has not been sold. It has changed custody: out of a vesting contract nobody can accelerate, into wallets where three signatures move the coins in one transaction. That crossing is what the Inflation metric exists to measure, and it is why this row carries the full quantum while the wallets themselves are tracked as overhang below.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **302M RAIN**, and it is the only RAIN that actually reached ordinary holders in the window. Exactly one Rain multisig distributes: it fell from **8,772M RAIN** to **8,470M RAIN** across **44,753** separate outgoing transfers to thousands of distinct addresses, an ecosystem and rewards distributor running continuously. Its inflow over the window was zero, its outflow was **301,823,335 RAIN**, and its measured balance change is the same figure with no residual at all. That cadence is steady enough to carry forward unchanged. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: there is no estate and no trustee attached to RAIN, and the one legacy claim on the token — the Credit Refund programme owed to early buyers — was bought out and destroyed rather than distributed, which puts it on the buy side of the ledger.

## Buy pressure: where new RAIN goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **38.6M RAIN**. Rain routes a slice of the trading volume of every prediction market on the protocol into buying RAIN and destroying it, and the destination is a true burn rather than a parking space: the protocol's own settlement contract executes a buyback-and-burn call, total supply falls by the same amount, and the unspendable burn address holds **415 RAIN**, so nothing accumulates anywhere to be resold. It ran on every single day of the window across **2,074** separate burns, which is about **429,000** coins a day, or **0.005%** of circulating supply over the whole quarter. Modest, but continuous and mechanical. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and that zero is bookkeeping rather than absence: the fee that funds the buyback is the same fee, so the burn is counted once, in Buy #1. Rain settles on Arbitrum and pays that network's gas in ETH, so there is no separate RAIN fee sink.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **7,419M RAIN** and it is the largest single event in the window. The Rain Foundation committed **$23,000,000** in stablecoins to buy out the remaining locked Credit Refund allocations at a fixed **$0.0031** per RAIN, and burned every coin it acquired. The chain shows one transfer of **7,419,354,838 RAIN** to the zero address on **Aug 25 2026**, sent from the Foundation multisig, and total supply steps down by the same amount — about **1%** of everything circulating, permanently gone. It was paid for in cash, the claim window is closed, and no second settlement has been announced, so it does not repeat and it carries **0** into the next 90 days. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**, and that is a measured zero too: the vesting escrow took in nothing at all over the window, only paid out, and Rain has no staking contract or vote-lock that removes RAIN from the tradable pool. Holding RAIN buys market access and a DAO vote, not a lockup.

## Foundation and overhang

Rain's overhang is the largest structural fact about the token, and it is fully enumerable because all of it sits in identified contracts. The Sablier vesting escrow still holds **424,738M RAIN** across six streams, and those six streams' remaining balances sum to exactly that figure, so nothing is hidden. Four of them are live: they end on **Feb 9 2027**, **Mar 9 2027** and **Sep 9 2027**, and together they are due to release **70,778M RAIN** over the next 90 days. A fifth stream completed on **Mar 9 2026**. The sixth is the one to diarise: **230,000M RAIN** to a recipient that has received nothing yet, which does not begin streaming until **Mar 9 2027** and therefore contributes nothing to this window.

Downstream of the escrow sit the Rain multisigs themselves, and none of them has sold. The largest holds **164,824M RAIN** and gained **37,151M** in the window; the next holds **138,903M** and gained **28,780M**; a third holds **68,135M** and gained **15,961M**; a fourth holds **49,442M** and gained **11,814M**. A fifth has been static at **100,470M RAIN** since its stream completed. The sixth, the distributor, is the only one that pays out, and it is down to **8,470M**. Together with the escrow that is **954,981M** of the **1,142,409M RAIN** that exists — **83.6%** of the token in project custody. Every one of these balances is read from the chain on a 24-hour cadence, and the standing rule is the plain one: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How RAIN compares to other hard-capped app tokens

RAIN belongs to the class of hard-capped application tokens that were minted in full at genesis and distributed on a vesting calendar — not to the class of continuously-issuing Layer 1s. That distinction matters more than it sounds. A proof-of-work chain with a halving schedule or an uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1 creates genuinely new supply every block, and its inflation number is an issuance number that no one can switch off. Rain creates nothing. Its total supply has fallen every month this year, and its regulated filing records no supply-adjustment mechanism at all. On the narrow question of issuance, RAIN is stricter than Bitcoin, because Bitcoin still pays a block subsidy and Rain pays nothing.

The pressure comes from the other end of the pipe. Where an exchange token with a quarterly buyback removes supply against a fixed float, and where a Layer 1 with a fee burn offsets its own issuance, RAIN's whole dynamic is a release schedule converting locked supply into liquid supply. That puts it alongside other recent app-layer launches with heavy insider allocations and multi-year vesting: the interesting number is never the cap, it is the percentage of the cap that is still locked and the date it stops being locked. RAIN's buy side is real but small in that comparison — a continuous fee-funded burn worth **0.005%** of circulating supply a quarter cannot offset a vesting stream worth **13%** of it. The **$23,000,000** DAO buyout that burned **7,419M RAIN** was an order of magnitude larger than the programmatic burn, and it was funded from the treasury rather than from revenue, which is the tell: it is a balance-sheet action, not a cash-flow mechanism, and balance-sheet actions do not recur on their own.

One structural feature genuinely distinguishes RAIN from its peers, and it cuts both ways. Because the unlocked supply moved into multisigs rather than to exchanges, the sell pressure is visible before it is felt. On a token whose unlock goes straight to thousands of individual wallets, the framework can only measure the release after the fact. Here the coins have a known address, a known signer threshold and a known history of never having sold — which means every future refresh either confirms the hold or catches the distribution on the day it starts.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the four Rain multisigs holding **421,304M RAIN** between them. None has ever sent RAIN out. The first outgoing transfer from any of them is the single most important event this token can produce, and it would land in Sell #3 at the next refresh. Second, the vesting escrow's undrawn backlog of **24,836M RAIN**, which has already vested on paper and can be withdrawn on any day without warning — the gap between what streams and what is actually drawn has been widening, and a catch-up withdrawal would show up as a step rather than a trickle. Third, **Feb 9 2027**, when the first of the live vesting streams completes and the forward release rate steps down again, after the fastest stream already finished on **Aug 9 2026**. Fourth, **Mar 9 2027**, when the dormant **230,000M RAIN** stream begins — a fifth of the entire supply starting to flow to a wallet that holds none of it today. Fifth, the DAO itself: the **$23,000,000** settlement was Rain's first executed governance action, and whether a second buy-and-burn resolution follows is the only realistic path to the buy side growing enough to matter.

## Summary

Rain (RAIN) is a hard-capped prediction-market token on Arbitrum that cannot issue new supply and has destroyed **7,458M RAIN** in the last 90 days, yet reads **+12.20%** inflationary on the MrNasdog Pressure Framework, against our monitor's **+13.90%**. The reason is entirely release rather than issuance: a Sablier vesting escrow paid out **93,706M RAIN** in the window, all of it into four multisig wallets the project signs for, which have never sold a coin and now hold **421,304M RAIN** between them. The key risk is therefore not dilution but distribution — **83.6%** of RAIN sits in project custody, another **70,778M** is scheduled out of the escrow over the next 90 days, and a dormant **230,000M RAIN** stream begins in **March 2027**. The ceiling is real and fixed at **1,150,000M RAIN**, and the buy side — a continuous fee-funded burn plus a one-off **$23,000,000** DAO buyout — is too small to offset the release schedule at its current pace.
