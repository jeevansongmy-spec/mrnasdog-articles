World Liberty Financial creates no new WLFI at all — not one coin was minted in the last 90 days, and the count of WLFI in existence fell — and the Pressure Framework still reads WLFI at **+4.69%** over the trailing 90 days and **+2.32%** over the next 90. All of it comes from one place: two project-controlled safes paid **1,487.5M WLFI** into the market between **Jun 11 2026** and **Sep 9 2026** and took nothing back. Sell pressure is **1,490.7M WLFI**, buy pressure is **0**, and **46.1B WLFI** sits in a lock contract that opens nothing until **May 6 2028**.


## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **31,777.1M WLFI**, the framework books **1,490.7M WLFI** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+4.69%** — and projects **+2.32%** for the next 90 on the recurring legs alone. The inflation monitor reads **+0.0094%** for the same window, a gap of **4.68 percentage points**, far outside the framework's half-point tolerance, so the page ships with a monitor-gap warning. That gap has a single, testable cause. World Liberty Financial publishes its own circulating-supply figure, and the figure moves if and only if the genesis multisig at **0x5be9a4959308a0d0c7bc0870e319314d8d957dbb** releases coins: it rose **1,117M** in the ten days after that multisig shed **1,100M** on **Mar 24 2026**, and **2,970M** in the ten days after it shed **3,000M** in early **Apr 2026**. Since **Apr 10 2026** that multisig has not moved a single coin, and the published float has not moved either — through five months in which the lock contract grew from **17.0B** to **46.1B**, the coin count fell by **3.22B**, and the project's downstream safes paid out billions. The label for WLFI is **a hard-capped token whose entire inflation is discretionary treasury deployment**: nothing is issued, and supply still reaches the market every month.


## Sell pressure: where new WLFI comes from

It does not come from issuance. World Liberty Financial has no block reward, no staking emission and no issuance schedule of any kind; WLFI is a governance asset on Ethereum with a fixed ceiling of **100,000M**. Every transfer into and out of the zero address was enumerated across the window twice, on independent passes, and the two agreed exactly: **zero** mints, one burn. The identity closes — mints minus burns came to **-20,000,000.000000 WLFI** against a measured change in the coin count of **-20,000,000.000000 WLFI**, a residual of zero. So **Sell #1, protocol inflation, is 0**. It is tagged as watched rather than closed, because the token address is an upgradeable proxy and the owner retains a live path to move coins between accounts; "WLFI cannot be minted" is a reading to repeat at every rebuild, not a permanent property.

**Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is 3.2M WLFI**, and the calendar would have said zero. The governance restructure that passed on **May 6 2026** put a 24-month cliff on every locked cohort — **17,043.7M WLFI** of early-supporter allocation and **45,238.6M WLFI** of founder, team and adviser allocation — with nothing releasing before **May 6 2028**. The unlock templates were read straight off the lock contract this session and confirm it: the retail track releases 20% at **Sep 1 2025** and the remaining 80% in a straight line from **May 6 2028** to **May 6 2030**, while the insider track releases nothing until **May 6 2028** and then runs to **May 6 2031**. What still moves is the backlog from that 2025 step. The contract's own claimed counter ran from **4,255.7M** to **4,259.0M WLFI** across the window — **3.2M WLFI** that stepped out of a contract nobody can sell from and into wallets that can. Scheduled release was **0**; realised release was **3.2M**; the framework books the realised figure.

**Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is 1,487.5M WLFI**, and it is the whole page. The multisig that received all **100,000M WLFI** at genesis has since paid out **95,204M** to 48 addresses, and every one of those 48 was read at both ends of this window. Exactly two moved. A distribution safe at **0xfef30c262676de9af5e5e9ba999cf774000b14b4** fell from **8,609.8M** to **7,622.3M WLFI** — **987.5M** out across nine destinations, with zero inbound — of which roughly **507M** landed on one major exchange hot wallet, **250M** went into the cross-chain lock pool on **Aug 6 2026**, and the rest went to smaller exchange and payout wallets. It fires monthly and it has done so all year, shedding **839.3M** in the preceding quarter. A second project safe at **0x9e8f9a6ea64cbba627749de83c32d2096ba0c787** sent its final **500.0M WLFI** into third-party custody on **Aug 31 2026**. **Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is 0**: World Liberty Financial is an operating business with no insolvency proceeding attached to WLFI and no trustee distributing it.


## Buy pressure: where new WLFI goes

Nowhere, this window. **Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is 0**, and it is the most important zero on the page. World Liberty Financial holders approved a buyback-and-burn in **Sep 2025** with roughly **99.8%** support, routing all of the fee income from project-owned liquidity into open-market WLFI purchases that are then destroyed. It bought nothing here. The only supply-reducing event on chain in these 90 days was a locked-allocation burn that has nothing to do with the open market, and neither the genesis multisig nor either downstream safe took in a single coin. A governance thread opened in **Mar 2026**, asking the team to honour the commitment, still carries no answer. An approved programme that has not executed moves no row.

**Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is 0**. WLFI is not a gas token and the network it lives on charges fees in something else, so no transaction destroys WLFI. Both surfaces a burn could appear on were read at both window ends rather than either alone: the unspendable address held **0.47 WLFI** on both dates, unchanged, while the coin count moved exactly once. Those two surfaces are independent here — the lock contract's burn call lowers the coin count without ever crediting the unspendable address, and a plain transfer to that address would do the reverse — so the disagreement between them is the finding, and it says the burn was not a fee burn. **Buy #3, foundation buy, is 0**: the genesis multisig held **12,620,954,712.41 WLFI** at both ends of the window, to the decimal, and every transfer the two safes made was outbound. There is no accumulation address to track because nothing is being accumulated.

**Buy #4, new long-term lock, is 0**, and this is a judgement worth stating out loud. Holders kept signing into the lock contract through the window and **193.5M WLFI** was newly committed to it, taking the total held there to **50,361.1M**. None of it counts, because the code will not let a holder who has not signed transfer at all — those coins could not be sold before they were locked and cannot be sold now, so locking them removed nothing from the market. The same test disposes of the one real burn. On **Sep 7 2026** a wallet signed into the new schedule, **20,000,000 WLFI** of its **200,000,000** allocation was destroyed under the 10% insider rule, **180,000,000** stayed locked, and the coin count fell by exactly that amount. Those coins sat inside the lock contract and nobody could sell them, so destroying them took nothing off the market either; it removes future supply, not present supply. It ships as a tracked row at **0**, and roughly **4,524M WLFI** more can be burned the same way as the remaining insiders sign.


## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on WLFI is enormous and it is fully enumerated. The largest item is the lock contract itself at **46,102.1M WLFI** — **47.7%** of every WLFI in existence — of which roughly **5,813M** is already unlocked and merely unclaimed, drawable by its holders on any day they choose, and the remainder is held behind the **May 6 2028** cliff. The second item is the genesis multisig at **12,621.0M WLFI**, unmoved through this window and the four months before it, and the only wallet whose releases the published float actually registers. Third is the distribution safe at **7,622.3M WLFI**, which is not an overhang in waiting so much as an overhang in motion — it has paid out every month of 2026 and holds enough to keep doing so for roughly two more years at the current pace. Fourth is a dormant allocation contract holding **3,583.6M WLFI** that has not moved at either end. Added up, **69%** of all WLFI sits in a contract or a project safe rather than in a public holder's wallet.

Two things are deliberately excluded. The cross-chain lock pool holding **1,422.8M WLFI** is backing, not overhang: it is matched by **637.8M** on BNB Chain and **785.0M** on Solana, and the identity closes to a residual of **3,371 WLFI**, so Ethereum's coin count is the global coin count and the mirrors are not additive. Exchange custodial balances are excluded too, because they belong to depositors. The trigger sentence applies to all four tracked items: if the lock contract, the genesis multisig, the distribution safe or the dormant allocation contract falls between refreshes by more than the schedule accounts for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.


## How WLFI compares to other hard-capped governance tokens

WLFI belongs to a class that looks reassuring on the surface and rarely is: fixed-ceiling tokens with no issuance at all. On the pure issuance axis WLFI is stricter than any halving-model chain. A halving chain still mints on every block, just at a decaying rate, so its inflation reading is positive but shrinking on a published clock. World Liberty Financial mints nothing whatsoever — the reading is a flat zero, not a small number — and the coin count has only ever fallen, from **100,000M** at genesis to **96,722.7M** today. Judged on issuance alone, WLFI would score at the top of the framework.

It reads **+4.69%** instead, and that is the lesson. A hard cap constrains total supply; it says nothing about tradable float, and the two move independently. WLFI is much closer in shape to a recently-launched token working through an investor and team vest than to a mature capped chain — except that the release is not on a calendar. A four-year vest has dated cliffs a holder can plan around; WLFI's pressure comes from a multisig deciding, month by month, how much to send to an exchange. That is the same category of risk as an unscheduled foundation reserve, and it is why the framework treats capacity and cadence as different things.

The other useful comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns. Those offset issuance with a demand-linked removal that scales with usage, and their inflation readings can go genuinely negative. World Liberty Financial has the same mechanism on paper — approved by a near-unanimous holder vote a year ago, funded from real fee income — and it produced exactly **0** across these 90 days. For the buyback to matter to this reading it would need to absorb something on the order of **1,490M WLFI** a quarter, which is more than **4%** of the float. That is the distance between a mechanism existing and a mechanism mattering.


## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the distribution safe at **0xfef30c262676de9af5e5e9ba999cf774000b14b4**: it fires monthly and its largest recurring leg stepped down from **170.0M** on **Aug 5 2026** to **150.0M** on **Sep 2 2026**, so whether the next firing holds at **150.0M** or reverts is worth about **60M WLFI** on the forward reading. Second, the genesis multisig — it is the one wallet whose movement the published float registers, it has been still since **Apr 10 2026**, and any release from it would show up in both readings at once and close this page's gap by itself. Third, the buyback: the coin count is read at every rebuild, so the first open-market burn would appear immediately, and it is the only event that can flip this page's sign. Fourth, the unclaimed backlog of roughly **5,813M WLFI** sitting unlocked inside the lock contract, which is drawable at any moment and is currently trickling out at about **3.2M** a quarter. Fifth, the insider signing rate — every founder or team wallet that signs burns 10% of its allocation, and up to **4,524M WLFI** remains to be destroyed that way.


## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads WLFI at **+4.69%** over the trailing 90 days and **+2.32%** projected forward: supply growing, projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is not inflation but deployment — World Liberty Financial minted nothing, burned nothing on the open market, and holds a fixed ceiling of **100,000M WLFI**, while two project-controlled safes paid **1,487.5M WLFI** into the market in 90 days on no published schedule. The key risk is precisely that there is no schedule: a vesting cliff can be planned around, a multisig cannot, and the distribution safe still holds **7,622.3M WLFI**. The ceiling is the one genuine comfort, and it is a narrow one — the coins are capped, but **46,102.1M** of them are queued behind a **May 6 2028** cliff that will open onto a market this one is still being fed into.
