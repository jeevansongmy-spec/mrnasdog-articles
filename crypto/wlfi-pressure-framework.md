# WLFI Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

WLFI supply is roughly steady: the circulating count rose about **0.02%** in the 90 days to Oct 24 2026, and we expect about the same over the next 90 days. The only new supply was **4.83M WLFI** claimed from the World Liberty Financial unlock contract by late sale buyers, against **31.78B WLFI** in circulation, with **0** bought back or burned from the float. The ceiling is the **46.11B WLFI** still held in that contract, and none of it is due before a cliff on **May 6 2028**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 26 to Oct 24 2026, WLFI sell pressure was **4.83M WLFI** and buy pressure was **0**, for a net change of **+0.02%** of the **31.78B** circulating supply. The next 90 days project the same **+0.02%**. Market-data trackers show the circulating count up **+0.03%** over the same window, a difference of about **0.02 points**, so no warning flag is needed and the two readings agree. In one line: WLFI is a locked-supply token sitting in a long quiet stretch before its 2028 cliff.

## Sell pressure: where new WLFI comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. WLFI pays no staking reward or block reward, and the current version of the WLFI token contract on Ethereum has no mint function at all. The contract can be upgraded by its owner, so we treat this as a checked fact rather than a promise that can never change.

Vesting unlocks are the one live row: **4.83M WLFI** in 90 days. When WLFI began trading on Sep 1 2025, sale buyers got 20% of their tokens and the rest stayed locked. Buyers who switch on their wallets late can still claim that 20% from the unlock contract, and a steady trickle of them does, about 1.2M to 1.8M WLFI a month. Holders voted on May 6 2026 to put the other 80% on a schedule: early supporters wait for a two-year cliff and then unlock over two years, while founders, team, advisers and partners who opted in burned 10% and then wait for a two-year cliff and a three-year release. Both schedules start on **May 6 2028**, so nothing scheduled lands in the next 90 days.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are **0**. The locked World Liberty Financial treasury wallet holds **12.62B WLFI** and has not sent a single token since Apr 7 2026. An operations wallet paid out **952.66M WLFI** in the window, in roughly monthly lots to partners and reward programs, but that wallet was part of the circulating count from launch day, so its payouts move coins within the market rather than adding new ones. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is also **0**: there is no bankruptcy estate, and the frozen wallet of a large early investor is a court matter, not new supply.

## Buy pressure: where new WLFI goes

The programmatic buyback is **0** in this window. WLFI holders voted in September 2025 to use the fees from the project's own liquidity pools to buy back and burn WLFI, and early buybacks did burn tokens, but no buyback burn showed up on Ethereum, BNB Chain or Solana between Jul 26 and Oct 24 2026. The last reported purchase was in November 2025, and there is no public schedule for the next one.

The protocol fee burn is **0**: WLFI has no fee burn built in. The total supply did fall by **20M WLFI** on Sep 7 2026, when an insider moved 200M WLFI into the 2028 unlock plan and the 10% entry burn destroyed 20M. Those coins were locked and never counted as circulating, so the burn removes nothing from the float. The total WLFI supply now stands at **96.72B**, down from the original 100B after this and earlier burns.

A foundation buy is **0**, and a new long-term lock is **0**. WLFI staking opened on Oct 1 2026 with a 180-day lock and rewards paid in the USD1 stablecoin, and it already holds **76.38M WLFI**. Staked WLFI still counts as circulating, so the lock does not shrink the count, even though it keeps those coins off exchanges for six months.

## Foundation and overhang

The biggest WLFI overhang is the unlock contract itself, holding **46.11B WLFI**, close to half of all WLFI in existence. It contains about 17B WLFI of early-supporter tokens and the founders' and team's opted-in stakes, including the Trump-linked founder stake reported at about 14.18B WLFI. We read its balance and its claim counter on-chain at every refresh. Next comes the locked treasury wallet at **12.62B WLFI**, which has no published release schedule; when it last sent tokens out, in March and April 2026, about 4.1B WLFI joined the circulating count within days. Around **5.9B WLFI** more sits in sale-buyer wallets that were never switched on; those tokens can only leave through the unlock contract, 20% at a time.

Inside the float, we also track the operations wallet (**7.36B WLFI** left), two smaller payout wallets, and the wallets of the listed company that holds about 6.9B WLFI as its treasury. These do not add new supply when they move, but they show where selling could come from. If the unlock contract or the treasury wallet's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How WLFI compares to other DeFi governance tokens

Most DeFi governance tokens unlock on a fixed monthly calendar: a team and investor tranche lands every 30 days for two to four years after launch, and the circulating count climbs a few percent each quarter. WLFI took the opposite path. After the 20% launch release, the rest was frozen with no date at all, and the May 2026 vote replaced that open-ended lock with one long cliff. The result is near-zero new supply today and a single large wall of releases from May 2028 onward.

WLFI also differs from fee-burn tokens. Chains with a built-in fee burn destroy coins with every transaction, and exchange tokens often burn on a fixed quarterly timetable. WLFI's burn depends on governance choices: a buyback program fed by the project's own pool fees, plus one-off insider burns when stakes enter the 2028 plan. Neither touched the float in this window.

Finally, WLFI has no mint, unlike staking-reward tokens whose supply grows every epoch. Its risk is not steady dilution but concentration: the unlock contract and the treasury hold more than 58B WLFI, almost twice the circulating supply, and a few decisions by the project decide when that supply moves.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the unlock contract's claim counter: the trickle of late claims has run at 1.2M to 1.8M WLFI a month, and a jump would mean many locked sale wallets switching on at once. Second, the locked treasury wallet holding 12.62B WLFI: any transfer out of it has, in the past, joined the circulating count within days. Third, any restart of the buyback-and-burn program, which would be the first buy-side flow since November 2025. Fourth, the staking program that began on Oct 1 2026, where a steady rise in locked WLFI would keep more coins off exchanges. Fifth, the court cases, including the lawsuit filed on Oct 22 2026 over the project's foreign deals and the dispute over a large early investor's frozen tokens; neither changes the count directly, but a ruling could free or move large balances.

## Summary

WLFI supply is roughly steady, rising about **0.02%** in the 90 days to Oct 24 2026, with the same pace expected next. The token has no mint, no fee burn and no buyback burn in the window; the only new coins were **4.83M WLFI** of late sale-buyer claims. The key risk is the **46.11B WLFI** in the unlock contract and the **12.62B WLFI** locked treasury, which together outweigh the whole circulating supply. The cliff on May 6 2028 caps what can reach the market before then, unless the treasury wallet moves first.
