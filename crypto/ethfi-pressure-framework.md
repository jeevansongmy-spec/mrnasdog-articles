## The verdict, in one paragraph

From Aug 26 to Nov 24 2026 no new ETHFI entered the count and none left it, so net supply change was **0.00%**, and the next 90 days look the same at **0.00%**. Other trackers read **−1.93%** over the same stretch, a gap of **1.93 points**. That gap comes from their supply figure, which stood at 1,018.21M ETHFI on Aug 26 2026, about 19.7M more than all the ETHFI that exists, then fell to 965.35M on Aug 28 and rose to 998.54M on Oct 24 2026. None of those steps was a coin being made or destroyed, so the coin page carries a warning note. The plain label for ETHFI today: a fixed-supply token whose team coins and buybacks only shuffle coins that are already counted.

## Sell pressure: where new ETHFI comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**: the ETHFI token minted 1B coins once at launch in March 2024 and holds no function to mint again, and the total stood at 998,535,999 ETHFI on both Aug 26 and Nov 24 2026.

Vesting unlocks book **zero**, but they are still running. Core contributors hold 21.47% of the supply on a three-year schedule, which works out to about **8.95M ETHFI** on the 15th of each month until early 2027. Three of those releases fell between Aug 26 and Nov 24 2026, and three more are due on Dec 15 2026, Jan 15 2027 and Feb 15 2027. Investor coins finished unlocking in March 2026. Because every ETHFI is already counted as circulating, these monthly releases move coins the count already includes, so they add nothing to it.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks also book **zero**. On Oct 4 2026 the DAO treasury moved **20M ETHFI** into a rewards reserve, as the Sep 3 2026 vote allowed, and that reserve has since paid 3.5M to a reward wallet and 5M to an operating wallet. Those coins were already counted, so the moves add no new ETHFI.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **zero**: no court or failed firm holds ETHFI for a later payout, and no long lock-up is set to open.

## Buy pressure: where new ETHFI goes

The programmatic buyback is real but books **zero**. Since Sep 8 2026 a Foundation wallet has bought ETHFI every week with protocol revenue from the card, swaps and staking, about **3.14M ETHFI** so far. It passed nearly all of that on as cashback and rewards and holds about 230K now. The bought coins stay in circulation and none are burned, so the buying takes nothing out of the count.

The protocol fee burn is **zero**: ETHFI has no burn built in, and the total did not fall between Aug 26 and Nov 24 2026.

The Foundation buy row is **zero**. A plan approved in November 2025 lets the Foundation buy ETHFI while the price sits under $3, but anything it buys lands in its own wallets, which already count as circulating.

New long-term locks are **zero**. About **109.04M ETHFI** is staked, down from 110.74M on Aug 26 2026, and staked ETHFI can be withdrawn and still counts as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

The DAO treasury wallet is the largest holder at **153.2M ETHFI**, down from 173.2M on Aug 26 2026 after the Oct 4 2026 move. The rewards reserve built from that move holds **11.5M ETHFI**. A second treasury wallet holds **12.88M**, the operating wallet **2.85M**, the reward wallet **3.5M**, and the buyback wallet about **230K**. The core-contributor coins still to come, about 35.8M across the four releases from Dec 15 2026 to early 2027, are not held in any on-chain vesting contract we could find.

We read these wallets again at every rebuild. If the treasury or the reserve sends coins to exchanges in size, that is selling the market will feel, even though the circulating count stays the same, and it will show up in the next rebuild's notes.

## How ETHFI compares to other DeFi revenue tokens

ETHFI sits with the fixed-supply DeFi tokens that turn protocol revenue into buying. Where some of those tokens send their bought coins to a burn address or a locked fund, ether.fi hands most of its bought ETHFI straight back to users as cashback. That makes the ether.fi buyback closer to a reward program paid for with bought coins than to a cut in supply.

Against tokens that burn, such as Ethereum with its fee burn or BNB with its quarterly burn, ETHFI removes nothing: the only burns happened before this window, about 1.46M ETHFI in total. Against tokens still deep in vesting, ETHFI is near the end, with investor coins done and the last core releases due by early 2027.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The core-contributor releases on Dec 15 2026, Jan 15 2027 and Feb 15 2027, about 8.95M ETHFI each, show how much team supply reaches exchanges, even though none of it changes the count.

Other trackers' 90-day figure for ETHFI will likely swing to about +3.4% around Nov 26 2026 and ease back after Jan 22 2027, because of the Aug 28 and Oct 24 2026 recounts, not because of new coins.

The weekly buyback size against card spending: the Sep 3 2026 vote set buying from protocol revenue, and a larger share kept in the Foundation treasury rather than paid out would make the buying matter more.

The rewards reserve: 11.5M ETHFI of the 20M moved on Oct 4 2026 is left. If it runs low, the Foundation tops it up from the treasury, which is more coins heading to users who may sell.

## Summary

ETHFI supply was flat at 0.00% from Aug 26 to Nov 24 2026 and is set to stay flat for the next 90 days, because the token cannot create new coins and every one of its 998.54M coins already counts as circulating. Core contributors still receive about 8.95M ETHFI a month until early 2027, and the DAO treasury holds 153.2M ETHFI, so selling by insiders remains the main risk even though it never changes the count. A weekly buyback bought about 3.14M ETHFI since Sep 8 2026, but nearly all of it went back to users as cashback, so it does not shrink supply. The hard ceiling is 1B ETHFI, and with no burn in place the supply can only stay where it is.
