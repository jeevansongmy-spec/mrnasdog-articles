# NEXO Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

NEXO did not inflate at all in the last 90 days: the supply moved **0.00%**, and the next 90 days should read the same. All **1B** NEXO were created in April 2018 and the token has no way to make more, so new selling can only come from coins Nexo already holds. Between Aug 17 and Nov 15 2026 new supply was **0 NEXO**, burns were **0 NEXO**, and other trackers show **+0.02%**, which is the same answer.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Aug 17 to Nov 15 2026, NEXO's supply changed by **0.00%**, and we expect **0.00%** again for the 90 days to Feb 13 2027. Other trackers put the same window at **+0.02%**, a gap of **0.02 points** that comes from small swings in how market data counts the supply, not from coins. That is small enough that the coin page carries no warning note. On-chain, the token reported exactly 1,000,000,000 NEXO at both ends of the window. NEXO is a fixed-supply company token: nothing is printed, nothing is burned, and the only open question is what Nexo does with the large share it still holds.

## Sell pressure: where new NEXO comes from

The first sell row, new coins from the protocol, is **0**. NEXO is a token on Ethereum, not a chain, so there are no validators or miners to pay. The token's code sets the total once, at launch, and has no function that can add to it; the supply read the same 1B NEXO on Aug 17 and Nov 15 2026.

Vesting unlocks are also **0**. The token was born with its own release timers: the team's **112.5M** share opened in 16 steps every three months from April 2018, the advisers' **52.5M** share over a year, and the community's **60M** share over eighteen months. The last of those timers ran out in 2022, so there is no cliff in this window or the next one.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are **0** as well. Nexo still has **353.85M NEXO** sitting in the four original release slots inside the token, fully open and movable whenever the company chooses. None of it moved in the 90 days, and every one of those coins already counts as circulating, so even a full withdrawal would not raise the count.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**. No court case, no failed lender and no seized stash is waiting to release NEXO.

## Buy pressure: where new NEXO goes

The buyback row is **0** for this window. Nexo ran three buybacks: a **$12M** round in December 2020, a **$100M** round in November 2021 and a further **$50M** on Aug 30 2022. The coins went into a buyback reserve wallet that now holds **114.80M NEXO** and did not move between Aug 17 and Nov 15 2026. They were kept, not destroyed, so they still count as circulating, and no new round has been announced since 2022.

The fee-burn row is **0**. The token has no burn function, and the common burn address held the same **10.71 NEXO** at the start and the end of the window while the total stayed at 1B.

A foundation buy is **0**. Nexo's token page says part of the company's profit goes to buybacks and payouts to holders, but it gives no amount and no date, so nothing can be booked for the next 90 days.

New long-term locks are **0**. Clients who keep NEXO on the platform to reach a better loyalty tier still hold circulating coins, so that holding takes nothing out of the count.

## Foundation and overhang

Nexo controls most of the supply. The four release slots in the token hold **208.33M** NEXO first set aside for credit lines, **98.44M** for the team, **33.33M** for the community and **13.75M** for advisers, **353.85M** in all. The company's treasury wallet holds **213.23M**, the buyback reserve **114.80M**, and the wallet that owns the token contract **86.15M**. All of these balances were flat across the 90 days.

Two busy platform wallets did move, which is normal for a company paying clients and handling deposits: one rose from about **57.72M** to **60.70M** NEXO, the other fell from about **11.35M** to **6.68M**. Those coins were already circulating, so the moves add nothing. We read every one of these wallets again at each rebuild; if the release slots, the treasury, the reserve or the owner wallet ever falls between checks, the outflow goes into the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next check.

## How NEXO compares to other exchange and lending tokens

NEXO belongs to the family of tokens issued by a crypto company rather than earned by securing a chain. Within that family the usual split is between tokens whose company burns what it buys back and tokens whose company keeps it. BNB is the best-known burner: a share of supply is destroyed every quarter, so its count falls over time. NEXO sits on the other side. Its buybacks went into a wallet that still holds the coins, so the 1B total has never shrunk.

Compared with chain coins such as Ethereum or Solana, which pay validators in new coins every day, NEXO has no built-in inflation at all. That makes its supply line as flat as Bitcoin's will eventually be, but the reason is different: Bitcoin is capped by a public rule, while NEXO is capped by a token that simply cannot mint. The open risk is not printing but selling from company wallets that already exist.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the **353.85M NEXO** in the release slots: any withdrawal is the clearest sign that Nexo is putting company coins to work, and we check it again at every rebuild before Feb 13 2027.

Second, a new buyback. Nexo's token page still promises buybacks out of profit; a dated round, and especially one that burns coins, would be the first real buy pressure since Aug 30 2022.

Third, the company's next quarterly figures after the Q3 2026 update, due in early 2027, which show how big the lending book is that pays NEXO rewards to clients.

Fourth, the Kraken listing that went live on Oct 22 2026 and Nexo's push back into the United States: more places to trade bring more buyers and sellers, but they do not change the count.
