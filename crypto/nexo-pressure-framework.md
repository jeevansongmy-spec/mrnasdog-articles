**TL;DR.** Nexo's NEXO token is one of the few assets where the Pressure Framework reads a clean **0.00%** in both directions. The NEXO contract on Ethereum has no mint function and no burn function in its published code, so the **1,000,000,000 NEXO** issued in 2018 is fixed in both directions; on-chain supply read exactly that figure at both ends of the 90-day window. The Nexo buyback — the mechanism most readers assume makes NEXO deflationary — did not fire this window, and structurally could not have shrunk the counted float even if it had, because repurchased NEXO is parked in an Investor Protection Reserve that the circulating figure already counts. Framework net **0.00%**, supply monitor **+0.04%**, a gap of **0.04 percentage points**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 14 2026**, the framework reads **NEXO at 0.00% net**. Sell pressure totals **zero**, buy pressure totals **zero**, against a circulating base of **1,000,000,000 NEXO** — which is also the total supply and the maximum supply. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.04%**, a gap of **0.04 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's half-a-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships on the NEXO overview page; that residual is rounding noise around a constant on-chain supply, not a disagreement about any event. Forward, the framework also reads **0.00%**, because nothing in the Nexo token design can change the number: issuance is impossible by code, vesting finished years ago, and the one discretionary lever parks tokens inside the float rather than removing them. NEXO is best characterised as **structurally flat — a hard-fixed supply with a float-neutral buyback**. Flat is not the same as deflationary, and the distinction is the whole story of this page.

## Sell pressure: where new NEXO comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** in the strongest form the framework recognises. Nexo is a company, not a blockchain: there is no consensus mechanism, no block reward, no validator subsidy and no staking emission attached to the NEXO token. The token itself is a plain Ethereum ERC-20 whose verified source code was read directly this session, and it contains no mint function, no internal mint helper, no minter role and no owner able to add one. The on-chain supply read **1,000,000,000 NEXO** at the block that opened this window on **May 15 2026** and the identical figure at the latest block on **Aug 14 2026** — unchanged to eighteen decimal places. The NEXO representations on Polygon, Fantom, Energi and Sora are bridged claims on that same fixed Ethereum supply, not independent mints, so no bridge adds a coin either.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the schedule is spent. The full billion NEXO was issued at the 2018 token sale, and unlock trackers now list NEXO at **100%** unlocked with circulating supply equal to maximum supply. There is no escrow contract holding a locked tranche, no cliff inside the next 90 days, and — because the contract cannot mint — no mechanism by which a new locked bucket could ever be created. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** as well: Nexo is an operating going concern with no estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered creditor distribution of NEXO. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero**, but it is the only row where something is genuinely being watched, and it gets its own section below.

## Buy pressure: where new NEXO goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the row that decides how NEXO gets scored, and it lands at **zero** for two separate reasons. The first is simple: it did not fire. Nexo repurchases NEXO on the open market with platform revenue and sends the tokens to a published on-chain Investor Protection Reserve, and that reserve held **114,800,950.27 NEXO** at the block opening this window and the same **114,800,950.27 NEXO** at the latest block — identical to the seventh decimal place. Sampling further back, the balance is unchanged at points in **Feb 2026** and **Aug 2025** too. The last documented tranche closed on **Mar 2 2023**, when the **$50M** programme finished buying **63,244,559.958 NEXO** at a weighted average of **$0.7906**. A claim of a fresh **$50M** approval circulated in an aggregator's generated summary, but no Nexo announcement supports it and the reserve shows no matching inflow.

The second reason is structural, and it survives even if the buyback restarts tomorrow. Repurchased NEXO is not burned. Under the published buyback terms each batch vests in the Investor Protection Reserve for **12 months** and is then recycled into NEXO-denominated daily interest payouts to Nexo clients and into strategic token mergers. The tokens come back. More decisively, the circulating figure this framework divides by is **1,000,000,000 NEXO** — equal to total supply — which means the reserve's **114.8M NEXO** is already counted as circulating. Buying coins off an exchange and into that reserve changes custody, not the counted float. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** and permanently so: the NEXO contract has no burn function at all, so no fee stream can be routed to destruction. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — no company entity disclosed a dated, sized open-market NEXO purchase inside the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**: a token-weighted governance staking module that locks NEXO for voting was reported live earlier in 2026 and would genuinely remove coins from order books, but no contract address and no locked quantum have been published, so there is nothing measurable to book.

## Foundation and overhang

Two team-controlled overhangs are tracked for NEXO, and neither moved. The first is the Investor Protection Reserve itself, the buyback accumulation destination, holding **114,800,950.27 NEXO** — about **11.5%** of the entire supply — at a published Ethereum address, refreshed by chain read at every rebuild. It is unscheduled: there is no calendar for when vested NEXO leaves it, only the stated intent to spend it on interest payouts and token mergers, and it has released nothing for over a year. The second is an opaque company bucket of roughly **354M NEXO**, implied by the divergence between the classifier this framework uses, which counts the full **1B** as circulating, and a rival classifier that counts about **646M**. It has no single published address and is refreshed by a bi-weekly walk of company disclosures. Both pools already sit inside the **1B** denominator, which is exactly why the buyback reads float-neutral — but they remain the only realistic source of a future NEXO sell row. If either balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How NEXO compares to other exchange and lending-platform tokens

The exchange-token class is defined by one mechanism: a company earns fees and spends part of them on its own token. What separates the members is what happens to the token afterwards, and that is where NEXO sits at the weak end. The strongest structures in this class run a quarterly burn — the company buys tokens and sends them to an address nobody can spend from, so total supply falls permanently and every remaining holder's share rises. Nexo does not do this. NEXO has no burn function in its contract, so the option is not merely unused, it is unavailable. The repurchase parks tokens in a reserve, vests them for a year, and then spends them on client interest and acquisitions, which returns them to circulation. Mechanically that is closer to a corporate share repurchase held as treasury stock than to a cancellation.

Against the other structural comparison — uncapped continuous-emission chains — NEXO looks excellent, and this is the fair credit it deserves. An emission-based Layer 1 pays validators in new units every block, so its holders face a permanent headwind that only stops if governance changes the curve. NEXO has no such headwind: the code cannot create a coin, the vesting calendar is finished, and there is no unlock cliff anywhere ahead. The honest summary is that NEXO removes downside without adding upside. It is a fixed supply, not a shrinking one, and the framework's inflation band treats capped-but-flat exactly that way — a fixed supply tops out in the middle of the scale, because the top of the scale is reserved for supply that is actively being destroyed. Readers who assume the Nexo buyback earns NEXO a deflationary score are applying a burn model to a mechanism that is not a burn.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Investor Protection Reserve balance: any increase would confirm the buyback has restarted, and any decrease would be the first real NEXO sell row this ledger has carried, since vested tokens leaving that reserve go to client interest payouts and reach the market. Second, whether Nexo publishes a governance-staking contract address and a locked amount — that would turn the current unmeasurable Buy #4 into a real, readable lock. Third, an official buyback announcement from Nexo itself, which is the only thing that would validate the unconfirmed new-programme claim now circulating. Fourth, the exchange listing-roadmap addition and the US relaunch, which are demand events rather than supply events but change who holds the float and how quickly the company bucket could be distributed. Fifth, any change in how the tradable float is classified: the roughly **354M NEXO** difference between the two published circulating figures is the single largest source of measurement risk on this page, and a reclassification would move the denominator without a single coin changing hands.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Nexo's NEXO token at **0.00%** net supply change over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** projected forward, against a supply monitor reading **+0.04%** — a **0.04 percentage point** gap that is rounding noise, not conflict. The structural mechanism is a fully issued, permanently fixed supply of **1,000,000,000 NEXO** on an Ethereum contract with neither a mint nor a burn function, which removes issuance risk entirely and also removes any path to deflation. The key risk is not inflation but custody and classification: **114.8M NEXO** sits in the Investor Protection Reserve and roughly **354M** more in an opaque company bucket, all of it already inside the counted float, so it can reach the market without the supply number ever changing. The ceiling is hard at **1B NEXO** and cannot rise; the buyback moves coins between pockets rather than destroying them, so on this metric NEXO is flat by design.
