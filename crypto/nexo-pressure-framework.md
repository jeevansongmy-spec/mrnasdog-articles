## Sell pressure: where new NEXO comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. NEXO is a token on Ethereum, not its own chain, so there are no validators or miners to pay. The token was written in 2018 with its 1B total set once at launch, and it has no function that can create more, so this row stays at zero for good.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. At launch the token split 1B NEXO into five batches: 525M for early investors with no lock, 250M as a loan reserve, 112.5M for the founders and team, 60M for community work and 52.5M for advisors. The team batch had the longest timer, 16 steps of three months each, and it ran out in 2022. No locked NEXO is left to release.

Company and unscheduled releases are **0**. Nexo can still pull **353.85M NEXO** out of the old release slots inside the token, but those balances did not change by a single coin between Aug 27 and Nov 25 2026, and every NEXO already counts as circulating, so a withdrawal would add nothing new to the count.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**. No court, trustee or failed firm is holding NEXO waiting to be paid out.

The fee burn is **0**. NEXO has no burn at all, the usual burn address held the same **10.71 NEXO** at both ends of the window, and the total never dropped below 1B.

Company buying is **0**: no purchase of NEXO by Nexo or a related firm was announced in the window. New long-term locks are also **0**, because NEXO that clients park on the platform to earn interest stays in their accounts and still counts as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

Nexo controls several large NEXO balances. The four old release slots inside the token hold **353.85M NEXO**: 208.33M from the loan reserve, 98.44M from the team batch, 33.33M from the community batch and 13.75M from the advisor batch. The wallet that owns the token contract holds **86.15M NEXO**, the buyback reserve holds **114.80M NEXO**, and the corporate treasury holds **213.23M NEXO**. Every one of these balances was the same on Nov 25 2026 as on Aug 27 2026.

Because all 1B NEXO already count as circulating, these wallets are not a hidden unlock waiting to land. They do matter for selling, though: together they are more than three quarters of all NEXO, so the market depends on Nexo choosing not to sell. We re-read each balance at every rebuild, and if one of them falls between checks, the coins that left will show up in the company-release row at the next rebuild.

## How NEXO compares to other exchange and platform tokens

Most platform tokens tie their supply to the business. BNB and the Bitfinex token LEO burn coins out of company income on a set rhythm, so their supply shrinks a little every quarter or every day. KCS and OKB have used regular buyback-and-burn rounds as well. NEXO takes a different path: its buybacks are kept in a reserve rather than destroyed, so the total never falls, and there has been no new round since 2022.

NEXO also differs from chain tokens such as ETH or SOL, which pay validators in new coins and grow their supply every day. NEXO pays holders through the Nexo app instead, in interest and cashback that the company funds from coins it already holds, so those payouts move NEXO between wallets without making any. That makes NEXO one of the flattest supplies we track: no printing, no burn, and a fixed 1B ceiling.

The trade-off is control. On a burn token, a holder can count the coins leaving. On NEXO, the big number to watch is how much the company still holds and whether it keeps holding it.
