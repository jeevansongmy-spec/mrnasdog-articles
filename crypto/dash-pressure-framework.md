## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 5 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **DASH at +0.80% net** realised over the last 90 days and **+0.75%** forward. Sell pressure is about **101.8K DASH** realised, dropping to about **95.3K DASH** on the forward view; buy pressure is **zero** in all four rows, against a circulating base of about **12.79M DASH**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.25%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.45 percentage points** — under the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip is attached to the DASH overview. The two figures differ only because the classified circulating series the monitor divides by reads a touch more growth than the chain's own mined-supply delta — a denominator wobble, not a real emission difference. Both agree on the shape: Dash is **mildly inflationary and disinflating on a fixed schedule** — a mined coin with one supply force and no offsets.

## Sell pressure: where new DASH comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the entire sell ledger — about **101.8K DASH** realised over the last 90 days and about **95.3K DASH** projected over the next 90. Dash is a proof-of-work coin using the X11 hashing algorithm, launched in **January 2014**, and mining the block reward is the only mechanism in the design that creates new DASH. Each block subsidy is split three ways: 80% is paid to miners and masternodes every block, and the remaining 20% is reserved for the on-chain treasury and minted only in a monthly superblock, up to the value of proposals the masternode network has approved. Any treasury budget with no approved proposal behind it is simply never minted, which makes the treasury a mild brake on issuance rather than an accelerant.

Read live from the chain, the coinbase generation is **1.77 DASH** per block — the 80% miner-and-masternode share — at roughly **548 blocks a day**. The always-minted portion works out to about **87.3K DASH** over 90 days, and the funded treasury superblocks add roughly **14K** more, which is how the realised on-chain supply grew about **101.8K DASH** over the last window. The forward figure is lower because Dash reduces its subsidy by one-fourteenth — about **7.14%** — every 210,240 blocks, or roughly once a year, and the twelfth such reduction lands inside the forward window on **Aug 16 2026** at block **2,522,880**. The framework re-bases the next-90-day number to the post-cut run rate rather than holding the pre-cut rate flat, which is why it books about **95.3K** instead of another 101.8K.

Every other sell row is zero, and each is zero for a structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, cannot exist: Dash was mined into existence with no ICO and no investor allocation, so there is no vesting contract anywhere in the design and no cliff can ever arrive. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because there is no premine reserve, no foundation escrow and no held treasury pool; the DAO treasury carries no standing balance, since it is funded per block and minted on demand only up to approved proposals. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero because no bankruptcy estate, court distribution or escrow applies to DASH.

## Buy pressure: where new DASH goes

The buy ledger is empty in all four rows, and that is the most important structural fact about DASH inflation. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** because Dash takes no protocol revenue beyond the block reward, and that reward is paid out to miners, masternodes and proposal owners rather than used to repurchase DASH. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** because Dash has no EIP-1559-style burn; transaction fees are paid to the block, never destroyed, so the protocol removes no DASH from supply. The unfunded slice of the treasury budget is never minted, but that is supply that is not created, not existing supply that is burned, so it lowers Sell #1 rather than adding to the buy side.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** because Dash Core Group is funded by the treasury in DASH and spends it, rather than accumulating DASH from the open market. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** in net terms: running a masternode locks **1,000 DASH** of collateral, and roughly **3.8M DASH** — about 30% of supply — sits in that collateral today across roughly 3,800 nodes, but the masternode count is broadly stable, so the net new lock over the window is about nothing. The practical consequence is that DASH has no mechanism capable of turning net-deflationary. Its inflation can only fall toward zero as the annual reductions compound; it cannot go negative while the protocol stands as written.

## Foundation and overhang

There is no team-controlled overhang for DASH to track. Dash had no premine held as a reserve, no ICO and no investor allocation, and the treasury holds no standing pool of coins — it is funded 20% per block and minted each month only up to approved proposals, so there is never a large custodial balance waiting to hit the market. That removes every sub-category the framework normally enumerates: no unscheduled unlock pool, no Labs treasury, no buyback accumulation wallet, no DAO treasury balance and no bankruptcy residual. The one large concentration of DASH is masternode collateral — about **3.8M DASH** across roughly 3,800 nodes — but that collateral sits inside circulating supply as a voluntary float sink, not a protocol lock, and it can be withdrawn at any time. Because there is no identified wallet to watch, the trigger sentence attaches to the emission itself: if the chain's live coinbase generation ever departs from the reduction schedule between refreshes, that departure enters Sell #1 at the next refresh.

## How DASH compares to other hard-capped proof-of-work chains

DASH belongs to the class of **hard-capped proof-of-work coins with a reducing subsidy** — the same structural family as Bitcoin and Litecoin, and the opposite of an uncapped continuous-emission proof-of-stake L1. The distinguishing feature is the shape of the reduction. Bitcoin and Litecoin halve their subsidy 50% in a single block every four years, producing a step function the market braces for; Dash cuts by only about **7.14%** but does it every year, so the emission curve descends in gentler, more frequent steps. The trade-off is that Dash carves a fixed **20%** of every block into a self-funding treasury, which Bitcoin and Litecoin do not have — that is the mechanism that pays Dash Core Group and proposal owners without a corporate balance sheet, and it is why part of the sell pressure here is discretionary on the network's own votes rather than purely mechanical.

Against uncapped staking chains, the contrast is sharper. A Cosmos-style L1 mints new supply forever to pay stakers, at a rate a governance vote can raise; DASH has a hard **18.92M** ceiling and a reduction schedule that only ever cuts issuance, and about **12.79M** is already mined. Its closest structural cousin is Decred, another proof-of-work chain that splits the block reward between miners, stakers and a treasury — but Decred's subsidy declines continuously rather than in annual steps. Against exchange tokens with quarterly buyback-and-burn programmes, DASH is the mirror image: those coins run net-deflationary because a revenue stream funds the removal, while Dash has no revenue stream to repurchase with and so no removal is possible. Against privacy coins with a permanent tail emission such as Monero, Dash is stricter — the ceiling here is a hard cap, not a perpetual minimum subsidy. For an inflation lens, DASH reads as a coin whose supply story is predictable and monotonically improving, but which will sit slightly above zero for as long as there is a subsidy left to pay.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the twelfth annual block-reward reduction on **Aug 16 2026** at block **2,522,880**, which cuts the subsidy about 7.14% and is the only scheduled supply event on the calendar — the coinbase generation should read about **1.64 DASH** per block afterward, and any other value would mean the emission code changed. Second, the monthly treasury superblocks: how much of the 20% budget is actually approved and minted swings the realised issuance by a few thousand DASH each cycle, so a run of heavily funded months would push Sell #1 up and a run of lean months would push it down. Third, the masternode count, since a sharp fall would release collateral back into the tradable float and a sharp rise would absorb it — the framework books net-zero today because the count is stable. Fourth, any governance proposal that alters the reward split or the treasury share, which would change the mix even though it cannot change the hard cap. Fifth, the spread between the chain's mined total and the widely-published circulating figure, which is the small residual behind this window's monitor gap.

## Summary

The Pressure Framework reads DASH at about **+0.80%** net realised over the last 90 days and **+0.75%** forward, from roughly **101.8K DASH** of proof-of-work and masternode emission — dropping to about **95.3K** next window — and nothing at all on the buy side. The structural mechanism is Dash's reducing subsidy, which cuts the block reward about **7.14%** every year and splits each block 80% to miners and masternodes and 20% to an on-chain treasury minted only against approved proposals. The key risk is not runaway dilution but the reverse over the long run: with a hard **18.92M** cap and no fee burn to replace the subsidy, Dash's security and treasury budget will eventually have to lean on transaction fees rather than issuance. Circulating is about **12.79M** against that ceiling, the schedule is fixed, and there is no mechanism anywhere in the protocol that can push supply the other way.
