# ASTER Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Aster is a perpetuals and spot exchange on BNB Chain whose ASTER token has a fixed supply of **8,000,000,000** and no mint function at all, and over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** the Pressure Framework reads **100.87M ASTER** of new sell pressure against **zero** buy pressure, for a net of **+3.75%** on a circulating base of **2.69B ASTER**. That zero is the whole story: Aster runs one of the most aggressive buyback-and-burn programmes in the sector — **99%** of daily platform fees buy ASTER, with a matching burn aimed at cutting total supply from 8B to **3B** — and neither leg removes a single tradable token, because the buyback pays its purchases straight back to veASTER stakers and the burn eats a locked team reserve that was never in the float. Our supply monitor reads **+4.29%**, a gap of **0.54 percentage points**, so a monitor-gap flag ships on this build. ASTER is **structurally inflationary on the active float while being genuinely deflationary on the fully-diluted ceiling**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 19 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ASTER at +3.75% net**: sell pressure of **100.87M ASTER** from one airdrop stage claim and the weekly staking emission, against buy pressure of **0**, on a circulating base of **2,692,846,388 ASTER**. The next 90 days read **+2.15%**, because the large stage claim is behind Aster now and what replaces it is smaller. Our supply monitor reads **+4.29%** for the same window, a gap of **0.54 percentage points** — just over the half-point tolerance, so the **monitor gap** flag is raised rather than suppressed. About **0.18 points** of that gap is arithmetic convention alone, since the monitor divides its supply change by the base from 90 days ago while the framework divides by today's; the remainder tracks the buyback round-trip, which a pure supply count books as new tokens even though those tokens were bought on the open market days earlier. The right label for ASTER is **a fixed-supply exchange token whose famous buyback is a reward-routing mechanism, not a float sink**.

## Sell pressure: where new ASTER comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **5.79M ASTER** over 90 days, and it is the only continuous source of new ASTER on the page. Since the emission overhaul of **Mar 30 2026**, the ecosystem allocation no longer unlocks on a linear monthly schedule — the old model released **78.4M ASTER** a month — and instead pays out only as staking rewards on Aster Chain. Each weekly epoch releases **450,000 ASTER**, split between a **150,000** base pool and a **300,000** loyalty pool, which over **12.857** epochs in a quarter gives **5,785,714 ASTER**. Because that change landed seven weeks before this window opened, the trailing figure and the forward figure are identical; there is no blended rate to unwind.

Sell #5, airdrop stage claims, is **95.09M ASTER** and dominates the quarter. The Aster airdrop is not a drip — it opens in numbered stages, each with a 30-day claim window, and tokens nobody claims are redirected rather than paid out. The Stage 5 vested tranche of **95,088,035 ASTER** opened on **Jun 9 2026** and ran to **Jul 9 2026**, and it is visible three independent ways: in the published tranche figure, in dated reporting of the opening, and in the supply feed itself, which stepped up by roughly **96.6M** on **Jun 10 2026** and then went quiet again. This is deliberately booked as its own row rather than folded into vesting, because an airdrop stage claim and a team vesting cliff are unrelated mechanisms.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** for the trailing window and **20.00M** for the next one. Aster's team allocation is **400,000,000 ASTER**, or **5%** of max supply, behind a 12-month cliff from the **Sep 17 2025** token generation event, and it has never released a token: the wallet holding it read exactly **400,000,000.00** at the start of this window. The cliff opens on **Sep 17 2026** and then pays about **10M ASTER** a month for 40 months, which puts two tranches inside the next 90 days. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: the **560,000,000 ASTER** foundation treasury is documented as fully locked until a governance-approved use, and the future-stage airdrop bucket of roughly **2,777.5M** carries no calendar at all — capacity without cadence is not sell pressure. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**, because ASTER has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new ASTER goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**, and this is the call the whole ASTER page turns on. Since **Jun 17 2026**, Aster has routed **99%** of daily platform fees into buying ASTER on the open market through a time-weighted average price mechanism. The purchases are real and they are large: roughly **11.09M ASTER** passed through the buyback wallet in this window. But that wallet is a conduit, not a vault — it read **0.00 ASTER** when the window opened and **25.45** at the end, because every token it buys is paid out to veASTER stakers the same epoch as a claimable loyalty reward, stacked on top of the **300,000** base pool. A token bought off the float and handed straight back to holders removes nothing from the float. Reading the balance alone would have been the trap here; the flow had to be sized separately, and it was, using the burn as its one-to-one counterpart.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is also **zero**, for a different reason. The burn is genuine and it is measurable: the dead address grew from **177,781,179** to **188,867,292 ASTER** across this window in four exact fortnightly settlements on **Jun 30 2026**, **Jul 14 2026**, **Jul 28 2026** and **Aug 11 2026**, totalling **11,086,113 ASTER**. The ASTER contract has no burn function, so total supply is a constant **8,000,000,000** and the published total is simply that number minus the dead-address balance, which is exactly **7,811,132,707**. What settles the row is the burn's source rather than its destination: all four settlements left the wallet holding precisely the **400,000,000** team allocation, and its entire decline over the window is those four burns. Destroying a token that was locked behind a cliff and never tradable removes zero tradable supply. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — no open-market purchase by the foundation has been disclosed or observed. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, because veASTER locking happens on Aster Chain against bridged units and cannot be read as a reduction in the BNB Chain float.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled overhangs are tracked for ASTER, and together they are why a fixed **8B** supply is not the same thing as a safe one. The largest is the future-stage airdrop bucket, roughly **2,777.5M ASTER** — about **34.7%** of max supply — with no published release calendar whatsoever; it moves only when Aster announces a new stage with a dated claim window, so it is walked by hand rather than by chain read. Second is the foundation treasury at **560,000,000 ASTER**, locked until governance releases it, with no wallet published and therefore no on-chain balance to watch. Third is the team reserve at **388,913,891 ASTER**, which is the one overhang currently shrinking: the fortnightly burn is eating it from the far end at roughly **2.8M** a settlement, even as its **Sep 17 2026** cliff prepares to release **10M** a month from the other. Fourth is the buyback wallet itself, which is structurally empty by design and holds **25.45 ASTER**. One address that a public explorer tags as an Aster treasury is deliberately excluded: Aster's own contract listing labels it the deposit bridge, so the **1.33B ASTER** sitting there is trader collateral belonging to depositors, not a reserve the team can deploy. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ASTER compares to other exchange tokens

The natural comparison set for ASTER is exchange tokens that route revenue back into their own supply, and the mechanism split inside that group is sharper than the marketing suggests. The classic model burns what it buys: the exchange repurchases tokens with a share of fees and sends them to an unspendable address, so every unit of revenue permanently shrinks the tradable float, and the framework reads the full quantum as buy pressure. Aster does something structurally different. It buys with a far higher share of fees than most — **99%**, against the **10–20%** typical of fee-share designs — but it then distributes those purchases to stakers as rewards, which recycles them into the float instead of retiring them. On a supply ledger that is a round-trip worth zero, however large the headline number.

The burn leg diverges from the standard model in the same way. A conventional exchange-token burn destroys circulating supply and is visible as a falling circulating figure. Aster's burn destroys reserve supply — the locked team allocation — so the effect appears in the fully-diluted ceiling rather than the float. That is not worthless: at roughly **5.5M ASTER** a month it is a real, permanent reduction in the overhang, and the **8B** to **3B** target, if it is ever reached, would remove more supply than most exchange tokens ever issued. But it is a promise about dilution years out, not about the float today, and the framework prices the float. The comparison that matters most is with tokens whose vesting is still ahead of them rather than behind: ASTER has released only about a third of max supply, its team cliff has not fired even once, and an airdrop bucket larger than the entire circulating supply has no calendar at all. Against a mature exchange token with expired vesting and a burn that strikes the float, ASTER carries structurally more forward supply risk, not less.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The first date is **Aug 25 2026**, the next fortnightly reserve burn, which should retire roughly **2.8M ASTER** from the team allocation and confirm whether the buyback run-rate is holding as trading volume moves. The date that changes the ledger is **Sep 17 2026**, when the team cliff opens for the first time in Aster's history and begins releasing about **10M ASTER** a month — worth watching not just for the release but for where those tokens go, since a transfer toward an exchange and a transfer to the burn address look identical in size and mean opposite things. The second tranche follows around **Oct 17 2026**. Then comes **Nov 4 2026**, when the Stage 6 deferred claim window opens for holders who chose to wait rather than take half in May, worth up to **32M ASTER** and running to **Dec 4 2026**. Finally, watch for any announcement of a new airdrop stage: the future-stage bucket is the single largest unscheduled overhang on this page, and one dated announcement would move ASTER's forward reading more than every other item combined.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Aster's ASTER at **+3.75%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** and **+2.15%** over the next 90, against a monitor reading of **+4.29%** and a flagged gap of **0.54 percentage points**. The structural mechanism is a fixed **8,000,000,000** supply with no mint path, released through weekly staking epochs and lumpy airdrop stage claims, set against a buyback-and-burn programme that removes **zero** tradable ASTER because its purchases are recycled to stakers and its burns are drawn from a locked reserve. The key risk is that only about a third of max supply has been released: the team cliff opens **Sep 17 2026** having never fired, and an unscheduled airdrop bucket of roughly **2,777.5M ASTER** sits above the market with no calendar. The ceiling is real and it is falling — **8B** toward a stated **3B**, at about **5.5M** a month — but on the float that a holder actually competes with, ASTER supply is still going up.
