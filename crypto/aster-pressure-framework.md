Aster runs one of the most aggressive buyback-and-burn programmes in crypto. **99%** of daily platform fees buy ASTER on the open market, and an equal amount is burned — a design the project markets as a **198%** effect. The Pressure Framework books that entire programme at **0**. Not because the flows are fake, but because the bought ASTER is paid straight back out to stakers as claimable rewards, and the matching burn comes out of a locked reserve. Neither side removes a single tradable token. Against that, staking emission plus a **Sep 17 2026** team cliff plus a **Nov 4 2026** airdrop stage claim put **57.79M ASTER** into the next 90 days — a net **+2.15%** of circulating supply, easing from **+3.75%** over the last 90. Our supply monitor reads **+4.23%** for the trailing window, a gap of just **0.48 percentage points**, so the two readings agree.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the Pressure Framework books **100.88M ASTER** of new float against **0** removed, a net **+3.75%** of the **2,690.60M** circulating supply. Looking forward that eases to **+2.15%**, because the airdrop stage that dominated the trailing window is not repeated, though the team cliff arrives to partly replace it. Our supply monitor reports **+4.23%** for the trailing period, a gap of only **0.48 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no warning flag is carried. The chain corroborates the mechanism: the ASTER contract on BNB Chain returns a total supply of exactly **8,000,000,000** at both ends of the window, and the wallet holding the team allocation returned exactly **400,000,000 ASTER** right up until its first reserve burn on **Jun 29 2026**. Nothing was minted and no insider supply moved. ASTER is best characterised as a hard-capped exchange token whose buyback recycles the float rather than shrinking it.

## Sell pressure: where new ASTER comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **5.79M ASTER** over 90 days and it is the only tap Aster still leaves open. The entire **8,000,000,000 ASTER** supply was minted once at the **Sep 17 2025** token generation event and the contract has no mint path, so this is not issuance in the monetary sense — it is release. Since the emission overhaul announced on **Mar 30 2026**, ecosystem tokens reach circulation only as staking rewards, at **450,000 ASTER** per weekly epoch: a **150,000** base-yield pool plus a **300,000** base loyalty pool. The overhaul cut the previous **78.4M**-per-month ecosystem unlock by roughly **97%**, and because it landed six weeks before this window opened, the trailing and forward columns run at the same rate.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** for the last 90 days and **20.00M ASTER** for the next 90, and that flip is the single most important thing on this page. Aster's team allocation is **5%** of supply, **400,000,000 ASTER**, held behind a twelve-month cliff from the **Sep 17 2025** launch. We read the wallet directly rather than trusting the calendar: it held exactly **400,000,000 ASTER** on **May 13 2026** and again on **Jun 17 2026**, and its only outflows since have been burns. Insider supply has genuinely been frozen. On **Sep 17 2026** that cliff opens and the allocation begins vesting at **10,000,000 ASTER** a month for forty months. Two of those monthly tranches fall inside the forward window, on **Sep 17 2026** and **Oct 17 2026**, and they are the first insider supply ASTER has ever had.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**, and unusually the evidence points the opposite way: Aster's treasury contract grew from **1,138.02M ASTER** on **May 13 2026** to **1,325.19M** today, absorbing about **187.17M** — mostly unclaimed airdrop tranches swept back in after the Stage 6 deadline, with a single week taking in **113.93M**. A wallet that accumulates is not a seller. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: there is no bankruptcy estate and no trustee distribution attached to ASTER.

Sell #5 is the coin-specific line — airdrop stage claims — and at **95.09M ASTER** trailing and **32.00M** forward it is the largest single force on this page. Aster's airdrop does not drip; it opens in discrete dated stages, which makes it the lumpiest supply source here. The trailing window carried the Stage 5 vested tranche of **95,088,035.78 ASTER**, which opened a thirty-day claim window on **Jun 9 2026** and closed on **Jul 9 2026** — a single event worth roughly **1.19%** of total supply. Stage 6 allocated **64,000,000 ASTER** and gave recipients a choice between half now and all of it later, with the deferred half becoming claimable on **Nov 4 2026**, four days before the forward window closes, so it books in full. Stage 6's immediate half ran **May 4 2026** to **Jun 4 2026** and is deliberately left unbooked, because the sweep destination shows it was materially under-claimed: the treasury absorbed **113.93M ASTER** in the week after that deadline. Booking the gross entitlement would have invented sell pressure that did not occur — and that deliberate omission is where the **0.48 percentage point** residual against our monitor comes from, explained rather than plugged.

## Buy pressure: where new ASTER goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**, and this is the call that defines the page. Since **Jun 17 2026** Aster has directed **99%** of daily platform fees into open-market ASTER buying, spread out across the day. That is real, and it is large. But the destination decides the ledger: **100%** of the repurchased ASTER is paid out to veASTER stakers as Loyalty Rewards, stacked on top of the **300,000**-per-epoch base pool and distributed by lock weight as claimable tokens. Bought off the float and handed back to holders, they never leave the float. The chain agrees — the buyback execution wallet held **0.00 ASTER** at both ends of the window and **25.45** today, the signature of a pass-through that accumulates nothing. This is genuine demand and it matters for price. It is not supply removal, and Metric 1 measures supply.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is also **0**, for a different and equally specific reason. The burns are real and we timed every one of them: four bi-weekly events inside the window, on **Jun 29 2026** (**2,937,125.53 ASTER**), **Jul 13 2026** (**3,083,815.69**), **Jul 27 2026** (**2,213,513.91**) and **Aug 10 2026** (**2,851,653.28**), totalling **11,086,113.20 ASTER**. Every one of them originated from the wallet holding the locked team allocation — the same address that read exactly **400,000,000 ASTER** before the programme began, and that has fallen only by these burns. Aster's own wording says as much: an equal amount is burned from reserve, team allocation first. Destroying a token that was never on the market removes nothing from the market. What the burn genuinely achieves is a falling ceiling, walking total supply from **8,000,000,000** toward the stated **3,000,000,000** target, so we track it as a shrinking overhang rather than as buy pressure it is not.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** — no discretionary treasury purchasing exists separate from the fee-funded programme. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: veASTER locking runs up to **208 weeks** but it is voluntary, uncapped and unannounced in size, and rewards are not force-relocked when paid, so there is no dated quantum to book.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs are tracked on ASTER. The first is the team allocation wallet at **388.91M ASTER** today, down from exactly **400,000,000**, locked until the **Sep 17 2026** cliff and shrinking by roughly **2.8M** every two weeks as the reserve burn consumes it from the far end — a rare case where a burn and a vesting schedule compete for the same tokens. The second is the treasury at **1,325.19M ASTER**, currently a net absorber. The third is the still-locked airdrop pool of roughly **2.9B ASTER**, released only through dated stage openings. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 — and for the team wallet, the distinction that matters is whether a decline is another burn to the dead address or a transfer toward an exchange, because only the second is sell pressure.

## How ASTER compares to other exchange tokens

The useful comparison class is not layer-one chains but exchange and perpetual-DEX tokens that recycle fee revenue, and within it there are three mechanically distinct designs. The first is buy-and-burn from circulating supply: a large centralised-exchange token repurchases on the open market and sends the coins to a dead address, permanently retiring float, and the framework books the full quantum as buy pressure. The second is buy-and-hold, where a protocol accumulates repurchased tokens in a fund it controls — that also removes float, though it creates a future overhang.

Aster is the third design: buy-and-redistribute. The fee revenue is real, the buying is real, and the burn is real, but they are pointed at two different pools — the purchase returns to circulation as staker rewards, the burn is drawn from a reserve that was never circulating. The result is a token whose ceiling falls quickly while its tradable float still rises. It is also why headline burn statistics misrank ASTER against peers: the totals are genuine, but on a like-for-like float basis they are not comparable to a programme that burns coins bought from the market.

## Summary

Aster has built a fee engine that most tokens would envy and pointed it somewhere the supply ledger cannot credit. The Pressure Framework reads ASTER at a net **+2.15%** of circulating supply over the next 90 days and **+3.75%** over the last, driven by **5.79M ASTER** of weekly staking emission, a **20.00M** team cliff opening **Sep 17 2026** and a **32.00M** airdrop stage claim opening **Nov 4 2026**, with **0** booked on the buy side. The structural mechanism is buy-and-redistribute: **99%** of fees buy ASTER that goes straight back out to stakers, while the matching burn takes **11.09M ASTER** from a locked team reserve rather than from the market. The key risk is that the falling **3,000,000,000** ceiling is read as float scarcity when the float is still expanding, and the constraint that keeps this contained is genuine — supply is hard-capped at **8,000,000,000**, nothing can be minted, and every token on this page is pre-minted supply moving rather than new supply being created.
