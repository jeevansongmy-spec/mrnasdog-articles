## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 26 to Oct 24 2026, ASTER's circulating supply rose by a net **+0.18%**: **4.5M ASTER** of new supply against **0** removed from circulation, on a base of **2.51B ASTER**. Other supply trackers read **−6.68%** for the same 90 days, a gap of **6.86 points**, so the page carries a ⚠ data-conflict note. The gap is not a flow of coins. On Oct 23 2026 the supply figure those trackers use dropped by **210.02M ASTER**, the day coins already sitting in the burn address stopped being counted as circulating. Measured the same way at both ends, circulating ASTER rose about **4.5M**, the same as our ledger. For the next 90 days the reading turns: **70.13M ASTER** is due to reach the market, or **+2.80%**, almost all of it on one day. ASTER is a quiet float today with a scheduled airdrop step ahead.

## Sell pressure: where new ASTER comes from

Protocol inflation added **4.5M ASTER**. Aster stakers earn **450,000 ASTER** a week, 150,000 as base pay and 300,000 as loyalty rewards, and these coins come from the locked ecosystem and community pool. The pool sends them over in blocks of **2.25M ASTER**, five weeks of pay at a time, about every 35 days. Two blocks landed in the window, on Aug 17 and Sep 21 2026. On the same rhythm three more are due by early January, around Oct 26, Nov 30 and Jan 4, which makes **6.75M ASTER** next. Aster replaced the old linear unlock of this pool with staking-only pay in early 2026, so this weekly rate is now the pool's only use.

Vesting unlocks were **0** in the last 90 days and are **63.38M ASTER** next. Aster's Stage 6 airdrop handed out **64M ASTER**. Holders could take half at once in May 2026 and lose the rest, or wait six months for the full amount. Almost everyone waited: only 620,608 ASTER went down the early path, half paid and half burned. The waiting holders can claim from Nov 4 to Dec 4 2026. In the two earlier rounds, Stage 4 on Apr 28 2026 and Stage 5 on Jun 9 2026, the whole waiting half left the locked airdrop reserve in one move on claim day, and the circulating count jumped by the same amount. We book Stage 6 the same way: **63.38M ASTER** on Nov 4 2026. The team allocation of **400M ASTER** was due to start vesting on Sep 17 2026, but Aster pushed the cliff a full year to Sep 17 2027, so it adds nothing in either window.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks were **0**. The treasury, the rest of the airdrop reserve and a separate reserve wallet did not move at all in the window. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is also **0**: there is no estate or court-held pile of ASTER.

## Buy pressure: where new ASTER goes

The programmatic buyback books **0**, even though it is large. Since Jun 17 2026, 99% of Aster's trading fees buy ASTER every day on the open market, plus a fixed 50,000 USDT fee from each new spot listing. Over the window that came to roughly **26M ASTER**. Every bought coin is then paid out to veASTER stakers as extra loyalty rewards each week, so it ends up back in holders' hands and is still counted as circulating. The buyback creates real buying in the market, but it does not shrink the supply.

The protocol fee burn also books **0** for circulation, and this is the part most often misread. For every ASTER bought back, Aster burns the same amount from a reserve, starting with the team allocation, every two weeks, until total supply falls to **3B ASTER**. In the window that burn sent **26.29M ASTER** to the burn address in eight batches, the latest **2.72M** on Oct 19 2026. All of it came from the locked team wallet, which fell from 393.98M to 367.69M. Total supply shrank, now **7.79B ASTER** after **210.09M** burned in all, but those coins were never for sale, so the burn did not take a single coin out of circulation. Foundation buys are **0**, and new long-term locks are **0**: staking locks ASTER for up to four years with an early-exit penalty, but staked coins still count as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

Only **32%** of ASTER's total supply circulates, so the locked wallets matter more than any single flow. The airdrop reserve holds **2.92B ASTER**, of which 63.38M leaves on Nov 4 2026; the rest has no published schedule. The ecosystem and community pool holds **1.24B ASTER** and pays only the weekly staking rewards. The treasury holds **560M ASTER**, untouched since launch and released only through governance. The team wallet holds **367.69M ASTER**, locked until Sep 17 2027 and shrinking every two weeks as the burn draws on it first. A separate reserve wallet has held **198.3M ASTER** since Nov 2025 without moving. Inside the float, a payout wallet holds 313.6M ASTER, and the buyback wallet keeps only dust, because bought coins pass straight to stakers. We read these wallets at every rebuild. If any locked balance falls between refreshes other than by a burn, the outflow enters the foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How ASTER compares to other perpetual exchange tokens

Among perpetual exchange tokens the big design choice is what happens to coins bought with fees. Hyperliquid routes most of its trading fees into a fund that buys HYPE and keeps it, so bought coins stay out of the market. Aster spends an even larger share, 99% of fees, but hands every bought coin to its stakers, so ASTER's buyback is a reward to holders rather than a cut in circulating supply. dYdX sits in between, buying back DYDX with a share of its revenue and staking it.

The burn is the second difference. Many tokens burn coins taken from the market, which shrinks what traders can buy. Aster burns from its own locked team allocation, which shrinks the total and the future team unlock, a real long-term cut, but not the coins in circulation today. ASTER is also earlier in its unlock life than most of its peers: about two thirds of its supply is still locked in airdrop, ecosystem, treasury and team wallets, so dated airdrop claims like Nov 4 2026 move the count far more than the slow staking pay does.
