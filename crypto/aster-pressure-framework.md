# ASTER Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed last 90D · projected to grow

ASTER supply grew **+0.25%** in the last 90 days and is set to grow about **+2.50%** in the next 90 — mixed last 90 days, projected to grow. The last window added **6.75M ASTER** of staking rewards; the next adds **4.5M** more plus a **63.38M ASTER** vested airdrop claim from Oct 28 2026. Aster's buyback and burn are real — **23.54M ASTER** bought and **23.54M** burned this window — but the bought coins go to stakers and the burned coins came from the locked team wallet, so neither shrinks the tradable float. The monitor reads **+1.11%**, because it counts burned coins as circulating.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

On the MrNasdog Pressure Framework, ASTER's net supply change over the last 90 days is **+0.25%** of the **2.71B** circulating ASTER, and the forward reading for the next 90 days is **+2.50%**. The inflation monitor reads **+1.11%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.86 percentage points** — above the 0.5-point line, so the Aster page carries a ⚠ monitor-gap chip. The gap has a clear cause: the monitor's supply figure counts coins in the burn address as circulating, so every burn from the locked team wallet shows up there as new supply, about **0.88 points** of it. We keep our number. In one line: ASTER is **a quiet float about to take one large airdrop payout**, with a burn that shrinks total supply but not the market.

## Sell pressure: where new ASTER comes from

Protocol inflation is the only sell row that moved in the last 90 days. Aster pays its stakers from the ecosystem pool, which was created at launch with the rest of the 8B ASTER supply. The pool sends **2.25M ASTER** every 35 days — five weekly reward rounds of 450,000 ASTER — to a payout wallet, which forwards it into Aster's deposit contract where stakers collect it. Those transfers went out on Jul 13, Aug 17 and Sep 21 2026, for **6.75M ASTER** in the window, read from the pool's own balance at both ends. On the same 35-day timetable the next two land around Oct 26 and Nov 30 2026, which puts **4.5M ASTER** in the next 90 days; the one after that falls in early January 2027.

Vesting unlocks added nothing in the window: the airdrop reserve did not move at all, and the team wallet only sent coins to the burn address. The next 90 days are different. Users who took the fully vested track in Aster's sixth airdrop round can claim from Oct 28 2026. That round was **64M ASTER**; **0.62M** was paid out on May 4 2026 to users who chose the early claim, which leaves **63.38M ASTER** for the vested claim. The fifth round shows how this works: its vested payout of **95.09M ASTER** left the airdrop reserve in a single transfer on its claim day, Jun 9 2026. So the vested airdrop counts in full on Oct 28 2026, and it is by far the largest item on the Aster ledger.

The team allocation of 400M ASTER was due to start unlocking at 10M a month on Sep 17 2026. On Sep 1 2026 Aster pushed that start back one year, to Sep 17 2027, so the team vesting cliff adds nothing in the next 90 days. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero: none of the reserve wallets released coins in the window. There is no bankruptcy estate and no long-term lock releasing ASTER, so that row is zero as well.

## Buy pressure: where new ASTER goes

Aster runs one of the most aggressive buybacks among exchange tokens. Since the tokenomics upgrade of Jun 17 2026, **99% of platform fees** buy ASTER on the open market. In this window that came to about **23.54M ASTER**. But the programmatic buyback books **0** on the framework, because every bought coin is paid out to veASTER stakers as loyalty rewards: it leaves the market and returns to it in the same cycle. The buyback wallet itself holds only dust, about 30 ASTER.

The burn matches the buyback one for one. **23.54M ASTER** went to the burn address in six burns between Jul 13 and Sep 21 2026, and **204.26M ASTER** has been burned since launch, which is why total supply now reads about 7.80B instead of 8B. The protocol fee burn still books **0** on the framework: every burn in this window was drawn from the locked team wallet, and those coins were never part of the circulating count. Burning them cuts total supply and shortens the team's future unlock, but it removes no coin anyone could sell today. There was no foundation buy in the window. Staking locks are real, but locked ASTER sits in the deposit contract, which is counted as circulating, so a new long-term lock also books zero.

## Foundation and overhang

Five Aster wallets sit outside the circulating count, and together they hold about 5.29B ASTER. The airdrop reserve holds about **2.92B ASTER**; it pays out in lumps on airdrop claim dates, most recently Jun 9 2026, and has no public schedule after the Oct 28 2026 claim. The ecosystem pool holds about **1.24B ASTER** and currently pays only the 2.25M staking tranches. The treasury holds **560M ASTER**, untouched since launch and released only through governance. The team wallet holds about **373.5M ASTER** and shrinks only by burns. A fifth reserve wallet holds **198.3M ASTER** and has been idle all window. Inside the circulating count, a staking payout wallet holding 313.6M ASTER only passes rewards through.

All five are tracked on-chain. If any of these wallets falls between refreshes and the coins go anywhere other than the burn address, that outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How ASTER compares to other perpetuals exchange tokens

Among perpetuals exchange tokens, the usual design is fees in, tokens out of the market. Hyperliquid's HYPE buys its token with trading fees and holds what it buys in a protocol fund, which takes coins off the market. dYdX and GMX send fee income to stakers instead. ASTER does both halves in its own way: it buys with fees like HYPE, but pays the coins back out to stakers like the fee-sharing designs, so the buyback itself does not shrink the float. The matching burn is the part that looks deflationary, and on ASTER it hits locked team coins rather than market coins.

The bigger difference is the size of the reserve. Only about 35% of ASTER's total supply is counted as circulating, and more than half of all ASTER still sits in the airdrop reserve, the ecosystem pool, the treasury, the team wallet and one more reserve wallet. Tokens with a large locked reserve are driven by release dates, not by fees: the Oct 28 2026 airdrop claim alone is about nine times the whole last window's staking emission. By contrast, the steady staking emission is small — 2.25M ASTER every 35 days is under 0.1% of the float per tranche.

Aster's stated goal is to burn total supply down to 3B ASTER. At this window's pace of about 23.5M per 90 days, that would take decades, and the team wallet alone would be used up in roughly four years. The goal matters for the long-run ceiling, not for the next 90 days.
