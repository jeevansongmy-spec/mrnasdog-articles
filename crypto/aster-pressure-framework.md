We traced it fully: the monitor's supply figure rises by exactly the amount burned from the team wallet each fortnight, because it counts the burn address as circulating. Our reading stays. Aster is a **buyback-for-stakers token with a burn that only shrinks locked supply**: quiet in the last 90 days, heavier in the next because of a scheduled airdrop.

## Sell pressure: where new ASTER comes from

**Protocol inflation — 6.75M ASTER.** ASTER has no mint schedule; every coin was created at launch on Sep 17 2025 and new supply means coins leaving a locked pile. Since Mar 30 2026 the ecosystem allocation is released only as staking rewards: **450,000 ASTER a week**, made of 150,000 for validator delegators and 300,000 for long-term lockers. The pool pays five weeks at a time, so **2.25M ASTER** leaves it about every 35 days. Three of those payments fell inside this window, the last on Sep 21 2026, and the pool fell from **1.2472B** to **1.2405B ASTER**. On that rhythm the next two payments land around Oct 26 and Nov 30 2026, so the forward figure is **4.5M ASTER**; the third lands just after the window, around Jan 4 2027.

**Vesting unlocks — 0 last, 63.38M ASTER next.** Aster pays its airdrops in stages, and the last three gave users a choice: take half now and let the other half burn, or wait and take all of it. Stage 6 was worth **64M ASTER** (0.8% of supply). Only **620,608 ASTER** was settled early, at half, on May 4 2026; everyone else waited, and their full share opens on **Nov 4 2026** — about **63.38M ASTER**. We expect it to leave the airdrop reserve in one move, because Stage 5 did exactly that: **95.09M ASTER** left the reserve on its claim day, Jun 9 2026. The airdrop reserve did not move at all in the last 90 days. The other locked share, the **400M ASTER** team allocation, was due to start paying 10M a month on Sep 17 2026, but on Sep 1 2026 Aster pushed that start back a full year, to **Sep 17 2027**. So the team adds nothing this window or next.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — 0.** The treasury, the airdrop reserve outside its announced dates, and a separate reserve wallet sent nothing to the market. **Long-term locked or bankruptcy — 0.** ASTER is a 2025 token with no estate, trustee or court distribution behind it.

## Buy pressure: where new ASTER goes

**Programmatic buyback — 0.** Since Jun 17 2026, **99% of Aster's trading fees** buy ASTER on the open market, spread across each day. About **20.62M ASTER** was bought in the six two-week periods from Jun 29 to Sep 21 2026, roughly 4M a fortnight lately. That is real buying, and it helps the price on the day. But every bought coin is paid straight out to stakers as rewards, so the coins move from sellers to stakers and stay in the market. The buyback wallet itself holds only dust.

**Protocol fee burn — 0.** For every ASTER bought, Aster burns the same amount from reserves, team share first, every two weeks until total supply falls from 8B to 3B. Six burns landed in the window, **23.54M ASTER** in all (including a one-off 2.92M catch-up on Aug 24 2026), and the team wallet fell from **397.06M** to **373.52M ASTER** by the same amount. Those coins were locked and never tradable, so burning them shrinks the total but not the float. **Foundation buy — 0**: no treasury purchase was announced or seen. **New long-term lock — 0**: staking can lock ASTER for up to four years, but staked coins sit in the platform's deposit wallet, which already counts as circulating, so more staking takes nothing out.

## Foundation and overhang

Most ASTER is still locked, so the overhang is large. The **airdrop reserve holds 2.92B ASTER** and pays out only on announced airdrop dates; after the Nov 4 2026 payout, no further stage has been announced. The **ecosystem pool holds 1.24B ASTER** and drains at 450,000 a week through staking. The **treasury holds 560M ASTER**, untouched since launch and meant to move only through governance. The **team wallet holds 373.52M ASTER**, locked to Sep 17 2027 and shrinking every fortnight through burns. A further **198.3M ASTER** sits in a reserve wallet that has not moved. Inside the float, a staking payout wallet holds **313.6M** and an unlabelled wallet **293.18M**; moving those adds no new supply. We read every one of these balances on-chain at each rebuild. If any locked balance falls between refreshes for any reason other than a burn, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ASTER compares to other perpetual-exchange tokens

Perpetual-exchange tokens mostly turn trading fees into buying. The difference is where the bought coins end up. On Hyperliquid, HYPE bought with fees is held by a protocol fund, which takes it out of trading. On Aster, the coins bought are handed to stakers, so the buyback works like a staking yield paid in ASTER rather than a removal of supply. The burn that comes with it targets locked team coins, which makes the total supply number fall fast — about 2.55% of all ASTER has been burned so far — without reducing what can be traded.

The bigger structural difference is the locked share. Around two thirds of ASTER still sits in reserves, and the coin is barely a year old. Older exchange tokens such as dYdX or GMX have most of their supply already out, so their supply story is mainly about emissions and fees. For ASTER the story is release dates: an airdrop payout of **63.38M** in one day is about **14 times** what stakers receive in the same 90 days, and the 400M team share is only one year away.

Aster has also cut its emissions hard: the ecosystem allocation used to release 78.4M ASTER a month until early 2026, and now releases about 2M a month through staking. Compared with platforms that still pay big trading rewards in their own token, that is a light drip. The risk sits in the large one-off releases, not in the weekly flow.
