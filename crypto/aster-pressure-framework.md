Aster's ASTER token grew its tradable float by **+3.53%** over the last 90 days, almost entirely because a single **95.1M ASTER** airdrop unlock opened its claim window on **Jun 9 2026**. That wave is over. For the next 90 days the Pressure Framework reads only **+0.24%**: **25.8M ASTER** of staking emission and first-ever team vesting against a **19.4M ASTER** buyback funded by **99%** of Aster's platform fees, on a float of **2,685.53M**. ASTER is a young perp-DEX token whose dilution is decelerating faster than its buyback.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Jul 27 2026** the Pressure Framework reads ASTER at **+3.53%** net supply growth and projects **+0.24%** for the next 90 days. Our monitor reads **+4.21%** for the historical window, a gap of **0.68 percentage points**, which is over the tolerance and raises the flag. Roughly a third of that gap is mechanical rather than a disagreement about facts: the Aster buyback wallet **0xa0edBaBcb48034e368de286b49F9603C7AfA1b60** held just **25 ASTER** when we read it on **Jul 27 2026**, because the **6,020,941 ASTER** bought between **Jun 17 2026** and **Jul 13 2026** was paid straight out to veASTER stakers instead of being burned or held — this page nets that open-market purchase, and a pure supply count cannot. The remaining **0.45 percentage points** stays open, and the honest reason is age: Aster held its token generation event on **Sep 17 2025**, so the 90-days-ago starting point sits in the middle of the stage-based airdrop claim period and is not a stable base. ASTER is best labelled a decelerating-dilution token with a live, verified fee buyback.

## Sell pressure: where new ASTER comes from

ASTER has no protocol inflation in the usual sense. The BEP-20 contract on BNB Chain reported a total supply of exactly **8,000,000,000** when we read it, and that is also the maximum: every ASTER that will ever exist was created at the token generation event, there is no block reward, and nothing is minted. What the framework calls **protocol inflation** is therefore the staking pool, which pays **150,000 ASTER** of base yield plus **300,000 ASTER** of loyalty rewards — **450,000** in all — out of the ecosystem allocation every seven-day epoch. Over 90 days that is **5.8M ASTER**, about **0.22%** of the float, and it is the residue of a March 2026 overhaul that replaced a **78.4M**-per-month linear release with staking-only emission, a cut of roughly **97%**.

**Vesting unlocks** is where the whole story turned. Aster distributed its **53.5%** airdrop allocation through numbered stages, each with an immediate claim and a vested claim roughly six months later, and the Stage 5 vested unlock of **95,088,036 ASTER** opened a 30-day claim window on **Jun 9 2026**. That single event is the reason the historical column reads heavy. Stage 6 was the final trading-mining round and settled only **620,600 ASTER**, half of it burned outright and half sent to the treasury contract, and its vested claim does not open until **Nov 4 2026** — outside this window. The airdrop stream is, for practical purposes, spent. What replaces it is the team allocation, which reaches its twelve-month cliff on **Sep 17 2026** and then releases **10.0M ASTER** per month for forty months, putting two tranches inside the window on **Sep 17 2026** and **Oct 17 2026** for **20.0M** in total. Aster also states that its matching burn consumes the team allocation first, which could shrink that number; the framework books the published cliff in full because that is the more cautious reading.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks** books at **zero** — no discretionary release was observed at either end of the window — and **long-term locked or bankruptcy** is zero because Aster has no estate, no trustee and no court-supervised distribution. The zero in the foundation row is a statement about the window, not about the risk.

## Buy pressure: where new ASTER goes

The **programmatic buyback** is the reason ASTER is worth reading at all, and unlike most announced buybacks it is verifiable. Since **Jun 17 2026**, **99%** of Aster's platform fee income is used to buy ASTER on the open market through a spread-out order that settles to a published wallet. Two cycles are on the record with exact figures — **2,937,126 ASTER** through **Jun 29 2026** and **3,083,816 ASTER** through **Jul 13 2026**, **6,020,941** accumulated — and the on-chain burn balance of **183,802,125 ASTER** reconciles with the documented history to within **0.25%**. At the observed pace of about **3.0M ASTER** every two weeks, the next 90 days should absorb roughly **19.4M ASTER**, which is why the framework books **6.0M** historically and **19.4M** forward. The caveat matters: the bought coins are not destroyed, they are handed to locked veASTER stakers as loyalty rewards, so the buyback is a real bid that transfers supply to long-term lockers rather than removing it.

**Protocol fee burn** reads **zero** and this is the most counter-intuitive line on the page, because Aster burns aggressively. Every buyback triggers an equal burn every two weeks, and the programme runs until total supply falls from **8,000.0M** to **3,000.0M**. The burn is simply pointed somewhere else: the destroyed coins come out of the locked reserve, team allocation first, so they were never tradable. Burning them lowers the ceiling and shrinks the team overhang — both genuinely good for holders — but it removes nothing from the float, and scoring it against a circulating base would overstate what actually leaves the market. **Foundation buy** is zero because every purchase already runs through the fee programme counted above, and **new long-term lock** is zero because no lock cap, lock-size announcement or newly deployed lock contract with a stated quantum landed in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is the dominant risk in ASTER and it is very large: **5,130.7M ASTER**, about **66%** of everything that exists, sits outside the float against a tradable base of **2,685.53M**. Most of it is the undistributed remainder of the **4,280.0M** airdrop allocation, which Aster describes as releasing gradually over 80 months subject to governance, but which in practice only ever reaches the market through a stage claim window — and no stage claim window opens before **Oct 25 2026**. Behind it sit a **560.0M** treasury that stays fully locked until a governance decision unlocks it, the remainder of the **2,400.0M** ecosystem pool that now feeds only staking rewards, and the **400.0M** team allocation that is simultaneously vesting and being burned. Two balances that look like overhangs are not: the buyback wallet held **25 ASTER** at the last read because it passes everything through within the epoch, and the wallet holding **1,318,900,756 ASTER** is the Aster deposit bridge — trader balances, not project supply. All of the real overhangs are read every 24 hours on-chain or walked on the web every two weeks, and if any of those balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the ASTER sell ledger at the next refresh.

## How ASTER compares to other perpetual-DEX tokens

ASTER belongs to the class of exchange tokens whose issuer earns real fee revenue and routes it back into the token — the same structural family as a centralised exchange token with a quarterly buyback, and the opposite of a governance token attached to a business that never pays it. At **99%** of platform fees, Aster's pass-through ratio is at the aggressive end of that class; most fee-sharing perp DEXs route a fraction. The difference from a burn-based exchange token is where the coins land. A quarterly-burn exchange token retires circulating supply, so its buyback is directly deflationary on the float. Aster splits the two legs: the market purchase goes to lockers and the destruction is applied to locked reserve, so the programme lowers the long-run ceiling from **8,000.0M** to **3,000.0M** while leaving the near-term float roughly unchanged.

Against a capped proof-of-work coin the comparison inverts. A halving-model asset releases its remaining supply on an algorithmic schedule that nobody controls, and its holders face no distribution risk beyond that schedule. ASTER has a hard cap too — **8,000.0M**, with no mint function firing — but two thirds of that cap sits in wallets whose release timing is a decision rather than a rule. A cap protects against dilution beyond the cap; it says nothing about the pace at which the existing cap is handed out, and for a token only ten months past its **Sep 17 2025** launch the pace is the entire question.

Against its nearest peers — young perp-DEX tokens launched in the last year with heavy airdrop allocations still unvested — ASTER now sits at the better end, and the reason is timing rather than design. Its largest scheduled unlocks are behind it, its emission was cut by **97%** in March 2026, and its buyback started firing in June 2026 with published, checkable quanta. The peers still working through their airdrop cliffs face the profile ASTER had last quarter.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things move this reading. First, **Sep 17 2026**: the team cliff opens and the first **10.0M ASTER** tranche is due, followed by a second on **Oct 17 2026** — watch whether the tranches are actually released or whether the burn has consumed them, because Aster has published both rules. Second, the bi-weekly buyback settlements: at **3.0M ASTER** per cycle the buyback nearly cancels issuance, but the quantum is a function of trading fees, so a volume slump would cut the buy side and push net growth back toward **+1%**. Third, any announcement of a new airdrop stage or a governance-approved community distribution from the **4,280.0M** airdrop allocation, which is the single largest thing that could break this reading. Fourth, **Nov 4 2026**, when the Stage 6 vested claim opens — small at roughly **310,000 ASTER**, but the marker for whether the stage model is truly finished.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ASTER at **+3.53%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+0.24%** over the next 90 — a sharp cooling, because the **95.1M ASTER** Stage 5 airdrop unlock that dominated the historical window has no successor inside the forward one. The structural mechanism is a genuine fee buyback: **99%** of Aster's platform fee income buys ASTER on the open market at about **19.4M** per 90 days, nearly matching the **25.8M** of staking emission and first team vesting. The key risk is the **5,130.7M ASTER** still held outside the float, most of it an airdrop allocation released at the project's discretion — capacity that dwarfs anything on the current calendar. The ceiling is real and falling: supply is hard-capped at **8,000.0M** with no mint, and the bi-weekly reserve burn is pointed at **3,000.0M**.
