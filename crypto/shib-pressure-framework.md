Shiba Inu is the rarest thing in this framework: a token where every single sell row is a real zero. The SHIB contract on Ethereum has no mint function and no owner — the published source lists twelve callable functions and none of them creates coins — nothing is left to vest, and no project treasury holds SHIB to release. Over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** the only movement was destruction: **3.64B SHIB** burned, against **589,239,342,652,219 SHIB** in circulation. That is **0.0006%** — the framework reads **−0.00%** net supply pressure and projects **−0.00%** forward. The 410-trillion burn headline you will see everywhere is almost entirely one transfer made in **May 2021**, and the trackers that publish it overstate the real destroyed total by **340 billion SHIB**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** the framework reads SHIB at **−0.000618%** net supply pressure — **0 SHIB** on the sell side against **3.64B SHIB** destroyed on the buy side — and projects the same **−0.000618%** forward, because the burn is continuous and nothing exists that could turn a sell row on. Our supply monitor reads **−0.5539%** for the same window, a gap of **0.55 percentage points**, and that gap is an artefact rather than a conflict, so no data-conflict flag ships on the overview. SHIB trades near **$0.0000053**, and the price feeds that monitor carry three significant figures; one tick at that magnitude moves an implied supply figure by **0.19%**, which is three hundred times the size of the real 90-day change. Recovering the supply series at full precision gives **589,243,253,266,754** on **May 28 2026** falling to **589,239,342,652,219** on **Aug 26 2026** — a fall of **0.000664%**, which matches the framework's reading to five decimal places and matches the on-chain burn to **272M SHIB**. SHIB is best labelled a **frozen supply with a decorative burn**: nothing can be added, and what is removed is a rounding error.

## Sell pressure: where new SHIB comes from

Nowhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and for SHIB that zero is provable from the verified source rather than inferred from a flat chart. The Shiba Inu ERC-20 at **0x95aD61b0a150d79219dCF64E1E6Cc01f0B64C4cE** is a single small Solidity file, not a proxy, and its complete externally callable surface is name, symbol, decimals, totalSupply, balanceOf, allowance, transfer, approve, transferFrom, increaseAllowance, decreaseAllowance and burn. The internal mint call appears exactly once, inside the constructor, and ran in **August 2020**. There is no Ownable, no owner, no transferOwnership and no minter role — which means SHIB was never renounced, because there was never anything to renounce.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because the whole quadrillion was placed on day one — half into a Uniswap pool, half to a single recipient — with no investor round, no team allocation and no cliff calendar. The unlock trackers record the Shiba Inu schedule as finished in **2020** with no future events. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because Shiba Inu has no foundation treasury and no DAO treasury denominated in SHIB. What is watched instead is an unlabelled bucket of **256.9B SHIB** that the classifier holds outside the counted float, a founder-donation wallet holding **50.5M SHIB**, a relief-fund wallet holding **2.25M SHIB** and the Shibarium bridge escrow holding **90.95M SHIB** — none of which moved a coin this window. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: there is no estate and no trustee distributing SHIB into this market.

Sell #5 is the one row that had to be argued rather than observed. The Shiba Inu staking vault, **0xb4a81261b16b92af0b9f7c4a83f1e885132d81e4**, fell from **3.59T SHIB** to **3.49T SHIB** across the window — a drain of **104.73B SHIB**, twenty-nine times the size of the burn. A draining lock is normally sell pressure. It is not booked here, and the reason comes straight off the vault's own published source: the withdraw function carries no timestamp condition at all, so there is no term, and the receipt token is an ordinary ERC-20 with transfer and transferFrom, so a staker who wants out can sell the receipt without ever touching the vault. The staked SHIB also sits inside the circulating figure the framework divides by, so unstaking moves nothing across the boundary being measured. Booking it would have flipped SHIB from a **−0.0006%** reading to a **+0.0172%** one on a mechanism that never removed float in the first place.

## Buy pressure: where new SHIB goes

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is the only non-zero number anywhere in the SHIB ledger, at **3.64B SHIB**. Getting it right needs two meters, not one, because Shiba Inu destroys supply in two different shapes. Most of it is transfers into graveyard addresses that the contract itself never notices: **0xdEAD000000000000000042069420694206942069** took in **3.49B SHIB** over the window and **0x000000000000000000000000000000000000dEaD** took in **148.68M SHIB**, while the null address took in nothing. The rest is real destruction through the contract's public burn call, which lowered the reported total supply by **517,922 SHIB**. Reading only the graveyard balances misses the second path; reading only the contract's total supply — which still reports the near-quadrillion genesis figure — would report almost no burning at all. Together they give **3,639,396,495 SHIB** destroyed, sourced from Shibarium fee revenue plus voluntary sends by exchanges, DEXes and trading apps.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. August 2026 coverage repeats a claim that the Shiba Inu founders are planning a **$500 million** SHIB buyback; it traces to a single social-media account, has no project announcement, no contract and no dated amount behind it, and nothing resembling it appears in either the supply series or the graveyard flows. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** for the same reason Sell #3 is: there is no treasury to buy with. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** because the staking vault is not a lock and it shrank anyway. It is worth saying plainly how small the live burn is against the marketing: the ecosystem talks in terms of burning about **1%** of supply a year, and the measured rate is **0.0025%** a year — roughly four hundred times less.

## Foundation and overhang

Shiba Inu has no foundation treasury, no DAO treasury holding SHIB and no buyback accumulation wallet, so the overhang list is short and every item on it is dormant. The largest is an unlabelled bucket of **256.9B SHIB** — the difference between the classified total supply and the classified circulating supply — with no published address and no release calendar; it grew by **186M SHIB** over the window, which is why the classifier's float fell slightly more than the burn alone explains. Next is the founder-donation wallet **0xb8f226ddb7bc672e27dffb67e4adabfa8c0dfa08** at **50,503,748 SHIB** and the India relief-fund wallet **0x68a99f89e475a078645f4bac491360afe255dff1** at **2,250,000 SHIB**, both re-read from the chain this window at identical balances on both dates. Then the Shibarium bridge escrow **0x6Aca26bFCE7675FF71C734BF26C8c0aC4039A4Fa**, holding **90,946,554 SHIB** unchanged to eighteen decimals. Exchange wallets — and they hold trillions of SHIB — are deliberately excluded, because those coins belong to depositors rather than to a coordinated group. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

One overhang deserves its own sentence because it only appears if you read both chains. Shibarium mints a pegged copy of SHIB against SHIB locked on Ethereum, so the two legs must never be added together — and here the mirror does not balance. The Shibarium copy of SHIB reports **92,353,467,859 SHIB** outstanding while the Ethereum escrow behind it holds **90,946,554 SHIB**. The residual, **92.26B SHIB**, is identical at both ends of the window to the wei, which is the signature of a mirror rather than a missing additive leg, so it contributed exactly zero to this reading. Shibarium produced **1,266,987** blocks over the window and moved no SHIB across the bridge at all — consistent with its transaction count per day falling to a multi-month low of **636** in August 2026.

## How SHIB compares to other fixed-supply meme tokens

Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, SHIB looks like the more extreme version of the same idea. A proof-of-work chain still issues new coins every block and only halves the rate on a schedule; Shiba Inu issues nothing, ever, because no code path exists to do it. Against an uncapped continuous-emission layer-1, the contrast is total — those chains fund security by printing, and their holders pay for it in dilution, while SHIB has no validators to pay and therefore no reason to print. Against exchange tokens that run quarterly revenue buybacks, SHIB is the opposite trade: an exchange token converts business revenue into supply removal at a rate that can reach several percent a year, and SHIB converts almost nothing, because the layer-2 whose fees feed the burn is running at a few hundred transactions a day.

The closest structural analogue is a fair-launched meme token with a burn address and no vesting, and there SHIB is unusual in two ways. First, its burn is genuinely uncoordinated: there is no protocol rule forcing a share of anything into the graveyard, so the number swings with whatever a single DEX or exchange decides to do that month — which is why third-party 30-day burn claims collected for this window range from **2.18B** to **3.90B SHIB** and none of them reconciles to the 90-day total. Second, its denominator is so large that even a well-run burn cannot matter: removing **3.64B** from **589T** is arithmetically indistinguishable from removing nothing. A fee-burning chain can flip deflationary in a busy month. SHIB, on the current mechanism, would need the burn to grow roughly four hundred-fold before the framework's headline moved off **0.00%**.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Shibarium privacy upgrade slated for the fourth quarter of **2026**, which brings encrypted smart contracts to the layer-2 — it is not a supply event by itself, but Shibarium activity is what feeds the burn, and the burn is the only live row. Second, whether the reported move toward a fixed-supply, DAO-governed model touches SHIB itself or only the smaller ecosystem tokens; SHIB's supply is already fixed in code, so a governance change cannot loosen it without a redeployment. Third, the Shiba Inu DAO governance elections now under way, and specifically whether the staking rework that ties voting power to staking duration introduces a real term lock — today there is none, and a genuine lock would move the **3.49T SHIB** vault from a footnote to a buy row. Fourth, the **256.9B SHIB** unlabelled bucket, which crept up **186M** this window and is the only classification line that moves at all. Fifth, any actual announcement behind the **$500 million** buyback rumour: an on-chain contract with a dated quantum would be the first buy row Shiba Inu has ever had.

## Summary

Shiba Inu is the cleanest sell-side zero in the framework. The SHIB contract cannot mint, has no owner to add a mint, has nothing left to vest and has no treasury behind it, so all four canonical sell rows are impossible by construction rather than merely quiet. The single live mechanism is the burn, and over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** it destroyed **3.64B SHIB** — real, verifiable across two on-chain meters, and equal to **0.0006%** of the **589 trillion** alive. The framework therefore reads **−0.00%** both ways: flat, not deflationary. The key risk is not dilution, because dilution is impossible; it is that the burn narrative does the work the burn does not, and that the numbers repeated to support it — a **410.84 trillion** cumulative headline that overstates real destruction by **340 billion**, and 30-day figures that contradict each other — are not measurements. The ceiling here is permanent and the floor is fixed; SHIB's supply simply does not move.
