## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **May 7 2026** to **Aug 5 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **SHIB at about 0.00% net**: sell pressure of **zero** against buy pressure of about **4B SHIB** of realised burn, on a circulating base of **589.24T SHIB**. In precise terms that is a fractional net burn of roughly **0.0007%** — deflationary in direction, but so small it rounds to flat. Our supply monitor reads **+0.003%** for the same period, a gap of only about **0.004 percentage points** that stays inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip is needed; the tiny positive on the monitor is market-cap-and-price rounding noise on a token whose true supply does not grow. The label for SHIB is a **fixed-supply, mint-renounced meme token whose only supply lever is a burn too small to register** — structurally incapable of inflating, yet burning far too slowly to count as deflationary.

## Sell pressure: where new SHIB comes from

None of it comes from anywhere, because no new SHIB can be created. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**: the entire **1 quadrillion** SHIB was minted at deploy in **August 2020**, the ERC-20 contract has **no mint function**, and ownership was renounced, so issuance is permanently locked. There is no staking issuance, no block subsidy and no emission curve — staking and ecosystem rewards are paid in the sibling tokens **BONE** and **TREAT**, never in fresh SHIB. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is also **zero**: Shiba Inu was a fair launch with no presale, no team or investor allocation and no vesting schedule. Half the original supply went into a renounced Uniswap liquidity pool, and half was sent to Vitalik Buterin, who burned roughly **410T** of it in **May 2021** and donated a further tranche to charity — so no cliff calendar exists to watch.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**, because there is no identified team, foundation or DAO SHIB treasury capable of releasing supply. The largest wallets are **exchange custodial** — one large exchange holds about **82T SHIB** and another about **54T** on behalf of depositors — which are not team-controlled and are excluded from the overhang the framework tracks. The only reserve on the board is a small **256.7B SHIB** classification bucket — the gap between total and circulating supply, about **0.04%** of the float — with no published address, no schedule and no observed outflow in the window. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: no estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches SHIB. The entire sell ledger is empty by design.

## Buy pressure: where new SHIB goes

Only one buy row is live, and it is small. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**: the Shiba Inu project runs no treasury buyback of SHIB, and where an ecosystem app buys SHIB with fees and destroys it, that removal is counted as a burn rather than as a protocol buyback. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is about **4B SHIB** over the window, the only mechanism that actually changes SHIB's supply. It aggregates several burn pathways to dead addresses: network base-fee burns, in-house DEX fees, third-party ecosystem DEXs, and a voluntary burn portal. The pace is lumpy — a **July 2026** surge, led largely by one ecosystem DEX, did most of the work, while May and June ran only a few hundred million SHIB each — and even the elevated recent rate of roughly **3.47B SHIB** a month is only about **0.0007%** of supply over 90 days. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**: no disclosed open-market accumulation by any project entity. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: no new escrow took SHIB off the market in the window. So the net effect of the whole ledger is a burn that is real but immaterial against a 589-trillion supply.

## Foundation and overhang

There is no team-controlled overhang on SHIB, which is unusual and central to the read. Because Shiba Inu was a fair launch with no allocation to founders, investors or a foundation, there is no insider pool of SHIB waiting to be released — the standard Sell #3 risk simply does not exist here. The largest SHIB wallets are **exchange custodial**: one major exchange holds about **82T SHIB** and another about **54T**, both on behalf of depositors rather than the project, so they are excluded from the overhang the framework tracks. The project's own lead has publicly maintained that no insider controls the large wallets. The small gap between the roughly **589.50T** total supply and the **589.24T** circulating figure is a **256.7B SHIB** classification detail — about **0.04%** of the float, no address, no schedule — not a team reserve, and it did not move inside the window. Because there is no monitored insider balance, there is no trigger sentence to attach — nothing can fall between refreshes and enter Sell #3, because there is no team wallet to watch, and even if that tiny bucket emptied entirely it could not move the reading.

## How SHIB compares to other fixed-supply memecoins

The right peer group for SHIB is fixed-supply, burn-only memecoins — not the uncapped, tail-emission coins it is often lumped with. The sharpest contrast is with the largest proof-of-work meme token, which has **no cap** and mints a fixed block reward forever, so its supply grows a few percent a year and dilutes holders over time. SHIB is the mirror image — a **hard-fixed** supply with no issuance at all, where the only possible change is downward through burns. On that structural axis SHIB has the better supply design: it cannot dilute holders, because the mint bounds the numerator to zero.

The nuance is that a fixed cap is not the same as a shrinking supply. Many burn tokens market themselves as deflationary, but the framework scores the realised on-chain number, not the narrative — and SHIB's burn, at roughly **0.0007%** of supply a quarter, is far too small to make the supply meaningfully deflationary. That is the defining trait of the class: the burn is real, on-chain and permanent, but the base is so enormous — **589 trillion** coins — that even a July surge barely dents it. A burn token only earns a deflationary score when the destroyed amount is large relative to the float; SHIB's is not, so it reads as flat rather than shrinking, and that is the honest reading even though it is a mint-renounced, capped asset.

Against exchange tokens that buy back and burn a share of quarterly revenue, SHIB is weaker on the buy side: those tokens can retire a visible percentage of supply each quarter, while SHIB's burn depends on organic network and ecosystem activity that has stayed modest. The upside for SHIB is the absence of any offsetting sell pressure — no unlocks, no team sales, no new mint — so it never has to burn just to stay even. For an inflation lens, SHIB is the cleanest possible example of a token whose **supply is structurally fixed** and whose deflation is, in practice, immaterial: the supply question is settled at flat, and only a large and sustained jump in on-chain burning would move it into genuine deflation.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether the network and ecosystem burn rate holds its elevated recent pace or falls back to the few-hundred-million-a-month levels of May and June — the July surge was driven largely by one ecosystem DEX, so it is worth confirming it persists. Second, whether any large one-off community or partner burn lands, of the kind that occasionally removes tens of billions of SHIB in a single event. Third, whether growth on the new L3 and higher network transaction volume translate into materially larger base-fee burns, which is the only path to a burn big enough to register against 589 trillion coins. Fourth, confirmation each rebuild that the supply remains mint-renounced and that no new SHIB allocation or lockup appears — none is expected, but it is the assumption the whole read rests on. Absent a step-change in burning, the next-90-day forecast stays flat.
