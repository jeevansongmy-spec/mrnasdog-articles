# FLR Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

The Flare coin page has the score, the price drivers and every question answered — [mrnasdog.com/research/flr](https://mrnasdog.com/research/flr). Below: supply, line by line.

**FLR** supply is growing. In the 90 days to **Oct 17 2026**, Flare holders claimed **635.0M FLR** of new rewards and took **314.1M FLR** out of rFLR vesting and the old sale escrow, while only **24.95M FLR** was burned or set aside. That is a net rise of **+1.06%** of the **87.45B FLR** in circulation, and about **+1.06%** again in the next 90 days. The monitor reads **+0.37%**; the gap comes from the monitor's own supply figure, not from the chain. Flare inflation is capped at 3% a year, and burns cover under 3% of the new coins.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Our 90-day net for **Flare (FLR)** is **+1.06%**: **949.16M FLR** of sell pressure against **24.95M FLR** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **87.45B FLR**. The next 90 days project **944.72M** in and **18.38M** out, also **+1.06%**. The inflation monitor reads **+0.37%**, a gap of **0.69 percentage points**, which is above our 0.5-point line, so the page carries a ⚠ monitor gap chip. We walked the chain, the unlock trackers, the official posts and the governance list, and found the difference inside the monitor's supply figure: it fell by **120M to 367M FLR** in single days on Jul 31, Aug 25, Sep 2, Sep 18 and Sep 29 2026, while Flare's own count of coins in the market never fell more than **3.1M FLR** in a day. Between Jul 11 and Oct 9 2026 the on-chain count rose **918.8M FLR**; the monitor shows 319M. Our number stays. In one line: **inflationary by design, with rewards and rFLR unlocks far ahead of the burns**.

## Sell pressure: where new FLR comes from

**Protocol inflation is the largest source: 635.0M FLR in 90 days.** Flare mints new FLR at 3% a year of its countable supply, with a hard cap of 3B FLR a year, since the FIP.16 vote that closed on Apr 24 2026 cut the rate from 5%. In this window Flare set aside **662.4M FLR** for validators, stakers, price and data providers and their delegators, and holders claimed **635.0M** of it. Rewards nobody claims in time, and rewards that providers fail to earn, are burned straight from the reward pots, so they never reach the market and never count here. The rate stepped from about 7.6M to about 7.3M FLR a day in mid-August, when Flare re-based its yearly inflation, so the next 90 days look the same.

**Vesting unlocks add 314.1M FLR.** Almost all of it is rFLR: Flare pays part of its rewards from a **20B FLR** incentive pool as rFLR, which vests over 12 months. In these 90 days holders took out **309.7M FLR**; those who left early lost half of their unvested share, and **12.3M FLR** was burned that way before it ever reached the market. The incentive pool moved another **437.9M FLR** into the rFLR program in the same window, so more is waiting to vest than comes out. The rest of the row is the old FIP.01 sale escrow, which paid out **4.4M FLR** once. Its vesting ended early in 2026 and its claims have no dates, so the next 90 days count only rFLR.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero.** The incentive pool still holds about **17.22B FLR**, but it only flows out through the rFLR program above. The seven wallets Flare lists as Foundation wallets sent nothing to the market. **Long-term locks and bankruptcy are zero** too: no estate or trustee holds FLR, and staked FLR already counts as circulating.

## Buy pressure: where new FLR goes

**The largest buyer is one holder: 17.0M FLR burned.** A large multisig that collects staking and data rewards sends a lump of FLR to the burn address about once a month. It burned **12.0M** on Aug 14 and **5.0M** on Sep 9 2026, and 11 times in the past year, from 5.0M to 29.2M each time. The burns are shrinking, so we count two burns of 5.0M (**10.0M FLR**) for the next 90 days.

**The protocol fee burn is small: 4.08M FLR.** Every unit of gas on Flare is burned, and FIP.16 raised the minimum fee 20 times in July 2026. But price and data providers, who send most of the transactions, get nearly all of their gas back, and other use is light, so the burn ran near 45K FLR a day. The FIP.16 plan hoped for about 300M FLR a year; the chain burned at roughly one-twentieth of that pace.

**Programmatic buyback: 3.87M FLR set aside, none bought yet.** FIP.16 created FIRE, a fund that takes fees from the data connector, FAssets and other services and is meant to buy FLR on the market and burn it. Since Aug 18 2026 a Foundation-listed wallet has received about 150K FLR every few days from the reward pot and now holds 3.87M. It sits outside circulation, so the coins count as removed, but we saw no purchase and no burn from it. At its pace it adds about **4.3M FLR** in the next 90 days. **Foundation buy and new long-term lock are zero**: no treasury bought FLR, and while more FLR is staked under the new rules, staked FLR still counts as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

The biggest overhang is the **incentive pool, about 17.22B FLR**, which pays rFLR rewards and has no fixed end date; we read it on-chain at every rebuild. Inside the rFLR program, a further pool of rewards is still vesting. The **old sale escrow holds 200.1M FLR** that is fully vested but not yet claimed. The **seven Foundation-listed wallets hold 3.95M FLR**, almost all of it in the new fee wallet. The multisig that burns each month is a holder, not a team wallet, and its balance changes all the time. Reward pots hold a few hundred million FLR that is either claimed into Sell #1 or burned. The team, Labs, venture fund and backer shares of the original distribution were handed out in full under FIP.01, so they already count as circulating and are not tracked as one unit. If any of these balances falls between rebuilds, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next rebuild.

## How FLR compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1s

**Flare vs Avalanche.** Flare is built on Avalanche technology, but the two treat supply in opposite ways. Avalanche has a hard cap of 720M AVAX and pays staking from the part not yet minted, and every fee is burned. Flare has no total cap, only a yearly cap of 3B FLR, so FLR supply keeps rising every year, and its fee burn is far smaller relative to new issuance.

**Flare vs Ethereum and Solana.** Ethereum and Solana also pay stakers in new coins with no total cap, and both burn part of their fees. Solana's rate is scheduled to fall each year toward a floor, while Flare's rate is a flat 3% that only governance can change. What makes Flare different is the second stream: rFLR rewards from a 20B pre-minted incentive pool, which adds about half as much again on top of inflation.

**Flare vs XRP.** Many FLR holders came from the 2023 airdrop to XRP holders, and Flare's apps are built around XRP. XRP itself mints no new coins and destroys its small fees, so its supply only shrinks. FLR is the opposite: supply grows by about 1% every 90 days today, and the FIRE buy-and-burn would need far more fee income than Flare earns now to change that.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**FIRE's first purchase.** The fee wallet holds 3.87M FLR and grows by about 43K a day. A first buy-and-burn from it would make the buyback row real; a transfer out to anywhere else would move it to Sell #3.

**The next monthly burn.** The multisig's burns came 23 to 58 days apart, so the next one is due around late October or November 2026. A burn far above 5.0M, or none at all, would move the buy side.

**The rFLR pace.** The incentive pool moves new FLR into rFLR faster than holders take it out. If withdrawals speed up, Sell #2 grows. FIP.16 also says early-exit penalties will go back to the pool instead of being burned; that changes nothing in our count, because those coins never reach the market either way.

**Nov 2 2026: Coincheck ends FLR support.** The Japanese exchange is delisting FLR, citing weak liquidity. It moves no supply, but it removes one place to sell and buy.

**The rest of FIP.16.** Protocol-run block building with captured MEV, and new staking weights on the P-chain, still need a network upgrade. Captured MEV is set to feed FIRE, which could finally give the buy side a real buyer.

## Summary

Flare (FLR) is **inflationary by design**: **635.0M FLR** of inflation rewards and **314.1M FLR** of rFLR and escrow unlocks entered the market in 90 days, against **24.95M FLR** burned or set aside, for a net **+1.06%** and the same ahead. The structure is a flat 3% yearly inflation capped at 3B FLR plus a 17.22B FLR incentive pool paid out as rFLR. The key risk is that the burns stay this small while both streams keep flowing; the FIRE buy-and-burn and captured MEV are the only planned ways to change that, and neither has bought FLR yet.
