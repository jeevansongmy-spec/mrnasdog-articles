# FLR Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

The Flare coin page has the score, the price drivers and every question answered — [mrnasdog.com/research/flr](https://mrnasdog.com/research/flr). Below: supply, line by line.

FLR supply is growing: about **1.01%** more FLR reached the market in the 90 days from Aug 27 to Nov 25 2026, and about **1.00%** more is expected in the next 90 days. Flare creates new FLR at **3%** a year to pay stakers and data providers, and rFLR rewards keep turning into FLR, for **913.02M FLR** of new supply against **26.49M FLR** taken back by fees, holder burns and the FIRE wallet. Other trackers show **+1.14%** for the same 90 days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Flare's FLR supply grew by a net **+1.01%** over the 90 days to Nov 25 2026, and the next 90 days point to about **+1.00%**. Other trackers read **+1.14%** for the same stretch, a difference of **0.13 percentage points**, small enough that the FLR coin page carries no warning note. The 3% inflation from the FIP.16 tokenomics vote and the steady flow of rFLR rewards do almost all of the work, and the burns are a small brake. Flare is an inflationary chain by design, with a fee burn that is real but still tiny next to what Flare pays out.

## Sell pressure: where new FLR comes from

The biggest source of new FLR is **protocol inflation**. Since May 14 2026, when FIP.16 cut the rate, Flare creates new FLR at 3% a year, with a cap of 3B FLR a year, and pays it out in 30-day slots of about 220M FLR to validators, stakers and the providers who run Flare's price feeds and data connector. From Aug 27 to Nov 25 2026 the network made **656.99M FLR**, and **611.92M FLR** of it reached holders. Another **26.86M FLR** of rewards that nobody claimed in time was burned before it ever reached a wallet, and a few million went to the FIRE fee wallet. Because the rate has not changed since May, the next 90 days should bring about the same 611.92M FLR.

The second source is **vesting unlocks**, and on Flare that mostly means rFLR. rFLR is a reward token that apps hand out to their users; it vests over 12 months and is paid from Flare's incentive pool. In the 90 days holders turned **313.26M rFLR** into FLR. Anyone who leaves before the vesting ends gives up part of it, and **16.59M FLR** of those penalties was burned, so **296.67M FLR** reached the market. Late claims from the old FlareDrop airdrop escrow added **4.44M FLR** on Sep 27 and Oct 7 2026, for a vesting total of **301.11M FLR**. The airdrop escrow finished vesting on Jan 30 2026, so those late claims are rare and not expected in the next 90 days.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks** book **0**: no Flare Foundation release was seen in the window, and the big reserves are covered in the overhang section below. **Long-term locked or bankruptcy** supply is also **0**, because no bankrupt firm or court holds FLR for a later payout.

## Buy pressure: where new FLR goes

The largest buy-side flow is **holder burns**. One large staking wallet burns most of the rewards it earns every month or two: it burned **5M FLR** on Sep 9 2026 and **11M FLR** on Nov 3 2026, **16.00M FLR** in all. It has burned 14 times since July 2025 and never waited more than 58 days, so we expect about the same in the next 90 days.

The **protocol fee burn** destroyed about **6.73M FLR**. The Jul 14 2026 upgrade raised Flare's base fee about 20 times, which made the burn bigger than before, yet fewer than one coin is burned for every hundred new ones. A small part comes from FXRP minting fees.

The **programmatic buyback** row holds **3.76M FLR**. FIP.16 created FIRE, the Flare Income Reinvestment Entity, to collect network fees and use them to buy back and burn FLR. Its fee wallet grew from **1.59M** to **5.35M FLR** between Aug 27 and Nov 25 2026, and those coins sit outside the market. FIRE has not yet bought FLR on the open market. **Foundation buy** is **0**, as the Flare Foundation bought no FLR, and **new long-term lock** is **0**, because staked FLR still counts as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest overhang is Flare's **incentive pool**, which still holds about **17.03B FLR** from the genesis supply. It has no lump-sum release date; it feeds the rFLR programme a little at a time, about 433M FLR in the last 90 days. The **rFLR pot** holds **897.99M FLR** waiting to be paid out, the FlareDrop **airdrop escrow** holds **200.07M FLR** that holders never claimed, and the **reward pots** hold about **242.6M FLR** earned but not yet collected.

The Flare Foundation's listed wallets hold **5.43M FLR**, almost all of it in the FIRE fee wallet. We read these balances on-chain at every rebuild. If any of them falls between rebuilds, the coins that leave are added to the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next rebuild.

## How FLR compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

FLR belongs to the group of proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains with no hard supply cap, where new coins pay the people who secure the network. Ethereum pays validators in new ETH and burns part of every fee, so a busy Ethereum can keep its supply almost flat. Flare copied the idea of a fee burn and pushed it harder with the Jul 14 2026 fee rise, but Flare's fees are far smaller next to its payouts, so FLR supply still grows about 1% every 90 days.

What sets Flare apart is the second stream. Most proof-of-stake chains pay only staking rewards; Flare also releases rFLR from a large pre-made incentive pool, which adds about half as much again on top of inflation. Against chains like Avalanche, which burns all of its fees, or Cosmos-style chains, which pay high staking rewards with no burn at all, FLR sits in the middle: a lower rate than many young chains since the FIP.16 cut, but a large reserve still to come.

FIRE is the piece to watch in any FLR vs Ethereum or FLR vs Avalanche comparison. If network fees grow and FIRE starts buying and burning FLR, Flare would gain a buyer most proof-of-stake chains do not have. For now the FIRE wallet holds a few million FLR, which is small next to roughly 900M FLR of new supply every 90 days.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Dec 1 to Dec 7 2026:** the FIP.17 vote on bringing Flare Confidential Compute to the main network. Its fees would be split between validators and FIRE, with 90% to FIRE at the start, so a yes vote could slowly grow the FIRE wallet.

**Dec 10 2026 and Jan 9 2027:** the next 30-day inflation slots begin. Each should be about 220M FLR; a clearly different amount would mean the rate or the base has changed.

**The first FIRE purchase:** FIRE has not yet bought FLR on the open market. A first buyback and burn would add a new buy-side flow to the FLR ledger.

**The next holder burn:** the large staking wallet last burned on Nov 3 2026 and has never waited more than 58 days, so a new burn is likely by early January 2027.

**The incentive pool pace:** rFLR funding ran at about 433M FLR in the last 90 days. A faster pace from the 17.03B FLR incentive pool would raise the vesting row.

## Summary

FLR supply is growing by about **1.01%** every 90 days, and the next 90 days look the same. Flare's 3% inflation adds about **611.92M FLR** to holders and rFLR adds about **301.11M FLR**, while fees, holder burns and the FIRE wallet take back only **26.49M FLR**. The key risk for holders is the 17.03B FLR still in the incentive pool, which keeps feeding rFLR for years. Flare has no hard supply cap, only a yearly limit of 3B FLR of new inflation, so FIRE buying and a much bigger fee burn are the only ways this picture can change.
