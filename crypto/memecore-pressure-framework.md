# M Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

MemeCore's coin M grew its circulating supply by **+42.18% net** over the last 90 days, and almost all of that came from one day: on **Aug 19 2026** a MemeCore reserve released **925.93M M** into circulation — the coins behind a Nasdaq-listed company's treasury deal. Underneath that one-off, the MemeCore chain mints **30 M** with every block, **33.33M M** a quarter, against a fee burn of about **600 M**, so the forward reading is **+1.46%** for the next 90 days. The supply monitor reads **+73.68%**; the **31.50 percentage point** gap comes from measuring the same flow against a smaller base, not from a different flow. Behind the float, seven MemeCore reserve contracts still hold **3.14B M** — more than the whole circulating supply of **2.28B**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 25 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **M at +42.18% net**: **961.05M M** entered the circulating float — **33.33M** from the MemeCore block reward and **927.73M** released from reserve contracts — while only about **600 M** was destroyed. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+73.68%**, a gap of **31.50 percentage points**. A full re-check of the chain, the unlock calendar, the project's own announcements and the supply data found no missing flow: the monitor added **969.38M** over the window against **961.05M** in this ledger, and the monitor simply divides by the supply of ninety days ago, **1,315.70M**, where this ledger divides by today's **2,278.35M**. On a single base the two readings sit **0.37 percentage points** apart. The forward column reads **+1.46%** because no reserve release is scheduled. The label for M is **inflationary, with a reserve larger than its float**: a steady block-reward chain whose biggest supply events are discretionary reserve releases.

## Sell pressure: where new M comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **33.33M M**. The MemeCore client mints **30 M** into the chain's reward contract with every block, and that contract rose by exactly thirty coins on every block read this window. The chain ran at a measured **7.00 seconds** a block, right on its target, so **1,110,922** blocks produced **33,327,660 M**, and the reward is paid per block with nothing that re-scales it for timing. The rate is set in code: the RewardTree hard fork at block 2,300,000 cut it from **112.5 M** to **30 M** in July 2025, and no MemeCore client release since has changed it. The next 90 days mint the same **33.33M M**.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and this is where a calendar and the chain disagree. An unlock calendar for MemeCore books a cliff of **56.11M M** on the second of every month — **168.33M** in this window and the same again in the next. But the MemeCore contracts sized to the foundation, core-contributor, investor and treasury allocations — **700M**, **650M**, **600M** and **100M** — hold exactly what they held when they were funded in March 2025. Coins that vest on paper but never leave the contract are not supply on the market, so the framework books what was released, which is nothing, and carries the undrawn balance as an overhang.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **927.73M M**, and it is the line that decides this window. Seven MemeCore reserve contracts hold every coin outside the circulating count: their balances add up to the gap between issued and circulating M to within a fraction of one coin, so any coin that leaves them joins the counted float by definition. On **Aug 19 2026** the largest reserve sent **925.93M M** out through the MemeCore bridge, and the coins were delivered to ZeroStack, a Nasdaq-listed company, as its treasury in exchange for shares; the deal priced them at **$1.08** each. In September a second reserve paid out another **1.80M M** in small transfers. Whoever holds those coins now, they moved from outside the float to inside it, so they count in full. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: there is no estate, no trustee schedule and no long-dated lock unwinding, and the lock-up in the treasury deal covers the company's shares, not the coins.

## Buy pressure: where new M goes

Very little leaves. Buy #1, a programmatic buyback, is **zero**: nothing in MemeCore's code routes fees or rewards into buying M back. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is about **600 M** over the window. On MemeCore the base part of each transaction fee is destroyed rather than paid to the validator — checked on a single transaction, where the sender paid the full fee, the validator received only the tip, and no address received the rest. Two random samples of **3,000** and **2,000** blocks, and a third estimate built from the window's **1.25M** transactions, all land between **587 M** and **606 M**. That is roughly **6.6 M** a day on about **14,000** transactions a day — about a fifth of one block's reward. The burn address itself did not move and the chain's supply field does not subtract fees, so the burn is only visible in the fee accounting, which is why it was read there.

Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**. The MemeCore Foundation approved a buyback of at least **$10M** on **Jul 2 2026**, after M lost more than seventy percent of its price in late June, and said the coins would go to its treasury wallet — but it published no address, no dates and no sizes. None of the seven reserve contracts grew this window, so any coins it bought are still inside the counted float and remove nothing from it. Buy #4, a new long-term lock, is **zero**: no new lock with a stated size appeared this window, coins held in the MemeCore staking contract stay inside the circulating count, and a validator's stake is handed back in full when it quits.

## Foundation and overhang

The MemeCore overhang is unusually large. Seven reserve contracts hold **3.14B M**, about **138%** of the circulating supply: the reserve that made the August release, now **724.07M**; three contracts at **700M**, **650M** and **600M** that have never moved; a payout reserve at **315.52M**; and two more at **100M** and **50M**. None of them runs on a published schedule, and the monthly calendar tranches have been accruing inside them rather than leaving. Two further balances are watched but not counted as reserves, because they already sit in the float: the **925.93M M** now held by the listed company, and the foundation's buyback wallet, whose address and size are undisclosed. All seven reserve contracts are read on-chain every refresh. If any of their balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How M compares to other memecoin chains

The closest mechanical comparison is Dogecoin, the oldest memecoin chain. Both mint a fixed number of coins per block with no cap on the schedule, so both carry a steady base inflation that never fades on its own. The difference is what sits behind the float. Dogecoin was mined into circulation from the start, so it has no project reserve and no unlock risk; its supply moves only with its block reward. MemeCore launched with **5B M** issued at genesis, most of it parked in project-controlled contracts, so its supply can jump by hundreds of millions of coins in a single day whenever a reserve decides to release — as it did on **Aug 19 2026**.

Against other proof-of-stake chains, MemeCore sits at the simple end. Many staking chains mint new coins but offset part of it with a fee burn large enough to matter; on MemeCore the burn is too small to offset anything, about **600 M** a quarter against **33.33M** minted. And against memecoins that live as tokens on another chain, M differs in the obvious way: those tokens have a fixed supply set at launch, while M keeps minting through the block reward until its **10B** maximum is reached. With **5.42B M** issued so far, that ceiling is a long way off — more than thirty years at the current block reward.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the seven MemeCore reserve contracts, holding **3.14B M**: any release like the one on **Aug 19 2026** would dwarf everything else in the ledger, and none is announced. Second, the unlock calendar's next tranches on **Oct 2 2026**, **Nov 1 2026** and **Dec 2 2026**, **56.11M M** each: so far no tranche has ever left its contract, and if one does it enters the ledger at the next refresh. Third, the **925.93M M** held by the listed company — already counted as circulating and still unmoved — the receiving wallet has never sent a transaction — but its sales would show up as market supply even though they add nothing new to this ledger. Fourth, the foundation's **$10M** buyback: if it names a wallet outside the float, bought coins would start to count as removed. Fifth, any MemeCore client release that changes the **30 M** block reward, which a hard fork has changed once before.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads MemeCore's M at **+42.18% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+1.46%** over the next 90. The structural mechanism is a block reward of **30 M** per block with almost no fee burn to offset it, plus a reserve of **3.14B M** that is larger than the entire float and releases on no published schedule — its **925.93M M** release on **Aug 19 2026** made this window. The key risk is the next reserve release, which can arrive without warning and at any size. The ceiling is the **10B M** maximum supply, of which **5.42B** has been issued so far.
