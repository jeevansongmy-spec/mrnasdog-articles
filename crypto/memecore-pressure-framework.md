MemeCore mints **30 M with every block** at a 7-second block time and does almost nothing to take any of it back, so the M supply grew **33.33M** over the last 90 days — **+2.50%** of the circulating float — and is on track to repeat that over the next 90. We measured that mint twice, straight off the chain, and it lands on exactly 30 M a block. Every other lever in the MemeCore ledger reads zero: not one of the big allocation contracts moved a single M all window, no protocol buyback exists, the gas burn is a rounding error, and the **$10M** foundation treasury buyback approved on **Jul 2 2026** has no disclosed timing, no published wallet and no purchase visible on-chain. Our supply monitor reads **+2.63%** for the same window — a gap of only **0.13 percentage points**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MemeCore Pressure Framework reading for M is **+2.50%** net new supply over 90 days: **33.33M** of block-reward emission against effectively **zero** absorption, on a circulating float of **1,335.47M M**. Our independent supply monitor reads **+2.63%** over the same window, a gap of just **0.13 percentage points** — well inside tolerance, so no warning flag is raised, and the two methods agree that M is a chain that only mints. The agreement is more than cosmetic: the monitor watches the counted float, the framework watches the chain, and the amount the float gained is almost exactly the amount the MemeCore validators were paid — which is what you would expect when no locked allocation reaches the market. M is **structurally inflationary on a thin, heavily-reserved float**: the emission is modest against the 10B cap, but it lands on a float that is only about an eighth of the eventual supply.

## Sell pressure: where new M comes from

One mechanism, and it is a clock. MemeCore mints **30 M** with every block under Proof-of-Meme and routes it through the system reward contract at **0x1234000000000000000000000000000000000002**. Block time is a flat **7 seconds** — measured across the full 90-day window at **7.000s** exactly, with no drift — which is **1,110,859 blocks** and **33.33M** new M in the trailing window; the next 90 days project to **33.33M** at the same rate. We derived that number two independent ways and they agree to four decimal places. First, the reward contract itself retained its documented **10%** Viral Grants cut, climbing from **10.03M M** to **13.37M M**. Second, the seven MemeCore validator reward wallets it pays gained **29.99M M** between them, each one topped up roughly every hour by about **1,983 M**. Retained plus paid out is **33.33M**, or **30.00 M** per block on the nose — no halving, no decay schedule, no mid-window change.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** — and on MemeCore that is a measured zero, not an assumed one. The large allocation contracts are readable on-chain, so we read every one of them at both ends of the window: **1,650M**, **700M**, **650M**, **600M**, **317M**, **245M**, **100M** and a tail of smaller pots, all holding exactly the same balance on **Aug 15 2026** as on **May 17 2026**. Nothing left them. MemeCore has never published a per-cliff release calendar for its non-community allocations and no unlock tracker carries a MemeCore schedule, so there is no calendar entitlement to diverge from either. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero** — the capacity is enormous but nothing fired. Sell #4 is **zero** too: M has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new M goes

Almost nowhere, which is the whole story of the M ledger. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: nothing in the MemeCore protocol repurchases or retires M, so there is no encoded absorption to read. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** in practice — but not in principle. MemeCore does burn a base fee on every transaction, and it is readable block by block. The problem is scale: the chain carries barely one transaction per block, and the **Mar 25 2026** hard fork cut gas a hundredfold to **15 gwei**, so a full 90-day window destroys roughly **560 M** — about a two-thousandth of one percent of the float. It rounds to zero and will keep rounding to zero until MemeCore actually gets used.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is the row worth watching and it still ships at **zero**. The MemeCore Foundation approved a strategic treasury buyback of at least **$10M** on **Jul 2 2026**, days after M fell roughly 82% between **Jun 22 2026** and **Jun 29 2026**. But the foundation explicitly declined to disclose execution timing or method to avoid front-running, the receiving treasury wallet has never been named, and repurchased M goes to that treasury rather than to a burn address — so even a fully executed buyback would move M from the float into a custodied overhang, not destroy it. We went looking anyway: sweeping the 300 largest accounts on MemeCore at both ends of the window turned up no accumulation remotely the size of a **$10M** purchase. Announced capacity with nothing observable behind it is not a flow, and the framework does not credit capacity. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**: no new lockup contract, staking cap or escrow with an announced size was deployed, and MemeCore staking unbonds freely, so staked M is never removed from supply.

## Foundation and overhang

The reserve behind M dwarfs its float. Against **1,335.47M** circulating sits an on-chain total supply of **5,402.79M** against a **10,000M** cap, leaving roughly **4.07B M** outside the counted float — split across a foundation allocation of **15%**, core contributors at **13%**, investors at **12%** and a meme treasury at **2%**, none of it on a published release calendar. That is the single largest unpriced risk in the MemeCore ledger: not what is being minted, but what could be released with no notice.

Three smaller overhangs sit beside it, and one of them is new. The seven MemeCore validator reward wallets now hold about **146.8M M** between them and have never paid a single M back out — every block reward they have ever received is still sitting there, growing at about **30M** a quarter. The system reward contract holds a further **13.37M M** of undistributed rewards and gains roughly **3.3M** every 90 days as it retains its 10% cut. And the unnamed foundation treasury wallet designated to receive buyback M is an overhang in waiting: opaque today, and trackable only through official disclosure until an address surfaces. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How M compares to other uncapped-emission Layer 1 chains

M sits in the awkward middle of the emission spectrum. Unlike a halving-model chain, where the subsidy is protocol-encoded to fall on a fixed calendar and the terminal supply is knowable to the coin, MemeCore's **30 M** per block carries no decay schedule at all — it is a governance variable, changeable by hard fork, rather than a constant. And unlike the fee-burning smart-contract Layer 1s, where a base-fee burn scales with usage and can flip a chain net-deflationary at high load, MemeCore has the burn but not the load. That is the sharpest structural contrast in this comparison: the same EIP-1559 mechanism that makes a busy chain deflationary does nothing on a near-idle one, because a burn is only as big as the traffic feeding it.

The closer analogue is the uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1 that pays validators from new issuance, but even there M differs on float. Most such chains circulate a large share of their supply, so new issuance lands as a modest percentage of an already-wide market. M circulates roughly a quarter of its on-chain total and about an eighth of its cap, which concentrates the same emission onto a much thinner base — that is why a mint worth only **0.33%** of the cap reads as **+2.50%** against the float. The comparison that matters most is with exchange tokens running quarterly buybacks: those retire supply on a published schedule against verifiable on-chain destruction. MemeCore has announced a buyback but sends it to a treasury, withholds its timing and does not name the wallet — mechanically the opposite of a burn, and unverifiable besides.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether any of the **$10M** treasury buyback approved on **Jul 2 2026** becomes visible — a published MemeCore treasury wallet address, or a disclosed execution report, would move Buy #3 off zero for the first time. Second, whether the seven validator reward wallets, now holding about **146.8M M**, ever start spending; the first outflow from that stack lands in Sell #3 and would be the biggest change to this reading in a year. Third, whether the system reward contract keeps retaining its 10% cut or begins releasing the **13.37M M** it now holds. Fourth, any hard-fork proposal touching the **30 M** per block reward, since that figure is the only input that changes the Sell #1 number. Fifth, any first-ever publication of a vesting calendar for the **4.07B M** of non-community allocations — the largest single thing that could change this reading.

## Summary

M is structurally inflationary at **+2.50%** per 90 days, driven by a single mechanism — **30 M** minted per block at a 7-second block time on MemeCore, or **33.33M** per window ahead — with no vesting cliff, no protocol buyback, no material fee burn and no confirmed foundation purchase offsetting it. The key risk is not the emission itself but the **4.07B M** sitting outside the float with no published release calendar, plus the **146.8M M** the MemeCore validators have quietly accumulated and never spent, against a float of only **1,335.47M**. The **10,000M** cap is real and about half of it is already minted, but the cap constrains the decade rather than the quarter. Our supply monitor agrees with the framework at **+2.63%**: on a chain whose only supply mechanism is minting, both reads point the same way — up.
