## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reading for MemeCore is **+42.38%** net new supply over the trailing 90 days — **959.26M M** of sell pressure against **0M** of absorption, measured against a circulating float of **2,263.67M M**. Our independent supply monitor reads **+73.20%**, a gap of **30.82 percentage points**, so the page carries a warning chip. The gap is not a disagreement about what happened: the monitor measures a supply increase of **953.64M M** and the framework measures flows of **959.26M M**, within six-tenths of a percent of each other. The whole difference is the base — the framework divides by today's float, the monitor by the float 90 days ago, and **959.26M** is **42.38%** of the first number and **73.63%** of the second. MemeCore is best described as **a quiet chain with a loud vault**: the protocol emission is small and predictable, and everything that matters comes out of reserves nobody has published a calendar for.

## Sell pressure: where new M comes from

Sell pressure on MemeCore has two sources this window and only one of them is a protocol mechanism. Protocol inflation contributed **33.33M M**: the MemeCore chain mints **30 M per block** at a 7-second block time, and the window from May 23 2026 to Aug 21 2026 contained **1,110,846** blocks. We do not take that 30 from a document — MemeCore's docs publish no per-block reward figure at all — we measure it. Reading the reward contract's balance one block apart returns exactly 30 M, at the start of the window, at the end, and at eight heights in between, so the emission is flat and no re-basing applies. Two further reads corroborate it: MemeCore's lifetime coin supply reconciles to within **0.05%** under a 30-per-block rule, and the counted float grew by exactly **0.37M M** on each quiet day, which is 30 M a block.

The second source is the one that moves the reading. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks contributed **925.93M M**. On Aug 19 2026 MemeCore's largest reserve contract fell from **1,650M** to **724.07M**, and the chain's bridge lock rose by the identical **925.93M**, putting the same quantity of M onto BNB Chain as a freely transferable token. The following day, Aug 20 2026, MemeCore principals announced they were contributing **925,925,926 M** — valued at **$1.08** each, **$1.0 billion** in total — to a Nasdaq-listed company in exchange for stock and warrants. The ten-year lock-up written into that deal attaches to the shares the sellers receive, not to the M they hand over. Vesting unlocks contributed **0**: MemeCore publishes allocation shares but no release calendar, no unlock tracker carries one for M, and across 120 days the float shows exactly one step — this one. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**; there is no estate, trustee or court schedule attached to M.

## Buy pressure: where new M goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **0**. There is no programmatic buyback written into MemeCore's own code, so nothing repurchases or retires M automatically. The protocol fee burn is real and readable block by block — MemeCore charges a 15 gwei base fee and destroys it — but the chain is close to empty at roughly one transaction per block, so the entire 90-day window burns about **663 M**, which against a float above two billion rounds to zero. Foundation buying is **0** as well: the MemeCore Foundation announced a Strategic Treasury Buyback of at least **$10M** on **Jun 30 2026**, after M fell roughly 80% in late June, but it explicitly declined to disclose the timing or method, repurchased M is routed to an unnamed foundation treasury wallet rather than a burn address, and no accumulation of anything near that size appears among the chain's largest accounts. Announced capacity with nothing observable behind it counts as zero. New long-term locks are **0** too — no lockup contract or staking cap with an announced size was deployed, staking on MemeCore unbonds freely, and the Nasdaq deal locks equity rather than tokens.

## Foundation and overhang

MemeCore's team-controlled overhang is the dominant fact about this token, and the Aug 19 2026 release is proof it is live rather than theoretical. What remains, all read on-chain at both ends of the window: the reserve contract that funded the Nasdaq contribution still holds **724.07M M**, and four more allocation contracts hold **700M**, **650M**, **600M** and **317.32M**, with a further **244.55M**, **100M** and a tail of smaller pots behind them — roughly **2.42B M** outside the counted float with no published release calendar, refreshed every 24 hours by chain read. The seven MemeCore validator reward wallets hold **148.85M M** between them and have never paid any of it out. The system reward contract retains a further **13.60M M** of undistributed rewards and gains about **3.33M** every 90 days. The buyback treasury wallet has never been named, so it is monitored through official disclosure rather than on-chain. One correction worth recording: the widely reported claim that the Aug 19 2026 bridge mint had no collateral on the MemeCore chain does not survive a direct read — the BNB-side M supply of **1,155.42M** matches the native bridge lock to five decimals. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How M compares to other Layer 1 chains

Mechanically MemeCore sits in the uncapped-per-block, capped-in-total family: a fixed **30 M** per block with no halving and no decay, running until the **10B** ceiling is reached. That is a very different animal from a halving chain, where the emission schedule is the whole story and the float is already most of the supply. On MemeCore the emission is the small half of the story — **1.47%** a year of grind against a float that is under a quarter of the eventual total. The risk lives in the other half.

Against continuous-emission Layer 1s that burn fees, MemeCore lacks the counterweight entirely. A fee-burning chain converts activity into absorption, so a busy chain can outrun its own mint. MemeCore cannot: at roughly **0.16** transactions per second, about **$9.60** of total network gas a day, seven validators and no chain-level value tracked by any aggregator, there is no activity to burn against, and the burn stays a rounding error no matter how the price moves. The nearest structural analogue is not another meme chain at all — it is a Layer 1 whose float is controlled by a small number of identified contracts, where the supply question is a governance question rather than a code question.

And that is the comparison that matters. On a halving chain you can compute the next decade of issuance from the protocol. On MemeCore the protocol tells you **33.33M** per 90 days and the reserves told you **925.93M** in a single day. Any chain whose largest supply event is a discretionary transfer rather than a coded emission belongs in a different risk bucket from one whose supply is fully determined by code.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether the Nasdaq contribution announced **Aug 20 2026** closes — the warrant portion needs a shareholder vote, and the M tokens themselves carry no lock either way, so the position is sellable regardless of how the equity side resolves. Second, whether the remaining **724.07M M** in that same reserve contract follows the first tranche; it is the single most consequential balance on the chain. Third, whether the **$10M** foundation buyback announced **Jun 30 2026** ever appears on-chain — if a wallet is finally named, the Foundation buy row can move off zero for the first time. Fourth, whether the **148.85M M** sitting in the seven validator reward wallets starts paying out, which would convert accrued emission into live float. Fifth, whether MemeCore ever publishes a release calendar for its non-community allocations; the absence of one is why the vesting row is a measured zero rather than a scheduled figure.

## Summary

MemeCore's coded emission is modest and fully measurable — **30 M** per block, **33.33M** per 90 days, flat, with no burn and no executed buyback to offset it. Its discretionary supply is not: a single unscheduled release of **925.93M M** on **Aug 19 2026** did more to the M float in one day than the protocol will do in two years, and roughly **2.42B M** still sits in reserve contracts with no published calendar. The framework reads **+42.38%** for the trailing 90 days and **+1.47%** for the next 90, and that second number is only honest for as long as the vault stays shut. The ceiling is **10B M**; the constraint that matters is not the ceiling but the discretion.
