That routine emission was then buried by one event: on **Aug 19 2026** MemeCore's largest reserve contract released **925.93M M**, and the tokens landed in a treasury address that held **zero** at the start of this window — a contribution by MemeCore principals to a Nasdaq-listed company in exchange for stock. Total sell pressure for the window is **959.26M M** against **zero** buy pressure, a net of **+42.32%** on a **2,266.74M** float. ## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reading for MemeCore is **+42.32%** net new supply over the trailing 90 days — **959.26M M** of sell pressure against **0M** of absorption, measured against a circulating float of **2,266.74M M**. Our independent supply monitor reads **+73.29%**, a gap of **30.97 percentage points**, so the page carries a warning chip. The gap is not a disagreement about what happened. The monitor measures a supply increase of **959.01M M** and the framework measures flows of **959.26M M** — within **0.03%** of each other. The whole difference is the base: the framework divides by today's float, the monitor by the float 90 days ago, and **959.26M** is **42.32%** of the first number and **73.31%** of the second. MemeCore is best described as **a quiet chain with a loud vault**: the MemeCore protocol emission is small and predictable, and everything that matters comes out of reserve contracts whose release calendar does not survive contact with the chain.

## Sell pressure: where new M comes from

Sell pressure on MemeCore has two live sources this window and only one is a protocol mechanism. Protocol inflation contributed **33.33M M**: the MemeCore chain mints **30 M per block** at a 7-second block time, and the window from May 31 2026 to Aug 29 2026 contained **1,110,846** blocks. We do not take that 30 from a document — MemeCore's docs publish no per-block reward figure — we measure it. Reading the reward contract's balance one block apart returns exactly 30 M, at the start of the window, at the end and at two heights between, so the emission is flat and no re-basing applies. A wholly separate read confirms it to four decimal places: MemeCore's seven validator reward wallets each gained **4,284,687 M** across the window, **29.99M** combined, which is precisely the 90% validator share of a 30-per-block mint, with the remaining 10% left sitting in the reward contract.

Vesting unlocks contributed **0**, and that zero is now a measured one rather than an absence of information. A dated release calendar does exist for M: **56.11M M** a month, on the 2nd, split across team, foundation, community and investor allocations, with three firings inside this window — **Jun 2**, **Jul 3** and **Aug 2 2026**, **168.33M M** on paper. Not one of them moved anything. The MemeCore contracts holding those allocations read **700M**, **650M**, **600M**, **317.32M**, **244.55M** and **100M** — identical to the token at both ends of the window, and identical again a full year back. The counted float shows no step on any cliff date either. When the escrow is readable and the calendar and the chain disagree, the chain wins: what vests on paper is not sell pressure until it moves, so the row is **0** and the undrawn entitlement is carried as overhang.

The source that moves the reading is the unscheduled one. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks contributed **925.93M M**. On Aug 19 2026 MemeCore's largest reserve contract fell from **1,650M** to **724.07M**, its bridge lock rose by the same amount, and BNB Chain minted the mirror — putting that quantity of M into circulation as a freely transferable token. We read the destination itself at both ends of the window: the receiving treasury address held **0 M** on May 31 2026 and holds **925,925,926 M** today, matching the issuer's disclosure to the unit. MemeCore principals contributed those tokens — valued at **$1.08** each, **$1.0 billion** in total — to a Nasdaq-listed company for stock and warrants. The ten-year lock-up in that deal attaches to the shares the contributors receive, not to the M they handed over. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**: there is no estate, trustee or court schedule attached to M.

## Buy pressure: where new M goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **0**. There is no programmatic buyback written into MemeCore's own code, so nothing repurchases or retires M automatically. The protocol fee burn was checked on both surfaces and on both chains M lives on: no dead address gained anything worth counting, and the token supply on the second chain rose rather than fell. MemeCore does charge a burnable **15 gwei** base fee, but the chain runs about one transaction per block, so the entire 90-day window destroys roughly **506 M** — two hundred-thousandths of one percent of the float. The direction is deflationary; the size rounds to zero. Foundation buying is **0** as well: the MemeCore Foundation approved a strategic treasury buyback of at least **$10M** on **Jul 2 2026**, after M fell about 82% between Jun 22 and Jun 29 2026, but it explicitly declined to disclose timing or method, routes repurchased M to an unnamed foundation treasury wallet rather than a burn address, and left no trace: the **28** largest accounts on the chain were read at both ends of the window and the only material movers were the reserve, the bridge lock and block reward. Announced capacity with nothing observable behind it counts as zero. New long-term locks are **0** too — no lockup contract or staking cap with an announced size was deployed, MemeCore validator stake is refunded on exit, and the Nasdaq deal locks equity rather than tokens.

## Foundation and overhang

MemeCore's team-controlled overhang is the dominant fact about this token, and the Aug 19 2026 release is proof it is live rather than theoretical. Every balance below was read on-chain at both ends of the window. The reserve contract that funded the Nasdaq contribution still holds **724.07M M** — static for a full year before it fired once. Six more allocation contracts hold **700M**, **650M**, **600M**, **317.32M**, **244.55M** and **100M**, none of which has moved a token in twelve months, for roughly **3.34B M** of identified reserve against **3.14B M** of total supply outside the counted float. The seven MemeCore validator reward wallets hold **151.61M M** between them and have never paid any of it out. The system reward contract retains a further **13.90M M** of undistributed block rewards and gains about **3.33M** every 90 days. The cross-chain bridge lock holds **1,155.42M M**, backing the mirrored supply exactly. The buyback treasury wallet has never been named, so it is watched through official disclosure rather than by address. One correction worth recording: the widely repeated claim that the Aug 19 2026 bridge mint had no collateral on the MemeCore chain does not survive a direct read — the mirrored supply matches the native bridge lock to four decimals at **both** ends of the window. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How M compares to other Layer 1 chains

Mechanically MemeCore sits in the uncapped-per-block, capped-in-total family: a fixed **30 M** per block with no halving and no decay, running until the **10B** ceiling is reached. That is a different animal from a halving chain, where the emission schedule is the whole story and the float is already most of the supply. On MemeCore the emission is the small half — about **1.47%** a quarter of grind against a float that is under a quarter of the eventual total. The risk lives in the other half.

Against continuous-emission Layer 1s that burn fees, MemeCore lacks the counterweight entirely. A fee-burning chain converts activity into absorption, so a busy chain can outrun its own mint. MemeCore cannot: at roughly **0.16** transactions per second, about **$9** of total network gas a day and seven validators, there is nothing to burn against, and the burn stays a rounding error however the price moves. The nearest structural analogue is not another meme chain — it is a Layer 1 whose float is controlled by a small number of identified contracts, where the supply question is a governance question rather than a code question.

That is the comparison that matters. On a halving chain you can compute the next decade of issuance from the protocol. On MemeCore the protocol tells you **33.33M** per 90 days, the published calendar tells you **168.33M** that never arrived, and the reserves told you **925.93M** in a single day. Any chain whose largest supply event is a discretionary transfer rather than a coded emission belongs in a different risk bucket from one whose supply is fully determined by code.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the three scheduled cliffs — **Sep 2 2026**, **Oct 2 2026** and **Nov 1 2026**, **56.11M M** apiece — and specifically whether the escrow contracts finally drain; three consecutive firings have now passed without moving a balance, and the first one that does flips the vesting row off zero. Second, whether the remaining **724.07M M** in the reserve contract follows the first tranche; it is the single most consequential balance on the chain. Third, whether the Nasdaq contribution closes — the warrant portion needs a shareholder vote, and the M carries no lock either way, so the position is sellable regardless of how the equity side resolves. Fourth, whether the **$10M** foundation buyback approved **Jul 2 2026** ever appears on-chain; if a wallet is finally named, the Foundation buy row can move off zero for the first time. Fifth, whether the **151.61M M** sitting in the seven validator reward wallets starts paying out, which would convert accrued emission into live float.

## Summary

MemeCore's coded emission is modest and fully measurable — **30 M** per block, **33.33M** per 90 days, flat, with no burn and no executed buyback to offset it. Its discretionary supply is not: a single unscheduled release of **925.93M M** on **Aug 19 2026** did more to the M float in one day than the MemeCore protocol will do in two years, and roughly **3.34B M** still sits in reserve contracts that have not moved a token in a year. The published unlock calendar is not the risk — **168.33M** of scheduled cliffs delivered nothing this window — the unscheduled release is. The framework reads **+42.32%** for the trailing 90 days and **+1.47%** for the next 90, and that second number is only honest for as long as the vault stays shut. The ceiling is **10B M**; the constraint that matters is not the ceiling but the discretion.
