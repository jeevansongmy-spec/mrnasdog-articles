POL supply shrank a little over the last 90 days, because one large fee burn outweighed the mint that runs every day. From Aug 25 to Nov 23 2026 the mint added **52.38M POL** while a single burn on Sep 23 2026 removed **100.00M POL**, a net change of **−0.45%**; other trackers show **−0.46%**. POL has no supply cap, and the next 90 days point to about **−0.31%** if the second burn goes ahead on Dec 22 2026.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

POL is in the mixed, roughly steady band. Over the 90 days to Nov 23 2026 the count of POL fell by **0.45%**, and other trackers read **−0.46%**, a difference of about one hundredth of a point, so the coin page carries no warning note. The shape is unusual: new POL arrives every single day, while the burn comes in rare, large batches. A quarter with a batch looks deflationary; a quarter without one would show plain growth of about **0.49%**. For the next 90 days we expect the mint to stay the same and a second batch of **85.93M POL** to be burned, which leaves the supply down about **0.31%**.

## Sell pressure: where new POL comes from

Protocol inflation is the only real source of new POL. Once a day the POL contract creates about **582K POL**, a rate of about 2% a year that rises slowly because it grows with the supply. Half goes to the validators and stakers who secure Polygon, and half goes to the Community Treasury. From Aug 25 to Nov 23 2026 the mint added **52.38M POL**, and nothing changed the rate along the way.

Vesting unlocks add nothing. POL replaced MATIC one for one in 2024, and MATIC had finished unlocking years earlier, so no locked batch is waiting to be released.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks also add nothing to the count. The Community Treasury moved **108.58M POL** to a newer treasury wallet on Oct 14 and Nov 10 2026, but those coins were already circulating before they moved.

Long-term locked coins and bankruptcy payouts add nothing either. About **70.49M POL** stolen in the Polymarket and Binance hacks of Oct 29 and Oct 30 2026 sits frozen in five blocked addresses. Those coins were circulating before the theft, and a return to their owners would move them, not create them.

## Buy pressure: where new POL goes

The protocol fee burn is POL's one real sink. Every transaction on Polygon pays a base fee, and that fee is saved up rather than destroyed on the spot. A collector then burns it in batches of up to **100M POL**, at most once every 90 days, and anyone may start a batch once the wait is over. The first batch burned **100.00M POL** on Sep 23 2026, sending the coins to a dead address on Ethereum where nobody can ever spend them.

The next batch opens on Dec 22 2026. The collector already holds **85.93M POL**, after the fee wallet passed on **64.31M POL** on Oct 30 2026, so that is the amount we book for the next 90 days. The fee wallet keeps a further **13.03M POL** of newer fees, and the batch could grow toward its 100M limit if that is passed on first.

There is no programmatic buyback. A draft plan to turn payment fees into POL purchases for the burn has no start date, so it books zero. There is no foundation buying either, and staking does not take POL out of circulation, so the 100.7M POL the treasury staked on Oct 14 2026 also books zero.

## Foundation and overhang

The Community Treasury, which receives half of each day's mint, held about **3.79M POL** on Nov 23 2026. The newer treasury wallet it funded holds **7.88M POL** in liquid coins and has **100.7M POL** staked. These are the coins the Polygon Foundation and Polygon Labs can spend on grants and growth, and they could reach the market at any time without changing the count.

The fee wallets are the other large holders: **85.93M POL** in the burn collector, which can only send its coins to be burned, and **13.03M POL** in the wallet that receives base fees first. That first wallet can also pay part of the fees back to apps under Polygon's gas rebate programs, so not every POL in it is sure to be burned.

## How POL compares to other Ethereum scaling chains

Most Ethereum scaling tokens have a fixed maximum supply and add coins through team and investor unlocks, with no burn at all. POL works the other way round: there is no cap and no unlock calendar, but a steady 2% yearly mint is balanced by a fee burn.

Ethereum itself is the closest match in design, paying new coins to validators and burning base fees. The difference is timing. Ether is burned in every block, so its supply moves smoothly; POL's fees wait in a wallet and leave in large batches, so its 90-day figure jumps between quarters with a burn and quarters without one.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Dec 1 2026**: the higher staking reward paid from saved-up priority fees is set to end. This changes how existing POL is shared among stakers, not how much POL exists.

**Dec 22 2026**: the second fee burn opens, with **85.93M POL** waiting. If nobody starts it, the next 90 days would show growth of about 0.49% instead of a fall.

**Dec 31 2026**: the gas rebate for AI-agent payments is due to end, which would send more of each base fee toward the burn.

**Proposed Nov 5 2026**: a Payments Growth Program would pay payment apps back part of the base fees they generate. If it passes, future burns would be smaller than the fees suggest.

**Proposed Nov 4 2026**: a hard fork to return the **70.49M** stolen POL to Polymarket and Binance. It has no date yet and would not change the supply.

The mint added **52.38M POL**, while one fee burn on Sep 23 2026 removed **100.00M POL**.

POL has no cap and no vesting, so the mint and the batch burns are the whole story.
