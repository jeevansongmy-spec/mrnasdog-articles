Over the last 90 days Polygon minted about **52.1M POL** in fixed 2% annual emission on a **~10.69B** circulating base, and nothing offset it on the token itself — no buyback, and the celebrated base-fee burn does not reduce the canonical POL supply. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads POL at **+0.49%** net for the window and **+0.49%** forward; our independent supply monitor also reads **+0.49%**, a gap of about **0.00 percentage points** — inside tolerance, no flag. POL is uncapped and slowly, structurally inflationary — the opposite of the "net-deflationary" headline the burn tracker suggests.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending Aug 5 2026, the Pressure Framework reads **Polygon at +0.49% net** on both the last-90-day and the forward view, because the same fixed 2% emission governs both. Our supply monitor independently reads the realised change at **+0.49%**, putting the gap at roughly **0.00 percentage points** — well inside tolerance, so there is **no monitor-gap flag** on this build. That agreement is itself the story: the monitor measures the on-chain **POL** total supply on Ethereum, and that supply rose by the full emission, confirming that the **EIP-1559** base-fee burn on Polygon PoS is not removing POL from the token the market actually holds. Total sell pressure was **52.1M POL** against **0** of buy pressure. The label that fits **Polygon** is **slowly inflationary and uncapped** — a low, one-directional emission with no offset and no ceiling.

## Sell pressure: where new POL comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, contributed **52.1M POL** over the window, and it is the only active sell row on the page. Polygon mints new **POL** at a fixed **2% a year**, compounded on the current total supply, and splits it in two: **1%** is paid to validators and stakers as rewards, and **1%** is minted into the **Community Treasury** that funds ecosystem grants. Both halves are freshly minted, so both increase the on-chain **POL** total supply — which is why the full 2% shows up as protocol inflation and drives the observed **+0.49%** exactly. Applying that rate across 90 days on the ~10.69B base gives the ~52.1M figure. Because **POL** is uncapped, this mint has no end date; it continues until **Polygon** governance votes to change it, and a draft proposal to do exactly that is discussed in the final section.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and structurally so. **POL** replaced **MATIC** one-for-one, and MATIC's original team, foundation and advisor allocations finished their multi-year vesting years ago — the final founder tranche was claimed back in 2022 — so there is no live unlock calendar and no cliff inside the window. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero** for this window: although the Community Treasury and the Polygon Foundation hold sizeable balances, neither was observed distributing into the market during the window, and unused capacity is not a release. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** — there is no bankruptcy estate and no locked tranche attached to **POL**.

## Buy pressure: where new POL goes

Polygon's buy side is empty on the token, and this is the most important and most counter-intuitive part of the read. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: Polygon runs no buyback contract, and a community forum proposal to introduce a treasury buyback-and-burn is still only a draft. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is also **zero** against the canonical supply — and that deserves care, because **Polygon** markets an **EIP-1559** base-fee burn as making the network "net-deflationary," with some trackers quoting roughly a million **POL** burned a day. That burn is real, but it runs on the **Polygon PoS** child chain, and it does not reduce the **POL** ERC-20 total supply on Ethereum — the token that exchanges list and that the market prices. The project's own burn documentation is explicit that sending POL to a collector or a dead address on PoS leaves the Ethereum contract's total supply unchanged, and under the March 2026 fee routing most base fees are held pending rebates rather than permanently destroyed. The on-chain proof is decisive: over the 90-day window the canonical **POL** supply rose by the full emission, with no burn visible in the total.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** — the Polygon Foundation has disclosed no open-market **POL** purchase programme and no accumulation wallet has been identified. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is also **zero**: staking on Polygon, including the **sPOL** liquid-staking token launched in April 2026, uses an unbonding period rather than a permanent supply-locking contract, so even a large staked balance is not removed from supply the way a burn or a multi-year lock would be. With every canonical buy row at zero, there is no offset anywhere on the page to the ~52.1M of fresh issuance.

## Foundation and overhang

Polygon has two team-controlled overhangs worth tracking, both governance-gated rather than sitting on a dump schedule. The first is the **Community Treasury**, which receives the 1% treasury tranche of emission — on the order of **100M POL a year** — and spends it through ratified governance proposals such as the Community Grants Program; every disbursement is dated and readable on-chain. The second is the **Polygon Foundation and ecosystem** holdings, a multi-hundred-million-POL balance used for operations and historically sold in measured amounts. Neither fired a coordinated market distribution in the window, so Sell #3 books zero — but if either balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. Because the treasury tranche is itself part of the 2% mint, it is already counted inside Sell #1 as new supply; the overhang note tracks where that minted POL sits before it is spent.

## How POL compares to other uncapped emission chains

Polygon sits with the **uncapped, continuous-emission** proof-of-stake L1s rather than the hard-capped halving chains. Against Bitcoin, with its fixed 21M ceiling and code-locked subsidy, **POL** has no cap at all and a rate that governance can change — its 2% is a policy choice, not a scarcity guarantee. The closer comparison is Ethereum and Solana. Ethereum burns its base fee at the protocol level, so heavy usage can push its net supply negative; Solana burns half of every transaction fee directly against its own issuance. The crucial difference is where the burn lands: Ethereum's and Solana's burns reduce the same canonical L1 supply their emission inflates, so the two net against each other. **Polygon's** base-fee burn happens on a child chain and does not net against the Ethereum **POL** mint, so however busy Polygon PoS gets, the burn does not turn the token deflationary.

That distinction is why **POL** can look deflationary on a burn dashboard while its actual token supply climbs. Compared with exchange tokens that run quarterly buyback-and-burns — which reduce their own circulating supply and can post genuinely deflationary quarters — **Polygon** currently removes nothing from the **POL** supply and adds a steady 2%. The practical result is that **POL's** supply growth is **low, uncapped and one-directional**: it is not fast inflation, but there is no mechanism today that can make the token scarce, which is the exact opposite of the marketing narrative and the single most important thing for a holder to understand.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the draft **tokenomics proposal** to eliminate the 2% emission and add a treasury buyback-and-burn: if it moves from forum idea to a ratified governance vote, Sell #1 would fall and a real Buy row could open for the first time. Second, any change to the **PIP-82** fee routing or the rebate cap that expires **Dec 31 2026** — a switch to permanently burning base fees on Ethereum, rather than holding them on PoS, would finally let the burn reduce the canonical supply. Third, any large **Community Treasury** or Foundation spend, which would move POL from a controlled balance into circulation and lift Sell #3. Fourth, the pace of the final **MATIC-to-POL** migration, now ~99% complete — the remaining tail is small but still mints POL as it converts. Fifth, staking-rate shifts from **sPOL** adoption, which change how much emission is claimed but not the total minted.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Polygon at **+0.49% net supply growth** over the last 90 days and **+0.49%** projected forward — supply roughly steady on the surface, but still one-directionally growing. The structural mechanism is a fixed **2% annual POL emission**, split 1% to stakers and 1% to the Community Treasury, with no buyback and no burn that reaches the canonical token supply. The key risk is precisely that there is no offset: **POL** is uncapped, the burn everyone cites runs on the **Polygon PoS** chain and does not reduce the Ethereum total supply, and only a governance vote can change the emission. On the supply the market actually prices, **Polygon** is slowly inflationary — a low, bounded-rate but ceiling-less mint that the celebrated base-fee burn never touches.
