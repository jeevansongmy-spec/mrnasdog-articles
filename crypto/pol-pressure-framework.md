POL is the gas and staking token of Polygon, and over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **54.21M POL** of sell pressure against **51.99M POL** of buy pressure, a net of **+0.02%** on a circulating base of **10.70B POL**. Polygon mints **2%** a year by protocol rule and the base fee on every Polygon transaction takes almost exactly the same amount back out, so the two cancel. Our supply monitor reads **+0.42%** for the same window, a gap of **0.40 percentage points**, inside tolerance. POL is an inflationary token by design, held flat by a fee sink that has never released a token.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 26 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **POL at +0.02% net**: sell pressure of **54.21M POL**, against buy pressure of **51.99M POL**, on a circulating base of **10,703,065,413 POL**. The next 90 days read **+0.00%**, because the emission is fixed by rule and the fee sink is projected at the rate it actually ran. Our supply monitor reads **+0.42%** across the same period, a gap of **0.40 percentage points** — under the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag is raised on this build and no deep walk was required. The gap is not noise and it is not an error on either side: Polygon emission is minted on Ethereum, where the counted supply lives, while Polygon base fees are taken out of circulation on the Polygon chain, where the counted supply cannot see them. A supply-delta reading of POL therefore sees the whole mint and none of the offset. The right label for POL is an uncapped chain running a fee sink almost exactly the size of its own emission.

## Sell pressure: where new POL comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **52.71M POL** over 90 days, and it is the dominant mechanism on this token. Polygon issues POL at **2%** a year, compounded on total supply, through a single emission contract, and this build did not take that rate on trust — it enumerated every mint on the Ethereum POL contract across the window. There were **91** of them, one for each day, and every one landed in the same emission contract, which split each batch exactly in half: **26,355,862 POL** to the Polygon staking contract for validators and delegators, and **26,355,862 POL** to the Polygon Community Treasury. Total supply moved from **10,650,353,689** to **10,703,065,413**, and the sum of the mints equals that difference exactly, which also proves no POL was destroyed on Ethereum. Measured against the schedule, realised issuance came in at about **101%** of the documented 2% — this is a mint-on-schedule design with no claim gate, so nothing goes unclaimed. The per-day mint itself grew from **577,838** to **580,666** across the quarter, which is the compounding appearing directly in the data and, incidentally, the proof that no mechanism change landed mid-window.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: the original MATIC allocation schedule finished, its final vested tranche was released in **Feb 2024**, and POL itself was created one-for-one against the old token with no vesting contract of its own. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **1.49M POL**, and it is smaller than it looks from the outside: the Polygon Community Treasury sent nothing at all this quarter. A sender-side scan of the entire window returned zero outgoing transfers, and the treasury balance simply rose from **60,117,329** to **86,473,191 POL** on its half of the mint. What reached holders came from two grant-paying contracts funded in earlier quarters, one draining **371,984 POL** to empty and the other **1,121,685 POL** to empty. Because those tokens were minted in earlier windows, booking them here does not double-count Sell #1. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** — POL has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution. A fifth row carries the MATIC migration at **zero**, and it is the row a careless reading of a migration token gets wrong: MATIC total supply is still exactly **10,000,000,000**, because migrated MATIC is escrowed rather than burned, and the **31,058,962 POL** released this quarter matched the MATIC received to four decimal places.

## Buy pressure: where new POL goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**. Polygon does not buy back POL. A governance proposal to end the 2% emission entirely and spend treasury revenue on buying and burning POL has been under discussion since **Oct 2025**, and it is still a draft — its own timetable put an on-chain vote in early 2026, no vote was held, and the mints kept arriving through **Aug 26 2026**. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **51.99M POL**, and it is the reason this page does not read as a straightforwardly inflationary token. Every Polygon transaction pays a base fee that leaves circulation, routed first into a fee wallet and then swept into a collector contract. Read at both ends of the window, the pair went from **109,991,747** to **161,976,598 POL**, a rise of **51,984,850**, and the trajectory across a full year is monotonic — **13.65M** a year ago, **74.73M** in February, **161.98M** now — with nothing ever released.

One honest qualification belongs on that number, and it is the difference between this reading and a marketing claim. The collector is described in its own governance proposal as a temporary holder contract, upgradeable by a multisig, and returning those tokens to Ethereum for a genuine burn would require a further protocol change. So the POL is out of the tradable float but not out of existence, and the framework treats it as sequestration rather than destruction — counted on the buy side, and listed again as an overhang. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**: no open-market purchase of POL by the project has been disclosed or observed. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, and asked in both directions it is arguably negative: the Polygon staking contract held **3,733,482,249 POL** in May and **3,655,242,078 POL** in August, and that is after receiving **26,355,862** of fresh rewards, so roughly **104.6M POL** came back out of staking. The liquid staking receipt launched this year is freely tradable, which makes it a claim on a position rather than a lock.

## Foundation and overhang

Three POL balances are worth watching and none of them is on a published release calendar. The Polygon Community Treasury holds **86,473,191 POL** and grows by roughly **26.36M** a quarter automatically; it deployed **80,000,000 POL** in a single day on **Mar 30 2026** and then paused for five months, so its spending is lumpy and undated rather than steady. The fee collector and its routing wallet together hold **161,976,598 POL**, which is larger than the treasury and is the single biggest discretionary balance attached to Polygon, because a multisig can upgrade the contract holding it. The migration escrow holds **380,636,869 POL** against **380,634,731** of un-migrated MATIC, but that belongs to MATIC holders rather than to Polygon, and its release is a swap of supply that already exists. Every one of these balances is re-read on each refresh, and if any of them falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How POL compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake chains

POL belongs to the uncapped continuous-emission class — the same structural family as the large proof-of-stake chains that pay validators out of new issuance rather than out of a fixed schedule. What separates them is whether anything runs the other way. A chain that mints for staking and burns nothing compounds its float forever, and its Pressure Framework reading is simply its emission rate. POL mints **2%** a year, which is at the low end of that class to begin with, and then routes the base fee out of circulation, which is a mechanism most staking chains do not have at all. The comparison that fits best is with the fee-burning smart-contract chains: like them, POL destroys demand-linked value rather than a fixed quantum, so the offset scales with usage and disappears when usage does.

Two things make POL unusual even inside that comparison. The first is that its mint and its burn live on different chains — POL is issued on Ethereum, where the counted supply is measured, and consumed on Polygon, where it is not — so every supply-delta reading of POL overstates its inflation by the size of the offset. That is a reporting artefact of a multi-chain design, not a flaw in the token. The second is that the offset is not yet permanent. A fee-burning chain destroys the token in the same transaction; Polygon parks it in a holder contract pending a future protocol change. That distinction does not change the float today, but it is the difference between a mechanism nobody can reverse and a balance a multisig could redirect, and it is why POL is read here as flat rather than as deflationary.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the fee sink's pace: it took in about **635,000 POL** a day over the first month of the window and about **508,000** a day over the last, while the mint is fixed — if that decline continues, POL turns visibly inflationary without any policy changing. Second, the Community Treasury's **86,473,191 POL**, which is loaded and whose grant contracts are all empty, making a fresh deployment more likely than at any point since **Mar 30 2026**. Third, the tokenomics proposal to end the 2% emission and start a treasury buyback: it has been a draft since **Oct 2025**, and an on-chain vote would be the single largest change to this page. Fourth, any protocol change that finally moves the collector's balance to Ethereum to be destroyed for real — that would convert **161.98M POL** of parked supply into a permanent removal. Fifth, the migration tail: **380,634,731** of the old token has still not crossed over, and while the swap is supply-neutral, a sudden acceleration signals a large dormant holder waking up.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads POL at **+0.02%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 26 2026** and **+0.00%** for the 90 days ahead: an uncapped chain whose fixed **2%** emission of **52.71M POL** is almost exactly matched by **51.99M POL** of base fees leaving circulation on the Polygon chain. The structural mechanism is a mint on one chain paired with a sink on another, which is why our supply monitor reads **+0.42%** for a token whose real float barely moved. The key risk is that the offset is usage-linked and already slowing, and that it is a holder contract rather than a burn — a multisig can upgrade it, so the tokens are parked rather than gone. POL has **no cap at all**, so nothing about this balance is guaranteed to persist: it holds for exactly as long as Polygon keeps collecting fees at roughly the rate it keeps minting.
