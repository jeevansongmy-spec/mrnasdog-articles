Polygon runs one mint and one burn, and over the last 90 days they almost exactly cancelled. POL's fixed **2%** annual emission minted **52.1M POL**, while the Polygon PoS base-fee burn removed **54.8M POL** — so the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **-0.03%** net, a supply that is flat with the faintest shrink. Our supply monitor reads **+0.54%**, and the **0.56 percentage point** gap is not a disagreement about facts: the burn happens on the Polygon chain and never reduces the Ethereum POL token's own supply number, so the monitor sees the mint with none of the burn. Vesting ended in **2022** and there is no buyback, which makes POL an unusually simple two-force token — and a finely balanced one.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 14 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **POL at -0.03% net** for the trailing window and **-0.02%** for the forward window. Our supply monitor reads **+0.54%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.56 percentage points**. That is above the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so a data-conflict chip ships on the POL overview page — but the cause is structural rather than an error on either side. Polygon's burn destroys POL on the Polygon PoS chain; the canonical supply figure everyone quotes is the ERC-20 token on Ethereum, and burning on Polygon does not shrink it. A supply-delta monitor therefore reads mint-only by construction, and the gap is the burn itself, almost to the decimal. POL is best characterised as **a fixed-emission token held near flat by a usage-dependent burn**.

## Sell pressure: where new POL comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **52.1M POL** over 90 days, and it is the entire sell side. Polygon mints a fixed **2%** of total supply each year, compounded, split evenly: **1%** to validator staking rewards and **1%** to the Polygon Community Treasury. That figure is not taken from the documentation — it is read off the POL token contract at both ends of the window, where total supply rose from **10,643.99M** on **May 16 2026** to **10,696.10M** on **Aug 14 2026**. Annualised that is a **1.98%** pace, and the trailing-year read of **+2.05%** confirms the rate has not moved. PIP-26 tapered the validator share from 2% to 1.5% to 1% and finished that taper in June 2025, so the June 2026 period boundary passed with no step-down; the next 90 days mint about **52.3M POL** on the slightly larger base. The treasury half counts here, as new supply, the moment it is minted.

Every other sell row is zero, and for structural reasons. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero** because Polygon has nothing left to unlock: the original MATIC allocations to the ecosystem, the foundation, the exchange sale, the team, advisors, early investors and the old staking-rewards pool all completed their release in **2022**, and the vesting contract was emptied years ago. POL inherited a fully unlocked cap table when it replaced MATIC one-for-one. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** for this window: the Community Treasury grew by exactly its share of the mint and distributed effectively nothing, and no discretionary Polygon Foundation release was disclosed. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**, as no bankruptcy estate or trustee schedule touches POL.

## Buy pressure: where new POL goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **54.8M POL** over 90 days, and it is the only thing standing against the mint. Polygon PoS runs an EIP-1559 fee market in which every transaction's base fee is destroyed rather than paid to validators. Since the Lisovo hardfork in **March 2026**, base fees land first in a routing wallet under the agentic-commerce gas programme and are then swept in batches into the burn collector contract, which has never released anything: its balance has climbed from **13.7M POL** a year ago to **121.6M POL** today. Reading the routing wallet and the collector together at both ends of the window — **101.1M POL** on **May 16 2026** against **155.9M POL** on **Aug 14 2026** — gives the **54.8M POL** figure. That is measured at the window edges deliberately, because Polygon's burn rate has swung violently: a burn averaged over the past half year would be dominated by an early-2026 spike that has since rolled off, and would overstate the offset.

The other three buy rows are zero. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**: Polygon has never repurchased POL, and the activist proposal to end the 2% emission and spend a fifth of net revenue on buying and burning POL has been debated since late 2025 without being enacted. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero**, with no open-market POL buying disclosed by Polygon Labs or the Polygon Foundation. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** as well: no new multi-year lock or escrow was announced, and staking does not qualify, since staked POL still counts inside the circulating figure. Worth noting anyway, the Ethereum stake contract **fell 43.6M POL** across the window, which loosens the float even though it changes no supply number.

## Foundation and overhang

Two team-controlled overhangs sit behind POL. The first is the Polygon Community Treasury, which held **83.0M POL** on **Aug 14 2026** against **56.9M POL** on **May 16 2026** — a rise of **26.1M POL**, which is precisely its 1% half of the window's mint, meaning it spent essentially nothing into the market. Over the trailing year it tells a different story: it received roughly **107M POL** and grew only **10.6M**, so about **96M POL** went out through the Community Grants Program, whose second season alone allocated **35M POL**. Those grants are not counted a second time, because that POL was already booked as new supply when the protocol minted it. The second overhang is the Polygon Foundation's own operational treasury, which publishes no wallet list — its size cannot be confirmed on-chain, and it drew community criticism in **July 2026** over unannounced sales, so it is tracked through disclosure rather than balances. If either the Community Treasury or the Polygon Foundation treasury sees its balance fall between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How POL compares to other fee-burning smart-contract chains

POL belongs to the **uncapped, fee-burning smart-contract chain** class — networks like Ethereum and BNB Chain that pair continuous issuance with an EIP-1559 base-fee burn, so their net supply is a race between a fixed mint and a usage-dependent destruction. Within that class POL is the cleanest example of the trade, because everything else has been switched off: vesting is finished, there is no buyback, no foundation programme and no lockup. Ethereum's equivalent balance shifts with staking participation and blob demand; POL's sell side is a single, invariant **2%** number, which means the entire question is whether Polygon transaction demand burns more or less than **52M POL** a quarter.

The structural quirk that separates POL from its peers is where the burn happens. On Ethereum the burn reduces the same supply figure the market quotes; on Polygon the burn destroys POL on the Polygon PoS chain while the quoted supply is the ERC-20 on Ethereum, so the headline number keeps rising at the full **2%** even in quarters when more POL is burned than minted. That is why a supply-delta reading of POL and a mechanism reading of POL diverge by roughly the burn — **0.56 percentage points** this window — and why Polygon can accurately call itself net-deflationary while every supply chart shows a rising line. Against a hard-capped halving asset the contrast is sharper still: that asset's issuance falls on a fixed calendar regardless of usage, whereas POL's issuance is fixed and its offset is entirely demand-driven.

The risk that distinguishes POL from a buyback-funded exchange token is that its buy side is not contractual. A token that spends a share of revenue on repurchases has a floor under its buy pressure; POL's floor is whatever people choose to transact. That matters right now, because the burn is fading: the newest month of the window burned near **15M POL** against about **22M POL** in the oldest month. The framework projects the trailing rate forward, so the forward reading stays at **-0.02%** — but at the newest pace, a full quarter would burn closer to **45M POL** and POL would read slightly inflationary rather than slightly deflationary.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the burn rate above all: it is the only variable on the buy side, and it has fallen from roughly **22M** to roughly **15M POL** a month across this window, which is the difference between POL reading deflationary and inflationary. Watch the Polygon governance forum for the tokenomics-revision proposal that would end the **2%** emission and add a treasury buyback and burn — it has been an undecided draft since late 2025, and passing it would rewrite both sides of this ledger at once. Watch the agentic-commerce gas programme, which recycles up to **$1M** of base fees away from the burn and runs until **Dec 31 2026**, for any extension or expansion. Watch the Community Treasury near **83.0M POL** for the next grants season, and the Polygon Foundation for the treasury transparency it was pressed for in **July 2026**, since either could turn Sell #3 non-zero. And watch the Ethereum stake contract near **3.68B POL**, which shed **43.6M** this window — continued unstaking loosens the float even when supply is flat.

## Summary

Polygon is a two-force token in fine balance. A fixed **2%** annual emission minted **52.1M POL** over the 90 days to **Aug 14 2026**, split between validator staking rewards and the Community Treasury, and the Polygon PoS base-fee burn removed **54.8M POL** over the same window — leaving the MrNasdog Pressure Framework at **-0.03%** net, essentially flat with a hair of shrink. With vesting finished in 2022, no buyback and no lockup, nothing else moves the number. The key risk is that POL's only offset is demand-driven and currently fading, so a quiet quarter tips the balance straight back to inflation. And the key caveat for anyone reading a supply chart is that Polygon's burn never reduces the Ethereum POL supply figure — which is why our monitor reads **+0.54%** against the framework's **-0.03%**, a **0.56-point** gap that is the burn rather than a mistake.
