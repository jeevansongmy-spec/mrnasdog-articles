# MNT Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Mantle put no new MNT into the market over the last 90 days and took almost none out, so the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads MNT at **0.00%** trailing and **0.00%** forward. That is a measured zero rather than an unexamined one: the number of MNT in existence was **6,219,316,794.889999** at both ends of the window, and all six Mantle Treasury wallets — **2,917.0M MNT**, **46.90%** of every MNT that exists — held balances identical to the last wei on both dates. Sell pressure is **0**, buy pressure is **0**, and the whole future of the number sits with a treasury that spends only when a vote says so.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **3,302.3M MNT**, the framework books **0** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **0.00%** — and projects **0.00%** for the next 90 days. The inflation monitor reads **+0.02%** for the same window, a gap of **0.02 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so this page ships with no monitor-gap warning. The double zero is earned on each side separately. On the sell side, both terms of the tradable float were read from chain state at both window ends: total supply, which never moved, and the excluded treasury bucket, which never moved either. On the buy side, both destruction surfaces were read at both ends, and the only MNT destroyed all quarter was **1 MNT** sent to a dead address on **Jul 19 2026** by an unrelated wallet. The label for MNT is a fully unlocked token with a switched-off mint and a very large treasury overhang: nothing is being added, nothing is being removed, and one governance decision is the entire risk.

## Sell pressure: where new MNT comes from

Nowhere, this window. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**. Mantle pays no block reward in MNT, and the count of MNT in existence read **6,219,316,794.889999** at the window's first block and at its last, identical to the wei. That flat reading was checked for the trap it usually is: the figure lives in a storage slot the contract writes to, and the value does not appear anywhere in the contract's compiled code, so the flatness is a real measurement and not a hardcoded constant being mistaken for stability. Mantle also does have a live mint function — but its cap parameter reads **0** at both window ends, and calling the mint from the owning multisig reverts on the contract's own check comparing the requested amount to that zero cap. A live mint with a zero cap is a different state from no mint at all, which is why this row is tagged as watched rather than closed permanently: raising the cap takes one multisig transaction, and the ceiling governance may set is **2.00%** of supply per 365-day interval.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because Mantle has nothing left to unlock. The release calendar finished in **2023**, no vesting or escrow contract holds MNT today, and an independent unlock tracker reports Mantle fully unlocked with no event inside the window and none scheduled. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**, and this is the row that decides the page. Mantle publishes its own supply definition — circulating supply is total supply minus Treasury Holdings — so the treasury balance is the only quantity on this chain that can create float. Six named Mantle Treasury wallets across the settlement layer and Mantle itself sum to **2,917,022,412.353159 MNT**, which is exactly total supply minus circulating supply with **0.000000** left over. That closure matters twice: it proves the bucket is fully enumerated, and it proves that every one of those six balances being identical to the wei on both dates means the counted float did not change. The dominant wallet, holding **2,900.0M MNT**, has not transferred MNT since **Aug 26 2025**, so the flat reading is not a pass-through that drained and refilled. The most recent release that actually reached the float was **Apr 28 2026**, when **24.4M MNT** moved into a custody wallet the classifier counts as tradable — 45 days before this window opens.

Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: MNT has no bankruptcy estate, trustee or court-ordered distribution. Sell #5 is the honest caveat, and it is booked at **0** with the number shown anyway. **3.2M MNT** left Mantle's budget wallets inside the window — **1.2M** on the settlement layer in three payments dated **Jun 30 2026**, **Jul 21 2026** and **Aug 17 2026**, and **2.0M** on Mantle itself. Mantle's published definition classifies budget wallets as circulating, so those coins were already counted as tradable and moving them released nothing new. Treat budget wallets as held back instead and this row reads **3.2M MNT** and the page's net becomes roughly **+0.10%**. That is the single largest judgment call on this reading, and it is one edit away from being reversed.

## Buy pressure: where new MNT goes

Also nowhere. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: Mantle runs no programme that spends treasury money repurchasing MNT, none was announced or executed inside the window, and the treasury's MNT balances did not rise by a wei. A phase-based treasury burn of **3–8%** of supply over 12 to 24 months was floated for community discussion on **Feb 25 2026** and never reached a vote, so under the framework's rules it moves neither side of the ledger. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and Mantle is an interesting case here. Unlike most Ethereum layer 2s, Mantle charges gas in its own token, which sounds like the setup for a usage-linked burn — but the fees are parked rather than destroyed. Mantle's three fee vaults grew together from **164.0K** to **205.3K MNT** across the window, collecting **41.2K MNT** that stayed in the float. Both destruction surfaces were read at both window ends on both networks rather than trusting either alone: total supply was unchanged, and of the three unspendable addresses only one moved, receiving exactly **1 MNT**. The two surfaces disagreeing is the finding, not an error — MNT is only ever removed by transfer to an unspendable address, never by a supply-reducing call. Tracing that one transfer by sender matters, because a burn funded out of a bucket already excluded from the float is worth nothing: this one came from a third-party wallet on **Jul 19 2026**, so it is genuine float destruction, of one coin.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** — all six Mantle Treasury wallets were flat, so there was neither accumulation nor distribution, and there is no accumulation wallet to track because there is no buyback feeding one. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**, and the only opt-in lock on the chain moved the other way: the two rewards-station contracts held **7.15M MNT** between them at the start of the window and **7.06M** at the end, a net release of **95.2K MNT**. It would not have counted even had it risen, because the only balances excluded from Mantle's tradable figure are those six treasury wallets, so MNT sitting in any staking or rewards contract is still counted as float and locking it there cannot reduce this reading. Buy #5 carries the largest movements of the quarter, and they are worth exactly nothing. MNT exists twice — as the settlement-layer token and natively as gas on Mantle — and the second is a mirror of what is locked in the rollup escrow, which rose from **350.5M** to **350.7M MNT**. A second mirror opened mid-window when Mantle moved its cross-chain portal to a new messaging provider in **Jul 2026**: a lock-and-release pool went from empty to **5.5M MNT**. Both lock coins on one ledger and issue matching coins on another, so global tradable supply is unchanged, and booking either as a lock would have manufactured a deflationary page out of plumbing.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on MNT is enormous, fully enumerated, and entirely discretionary — the opposite shape from a coin on a vesting schedule. The dominant item is the main Mantle Treasury multisig at **2,900.0M MNT**, refreshed from chain state at every rebuild. Beside it sit **10.0M MNT** in a retiring settlement-layer treasury wallet and **7.0M MNT** spread across four treasury wallets on Mantle itself, all four flat. Those six together are the excluded bucket, and their sum reproduces total supply minus circulating supply with a residual of **0.000000**, so there is no seventh wallet hiding anywhere. A seventh item is worth watching even though it is already counted as tradable: a custody treasury wallet holding **24.4M MNT**, which is where the **Apr 28 2026** release landed. There is no buyback accumulation wallet and no bankruptcy residual. One more overhang is not denominated in MNT at all: **113.9M** of the legacy token that MNT was converted from remains unmigrated, of which the treasury holds **105.5M**. The conversion is still open and still trickling — **12.2556** legacy tokens converted inside the window, on **Jul 17 2026** — but it creates no MNT, because total supply did not rise and no excluded wallet moved, so the MNT handed over came from inside the float. The trigger sentence applies to all of it: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How MNT compares to other layer-2 gas tokens

Mantle belongs to a small class: Ethereum layer 2s that charge gas in their own token rather than in ETH. That choice ties token demand to chain activity, and it is usually paired with a fee burn — the mechanism that lets an active chain read genuinely negative. Mantle has the first half and not the second. Its fees accumulate in protocol vaults instead of being destroyed, so usage cannot compress supply no matter how busy the chain gets. Against a layer 2 that pays gas in ETH and emits its own token on a published schedule, MNT is the stricter design on paper: those chains print into the float every quarter on a calendar nobody has to vote on, while Mantle finished its release calendar in **2023** and has issued nothing since. Against an exchange token running quarterly buybacks and burns, MNT is far weaker — those tokens convert revenue into removal and can hold a negative reading for years, while MNT's destruction over 90 days was **1 MNT**.

The comparison that actually explains MNT is with a treasury-heavy DAO token rather than with any layer 2. Roughly half of all MNT — **46.90%** — sits in a governance treasury that is outside the counted float and has no release schedule at all. That is not a hard cap protecting a holder; it is a queue with a voter at the front of it. A capped, fully-unlocked token with a live-but-zeroed mint reads flat until someone decides otherwise, and the decision can go either way: the same treasury that could deploy **2,917.0M MNT** into the market is also the treasury a burn proposal would draw from. Because that bucket sits outside the float, a treasury burn would genuinely reduce supply on this reading rather than merely reclassifying it — which is why the unvoted **3–8%** proposal is the single most consequential thing on Mantle's governance forum.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the six Mantle Treasury wallets, which are the only balances that can change this number: they are read from chain state at every rebuild, and any movement off **2,917.0M MNT** shows up immediately. Second, the mint cap parameter, currently **0**: the 365-day interval gate elapsed back on **Jun 19 2024**, so the only thing standing between MNT and up to **2.00%** annual issuance is one multisig transaction setting that parameter above zero. Third, the phase-based treasury burn floated on **Feb 25 2026** — if it reaches a vote and passes, it would be the first mechanism in MNT's history capable of pushing this page negative, and at **3–8%** of supply it would push it hard. Fourth, the core budget for Mantle's third cycle, opened for discussion on **Aug 28 2026**: budget wallets are classified as circulating, so a refill from the treasury into a budget wallet would register as a real release even though the spending afterwards would not. Fifth, the cross-chain escrow and the lock-and-release pool, at **350.7M** and **5.5M MNT**, which are mirrors today — if either stopped issuing matching coins on the destination side, they would stop being mirrors and start being locks.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads MNT at **0.00%** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** projected forward: mixed flows, supply roughly steady. The structural mechanism is absence — Mantle minted nothing, has nothing left to vest, runs no buyback and burns no fees, and held a fixed on-chain ceiling of **6,219.3M MNT** throughout the window. The key risk is not issuance but concentration: **2,917.0M MNT**, **46.90%** of the total, sits in a governance treasury with a spender rather than a schedule, and a single passed proposal could move more supply in one transaction than most chains issue in a year. The comfort is that the same structure works in reverse — because that treasury sits outside the counted float, a burn drawn from it would be a real reduction, not an accounting change.
