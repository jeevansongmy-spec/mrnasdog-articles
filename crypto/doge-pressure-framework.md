# DOGE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

DOGE supply is growing and is projected to keep growing. Dogecoin pays its miners a flat **10,000 DOGE** in every block, and in the 90 days to Sep 29 2026 that created **1,227,230,000 new DOGE** while nothing was burned, bought back or locked — a net rise of **+0.79%** of the circulating supply, against **+0.81%** on our independent monitor. Dogecoin has no supply cap and no halving, so the same number of new coins arrives every 90 days, forever, unless the code is changed.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the Dogecoin supply grew by **+0.79%** of the **156.11B DOGE** in circulation, and the next 90 days are projected at the same **+0.79%**. Our independent monitor, which reads the supply a different way, shows **+0.81%** for its own 90 days — a gap of only **0.03 percentage points**, well inside our half-point tolerance, so no conflict warning is shown. Across a year the same rule adds about **4.98B DOGE**, or about **3.19%** of today's supply. DOGE is a steady, uncapped proof-of-work coin: built to be inflationary, with nothing on the other side of the ledger.

## Sell pressure: where new DOGE comes from

All of the new DOGE comes from one place: protocol inflation, the mining reward. Since block 600,000 in 2015 every Dogecoin block pays its miner exactly **10,000 DOGE**, and the reward never halves. We counted every block in the window, from block 6,271,272 on Jul 1 2026 to block 6,393,994 on Sep 29 2026: **122,723 blocks**, each one paying exactly 10,000 DOGE, for **1,227,230,000 DOGE** of new supply — about 13.6M DOGE a day.

The chain is running a little slow. Dogecoin aims for one block a minute, but blocks in this window came about every **63 seconds**. Because the reward is paid per block, not per minute, a slower chain issues fewer coins: the textbook figure of 129,600 blocks would have overstated the real Dogecoin issuance by about 5.6%. We use the measured count, and the forward estimate keeps the same pace because nothing in the Dogecoin code has changed. Miners also collected about 818,000 DOGE in fees over the window, but fees are existing coins changing hands, not new supply.

The other three sell rows are zero. There are no vesting unlocks: Dogecoin launched in December 2013 with no premine, no token sale and no team or investor share, so every DOGE that exists was mined and nothing is waiting to be released. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero because no team-held wallet moved coins into the market this window. The long-term locked and bankruptcy row is zero because no estate, trustee or long lock holds DOGE. A listed company did sell about 463M DOGE on Jul 20 2026, but it had bought them on the open market, so those coins were already circulating and the sale created no new supply.

## Buy pressure: where new DOGE goes

Nothing takes DOGE out of supply. There is no programmatic buyback: the Dogecoin network earns no protocol revenue, and every fee goes straight to the miner who found the block. There is no protocol fee burn either. The supply rose by exactly the mining rewards, block for block. An old burn address holds about **1.85B DOGE** and received only about 14 DOGE this window from people sending coins there by choice, but those coins still count as circulating, so they remove nothing.

There was no Foundation buy: the Foundation's listed company reported holding no DOGE at Jun 30 2026, and its planned reserve arm is not operating. There is no new long-term lock, because Dogecoin is a proof-of-work chain with no staking. DOGE moved to another chain through a new gateway that opened on Sep 7 2026 still counts as circulating, and fund shares hold coins bought on the open market. The buy side of the Dogecoin ledger is **0 DOGE** on all four rows.

## Foundation and overhang

Because Dogecoin never had a team allocation, its overhang is small. The one team-controlled wallet we track is the Dogecoin Foundation's development-fund multisig, which holds about **5.0M DOGE** and did not move this window; we re-read it on the chain at every rebuild. The Foundation's corporate arm, which went public on Jul 1 2026, reported no DOGE holdings, and its reserve subsidiary does not yet operate — we check its quarterly filings.

Exchange custody wallets are much larger — two of them hold tens of billions of DOGE — but those coins belong to depositors, not to any team, so they are not an overhang. Only about 470,000 DOGE sits outside the circulating count at all, so every wallet we watch is already inside the float. If the development fund's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation row of the sell ledger at the next refresh.

## How DOGE compares to other proof-of-work coins

Most proof-of-work coins follow the Bitcoin model: a reward that halves on a schedule and a hard cap on supply. Bitcoin stops at 21M coins, and Litecoin — the chain Dogecoin is merge-mined with — stops at 84M. Dogecoin gave up both. Its reward is fixed at 10,000 DOGE a block and there is no cap, so DOGE issuance never falls in coins. What falls is the rate: the same 4.98B DOGE a year is a smaller share of a bigger supply each year, so DOGE inflation drifts down slowly — about 3.19% a year today, and about 2.4% a year in ten years at the same pace.

The closest match is a tail-emission coin such as Monero, which also pays a small fixed reward per block forever instead of stopping. The difference is size: Dogecoin's fixed reward was set when its supply was already about 98B DOGE, so the flat reward still adds a few percent a year. Unlike Ethereum, which burns part of every fee, Dogecoin burns nothing, and unlike exchange tokens, nothing buys DOGE back.

Against coins with team or investor unlocks, DOGE is simpler to read: there is no unlock calendar and no cliff, so the DOGE supply path is one straight line set by block count. Its risk is not a surprise release but the steady, uncapped flow to miners, who sell part of it to pay their costs.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Oct 14 2026** — one spot DOGE fund makes its last trade and returns its coins to its investors; those coins are already circulating, so the Dogecoin supply reading does not change.

**The block pace** — blocks now arrive about every 63 seconds; if more mining power returns and the chain runs at its 60-second target, issuance would rise about 5.6%, to about 1.30B DOGE per 90 days.

**The reward-cut idea** — a proposal to cut the reward to 1,000 DOGE a block made the news again on Sep 16 2026, but it was closed in January 2025, and any change would need a new Dogecoin Core release; the last one came out on Dec 1 2024.

**Foundation wallets** — the 5.0M DOGE development fund, and the next quarterly filing of the Foundation's listed company for any sign that its reserve starts buying or holding DOGE.

## Summary

DOGE is steadily inflationary: Dogecoin paid miners **1,227,230,000 new DOGE** over the 90 days to Sep 29 2026, nothing was burned, bought back or locked, and supply grew **+0.79%**, with the same projected for the next 90 days. The mechanism is a flat 10,000 DOGE block reward with no halving and no cap, and the independent monitor agrees within 0.03 points. The key risk is the constant flow of new coins to miners, about **4.98B DOGE** a year; the only ceiling is the block count, and only a hard fork could change the reward.
