# DOGE Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
Looking for the buy-or-sell answer? It is on the DOGE coin page, with demand and price drivers: [mrnasdog.com/research/doge](https://mrnasdog.com/research/doge)
DOGE supply is growing, and it is set to keep growing at the same pace. Every Dogecoin block pays its miner **10,000 new DOGE**, with no cap, no halving and no burn to take any back. From Aug 21 to Nov 19 2026 miners received **1.23B DOGE**, nothing left the supply, and the net change was **+0.78%** in 90 days, close to the **+0.69%** other trackers show.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Aug 21 to Nov 19 2026, Dogecoin's supply grew by **+0.78%**, and the next 90 days should add the same **+0.78%**. Other supply trackers show **+0.69%** for the same stretch, a difference of **0.10 points**, small enough that the coin page carries no warning note. Every new DOGE comes from mining, and nothing in the network removes coins, so the whole reading is one steady flow. The label that fits: a fixed-mint proof-of-work coin with nothing pulling supply back.

## Sell pressure: where new DOGE comes from

The only source of new DOGE is the mining reward. Each block pays **10,000 DOGE**, a flat amount that has not changed since 2015 and has no planned end. The network found **122,890 blocks** between Aug 21 and Nov 19 2026, a little under one a minute, so miners were paid **1,228,900,000 DOGE**, about **13.65M DOGE a day**. Miners usually sell part of what they earn to pay for power and machines, which is why this flow counts as sell pressure. If blocks come exactly once a minute, the 90-day mint can reach about 1.30B DOGE; the recent pace has run a bit slower than that.

Vesting unlocks are **zero**. Dogecoin launched in December 2013 with no presale, no team share and no investor share, so there is no locked batch waiting to be released. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are also **zero**: the Dogecoin Foundation's developer fund holds **5,003,401.97 DOGE** and has sent out only 0.1 DOGE since it was set up, and those coins already count as circulating. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **zero** too. No court or failed firm is holding DOGE for a later payout, and CleanCore's sale of its **463M DOGE** treasury in July 2026 moved coins that were already circulating, so it added no new DOGE.

## Buy pressure: where new DOGE goes

Every buy row is **zero**. There is no programmatic buyback, because Dogecoin has no company, no revenue and no contract that could buy coins back. There is no protocol fee burn either: transaction fees are paid to miners on top of the reward, so they stay in circulation. People can still send DOGE to a dead address by choice, but only **0.13 DOGE** went to the best-known one between Aug 21 and Nov 19 2026.

There is no Foundation buy: the Dogecoin Foundation funds developers and does not buy DOGE on the market. Spot funds and treasury companies trade coins that are already circulating, so their buying does not shrink the count. There is no new long-term lock, since Dogecoin has no staking and no lock-up. A public proposal to cut the block reward to 1,000 DOGE was opened and closed in January 2025 and never adopted, so the reward stays at 10,000 DOGE a block.

## Foundation and overhang

Dogecoin has very little team-controlled supply. The one known wallet is the Dogecoin Foundation's developer fund, a multi-signature wallet that holds **5,003,401.97 DOGE**. It pays **500,000 DOGE** to contributors with each qualifying Dogecoin Core release, and no Core release has come since December 2024, so it sent nothing out in this window. House of Doge, the Foundation's former business arm, reported no DOGE of its own on Jun 30 2026, and its next quarterly report is late. Both are checked on chain and in filings at every rebuild; if the developer fund's balance falls between checks, that outflow shows up as sell pressure in the next update.

## How DOGE compares to other proof-of-work chains

Bitcoin and Litecoin, the chain Dogecoin is mined alongside, both cut their block reward in half every four years and stop near a fixed cap of 21M BTC and 84M LTC. Their new supply shrinks over time. Dogecoin chose the opposite: a flat **10,000 DOGE** per block forever, with no cap. Because the amount is fixed while the pile keeps growing, the yearly rate slowly falls on its own, to about **3.2% a year** today, but it never reaches zero.

Monero is the closest match. It also settled on a small reward that never ends, so miners are always paid in new coins rather than fees alone. Like Monero, Dogecoin has no burn and no buyback to soften the new supply. Unlike Ethereum, where part of every fee is destroyed, a busy day on Dogecoin does not remove any coins. Demand alone has to absorb about **13.65M DOGE** every day.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The DogeOS app layer, which finished its audits and was called "very, very close" to launch on Nov 11 2026, could bring new uses for DOGE, but it creates no new coins.

House of Doge must drop "Doge" from its name by Dec 31 2026 and still owes its late quarterly report, which would show any DOGE it now holds.

A new Dogecoin Core release would trigger a **500,000 DOGE** payout from the developer fund; none is scheduled as of Nov 19 2026.

The block pace sets the mint: at one block a minute the next 90 days would bring about 1.30B DOGE, against **1.23B DOGE** at the recent pace.

The US spot Dogecoin funds have had no money in or out since Oct 13 2026; a return of inflows would be the first sign of fresh buying from funds.

## Summary

Dogecoin's supply grew **+0.78%** from Aug 21 to Nov 19 2026, and the next 90 days should add the same. Each block pays miners **10,000 new DOGE**, which came to **1.23B DOGE** in the window, while no burn, buyback or lock takes any back. There is no cap and no halving, so the main risk is simple: buyers must keep absorbing about 13.65M new DOGE a day. Nothing is waiting to vest, and the only known team wallet, the Foundation's 5.0M DOGE developer fund, sent nothing out.
