# DOGE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Dogecoin pays its miners a flat **10,000 DOGE** per block, has done so without a halving since 2015, and nothing in the protocol takes a coin back out. Across the 90 days to **Sep 24 2026** the Dogecoin chain produced **122,757** blocks and created **1,227,570,000 DOGE**, against a buy side of **zero**. That is **+0.79% net** on the MrNasdog Pressure Framework, against a supply-monitor reading of **+0.81%** — a gap of **0.03 percentage points**, which is agreement, not conflict. DOGE is inflationary by design and uncapped, adding about **4.98 billion DOGE** a year with no mechanism that offsets it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 24 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **DOGE at +0.79% net**: the sell side is one row — the Dogecoin block subsidy, **1,227,570,000 DOGE** — and all four buy rows are zero. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+0.81%**. The gap is **0.03 percentage points**, well inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so DOGE ships with **no data-conflict flag**. The forward column reads the same **+0.79%**, because the Dogecoin subsidy is a constant and the block rate held flat across three separate 30-day legs of the window. The label for DOGE is **permanently inflationary by fixed tail emission**: a proof-of-work coin with no cap, no halving, no burn and no lock, whose float grows by about **3.2% a year** — a percentage that falls only because the float underneath it keeps growing.

## Sell pressure: where new DOGE comes from

It comes from exactly one place, and measuring it correctly is the whole job on this page. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1,227,570,000 DOGE**. The Dogecoin subsidy is paid per block, not per unit of time, and nothing in the protocol compensates when blocks run late — so the honest number is the block count, never the target rate. Reading the chain at both ends of the window gives Dogecoin blocks **6,264,270** through **6,387,026**, or **122,757** blocks in ninety days: a realised interval of **63.3 seconds** against Dogecoin's 60-second target, about **1,364 blocks a day** against a nominal 1,440. Taking the target on faith would have booked **1,296,000,000 DOGE** and overstated Dogecoin issuance by **5.6%**. Every block in the range was counted rather than sampled, and the total paid out equals exactly 10,000 DOGE times the block count — no block paid anything else.

The other three Dogecoin sell rows are zero, each for a different structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because Dogecoin has never had a vesting schedule: the coin launched in **December 2013** with no premine, no presale and no team or investor allocation, so every DOGE that exists arrived through a block reward. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** because no Dogecoin reserve is waiting to be released — the Dogecoin Foundation's development fund holds about **5.0 million DOGE** and has not sent a coin since 2022, and there is no DAO or allocation behind a locked contract. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: no estate distributes DOGE on a schedule. One of the window's biggest Dogecoin headlines sits here and still books nothing — the listed company that ran the best-known corporate DOGE reserve sold its last **463 million DOGE** on **Jul 20 2026** and left the asset, but it had bought those coins on the open market, so they were already inside the counted float and the sale added no new supply.

## Buy pressure: where new DOGE goes

Nowhere. The buy ledger is not merely small but empty on all four rows, and that is why DOGE cannot leave its band. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: Dogecoin takes no protocol revenue, splits no fees and holds no accumulation wallet, so there is nothing to fund a buyback with. One Dogecoin story reads like a buyback and is not: a network that merge-mines Dogecoin advertises a buyback-and-burn loop, but reading the mechanism rather than the label, it **sells** the DOGE it mines for stablecoins and buys back and burns its **own** token. No DOGE is bought and none is destroyed, and the coins it sells were already booked as new supply in Sell #1.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and both surfaces were read rather than one. Dogecoin issuance rose by exactly the block rewards across the window, so no protocol burn removed a coin. The chain's one keyless burn address was read too, and it **did** receive: **13.79 DOGE** across five transfers from outsiders, against a lifetime balance of **1.85 billion DOGE** it has held since 2014 and never spent. Those coins are still counted as circulating supply, so a send there moves DOGE within the float rather than out of it. What settles the fee question is that every Dogecoin block pays its miner the subsidy plus the fees inside it: the **826,994 DOGE** of fees paid in the window went to miners in full. There is no Dogecoin equivalent of a base-fee burn. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero** — no Dogecoin treasury accumulates, and no on-chain flow this window shows an identified group buying. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero** because Dogecoin is proof-of-work: no staking, no bonding and no lockup contract, so not one coin of the **156.04 billion** outstanding is held back from the float by the protocol.

## Foundation and overhang

Dogecoin has almost nothing outside its float, and that fact decides every overhang question on the page. The classified supply leaves only about **1.23 million DOGE** outside circulation, so any wallet larger than that is, by simple arithmetic, already counted — and its coins cannot add new supply when they move. The Dogecoin Foundation's development-fund wallet holds about **5.0 million DOGE**; it grows only by small donations, has not spent since **Dec 2022**, and is re-read on every refresh. The Foundation's listed corporate arm reported **no digital assets** at all on **Jun 30 2026**, four days into this window, and its next quarterly report is where a reserve could reappear. Beyond that, the framework watches balances it deliberately does not book: exchange custody wallets — a single custody address alone holds **27.16 billion DOGE** — which belong to depositors, and a cross-chain gateway that opened on **Sep 7 2026** locks native DOGE that was already counted. If the development fund's balance falls between refreshes, or any of these balances moves in a way that is not custody churn, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How DOGE compares to other proof-of-work coins

DOGE sits in a small group: proof-of-work with a fixed tail emission and **no cap at all**. Against a hard-capped proof-of-work chain the contrast is the halving — there the subsidy halves on a published schedule and issuance falls toward zero, while Dogecoin's subsidy never halves, so its yearly issuance stays a constant **4.98 billion DOGE** and only the percentage falls. A tail-emission privacy coin is the nearest analogue, but those tails were sized to be a fraction of a percent a year; Dogecoin's runs at about **3.2%**. Against an uncapped proof-of-stake chain the difference is which side the offset sits on: those chains usually pair issuance with a fee burn that claws part of it back, and DOGE has no burn of any kind.

The deeper contrast is with coins that beat their issuance. A chain that burns part of every fee, or funds a scheduled destruction out of reserves, can turn a positive mint into a negative net. Dogecoin has no such lever, and the fee base that would power one is small: the chain collected **826,994 DOGE** of fees over the window, roughly **$260,000** a year at the window's prices, against a market value near **$14.64 billion** — about **0.002%**. Even burning every Dogecoin fee would offset less than a tenth of one percent of the mint. DOGE's supply story is not a policy that could be tuned at the margin; it is the absence of any policy at all.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First and largest: any revival of the proposal to cut the Dogecoin block reward from **10,000** to **1,000 DOGE**, which would take yearly issuance from about 5 billion to about 500 million. Its issue was closed without a merge, no Dogecoin Core release has shipped since **Dec 1 2024**, and there is no activation height; if one ever appears, it rewrites the only row on this page. Second, the block rate itself — Dogecoin is merge-mined with Litecoin, so combined hashpower sets the interval, and the **63.3 second** average measured here is what keeps issuance 5.6% under nominal. Third, the keyless burn address, which took **13.79 DOGE** in ninety days; an organised Dogecoin burn would show there first. Fourth, the cross-chain gateway that opened on **Sep 7 2026**, float-neutral today. Fifth, the Foundation's corporate arm and its development fund — the next quarterly filing and the fund's **5.0 million DOGE** balance are the only places Sell #3 could stop reading zero.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads DOGE at **+0.79% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.79%** over the next 90, on a ledger with one non-zero row. The mechanism is a flat, halving-free Dogecoin subsidy of **10,000 DOGE** per block that created **1,227,570,000 DOGE** this window, set against a buy side that is empty — no buyback, no fee burn, no treasury bid, no lock. The key risk runs the unusual direction: the danger to holders is not that something breaks but that nothing changes, because the protocol has no lever to slow the mint and the one proposal to add one never reached an activation date. There is no ceiling either — Dogecoin is uncapped, adding about **4.98 billion DOGE** a year forever, roughly **3.2%** of today's **156.04 billion** and a slowly falling share of an ever-larger float.
