# DOGE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Dogecoin pays its miners a flat 10,000 DOGE per block and has done so, without a halving, since 2015 — and nothing anywhere in the protocol takes a coin back out. Across the 90 days to Sep 9 2026 the Dogecoin chain produced 122,861 blocks and created 1,228,610,000 DOGE, against a buy side of zero. That is +0.79% net on the MrNasdog Pressure Framework, against a supply-monitor reading of +0.79% — a gap of 0.002 percentage points, which is agreement, not conflict. DOGE is inflationary by design, uncapped, with roughly 4.98 billion DOGE added every year and no mechanism that could offset it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending Sep 9 2026, the Pressure Framework reads DOGE at +0.79% net: the sell side is one row and one row only — the Dogecoin block subsidy, 1,228,610,000 DOGE — and all four buy rows are zero. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at +0.79%. The gap is 0.002 percentage points, so DOGE ships with no data-conflict flag. The forward column reads the same +0.79%, because the Dogecoin subsidy is a constant and the block rate held flat across three separate 30-day legs of the window. The label for DOGE is permanently inflationary by fixed tail emission: a proof-of-work coin with no cap, no halving, no burn and no lock, whose float rises by about 3.2% a year and will keep rising by a shrinking percentage only because the denominator grows.

## Sell pressure: where new DOGE comes from

It comes from exactly one place, and measuring it correctly is the whole job on this page. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is 1,228,610,000 DOGE. The Dogecoin subsidy is paid per block, not per unit of time, and nothing in the protocol compensates when blocks run late — so the honest number is the block count, never the nominal rate. Reading the chain at both ends of the window gives Dogecoin block 6,243,228 and block 6,366,089, 122,861 blocks apart, over ninety days: a realised interval of 63.29 seconds against Dogecoin's 60-second target, or 1,365 blocks a day against a nominal 1,440. Taking the nominal rate on faith would have booked 1,296,000,000 DOGE and overstated Dogecoin issuance by 5.5%. Every block in that range was checked rather than sampled — the smallest and largest coinbase in the window were both exactly 10,000 DOGE, so no block paid anything else.

The other three Dogecoin sell rows are zero, and each for a different structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because Dogecoin has never had a vesting schedule: the coin launched in December 2013 with no premine, no presale and no team or investor allocation, so every DOGE that exists arrived through a block reward and an unlock tracker carries no entry at all. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because there is no Dogecoin reserve to release — the Dogecoin Foundation discloses no DOGE treasury and has published nothing since 2022, there is no DAO, and no allocation sits behind a locked contract. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is zero: no estate distributes DOGE on a schedule. The listed company that ran the best-known corporate DOGE reserve sold its last 463 million DOGE on Jul 20 2026 and exited the asset, but it had bought those coins on the open market, so they were already inside the counted float and the exit added no new supply.

## Buy pressure: where new DOGE goes

Nowhere. This is the rarest shape the framework meets — a buy ledger that is not merely small but empty on all four rows, and it is the reason DOGE cannot escape its band. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero: Dogecoin takes no protocol revenue, splits no fees and holds no accumulation wallet, so there is nothing to fund a buyback with even if anyone wanted one. One Dogecoin story reads like a buyback and is not, and it is worth naming because it is easy to book wrongly: the network that took a large share of Dogecoin hashpower in April 2026 advertises a buyback-and-burn loop, but reading the mechanism rather than the label, it sells the DOGE it mines for stablecoins and buys back and burns its own token with the proceeds. No DOGE is bought and no DOGE is destroyed — and the coins it sells were already booked as new supply in Sell #1. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero, and this was tested on both surfaces rather than assumed. Dogecoin supply never fell across the window, which alone would not be proof — plenty of coins burn by sending to a keyless address while total supply stays flat. So the chain's one keyless burn address was read too, and it did receive: 13.79 DOGE, across four transfers from outsiders, against a lifetime balance of 1.85 billion DOGE it has held since 2014 and never spent a satoshi of. Thirteen coins against 1.23 billion issued is dust from strangers, not a mechanism.

What settles the fee question is an identity rather than a claim: on Dogecoin every block's reward equals its subsidy plus the fees inside it, at both ends of the window, so the 826,418 DOGE paid in fees over ninety days went to miners in full. There is no Dogecoin equivalent of a base-fee burn. Buy #3, foundation buying, is zero — there is no Dogecoin treasury to accumulate into, and no announcement or on-chain flow this window shows an identified group buying. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is zero because Dogecoin is proof-of-work: there is no staking, no bonding and no lockup contract, so not one coin of the 155.83 billion outstanding is held back from the float by the protocol.

## Foundation and overhang

There is no team-controlled DOGE overhang to enumerate, and that is a measured finding rather than a gap in the walk. Dogecoin has no premine, so no founder allocation ever existed; there is no DAO treasury and no bankruptcy residual. The one candidate was checked rather than assumed: the Dogecoin Foundation's corporate arm became a US-listed public company on Jul 1 2026 and now files regulated quarterly reports, and it owns a wholly-owned subsidiary named for a Dogecoin treasury and reserve — but its report for the period ended Jun 30 2026 states it held no digital assets at all on that date, and bought and sold none in the quarter. That is the day this 90-day window opened, so the only identified Dogecoin reserve in existence was empty when the window began. It is re-read at the next quarterly filing. What the framework watches instead are balances it deliberately does not book. The largest Dogecoin addresses are exchange custody — the biggest single address holds 27.16 billion DOGE, about a sixth of supply — and those coins belong to depositors, not to an identified group, so they are excluded by rule, as are unlabelled large holders. One genuinely new balance joined the watch list this window: a cross-chain gateway that opened on Sep 7 2026 and locks native DOGE in order to represent it on another chain. Those coins are already inside the counted float, so the gateway changes nothing today. If any of these balances moves in a way that is not custody churn, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — but on the evidence of this window, the reason Sell #3 reads zero is that there is nothing there.

## How DOGE compares to other proof-of-work coins

DOGE sits in a category almost by itself: proof-of-work with a fixed tail emission and no cap at all. Compare it to a hard-capped proof-of-work chain, where the subsidy halves on a published schedule and issuance falls toward zero — the float there rises at a rate that keeps shrinking whether or not anyone does anything. Dogecoin's subsidy never halves, so its yearly issuance is a constant 4.98 billion DOGE and only the percentage falls, and only because the denominator grows underneath it. A tail-emission privacy coin is the nearest analogue, but even those tails were sized to be a fraction of a percent a year; Dogecoin's runs at about 3.2%. Against an uncapped proof-of-stake chain the difference is which side the offset sits on: those chains usually pair issuance with a fee burn that claws part of it back, and DOGE has no burn of any kind to claw with.

The deeper contrast is with the coins that beat their issuance. A chain that burns part of every fee, or funds a quarterly destruction out of reserves, can turn a positive mint into a negative net — the mechanism exists and can be scaled. Dogecoin has no such lever. The fee base that would power one is tiny: the chain clears about 33,000 transactions in a 24-hour period and collects roughly $303,000 of fees a year against a market value near $14.07 billion, about 0.002% — a few thousandths of one percent, the quiet end of every chain the framework tracks. Even a total burn of every Dogecoin fee would offset well under one percent of the mint. That is the honest structural read: DOGE's supply story is not a policy that could be changed at the margin, it is the absence of any policy at all.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First and by far the largest: the open proposal to cut the Dogecoin block reward from 10,000 to 1,000 DOGE, which would take yearly issuance from about 5 billion to about 500 million. Its issue is closed and its companion discussion is still open with comments into Aug 2026, where a Dogecoin maintainer describes such a change as a severe breach of the contract between the protocol and everyone who ever used it. There is no activation height and no fork date; if one ever appears, it rewrites the only row on this page. Second, the block rate itself — Dogecoin is merge-mined with Litecoin, so Litecoin's hashrate sets Dogecoin's block interval, and the 63.29 second average measured here is what keeps issuance 5.5% under nominal. Third, the keyless burn address: any organised Dogecoin burn would show up there first, and it took 13.79 DOGE in ninety days. Fourth, the cross-chain gateway that went live on Sep 7 2026 — float-neutral today, but a large locked balance would become a tracked overhang. Fifth, the Foundation's listed corporate arm, which held no digital assets at Jun 30 2026: its next quarterly report is the first place a Dogecoin reserve could reappear, and the only place Sell #3 could stop reading zero.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads DOGE at +0.79% net over the trailing 90 days and +0.79% over the next 90, on a ledger with one non-zero row on either side of it. The mechanism is a flat, halving-free Dogecoin subsidy of 10,000 DOGE per block that created 1,228,610,000 DOGE this window, set against a buy side that is empty — no buyback, no fee burn, no treasury bid, no lock. The key risk runs the unusual direction: the danger to holders is not that something breaks but that nothing changes, because there is no lever in the protocol to slow the mint and the only proposal to add one has been argued for years without an activation date. There is no ceiling to walk toward either — Dogecoin is uncapped, adding about 4.98 billion DOGE a year forever, which is roughly 3.2% of today's 155.83 billion and a slowly falling percentage of an ever-larger float.
