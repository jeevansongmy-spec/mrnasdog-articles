# DOGE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Dogecoin (DOGE) minted **1.23B DOGE** in the last 90 days from a flat, uncapped proof-of-work block subsidy of **10,000 DOGE** per block, against a buy ledger of exactly **0** — no burn, no buyback, no vesting, no lock. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.79% net** on a **~155.76B** circulating base with no supply cap, and the independent monitor reads **+0.90%** — a gap of **0.11 percentage points**. Because every DOGE ever mined is already inside that circulating base, mining is the only thing that can add supply, and the forward read is the same **+0.79%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 3 2026**, the framework reads **DOGE at +0.79% net inflation** against a monitor reading of **+0.90%** — a gap of about **0.11 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag is raised on this page. The two readings are measuring the same thing and agree: Dogecoin issuance is the whole ledger, and the small residual is snapshot noise in how the monitor derives supply from price. That agreement is itself the finding. Dogecoin has no non-circulating bucket — the published circulating supply of **155.76B DOGE** matches the chain's own issued supply to within **25 blocks** of mining — so there is no treasury, no reserve and no escrow whose release could ever surprise the number. Dogecoin is the purest passthrough chain in coverage: new coins are minted at a fixed rate and reach the market in full, forever, because nothing in the Dogecoin protocol can take a coin back. That makes DOGE **structurally, mildly inflationary** at a low and fully predictable pace.

## Sell pressure: where new DOGE comes from

Only one row on the Dogecoin sell ledger mints anything. Sell #1, protocol inflation, booked **~1.23B DOGE**. The Dogecoin block subsidy pays a flat **10,000 DOGE** and has not halved since block 600,000 in Feb 2015, so issuance is a permanent tail of roughly **4.98B DOGE** a year that never steps down. This build measured that rather than assuming it: the chain moved from block **6,235,713** to block **6,358,523** over the window, which is **122,810** blocks — about **1,363** a day against the nominal **1,440**, because real Dogecoin block spacing drifts with merge-mining hashrate and measured out at **63.3 seconds** a block rather than 60. That slower pace matters only because of how the Dogecoin reward is defined: it is paid per block, difficulty retargets every block, and nothing in the protocol mints extra to make up a slow stretch, so the missing blocks are issuance the chain simply never produced. Taking the nominal rate would have over-stated Dogecoin issuance by roughly **68M DOGE**. The count also closes against a completely separate measurement — an independent chain-derived supply series moved **1,214,519,804 DOGE** across the matching stretch against **1,214,520,000** implied by the block count, a residual of **196 DOGE** on more than a billion.

Every other Dogecoin sell row is **0**, and they are zero for the same structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because Dogecoin launched in Dec 2013 with no presale and no insider allocation, so no unlock calendar exists, and the chain has no smart-contract layer that could hold a vesting escrow. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because Dogecoin has no foundation reserve, no team allocation and no unscheduled pool — every DOGE was mined and is already counted as circulating, so there is nothing left outside the float that could unlock into it. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: no estate, no trustee, no escrow anywhere in Dogecoin's history.

## Buy pressure: where new DOGE goes

Nowhere. Every Dogecoin buy row reads **0**. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0** because Dogecoin collects no protocol revenue and has no treasury contract — there is no cash flow to fund a buyback and no contract that could execute one. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and this build read that zero on both of the surfaces a burn can hide behind rather than trusting either one alone. The supply surface first: Dogecoin's total supply never fell, rising monotonically by exactly the mined amount, so any supply-destroying burn in the window is bounded at under **200 DOGE**. The unspendable surface second, which on a Dogecoin-style chain can move while supply does not: the long-standing keyless proof-of-burn address holds **1.85B DOGE** from a 2014 community event and did receive coins inside this window — but only **13.79 DOGE** across four transfers, dust from an unrelated side protocol, and the address has never spent a single coin in twelve years and 22,736 outputs, so it is a genuine sink rather than an escrow that drains and refills. Those keyless coins are gone for good but are still counted in published supply, so they do not shrink the denominator either. Dogecoin has never carried a burn opcode or a base-fee sink: every block pays its miner the **10,000 DOGE** subsidy plus the entire fee total, checked at both ends of the window. The burn stories that resurface in Dogecoin coverage — community burn addresses, layer-2 fee sinks, a mining pool marketing a deflationary loop that in fact sells the DOGE it mines and burns its own token instead — are proposals or other coins' mechanisms.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**: there is no protocol-level accumulation programme, and the Dogecoin Foundation's corporate arm has held a small payments reserve near **10M DOGE** since early 2025 with no disclosed purchase or sale in this window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: Dogecoin has no staking, no lockup contract and no vault, so coins are either mined or simply held. The spot DOGE funds do hold coins directly, but they are freely redeemable and belong to their investors, which is depositor custody rather than a structural lock. An empty buy ledger is the single most important structural fact about DOGE: the Dogecoin mint has no counterweight at any price. It is also why no part of this page claims Dogecoin supply is final — the emission is uncapped, it never halves again, and there is no mechanism that could make it stop.

## Foundation and overhang

Dogecoin has no team-controlled overhang at all, and that is a measurable claim rather than a stylistic one: the published circulating supply and the chain's issued supply are the same number to within **250,000 DOGE**, so there is no non-circulating allocation, no Foundation treasury, no DAO treasury, no unscheduled unlock pool, no buyback accumulation wallet and no bankruptcy residual sitting outside the float. The only identified item is the Foundation corporate arm's payments reserve near **10M DOGE**, refreshed by walking its regulated filings. The very largest Dogecoin addresses are deliberately left out: they are exchange cold wallets holding depositors' coins, and counting them would book ordinary users' balances as team overhang. What the framework monitors instead, and monitors explicitly outside Sell #3, is a newer class of holder — listed companies running Dogecoin treasuries — because their coins belong to their shareholders and were already in the circulating base, exactly as exchange custodial coins belong to depositors. The largest of them sold substantially all **463M DOGE** on **Jul 20 2026** for roughly **$33.4M** and exited the business, a disposal it later described in its own securities filing; two smaller listed holders remain, at roughly **70.5M DOGE** and **21.7M DOGE**. If any of those balances falls between refreshes, the framework records it as market pressure on this class — but not as new Dogecoin supply, because no DOGE is created or released when one already-circulating coin changes owner.

## How DOGE compares to other proof-of-work chains

Dogecoin is the structural opposite of a halving chain. Bitcoin and Litecoin both pair proof-of-work issuance with a hard cap and a scheduled subsidy halving, so their inflation rate falls on a known calendar and converges toward zero; a holder can name the year the mint effectively ends. Dogecoin halved four times and then stopped: since block 600,000 the Dogecoin block reward has been permanently pinned at **10,000 DOGE**, which means absolute issuance is constant and the percentage rate declines only very slowly as the base grows. At **~155.76B** circulating, a fixed **4.98B DOGE** a year is about **3.2%** annually — low enough not to be alarming, permanent enough never to disappear. Against tail-emission privacy coins such as Monero, Dogecoin looks similar in shape but larger in magnitude: both accepted permanent issuance as the price of paying miners forever, and both are uncapped by design rather than by accident.

The uncapped model has a second consequence that shows up in the fee economy. A hard-capped chain has to grow fee revenue over time, because fees eventually replace the subsidy; an uncapped chain never has to. Measured this session the same way across four chains, Dogecoin's annualised transaction fees are **0.00215%** of its market value, against **0.00423%** for Litecoin and **0.00554%** for Bitcoin, with Bitcoin Cash lower still at **0.00044%**. A low ratio means the chain earns very little in fees per dollar of market value, and Dogecoin sits near the bottom of that field — roughly **half** of Litecoin's ratio and **two-fifths** of Bitcoin's. Put against its own issuance rather than its price, Dogecoin fees are about **0.067%** of what the subsidy pays out, so miners are funded almost entirely by new coins. Against fee-burning smart-contract chains the contrast is starker again: Ethereum offsets issuance with a base-fee burn that can flip it net-deflationary during busy periods, and BNB runs a quarterly buyback-and-burn against exchange revenue. Dogecoin has neither, and cannot acquire either without a hard fork. It is unusual in one way that flatters it, though: because its circulating supply and its issued supply are identical, it carries none of the unlock-cliff risk that dominates the inflation reading on most vesting-era tokens.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the block-reward proposal in the Dogecoin Core repository that would cut the subsidy from **10,000 DOGE** to **1,000 DOGE** per block: it is unmerged, has no client implementation and no activation height, but it is the only change that would move Sell #1 materially, so it stays the single structural watch line. Second, Dogecoin Core itself: the last tagged release is from Dec 2024 and every commit in this window was build, packaging or peer-connection work, so any consensus-touching change would be the first real signal that Dogecoin issuance or the fee path is moving. Third, observed block spacing: Dogecoin ran **1,363** blocks a day this window against a nominal **1,440**, and a merge-mining hashrate swing in either direction moves quarterly Dogecoin issuance by tens of millions of DOGE. Fourth, the two remaining listed Dogecoin treasuries near **70.5M** and **21.7M DOGE**, whose next quarterly filings are the refresh point. Fifth, any live burn mechanism — a layer-2 fee sink or a protocol-level burn actually shipping would be the first buy row Dogecoin has ever had.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Dogecoin (DOGE) at **+0.79% net inflation** over the last 90 days and the same **+0.79%** forward, against a monitor reading of **+0.90%**. The mechanism is the simplest in coverage: a flat, uncapped, non-halving proof-of-work subsidy of **10,000 DOGE** per block that minted **1.23B DOGE** across **122,810** measured blocks, set against a buy ledger of exactly zero. The key risk is that the empty buy side is permanent, not temporary — Dogecoin has no burn, no buyback, no staking and no protocol revenue with which to build one, so the mint passes through to the market in full and forever. The ceiling is that there is no ceiling: Dogecoin has no supply cap, and at the current base the fixed **4.98B DOGE** of annual issuance works out to roughly **3.2%** a year, declining only as the denominator grows.
