# XLM Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
**Main page:** the full XLM coin page — signal, price drivers and the buy/sell questions → [mrnasdog.com/research/xlm](https://mrnasdog.com/research/xlm)

XLM supply is growing, but not because the network prints coins: the Stellar network has created no new XLM since 2019, and every new coin in the market comes out of the Stellar Development Foundation's own funds. From Aug 11 to Nov 9 2026 the Foundation released **891.37M XLM**, while transaction fees parked **660,739 XLM** in a pool no one can spend, so the coins in circulation rose by **+2.52%**. Other trackers show **+2.62%** for the same stretch, and the next 90 days look about the same.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 9 2026, Stellar's circulating supply grew **+2.52%**, from about 34.48B XLM to **35.37B XLM**. Other trackers put the same window at **+2.62%**, a difference of **0.10 percentage points**, and the coin page carries no warning note. If the Foundation keeps paying out at the same speed, the next 90 days add another **+2.52%**. The plain label for XLM is a fixed-supply coin with a foundation-driven float: the total never rises, yet the part people can trade grows every week as the Foundation spends its share.

## Sell pressure: where new XLM comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**. Stellar once paid out 1% new XLM a year, but validators voted to switch that payout off on Oct 28 2019, and the number of coins recorded on the ledger stayed exactly the same from Aug 11 to Nov 9 2026.

Vesting unlocks are **zero**. XLM never had an investor or team vesting calendar, and the two old escrow wallets that used to release coins once a year now hold just 2 XLM each.

Foundation releases are the whole story: **891.37M XLM** in 90 days, about **9.9M XLM a day**. The Stellar Development Foundation keeps its coins in four funds that are not counted as circulating, and it pays out of them for grants, partner deals, liquidity and its own running costs, partly by selling on public exchanges. The pace was steady across the window: roughly 322M XLM from mid-August to mid-September, 244M to mid-October, and 325M to early November. Over the last full year the float grew by about 3.26B XLM the same way.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy coins are **zero**. No court case or failed company is waiting to hand XLM back, and the separate upgrade reserve, kept since 2015 for holders of the old network's tokens, sent nothing out in the window.

## Buy pressure: where new XLM goes

Programmatic buyback is **zero**. Stellar has no contract or treasury that spends income on buying XLM back.

Fee burn is **zero**. Stellar does not destroy fees. A few people did send coins to the network's dead wallet, about 2.5 XLM in the whole window, which is too small to register.

Foundation buying is **zero**. The Foundation is a seller of XLM, not a buyer, and when funds or companies buy XLM they buy coins that are already circulating, which removes nothing from the count.

New long-term locks are **zero**. Stellar has no staking, so holders have no place to lock XLM away in return for a reward.

The one thing that does pull coins back is the fee pool: **660,739 XLM** in 90 days, about 7,340 a day. Every transaction fee lands in a network pool that belongs to no account, so those coins stop circulating. The pool now holds **11.0M XLM**. It is not a burn, because validators could in theory vote to pay it out, and a fee-funded rewards idea for holders and users was raised at the Meridian 2026 conference, but nothing has been approved. Even so, the pool takes back less than one coin for every thousand the Foundation releases.

## Foundation and overhang

The Stellar Development Foundation holds **14.36B XLM** across fifteen known wallets, grouped into four funds: Development with **2.54B**, Growth with **5.21B**, Product and Innovation with **3.34B**, and Assets and Liquidity with **3.27B**. That is about 29% of all XLM and the only large pool of coins outside circulation. Money also moves between these funds often, for example about 707M XLM shifted into the Development fund in early October 2026, but a move between Foundation wallets adds nothing to the market. The Foundation says it is updating how the coins are split between the funds.

The second holder is the upgrade reserve with **258.89M XLM**, set aside for people who still hold tokens from Stellar's first network and can swap them one for one. It did not move in the window. Both are watched on-chain: if the Foundation's wallets or the upgrade reserve fall between checks, the outflow is added to the Foundation releases at the next check.

## How XLM compares to other fixed-supply payment chains

XLM sits in a small group of payment-focused Layer 1 chains whose total supply was set at launch and never grows, alongside XRP, Hedera's HBAR and Algorand's ALGO. On all four, new coins in the market come from an organisation releasing its own holdings, not from rewards paid to validators. That makes the supply story a question of how fast one insider spends, rather than a question of network security costs, as it is on Ethereum or Solana.

The differences sit in the release rules. XRP's company coins are held in time-locked escrow that opens on a fixed monthly schedule, with most of each month's batch locked again, so the pace is written into the chain. XLM has no such lock: the Foundation's funds are ordinary wallets, and the pace is a spending choice that the Foundation reports publicly but could change at any time. XRP also destroys every fee, while XLM only parks fees in a pool that a future vote could reopen.

Compared with chains that pay validators, the XLM model has one upside: once the Foundation's funds are spent, nothing else adds coins, so supply growth has a clear end. The 14.36B XLM still held would last about four more years at today's pace.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

On Nov 23 2026 validators vote on Bell, Protocol 30, which went live on the test network after the Nov 3 2026 vote. It adds smart-account addresses, post-quantum signature checks and finer ledger timing, and it changes no supply, fee or burn rule.

The fee-funded rewards idea is the one item that could move the ledger: if validators ever approve paying holders or users out of the fee pool, the 11.0M XLM held there would flow back into the market. Reports point to early 2027 at the soonest, and no vote date is set.

The Foundation's monthly pace is the number that decides the next reading. It ran near 325M XLM between Oct 9 and Nov 8 2026; a faster month after the Foundation finishes resetting how its coins are split between funds would push the next 90 days above +2.52%.

The Foundation's next quarterly report, covering October to December 2026, should show where the released XLM went and whether the yearly spending plan changes.

## Summary

XLM's circulating supply grew **+2.52%** in the 90 days to Nov 9 2026, and the next 90 days look the same. No new XLM is created; the growth is the Stellar Development Foundation releasing **891.37M XLM** from the 14.36B it still holds. Fees parked in an unspendable pool took back only **660,739 XLM**, and Stellar has no burn, buyback or staking lock. The total stays near 50B XLM with no minting, so the key risk is not endless printing but the speed at which the Foundation spends its remaining coins.
