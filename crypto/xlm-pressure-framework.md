# XLM Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

Stellar (XLM) creates no new coins, yet its circulating supply grew **+2.97%** in the 90 days to Oct 1 2026, and the MrNasdog Pressure Framework projects about the same for the next 90 days. All of it comes from one source: the Stellar Development Foundation moved **1.04B XLM** out of its wallets into the market, while only **659,593 XLM** of fees left the float. The Foundation still holds **14.71B XLM**, so this release can run for years.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 3 to Oct 1 2026, the XLM float grew by a net **+2.97%**: 1,040,966,792 XLM released by the Stellar Development Foundation, less 659,593 XLM of fees parked in the network's fee pool, on a circulating base of **35.02B XLM**. Our supply monitor read **+3.12%** over the same 90 days. The gap is **0.15** percentage points, inside our half-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown and the two readings agree. The next 90 days are projected at the same **+2.97%**. In one line: Stellar is a fixed-supply chain whose float is inflating because its foundation is steadily spending its share.

## Sell pressure: where new XLM comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. The Stellar ledger keeps a count of every coin that exists, and that count read **105.44B** at both ends of the window and a year earlier too. It did move in the past, from 100B at launch to 105.44B while Stellar's old yearly issuance ran, so a flat reading today is a real measurement, not a frozen field. Stellar switched issuance off in 2019; turning it back on would need a protocol vote by the validators, and none is proposed.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. XLM has no investor or team vesting schedule. The two time-lock wallets the project still lists hold 2 XLM each and are spent.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are the whole story at **1.04B XLM**. The Stellar Development Foundation holds the coins that are not yet circulating in 15 listed wallets, split into four programs: development, growth, product and innovation, and assets and liquidity. Between Jul 3 and Oct 1 2026 those wallets fell from **15.75B** to **14.71B XLM**. Every payment out of them was traced: 1,040,966,792 XLM went to outside addresses, net of 81.2M that came back, and the payment total matches the change in the wallet balances to the cent. By month the release was 283M in July, 426M in August and 332M in September. The Foundation's own quarterly report counts 647.5M XLM of spending for April to June 2026 and says its XLM sales can run ahead of or behind that spending. The release has fallen in every quarter since January 2025, and the last 90 days were the largest quarter in that run.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**. No estate, trustee or long lock is paying out XLM. Funds and listed products that hold XLM bought their coins in the open market, so those coins were already counted.

## Buy pressure: where new XLM goes

There is no programmatic buyback (**0**). No contract, company or treasury buys XLM back on a rule.

The protocol fee burn is also **0**. Stellar does not destroy fees. The one burn wallet, which holds 55.44B XLM burned years ago, gained only 0.21 XLM in the window, which rounds to nothing.

The Foundation buy row is **0**. The Foundation spends and sells; no report or wallet flow shows it buying. The 81.2M XLM that returned to its wallets from two outside addresses is already netted inside the 1.04B release.

New long-term lock is **0**. Stellar has no staking. Every account must keep a small minimum balance, but those coins still count as circulating.

The one buy-side flow is the fee pool at **659,593 XLM**. Every Stellar transaction fee goes into a network fee pool that nobody can spend since issuance stopped, and the supply count leaves it out of circulation. It grew from 10.07M to 10.73M XLM over the window, about 7,300 XLM a day. Against a 1.04B release, the fee pool offsets well under one tenth of one percent.

## Foundation and overhang

The Stellar Development Foundation is the only team holder, and its pile is the overhang: **14.71B XLM**, or 29% of all XLM. By program it holds about 1.87B for development, 5.81B for growth, 3.63B for product and innovation and 3.40B for assets and liquidity. None of it has a public release schedule, and all of it can enter the float. A separate network-upgrade reserve holds 258.9M XLM and did not move in the window. We re-read every one of these wallets at each rebuild. If any Foundation wallet balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh. The fee pool and the burn wallet are not overhangs: coins there cannot be spent.

## How XLM compares to other payment Layer 1s

Stellar belongs with the payment chains that were fully minted at launch and handed most of the supply to a company or foundation, rather than with chains that pay validators in new coins. XRP is the closest match: a fixed total, no issuance, and supply that reaches the market when the issuer releases it. The difference is the release rule. XRP's escrow releases on a fixed monthly schedule, while Stellar's foundation releases by decision, with no schedule at all.

Against proof-of-stake chains such as Ethereum, Solana or Cardano, Stellar works the other way round. Those chains create new coins every day for stakers and burn or keep some fees. Stellar creates nothing and pays its validators nothing in XLM, so all of its supply growth is a custody question: who holds the unreleased coins and how fast they spend them.

Fee handling sets Stellar apart too. Ethereum destroys its base fee, so heavy use can shrink supply. Stellar parks fees in a pool that no one can spend. The effect on the float is the same, coins leave it, but at current fee levels the amount is tiny next to the foundation release.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the monthly pace of the Foundation release: 283M, 426M and 332M XLM in the last three months. A month well above 400M would push the next reading above +3%.

Second, the Foundation's third-quarter 2026 report, which should list XLM spent by program for July to September and any change to how the 14.71B is allocated.

Third, the Meridian 2026 conference on Oct 28 to 29 2026, the Foundation's yearly event, where any change to its plans for the 14.71B would likely be shared.

Fourth, any validator vote on a new protocol version. Protocol 28 went live on Sep 16 2026 and changed nothing about supply; a future upgrade would only matter if it touched fees or issuance.

## Summary

Stellar (XLM) has a fixed supply of about 50.0B coins and creates none, but its circulating float grew **+2.97%** in the 90 days to Oct 1 2026 because the Stellar Development Foundation released **1.04B XLM** from its wallets. Fees parked in the fee pool took back only 659,593 XLM. The Foundation still holds **14.71B XLM** with no public schedule, so the key risk is a faster release; the ceiling is that pile, after which XLM supply would be flat.
