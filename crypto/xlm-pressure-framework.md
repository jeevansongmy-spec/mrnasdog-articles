Stellar (XLM) added **+3.02%** to its tradable supply over the last 90 days and is projected to add the same **+3.02%** over the next 90 — even though the Stellar network has not minted a single lumen since **2019**. The entire increase comes from one place: the Stellar Development Foundation released **1,046.8M XLM** from its thirteen mandate wallets into circulation, against a fee pool that quietly absorbed just **607.0K XLM**. XLM is a fixed-supply asset with a discretionary float — the cap is real at **50.00B**, but the pace at which the remaining **15,133.7M** reaches the market is a foundation decision, not a protocol rule.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Stellar's 90-day net supply change at **+3.02%** of circulating XLM, against an independent monitor reading of **+3.15%** — a gap of **0.13** percentage points, comfortably inside the framework's tolerance, so no data-conflict flag is raised on the XLM overview. The residual is a base convention, not a disagreement: the monitor divides by the supply as it stood 90 days ago, the framework by today's. Measured on the same base, the primary on-chain read is **+3.12%** against the monitor's **+3.15%**. The characterisation for Stellar is a **fixed-supply chain with a discretionary float** — a network with no issuance whatsoever whose tradable supply nonetheless expands at roughly three percent a quarter because a single foundation is spending down its reserve.

## Sell pressure: where new XLM comes from

Sell row one, protocol inflation, is **zero** — and this is the fact most Stellar coverage stops at. Stellar ran a **1%** annual inflation mechanism for roughly five years, creating **5,443,902,087** lumens in total, until Stellar validators voted it out of the protocol on **Oct 28 2019**. The operation was deleted, not paused. Reading the Stellar ledger's own coin count at both ends of this window returns the identical figure, **105,443,902,087**, to the last decimal — the strongest possible confirmation that no lumen was minted. That same ledger number is also a trap: it still counts the **55.44B** lumens the Stellar Development Foundation retired in November 2019, because Stellar has no destroy operation for its native asset and the burn was executed as a transfer to an account with no signers. Only the published supply metric nets that account out to reach the real total of **50.00B**.

Sell row two, vesting unlocks, is **zero** and permanently so. The 2019 mandate placed lumens in three dated escrows releasing on Jan 1 of 2021, 2022 and 2023; all three now hold exactly **2 XLM** of account minimum and nothing else. The mandate in force today publishes no escrow account, no release calendar and no dated cliff anywhere, which is why XLM appears on no token-unlock tracker. There is no vesting schedule left for the framework to read.

Sell row three, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, carries **1,046.8M XLM** and is the whole of Stellar's supply story. Reading all thirteen Stellar Development Foundation mandate wallets on-chain at both ends of the 90-day window shows the foundation reserve falling from **16,180.5M** to **15,133.7M** lumens. That measurement is a realised outflow, not an entitlement: it comes from walking every credit and debit on every one of the thirteen accounts, so transfers between foundation wallets cancel out and only lumens that genuinely left custody are counted. Sell row four, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: no Stellar entity has ever been in administration, so there is no estate distributing XLM to creditors.

## Buy pressure: where new XLM goes

Buy row one, programmatic buyback, is **zero**. The Stellar Development Foundation funds itself by selling lumens, not buying them; no buyback contract exists on Stellar and none has been proposed. Buy row three, foundation buy, is **zero** for the same structural reason — the foundation is a net seller by design, converting XLM to cover operating costs, grants and ecosystem investment.

Buy row two, protocol fee burn, carries **607.0K XLM** and is the only genuine offset Stellar has. Every Stellar transaction fee drains into a network fee pool that no account can reach. Until 2019 the inflation operation was the one route back out of that pool; when validators deleted the operation they also deleted the payout path, so fee accrual became a permanent removal from the float in everything but name. The Stellar fee pool grew from **9.83M** to **10.44M** lumens across this window. Against **1,046.8M** released, that offsets about **0.06%** — real, but not remotely a counterweight. Buy row four, new long-term lock, is **zero**: the signer-less burn account took in **0.002 XLM** over the entire window, and the separate **258.9M** network-migration escrow did not move a single unit.

## Foundation and overhang

The Stellar Development Foundation overhang is the largest single fact about XLM tokenomics, and it is fully enumerated. Across four published buckets and thirteen on-chain wallets the foundation holds **15,133.7M XLM**, roughly **44%** of the tradable float. SDF Development holds **2,067.2M** across four accounts; Stellar Growth holds **5,867.3M** across four; Product and Innovation holds **3,779.4M** across three; Assets and Liquidity holds **3,419.7M** across two. Those thirteen balances sum to within **4 XLM** of the officially published foundation aggregate, which is how the framework knows the enumeration is complete rather than merely long. Two further non-circulating pools sit outside the foundation mandate: the **258.9M** migration escrow set aside when Stellar relaunched its consensus in 2015, still claimable one-for-one by holders of the old network token and motionless all window, and the **10.44M** fee pool already counted on the buy side.

Every one of these balances is read on-chain at each rebuild. If any foundation wallet balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell row three at the next refresh — which is exactly what produced this build's **1,046.8M** figure. The migration escrow is valued at zero for the opposite reason: it has fired no release in over a decade, and capacity to move is not evidence of movement.

## How XLM compares to other fixed-supply payment chains

XLM belongs to a small class of payment-oriented chains whose supply is capped and whose float is nonetheless controlled by a foundation treasury rather than a mining schedule. The instructive contrast is with a proof-of-work payment coin such as Litecoin or Bitcoin Cash. Those chains issue new units continuously on a fixed halving schedule, so their inflation is high but perfectly predictable and steadily falling, and no entity can accelerate it. Stellar is the mirror image: issuance is exactly zero and can only be restarted by a validator vote, yet the float grows faster than most halving chains because a treasury is spending down a reserve on its own judgement. A reader who scores XLM as low-inflation because it has no mint is reading the protocol and ignoring the ledger.

The closer structural analogue is a foundation-governed chain like Hedera, where a governing body releases pre-minted units from allocated accounts onto the float. Stellar and Hedera share the same shape — a hard cap, no issuance, and a release rate set by an entity rather than by code — and the same consequence: the supply forecast is a forecast of institutional behaviour, not of block production. The difference is that Stellar publishes every wallet address, which makes the overhang measurable to the lumen. Against fee-burn chains such as Ethereum or BNB Chain, Stellar has almost no destruction mechanism worth the name: its fee pool absorbs a fraction of a percent of the foundation's releases, where a burn chain can offset or exceed its own issuance entirely. XLM has the cap those chains lack and lacks the sink those chains have.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the release pace itself. The trailing 30-day foundation drain runs at **403.2M XLM** against a 90-day average of **348.9M** per 30 days — roughly **16%** hotter. The framework projects the trailing 90-day rate forward because no dated mechanism change is documented, which means this forecast is more likely to prove low than high. Second, the Stellar Development Foundation's Q3 2026 quarterly report, expected around early Nov 2026 on the pattern of the Q2 2026 report published **Aug 3 2026** — it is the one document that states intent rather than outcome. Third, any amendment to the foundation mandate itself, last revised in Aug 2025; a new bucket or a published release calendar would move Sell row three from measured history onto a schedule. Fourth, Protocol 28, the Stellar consensus upgrade slated for the Aug to Sep 2026 stretch: it targets consensus speed and contract management rather than issuance, but any Stellar protocol vote is the mechanism by which the retired inflation operation could return, and it is the only thing that could make Sell row one non-zero again. Fifth, the fee pool: a step change in Stellar transaction volume is the only path by which Buy row two grows into a meaningful offset.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Stellar at **+3.02%** net supply growth over 90 days and projects the same for the next 90, against a monitor reading of **+3.15%** and a gap of **0.13** percentage points. The structural mechanism is unusual and easy to misread: Stellar mints nothing, burns almost nothing, and holds a hard cap of **50.00B** lumens, yet its tradable float still expands about three percent a quarter because the Stellar Development Foundation released **1,046.8M XLM** from its mandate wallets into circulation. The key risk is that this pace is discretionary and currently accelerating — no vesting schedule binds it, no burn absorbs it, and the recent 30-day run rate is above the 90-day average. The ceiling is genuine: **15,133.7M XLM** remains in foundation custody, and once that is spent the float can only grow again if Stellar validators vote issuance back into the protocol.
