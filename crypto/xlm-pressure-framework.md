# XLM Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
**Main page:** the full XLM coin page — signal, price drivers and the buy/sell questions → [mrnasdog.com/research/xlm](https://mrnasdog.com/research/xlm)

XLM supply is growing, and it should keep growing at about the same pace: between Aug 21 and Nov 19 2026 the Stellar Development Foundation paid **868.13M XLM** out of its accounts, while network fees moved **649,621 XLM** into a pool no one can spend, so the circulating supply rose **+2.45%** in 90 days. Stellar creates no new coins at all; the total has been fixed at about **50.00B XLM** since the 2019 burn, and every new coin on the market comes from the Foundation's store, which still holds **14.29B XLM**. Other trackers show **+2.34%** for the same window, close to our reading.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 19 2026, the XLM float grew **+2.45%**, and the next 90 days look the same. Other trackers put the same window at **+2.34%**, a gap of **0.10** percentage points, so the coin page carries no warning note. All of the growth is the Foundation handing out coins it already holds; none of it is new XLM being created. The label that fits Stellar is a fixed-supply chain whose float is fed by a foundation: inflationary on the market, flat in total.

## Sell pressure: where new XLM comes from

Protocol inflation is zero. Stellar used to pay out new lumens every week, but validators voted to switch that off on Oct 28 2019, and the network's own count of lumens in existence read exactly the same on Aug 21 and on Nov 19 2026.

Vesting unlocks are zero as well. XLM never had a team or investor vesting schedule, and the two old time-locked escrow accounts that once held Foundation coins are down to 2 XLM each, so no batch is waiting for a date.

Foundation releases are the whole story. The Stellar Development Foundation keeps its XLM in 15 public accounts grouped into four funds, and it pays from them almost every day: grants to builders, money for partners and liquidity, and sales of its Development money on public exchanges and in direct deals to pay its own costs. Between Aug 21 and Nov 19 2026 those accounts sent out **868,134,930 XLM** more than they received, close to **9.6M XLM** a day. The pace was faster in late summer and slower in November, at nearly 7.7M a day. Large transfers between the Foundation's own accounts, such as the sweeps of early October 2026, change nothing, because those coins never reached the market.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is zero. No bankrupt firm or court holds XLM for a later payout, and no locked batch is due to come back.

## Buy pressure: where new XLM goes

There is no programmatic buyback. No contract or treasury buys XLM back, and the Foundation sells XLM rather than buying it.

The fee burn is zero too, because Stellar does not burn fees. A few people still send small amounts to the locked burn account, but only 2.5 XLM arrived there in the whole window, far too little to count. The big burn of **55.44B XLM** in November 2019 left the total long ago and does not repeat.

Foundation buying is zero. Funds and companies that buy XLM buy coins that already circulate, so their trades move the price but not the count.

New long-term locks are zero. Stellar has no staking, so no coins are tied up to earn a reward.

The one real sink is the fee pool. Every Stellar transaction pays a small fee, and those fees go into a network pool that nobody can spend today. From Aug 21 to Nov 19 2026 the pool grew by **649,621 XLM** to **11.06M XLM**. That is held, not destroyed: a validator vote could open it again, so it counts as coins leaving the market, not as a burn. Next to the Foundation's payouts it is tiny, under a tenth of one percent of the coins released.

## Foundation and overhang

The Stellar Development Foundation is the overhang. On Nov 19 2026 its four funds held **14.29B XLM**, about 29% of all lumens: Growth **5.20B**, Product and Innovation **3.33B**, Assets and Liquidity **3.26B** and Development **2.50B**. A separate network upgrade reserve holds **258.89M XLM** and did not move in the window. We read these balances straight from the chain every day, and if any of them falls between our checks, that outflow joins the Foundation release figure at the next check.

The Foundation publishes how it plans to use these coins and reports each quarter, and its mandate page says it is updating how the coins are split between funds. Even at the slower November pace, the store would last for years, so Foundation payouts will keep feeding the float long after the next 90 days.

## How XLM compares to other fixed-supply Layer 1 chains

XLM sits in a small group of Layer 1 coins whose total was set at launch and where new coins reach the market only when a central holder lets them go. XRP is the closest cousin: it mints nothing, and Ripple releases coins from escrow every month. The difference is that XRP destroys its fees, while Stellar parks them in a pool that a vote could reopen. Hedera works in a similar way too, with a fixed total and a treasury that releases coins over time.

That is the opposite of proof-of-stake chains like Ethereum, Solana or Cardano, where new coins are paid to validators or stakers by the protocol itself and the supply rises without anyone deciding to sell. On Stellar, a person decides each payout, which cuts both ways: the pace can slow down at once, but it can also speed up without a vote. Unlike exchange tokens such as BNB, XLM has no buyback or scheduled burn to take coins back off the market.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

On Nov 23 2026 at 17:00 UTC, validators vote on the Bell upgrade, Protocol 30. It adds new smart-contract features and faster ledger timing, but it changes nothing about how many XLM exist or where fees go.

The payout pace is the number that matters most. It slowed to about 7.7M XLM a day in November; a return to 10M a day or more would lift the next reading, and a longer slowdown would lower it.

The Foundation's next quarterly report, expected around January 2027, and its updated split between funds will show how much it plans to hand out in 2027.

The rewards idea floated at the Meridian conference on Oct 28–29 2026 would pay holders from the fee pool. It is not a formal proposal and has no date; if it ever passed, part of the **11.06M XLM** in the pool would return to the market.

## Summary

XLM supply grew **+2.45%** between Aug 21 and Nov 19 2026, because the Stellar Development Foundation paid **868.13M XLM** out of its accounts while fees moved only **649,621 XLM** into a locked pool. Stellar creates no new coins and has a fixed total of about **50.00B XLM**, so every new coin on the market comes from the Foundation's store of **14.29B XLM**. The main risk is that store: the Foundation decides the pace, and nothing on the chain buys the coins back. The next 90 days look much the same, at about **+2.45%**.
