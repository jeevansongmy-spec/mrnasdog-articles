Raydium paid holders about **0.43M RAY** over the last 90 days from a fixed mining reserve, while the Raydium buyback funded by **12%** of every pool's trading fee took **2.67M RAY** off the open market and parked it in a public Solana wallet. RAY minting is permanently switched off on-chain, so the buyback outpaces the emission roughly six to one and the Pressure Framework reads **−0.83% net** on the active float. Our supply monitor reads **+0.38%** because it still counts the held buyback wallet as circulating RAY — a **1.21-point** gap that is mechanical, not an error.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 1 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **RAY at −0.83% net**: **0.43M RAY** of sell pressure against **2.67M RAY** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **269.54M RAY**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change over the same 90 days at **+0.38%**, a gap of **1.21 percentage points**. That exceeds the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the RAY overview. The gap resolves arithmetically: the Raydium buyback does not burn RAY, it accumulates it in a public wallet that the monitor's upstream classification still treats as circulating supply. The **2.67M RAY** absorbed is worth **0.99 points** of the gap on its own, and the remaining **0.22 points** is the difference between the reserve release we measured and the float growth the upstream series recorded. Raydium is best read as **structurally deflationary on the active float**, with the held buyback stack sitting behind it as a governance-reversible overhang.

## Sell pressure: where new RAY comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the only live sell row on RAY and it is small: **0.43M RAY** over 90 days. Raydium's RAY mint authority is renounced — a direct on-chain read of the RAY mint returns a null mint authority and a null freeze authority — so no new RAY can ever be created. On-chain supply sits at **554.998M RAY** against a **555M** hard cap and cannot rise. What reads as inflation is the fixed mining reserve paying already-issued RAY out as staking and farm rewards. This build measured that flow rather than assuming it. Raydium's emission wallet released exactly **200,000 RAY** on **Jun 24 2026** and another **200,000 RAY** on **Aug 10 2026** into the RAY staking reward vault, plus **136,000 RAY** across **Jun 12 2026** and **Jun 24 2026** into four short concentrated-liquidity farm campaigns. Netting off the **105,000 RAY** still sitting undistributed inside those vaults, about **431,000 RAY** actually reached holders. The wallet arithmetic closes to the token: the emission wallet started the window at **1,115,359 RAY**, released **535,986 RAY**, and reads **579,373 RAY** today.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** and structurally so: team and seed allocations finished vesting on **Feb 21 2024**, RAY is fully unlocked, and no cliff of any size falls inside or after the window. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because nothing was released. That is a measurement, not an assumption — the reserve and ecosystem authority that holds **282.7M RAY** across seven token accounts recorded zero transactions on every one of those accounts during the whole 90 days. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: Raydium has no bankruptcy estate and no trustee release plan attached to RAY, so the row has nothing that can fire.

## Buy pressure: where new RAY goes

Buy #1, the programmatic Raydium buyback, is the whole story on the buy side: **2.67M RAY** over 90 days. The mechanism is fixed in the protocol's fee split — concentrated-liquidity and constant-product pools send **84%** of fees to liquidity providers, **12%** to the RAY buyback and **4%** to the treasury, while the legacy AMM v4 pools send **88%** to liquidity providers and the same **12%** to the buyback. The framework does not model that split into a number; it reads the destination wallet directly at both ends of the window across the six RAY accounts that wallet owns, and the balance rose from **82.07M RAY** to **84.74M RAY**. Every one of the **31** transactions that wallet signed in the window was checked, and not a single RAY left it. As a sanity check on the mechanism rather than a source for the number, Raydium collected about **$16.8M** in trading fees over the same 90 days, and **12%** of that at the window's average RAY price implies roughly **3.09M RAY** — the measured figure sits comfortably inside that.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and this build checked both of the surfaces a burn can move. The Solana incinerator's RAY balance **fell** from **35.8 RAY** to nothing rather than rising, and total supply moved by a single destruction of **35.8 RAY** on **Jul 17 2026**. Across RAY's entire life, only about **2,429 RAY** of the **555M** cap has ever been destroyed. Raydium runs no fee burn at all: the **12%** share buys and parks RAY, and the **4%** treasury share is taken in dollars. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** — both Raydium fee-treasury wallets were read this session and hold no RAY token account whatsoever, so there is no separate treasury purchase to count. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: no new lockup contract and no staking cap was announced, and RAY staking can be withdrawn at will, so it is not a lock.

## Foundation and overhang

RAY carries three tracked team-controlled overhangs and all three are readable on-chain. The largest is the reserve and ecosystem allocation: **282.7M RAY** under a single authority, more than the entire circulating float, with no published release plan — and, as noted above, no movement at all inside the window. The second is the buyback wallet itself: **84.7M RAY**, about **31%** of circulating supply, which has only ever grown. The third is the emission wallet that funds staking and farm payouts, currently about **0.58M RAY**. The Raydium fee treasury is deliberately not on this list, because it takes its cut in dollars and holds no RAY. All three RAY overhangs are refreshed from the chain on every rebuild. If any of their balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — which is exactly what would happen if governance ever decided to redeploy the buyback stack.

## How RAY compares to other DEX tokens

The useful comparison for RAY is not against other Solana venues by volume, but against the three ways a decentralised exchange token can handle fee revenue. The first model routes fees into an open-market buy and then **destroys** the tokens; supply falls permanently and the reduction is irreversible. The second routes fees into an open-market buy and **holds** the tokens; the float shrinks but the supply does not, and the coins can come back. The third does neither, paying fees entirely to liquidity providers and leaving the token with no structural bid. Raydium sits squarely in the second camp, and that is the single most important structural fact about RAY: the buyback is real, it is protocol-encoded rather than discretionary, and it has absorbed **2.67M RAY** in 90 days — but every one of those coins still exists.

Two further structural contrasts matter. Against uncapped emission models, RAY looks unusually clean: the mint authority is renounced, the cap is **555M**, and the mining reserve is a finite pool paying out at a slowing rate rather than an open-ended issuance curve. Against its own peer group on revenue intensity, Raydium is at the demanding end rather than the thin end — annualised protocol revenue of roughly **$21.6M** against a market capitalisation near **$217M** is about **10%**, which places RAY second-highest among the twenty-two coins we track on that ratio. That is what funds a buyback six times the size of the emission on a mid-pack Solana DEX. It also means the buy row is a volume derivative: if Solana trading activity cools, Buy #1 cools with it, and the deflation thins.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the buyback run rate: a research desk logged roughly **$150,000** of RAY bought in the three days to **Aug 8 2026** and called it the fastest pace of 2026, so the trailing figure used here is conservative — but it cuts both ways, and a fall in Solana trading volume shrinks Buy #1 directly. Second, any governance decision on the **84.7M RAY** held in the buyback wallet; a decision to burn it would remove the monitor gap outright, while a decision to deploy it would open Sell #3 for the first time. Third, the reward-vault refill rhythm — the next top-up after **Aug 10 2026** tells you whether the mining reserve is still paying at the same rate. Fourth, new farm campaigns: the four concentrated-liquidity campaigns funded inside this window added about **79,000 RAY** to the sell side and have now largely run out, so a heavier campaign season would lift Sell #1. Fifth, product changes that move where fees are collected, such as the **Aug 17 2026** LaunchLab quote-asset expansion and the **Jul 22 2026** AMM v4 legacy-dependency removal, which have no direct supply effect but do move the revenue that funds the buyback.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Raydium's RAY at **−0.83% net supply over 90 days**, and projects the same for the next 90: a thin **0.43M RAY** mining-reserve emission against a **2.67M RAY** fee-funded buyback, on a **269.54M RAY** float that is hard-capped at **555M** and can never grow, because the mint authority is renounced on-chain. The structural mechanism is the **12%** trading-fee buyback, and the key risk is that it is a **hold, not a burn**: **84.7M RAY** — about **31%** of circulating supply — now sits in a protocol wallet whose future use is a governance decision, and that same held stack is why our supply monitor reads **+0.38%** where the framework reads **−0.83%**. The ceiling is fixed and the emission is finite; the variable is fee revenue, and with it the size of the buyback.
