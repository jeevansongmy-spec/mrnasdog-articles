# RAY Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

Raydium released **0.45M RAY** to holders over the last 90 days through a single reward hopper, while the Raydium buyback funded by **12%** of every pool's trading fee took **2.78M RAY** off the open market and parked it in one public Solana wallet. RAY minting is permanently switched off on-chain, so the buyback outpaces the emission roughly six to one and the Pressure Framework reads **−0.87% net** on the active float. Our supply monitor reads **+0.41%** because it still counts the held buyback wallet as circulating RAY — a **1.28-point** gap that is mechanical, not an error.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 6 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **RAY at −0.87% net**: **0.45M RAY** of sell pressure against **2.78M RAY** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **269.54M RAY**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change over the same 90 days at **+0.41%**, a gap of **1.28 percentage points**. That exceeds the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the RAY overview. The gap resolves arithmetically. The Raydium buyback does not burn RAY, it accumulates it in a public wallet that the upstream supply classification still treats as circulating, and the **2.78M RAY** absorbed is worth **1.03 points** of the gap on its own. The remaining quarter-point is noise in a supply series derived from market capitalisation and price rather than read from the Solana chain — because on-chain, every identified Raydium reserve wallet held exactly the same balance at both ends of the window. RAY is best read as **structurally deflationary on the active float**, with the held buyback stack behind it as a governance-reversible overhang.

## Sell pressure: where new RAY comes from

The first thing to understand about RAY inflation is that no new RAY can be created. The RAY mint on Solana carries a **null mint authority** and a null freeze authority, both read directly from the chain this session, so the **555,000,000 RAY** genesis cap is not a policy that governance could revise — it is the absence of a function. Live total supply reads **554,997,560.29754 RAY**, which is **2,439.7 RAY** below genesis: the entire lifetime destruction of the token, spread over five years. Every unit of RAY that reaches the market therefore comes out of an existing reserve, never out of a mint.

Sell source #1, protocol inflation, is the only live one, and it is small. Raydium's mining reserve does not pay holders directly; it feeds a single emission hopper, which funds the reward vaults behind RAY staking and the liquidity-mining farms. Over the window that hopper sent **535,986 RAY** to reward vaults in seven transfers, and **90,135 RAY** of that is still sitting inside those vaults unclaimed. What actually reached holders is the difference: **445,852 RAY**, which we ship as **0.45M**. The hopper's own balance closes the arithmetic exactly — its opening balance minus the seven transfers equals the live on-chain reading to six decimal places, with a residual of zero. As a second angle, Raydium's own documentation puts the mining-reserve release at roughly **1.9M RAY** per year, which is **468,493 RAY** over 90 days; the measured figure is **95%** of that.

Sell source #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and there is nothing left to release. Team and seed allocations, together **25.9%** of supply, were locked for twelve months after the token generation event and then released each day through month 36. That schedule concluded on **Feb 21 2024**. There is no cliff, no linear tail and no calendar left to fire. Sell source #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero** for the window — not because the reserves are small, but because none of them moved. Sell source #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy distributions, is **zero** for the simplest reason available: Raydium has no bankruptcy estate and no trustee schedule.

## Buy pressure: where new RAY goes

Buy source #1, the programmatic buyback, is the whole story of RAY inflation. Raydium routes **12%** of every pool's trading fee into open-market RAY purchases. On concentrated-liquidity and constant-product pools the split is 84% to liquidity providers, 12% to the buyback and 4% to the treasury; on the older standard pools it is 88% to liquidity providers and 12% to the buyback. That share is a fee share, not a revenue share, and the distinction matters when sizing it: over this window the protocol's pools generated **$18,482,978** in trading fees, so the buyback line is **$2,217,957**. Converted at each day's RAY price, that implies about **3.26M RAY** — and the buyback wallet actually gained **2,782,697 RAY**, which is **85%** of the modelled figure and comfortably inside the framework's triangulation band. We ship the measured number, **2.78M**, not the modelled one.

The destination question is the one that decides how this row is read, and the answer is unambiguous. Bought-back RAY is **held, not burned**. Raydium's documentation says the protocol holds it at a public on-chain address; the wallet **DdHDoz94o2WJmD9myRobHCwtx1bESpHTd4SSPe6VEZaz** now holds **85,015,308 RAY** across six token accounts, all of them enumerated; and across the full 90 days those accounts recorded **zero outflow**. On **Aug 31 2026** the protocol confirmed the stack had passed **30%** of RAY circulating supply — our reading puts it at **31.5%** — and stated that its position is to hold while the final disposition stays an open governance question. So the RAY buyback counts as buy pressure today and is carried as an overhang at the same time; it is not a burn, and no page should call it one.

Buy source #2, a protocol fee burn, is **zero**. Raydium runs no burn mechanism at all. We checked both surfaces a burn can show up on. The Solana incinerator's RAY account fell from **35.785509** to **0.000001** during the window, and the instruction that emptied it was a real destruction on **Jul 17 2026** rather than a transfer out — worth **35.79 RAY**, or roughly one hundred-thousandth of one percent of circulating supply. On the total-supply surface, lifetime destruction is capped at **2,439.7 RAY** by the gap between the genesis cap and the live reading, so even if every destroyed RAY had happened inside this window the row would still round to zero. Buy source #3, a foundation buy, is **zero**: the 4% treasury share is kept in whatever token the fee arrived in and is never spent buying RAY. Buy source #4, a new long-term lock, is **zero** as well, because the accumulated RAY sits in an ordinary wallet with no lock contract and no announced lockup.

## Foundation and overhang

Raydium's team-controlled overhang is large and completely visible. One authority, **8pFhUqCU7Fkxfg2DLytRDf7a9oK4XGtN92PrYwtVQc6G**, holds **282,662,066 RAY** across seven token accounts: a **123.32M** mining reserve, a **138.59M** ecosystem and partnership reserve, **11.10M** for advisors, and **9.65M** split across two smaller accounts. Every one of those accounts recorded **zero transactions** inside the 90-day window; the mining reserve has not moved since **Sep 29 2025** and the ecosystem reserve not since **Dec 10 2025**. Their combined change over the window was one millionth of a single RAY. The second overhang is the buyback stack itself, **85.02M RAY** or **31.5%** of circulating supply, which grows every day the protocol collects a fee. All of these are read from the Solana chain on each rebuild. If any of their balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters sell source #3 at the next refresh.

## How RAY compares to other DEX tokens

Mechanically, RAY sits in a small class: a hard-capped token whose mint authority has actually been renounced, whose vesting is finished, and whose fee revenue is routed into buying its own token. Most decentralised-exchange tokens fail at least one of those tests. The common shape is an uncapped or governance-mintable supply still funding liquidity mining out of an open-ended reserve, where emission is the dominant supply force and any buyback is a rounding error against it. RAY inverts that ratio: the buyback is roughly **six times** the emission, and the emission itself cannot grow beyond what the reserve still holds.

The sharper comparison is with the burn model. Exchange tokens that run quarterly auto-burns destroy supply permanently, and their reduction is irreversible by construction — the tokens are gone, and no vote can bring them back. Raydium does the opposite with the same money: it parks. Economically the two look identical for as long as the parked tokens stay parked, which for Raydium is the entire history of the programme so far. Structurally they are not identical, because a burn is a one-way door and a wallet is not. The **85.02M RAY** in the Raydium buyback wallet is one governance decision away from being burned, redistributed, or sold — and the community was asking exactly that question when the **Aug 31 2026** milestone landed.

There is also a fee-switch class of DEX tokens that returns value by letting holders redeem the token against protocol reserves. That mechanism shrinks supply when the token is cheap and stops when it is expensive, so its buy pressure is price-elastic. The Raydium buyback is volume-elastic instead: it scales with trading activity, not with the token's discount, which is why it accelerated through August 2026 as LaunchLab and the concentrated-liquidity pools widened the fee base.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, any governance proposal on the disposition of the buyback wallet. A vote to burn the **85.02M RAY** stack would remove **31.5%** of circulating supply permanently and close the monitor gap in one transaction; a vote to redistribute it would convert the largest buy row on this page into the largest sell row. Second, the emission hopper at **4u5GmwvqKjeSRnaWnSnqU3DsdDV1mwD8YBn486LLF9rL**, which now holds **579,373 RAY** — roughly one more quarter of releases at the current pace — and whose next top-up from the mining reserve would be the first reserve movement since **Sep 29 2025**. Third, Raydium's trading-fee run rate, since the buyback is a fixed 12% of it and a fee collapse would shrink the buy side proportionally. Fourth, the RAY price itself: the buyback spends dollars, so a higher price buys fewer coins for the same fee revenue. Fifth, any further destruction on the RAY mint, which would show up as a fall in total supply below **554,997,560**.

## Summary

Raydium is structurally deflationary on its active float. Over the 90 days to **Sep 6 2026** the protocol released **0.45M RAY** to holders and bought **2.78M RAY** back with **12%** of its trading fees, a net of **−0.87%** against a **269.54M RAY** float, on a token whose **555M** supply cap is enforced by a renounced mint authority rather than by policy. The key risk is not inflation but reversibility: the **85.02M RAY** the buyback has accumulated is held in a wallet, not destroyed, and its future is an open governance question. The ceiling is hard and the emission is finite, but the deflation is a policy that a vote could undo.
