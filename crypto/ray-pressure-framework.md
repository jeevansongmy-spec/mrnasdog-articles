# RAY Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

RAY, the token of the Raydium exchange on Solana, can never be minted again — the RAY mint authority is gone — so new RAY supply comes only from the Raydium mining reserve, whose incentive programmes paid out **0.62M RAY** over the last 90 days. Against that, the Raydium buyback, funded by **12%** of trading fees, added **3.76M RAY** to a holding wallet that keeps what it buys rather than burning it. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads RAY at **−1.16% net** over the last 90 days against a supply-monitor reading of **−0.07%** — a gap of **1.09 percentage points** that comes from where the bought RAY sits, not from a missing flow. RAY is hard-capped at **555M**, with **269.74M** circulating.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 14 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **RAY at −1.16% net**: the Raydium buyback took in **3.76M RAY** while the mining reserve's incentive programmes paid out **0.62M RAY**, a ratio of about six to one. The independent supply monitor reads the same 90 days at **−0.07%**. The gap is **1.09 percentage points**, above the framework's half-point tolerance, so RAY ships with a **monitor-gap flag**. The flag explains itself: the monitor counts the Raydium buyback wallet as circulating, so a purchase that moves RAY from the market into that wallet changes nothing in its count, while the framework books it as removed. The forward column also reads **−1.16%**, because both mechanisms are continuous and the trailing rate is the predictor. The label for RAY is **deflationary by buyback-and-hold**: a capped token whose float shrinks because fees buy it into a wallet, not because anything is destroyed.

## Sell pressure: where new RAY comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0.62M RAY**, and it is the only RAY sell row with a number in it. RAY is an SPL token whose mint authority is null, which cannot be undone, so no new RAY can ever be created; the published hard cap of **555M RAY** is enforced by the chain rather than promised. New float instead comes out of the Raydium mining reserve, a **123.32M RAY** vault that did not move at all this window. What moved was the release wallet the reserve funds: it held **989,373 RAY** at the start of the window and **379,373 RAY** at the end, after sending **200,000 RAY** on **Jun 24 2026**, **Aug 10 2026** and **Sep 10 2026**, plus **10,000 RAY** to a pool incentive on **Jun 24 2026**. Every transaction in that wallet's history was read, and the balance it implies matches the live balance to the last unit. The framework books what reached users, not what was funded: the incentive pools that wallet feeds were read at both ends too, and they ended the window **10,835 RAY** lighter, so **620,835 RAY** was actually paid out. The project's own documentation describes roughly **1.9M RAY** a year of reserve emissions; this window ran a little faster than that pace. One caution belongs here: the release wallet now holds less than the trailing 90-day payout, so another **0.62M RAY** needs a fresh transfer from the reserve.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**. The Raydium team and seed allocation unlocked linearly and finished on **Feb 21 2024**, and no unlock tracker carries a remaining RAY event. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** as a flow, but it is where the largest RAY overhang lives, covered in full below: **282.66M RAY** sits in five project vaults under one key, and none of them sent a single RAY this window. That zero was tested rather than assumed — the same vaults have paid out before, so they can move, and their transaction logs show nothing inside the window. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: no estate or long-dated lock is releasing RAY, and the Raydium governance realm recorded no action during the window.

## Buy pressure: where new RAY goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **3.76M RAY** and it carries the whole RAY buy side. Raydium routes **12%** of trading fees on its concentrated and constant-product pools to RAY buybacks, with **84%** to liquidity providers and **4%** to the treasury, and **12%** on its older standard pools, where the other **88%** goes to liquidity providers. Three fee-collection addresses convert that share and send RAY on to one holding wallet. Across the window that wallet rose from **82.48M RAY** to **86.25M RAY**, a gain of **3,762,853 RAY** built from more than **5,900** separate deposits, and not one RAY left any of its accounts. The balance at each edge was read from chain state rather than summed from transfers, and the two methods agree to the unit. A second angle points the same way: the fee share over the same 90 days, priced day by day, comes to about **4.4M RAY**, a little above the on-chain figure because purchases settle after fees are collected, so the ledger ships the lower, measured number.

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is effectively **zero**. RAY supply does fall, but by dust: only **2,493 RAY** have been destroyed in the token's whole history, under a thousandth of a percent of circulating supply, so no fee stream is burned at a size that registers. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**: none of the project vaults received RAY from the market. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**: nothing new was locked with a stated size this window.

## Foundation and overhang

RAY carries a team-controlled overhang larger than its own circulating supply. A single project key holds **282.66M RAY** across five vaults: partnership and ecosystem at **138.59M**, the mining reserve at **123.32M**, advisors at **11.10M**, team at **7.30M** and community at **2.35M**. That is more than the **269.74M RAY** in circulation. None of those vaults has sent RAY out since **Feb 2024**, and the circulating count already excludes them, so they add nothing today. A separate protocol multisig holds **8.87M RAY**, unmoved since **Aug 2024**. The Raydium buyback wallet is the third overhang and the one that matters most for this reading: **86.25M RAY**, about **32%** of circulating supply, held by a single key with no lock and no burn commitment — the project's stated position is to hold and leave the final use to governance. Nothing left it this window, but it has moved RAY out before: **100,000 RAY** on **Apr 18 2022** and **200,000 RAY** on **Feb 24 2023**, after passing along a **235,000 RAY** transfer in **2021**. The hold is a choice, not a lock. Every one of these balances is re-read on each refresh. If any of them falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How RAY compares to other DEX tokens

RAY sits in the small group of exchange tokens that turn fee revenue into supply reduction, and the design choice that separates them is what happens to the bought tokens. A buyback-and-burn sends purchased tokens to an address nobody controls, so the reduction is permanent and visible in total supply. RAY's buyback-and-hold keeps them in a wallet, so the float shrinks exactly as much while that wallet stays shut, but the reduction is reversible by a single decision. That is also why supply monitors and the framework disagree on RAY and agree on burn-based tokens: a burn changes the count everyone reads, while a held buyback changes only who owns the coins.

Against DEX tokens that pay nothing back, RAY's mechanism is unusually strong on fees. Raydium collected about **$143M** of trading fees over the past year, of which about **$22.6M** stayed with the protocol, roughly **6%** of a **$378.5M** market capitalisation, and the last month alone ran near **$22.8M** of fees. Against uncapped tokens that pay liquidity incentives from fresh issuance, RAY is cleaner by design: its cap is enforced on-chain and its only emission is a pre-minted reserve paid out at a measured pace. The weakness is concentration. Most of the RAY that is not circulating sits under project keys, and a third of what is circulating sits in one more.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the buyback wallet at **86.25M RAY**: any outflow, or a governance decision to burn or distribute it, changes the largest line on the page — a burn would make today's reduction permanent, a release would put up to a third of circulating supply back on the market. Second, the reserve release wallet at **379,373 RAY**: it cannot fund another full 90 days at the recent pace, so the next transfer out of the **123.32M RAY** mining reserve, its size and its date, sets the forward Sell #1 line. Third, the fee run rate: Raydium fees accelerated through late August and September 2026 with tokenized stocks and new launches, and a rising RAY price means each fee dollar buys fewer RAY, so the forward buyback in RAY can soften even while fees grow. Fourth, the five project vaults: a first outflow since **Feb 2024** would open a Sell #3 row that has been zero for over two years.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads RAY at **−1.16% net** over the trailing 90 days and **−1.16%** over the next 90, with a **1.09 percentage point** gap to the supply monitor that comes from the monitor counting the buyback wallet as circulating. The structural mechanism is a token that cannot be minted, a mining reserve paying out **0.62M RAY**, and a fee-funded buyback taking in **3.76M RAY** and holding it. The key risk is that the hold is reversible: **86.25M RAY** sits under one key with no burn commitment, beside **282.66M RAY** in project vaults. The hard limit is the **555M RAY** cap, enforced on-chain, which no future decision can raise.
