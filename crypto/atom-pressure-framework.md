# ATOM Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

ATOM supply grew about **+3.04%** in the 90 days from Aug 15 to Nov 13 2026, and it should grow about **+3.17%** in the next 90 days: supply growing, and projected to keep growing. The Cosmos Hub pays its stakers in brand-new ATOM every block, **16,479,086 ATOM** in the window, while penalties on offline validators destroyed only **1,696 ATOM**, and there is no cap on how many ATOM can exist. Other trackers show **+2.79%** for the same 90 days, close to our figure, so the coin page carries no warning note.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

ATOM is a steadily inflating coin. Over the 90 days to Nov 13 2026 the Cosmos Hub added **16.48M ATOM** to a supply of **541.69M**, and took back almost none, so net supply rose **+3.04%**. The next 90 days should add about **17.20M ATOM**, or **+3.17%**, a little more because the base is bigger and because the chain lost a day of blocks to an emergency stop in September. Other trackers read **+2.79%**, a gap of about a quarter of a point, so there is no warning note. In plain words: ATOM is a supply-growing coin, and a holder who does not stake is diluted by roughly 12% a year.

## Sell pressure: where new ATOM comes from

Protocol inflation is the whole story on the sell side. Every block, the Cosmos Hub mints new ATOM and hands it to stakers and validators, and **2%** of it goes to the community pool. The chain's rate setting moves between 7% and 10% a year depending on how much ATOM is staked, aiming for 67%. Only about **63%** was staked all window, so the rate sat at its **10%** ceiling the whole time.

The setting counts a year as 4.36 million blocks, one every 7.2 seconds or so. The Cosmos Hub actually produces a block about every **5.7 seconds**, so it fits in many more blocks a year than the setting expects, and each block still pays its full share. The result is a real pace close to **12.5%** a year instead of 10%. Between Aug 15 and Nov 13 2026 that came to **16,479,086 ATOM**, about 183,000 a day.

Vesting unlocks add nothing. ATOM launched in March 2019, and the early sale and team coins finished unlocking long ago, so no locked batch is waiting to open. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks also add nothing: the coins held by the community pool and the Interchain Foundation are already counted as circulating. No court case or failed firm is sitting on ATOM either, so the long-term locked row is zero too.

## Buy pressure: where new ATOM goes

Very little comes back. No contract or treasury buys ATOM back, so the buyback row is zero. Transaction fees are not destroyed either: part of each fee is parked in a protocol account that held **62,958 ATOM** on Nov 13 2026, up from 60,893 ATOM on Sep 30 2026, and those coins still exist. The Interchain Foundation stakes its ATOM rather than buying any back, and staked ATOM still counts as circulating, so a bigger stake removes nothing.

The one real removal is the validator penalty burn. When a validator stays offline too long, a small slice of its stake is destroyed. Between Aug 15 and Nov 13 2026 that happened in 52 blocks and burned **1,696 ATOM**, the biggest burst **582 ATOM** across two blocks on Sep 3 2026. No validator was punished for signing twice. Set against the new supply, the penalty burn is about one ATOM out of every ten thousand minted.

## Foundation and overhang

Three pools are worth knowing, and none of them changes the count, because every coin in them is already circulating. The community pool holds **11.55M ATOM** and can only be spent by a passing vote; it grows each block from the 2% cut of new coins. The Interchain Foundation has **14.47M ATOM** delegated to 50 validators, as it reported on Nov 2 2026. The fee account above holds **62,958 ATOM** and only a software upgrade could move it.

Two events this autumn moved large amounts without creating any ATOM. After the Sep 22 2026 Neutron attack, the Cosmos Hub stopped for about a day and moved **1.23M ATOM** still held by the attacker into a recovery wallet, and a later vote on Oct 12 2026 sent it toward the Neutron recovery process. In early November 2026, a flaw at Keplr's Google and Apple login provider let coins be moved out of some wallets, and the provider shifted about **2.49M ATOM** to recovery addresses. Both moved coins that already existed, so the supply count is unchanged.

## How ATOM compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most proof-of-stake chains pay stakers with new coins, so the useful question is what offsets it. Ethereum pays validators in new ETH but burns part of every fee, and its supply grew only about 0.21% over its last 90 days. The Cosmos Hub has no working fee burn at all, so nothing offsets its issuance except a tiny penalty burn.

Solana starts from a set rate and cuts it a little every year toward a low floor. The Cosmos Hub has no such schedule: its rate only falls if more than 67% of ATOM is staked, and staking has stayed near 63%. On top of that, its per-block payout runs ahead of the 10% setting because blocks come faster than the setting assumes. That puts ATOM among the higher-issuance large chains, with dilution falling mostly on holders who do not stake.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Nov 17 2026**: voting ends on proposal 1061, the Gaia v29.1.0 upgrade, planned for about Nov 18 2026. It fixes old proposal queries and updates smart-contract code, and it does not change the mint rate.

**Around Nov 20 2026**: Cosmos Labs plans to publish the schedule for its ATOM token plan. Its research found that selling comes mainly from how fast rewards reach exchanges, and it proposes a new issuance rule with a security floor, more reward for longer-term staking, and a home for Hub income.

**From December 2026**: those changes go to governance one proposal at a time. Any vote that lowers issuance would cut the 17.20M ATOM expected in the next 90 days, but nothing is passed or dated yet, so the forecast keeps today's rate.

**From Dec 1 2026**: staked coins pulled out of hacked Keplr wallets start to finish their 21-day unbonding and could reach exchanges. They are existing coins, so they would add selling, not supply.

## Summary

The Cosmos Hub minted **16,479,086 ATOM** for stakers between Aug 15 and Nov 13 2026 and destroyed only **1,696 ATOM**, so ATOM supply grew **+3.04%** in 90 days.

ATOM issuance runs near 12.5% a year rather than the 10% setting, because blocks arrive faster than the setting assumes, and about **17.20M ATOM** should be added in the next 90 days.

ATOM has no vesting, no buyback and no working fee burn, and the community pool and the Interchain Foundation hold coins that are already counted.

The one thing that could change the ATOM picture soon is the Cosmos Hub token plan, which goes to votes one proposal at a time from December 2026.
