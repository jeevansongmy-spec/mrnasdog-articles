# ATOM Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

ATOM is clearly inflationary: Cosmos Hub supply rose **+3.04%** in the 90 days from Aug 6 to Nov 4 2026, and is on course for about **+3.18%** in the next 90 days. Stakers were paid **16,431,622 ATOM** of new issuance, while penalties on offline validators burned only **1,972 ATOM**, and ATOM has no supply cap, no vesting and no buyback. Other trackers show **+3.28%** for the same period, close to our reading.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 4 2026, ATOM supply rose by a net **+3.04%**, from about 523.43M to 539.86M ATOM. Other trackers report **+3.28%** for the same 90 days, a difference of **0.24 percentage points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. The next 90 days look a little faster, at about **+3.18%**, because the Cosmos Hub pays a fixed rate on a supply that keeps getting bigger, and the one-day halt of Sep 22 2026 is not expected to repeat. In one line, ATOM today is a fast-inflating proof-of-stake coin whose only burn is too small to matter.

## Sell pressure: where new ATOM comes from

Staking issuance is the only source of new ATOM. Every block, the Cosmos Hub creates fresh ATOM and pays it to the people who stake, and from Aug 6 to Nov 4 2026 that came to **16,431,622 ATOM**, about **182,600 ATOM a day**. The Hub sets its yearly rate between 7% and 10% and pushes it up whenever less than 67% of all ATOM is staked. Only about **63%** was staked through the whole window, so the rate sat at its **10%** ceiling the entire time.

The Cosmos Hub pays a fixed amount per block, and the rule assumes a slower chain than the real one. Blocks arrive about every 5.7 seconds, so the real yearly pace is close to **12.7%**, not 10%. For the next 90 days, with the same rate and a larger supply, the Hub should pay out about **17.15M ATOM**. ATOM has no vesting schedule: the 2017 sale coins and the genesis coins have been spendable since the Hub went live in 2019, so no unlock adds to this. No court estate or locked pile of ATOM is waiting to be paid out either.

## Buy pressure: where new ATOM goes

The only real removal is a penalty burn. When a validator goes offline for too long, the Cosmos Hub destroys a small slice of the ATOM staked with it. From Aug 6 to Nov 4 2026 that burned **1,972 ATOM** across 58 blocks, most of it in short bursts on Sep 2 and Sep 8 2026. That cancels about **0.01%** of new staking pay, which is why the burn barely moves the result.

Fees are not burned. The Cosmos Hub sets the base fee aside in a locked account that now holds **62,304 ATOM**, with **3,525 ATOM** added in the window, but those coins still count as supply, so they take nothing out of the count. Nothing buys ATOM back on the Hub's behalf, neither the Interchain Foundation nor the community pool bought ATOM in the window, and staked ATOM still counts as circulating, so the **340.7M ATOM** now staked removes nothing either.

## Foundation and overhang

Two large holders could sell ATOM, but neither would add new supply, because every ATOM already counts as circulating. The community pool, which the Cosmos Hub governs by vote, holds **11.52M ATOM** and grew by about 884K ATOM in the window; the votes that spent money in the window paid in USDC, not ATOM. The Interchain Foundation holds about **15M ATOM** across its wallets and lends part of it to validators, including **597,794 ATOM** to InfStones this quarter to build a staking service for custodians and brokers.

One more pile made the news. After the Neutron attack on Sep 22 2026, the Cosmos Hub stopped for about a day, and when it restarted on Sep 23 2026 it moved **1,227,121 ATOM** out of the attacker's wallet into a recovery wallet. A vote on Oct 12 2026 sent those coins on to the Neutron recovery team. A separate proposal to pay the attacker a refund and bounty was vetoed on Sep 30 2026. None of these moves created or destroyed ATOM.

## How ATOM compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most large proof-of-stake chains pay stakers in new coins, but many now cancel part of that pay with a fee burn or a falling rate. Ethereum burns a slice of every fee and pays a rate that shrinks as more coins are staked. Solana starts each year with a lower rate than the last. The Cosmos Hub does neither: its rate rises toward the ceiling when staking is low, it burns no fees, and it pays per block on a chain that runs faster than its rule assumes. That puts ATOM among the fastest-inflating large Layer 1 coins, at about **3%** of new supply every 90 days.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Around Nov 20 2026:** the Cosmos Hub team plans to publish the process and timeline for its tokenomics changes. The research is done, and three ideas are on the table: a new issuance rule with a security floor that can respond to revenue, rewards that favour longer-term staking, and a place for Hub revenue to flow into rewards.

**From December 2026:** each change goes to governance as its own proposal, one at a time, with forum talks and validator calls first. Until a vote passes, the rate stays at its 10% ceiling, and no vote dates or new numbers have been published.

**Staking ratio:** if staked ATOM climbs above **67%** of supply, the rate starts to fall toward 7%; it was about 63% on Nov 4 2026.

**Jan 12 2027:** Noble-issued USDC stops working, and the community pool is moving its 566,067 USDC to a new version. No ATOM is involved, but it shows how the pool is being managed.

## Summary

ATOM supply grew **+3.04%** from Aug 6 to Nov 4 2026, and the next 90 days look like about **+3.18%**. The Cosmos Hub paid stakers **16,431,622 ATOM** in new coins at its 10% ceiling, while penalties on offline validators burned only **1,972 ATOM**. ATOM has no supply cap, no vesting, no buyback and no fee burn, so new issuance is the whole story. A tokenomics review could change the rate, but no proposal has a vote date yet, so the Cosmos Hub stays on its current fast-inflating path for now.
