# ATOM Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

ATOM supply is growing fast and is set to keep growing: between Aug 25 and Nov 23 2026 the Cosmos Hub created **16.54M ATOM** to pay its stakers, while penalties on offline validators destroyed only **1,712 ATOM**, so net supply rose **+3.04%** in 90 days, with about **+3.17%** expected next. ATOM has no supply cap; the yearly issuance rate is stuck at its 10% ceiling, and because blocks arrive faster than that rate assumes, the real pace is close to 13.5% a year. Other trackers show **+3.31%** for the same window, close enough that no warning is needed.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 23 2026, Cosmos Hub supply grew by **16.54M ATOM**, a net rise of **+3.04%**, and the next 90 days project **+3.17%**, a little higher because the coming window has no chain halt in it and each block pays on a larger supply. Other trackers print **+3.31%**, a gap of **0.27 points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. Almost nothing leaves the supply, which makes ATOM one of the most inflationary large proof-of-stake coins: inflationary by design, with every new coin landing straight in the hands of stakers.

## Sell pressure: where new ATOM comes from

The first and only real source of new ATOM is staking issuance. Every block, the Cosmos Hub mints fresh ATOM and pays it to the people who stake, after a 2% cut that goes to the community pool. From Aug 25 to Nov 23 2026 that came to **16.54M ATOM**, roughly **184K ATOM** a day.

The rate is set by a simple rule: the Hub wants two thirds of all ATOM staked, and while the staked share stays below that line the yearly rate climbs to its ceiling. The ceiling is **10%** and the floor 7%, and with only about 63.6% of ATOM staked on Nov 23 2026, the rate sat at the 10% ceiling for the whole window. The rule counts on about 4.36M blocks a year, but the Hub now makes a block every 5.7 seconds or so, closer to 5.5M a year, and every block pays in full. That is why the real pace runs near **13.5%** a year rather than 10%. The chain stopped for about a day on Sep 22 2026 after the Neutron attack, which trimmed the window a little; with no stop planned, the next 90 days should bring about **17.26M ATOM**.

Vesting adds nothing. The locked batches from the 2019 launch finished unlocking in March 2021, and no new schedule has been added since. The Foundation row is also zero: the Interchain Foundation and the community pool hold large amounts, but every one of those coins is already counted as circulating, so spending or selling them adds no new ATOM. The last row, long-term locks and bankruptcies, is zero too: no court or failed firm is holding ATOM for a later payout, and the coins moved to safety after the Neutron attack and the Keplr login breach were already in the count before they moved.

## Buy pressure: where new ATOM goes

Almost nowhere. There is no buyback: no contract, treasury or company buys ATOM back for the network today. A new token plan is being prepared, but it has no buyback with an amount or a date yet.

There is no fee burn either, even though the Hub holds back part of every fee. That part is parked in a network account that has no power to destroy coins, and it held **63.29K ATOM** on Nov 23 2026. Those coins still count as supply, so the burn row stays at zero. The Foundation buy row is zero as well, since neither the Interchain Foundation nor Cosmos Labs buys ATOM on the market. Staking is not a lock in this ledger, because staked ATOM still counts as circulating and comes back after a 21-day wait.

The one real sink is the validator penalty. When a validator misses too many blocks, a small slice of the ATOM staked with it is destroyed. From Aug 25 to Nov 23 2026 that burned **1,712 ATOM** across 55 penalties, with the largest on Sep 3, Sep 9 and Sep 22 2026. Next to issuance it is a rounding error: new ATOM outran burned ATOM by nearly ten thousand to one.

## Foundation and overhang

The Cosmos Hub has no locked overhang, because every ATOM already counts as circulating. The large holders worth naming are the Interchain Foundation, which said on Nov 2 2026 that it had **14.47M ATOM** delegated to 50 validators, and the community pool, which held **11.59M ATOM** on Nov 23 2026 and can only spend after a public vote. The Foundation's two original launch wallets are now close to empty. Separately, the **1.23M ATOM** taken back from the Neutron attacker is waiting to go back to the users it was taken from, after a Hub vote approved the return on Oct 12 2026.

We re-read these balances at every rebuild. Because the Foundation, the pool and the recovery wallet all sit inside the circulating count, a sale from any of them is a change of owner, not new supply, and it would not move the numbers on this page. It can still matter for the price, and we name it in the watch list below when one is announced.

## How ATOM compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most large proof-of-stake chains pay stakers in new coins, but they differ in how fast and in what sinks them. Ethereum also has no cap, yet its pay grows only slowly with the amount staked, and it burns the base fee of every transaction, so its net supply moves by a fraction of a percent in a quarter. The Cosmos Hub pays at a much higher rate and burns no fees at all, so its supply grows more than ten times faster.

Many chains built with the same Cosmos toolkit use the same issuance rule, with their own floor, ceiling and staking target, and the same quirk applies to all of them: when blocks come faster than the rule assumes, the real rate runs above the number on paper. Chains with a fixed, falling schedule work the other way, since their new supply shrinks on a known date whatever the staking share does. ATOM has neither a falling schedule nor a cap, so its supply path depends on the staked share and on any change the Hub votes for.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the token plan. The team behind the Hub said on Nov 19 2026 that it would publish its tokenomics update in the last week of November 2026, with proposals going to a vote one at a time from December 2026. A lower issuance ceiling or a burn would change this page; until a vote passes with numbers, nothing is booked.

Second, the staked share. Issuance stays at its ceiling while less than two thirds of ATOM is staked; it was 63.6% on Nov 23 2026. Only a rise above 67% would let the rate start to fall.

Third, coins moving after the November 2026 Keplr login breach. Some of the staked ATOM taken in the breach, which began on Nov 6 2026, entered the 21-day unstaking wait during the week after, so it frees up by early December 2026. This moves coins between owners and adds no new supply, but it can show up as selling. Fourth, the Neutron vote that sends the **1.23M** recovered ATOM back to the users it was taken from, also a change of owner with no new supply.

## Summary

ATOM supply grew **+3.04%** between Aug 25 and Nov 23 2026: the Cosmos Hub paid stakers **16.54M ATOM** in new coins, while validator penalties destroyed only **1,712 ATOM**. The Cosmos Hub has no supply cap, no vesting, no buyback and no fee burn, and its issuance rate sits at a 10% ceiling that runs closer to 13.5% a year because blocks are faster than the rule assumes. The next 90 days point to about **+3.17%**. The one thing that could change that path soon is the token plan due for public review from late November 2026.
