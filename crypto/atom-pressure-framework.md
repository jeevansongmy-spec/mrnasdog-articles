# ATOM Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

Cosmos Hub (ATOM) supply is growing fast: **+3.04%** over the 90 days from Jul 27 to Oct 25 2026, and about **+3.18%** over the next 90 days. Stakers were paid **16.38M ATOM** of new coins, while validator penalties destroyed only **1,950 ATOM**. ATOM has no supply cap, no vesting and no fee burn, so the staking mint is the one force that moves it, and right now it runs at its highest allowed setting.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Net new supply over the last 90 days was **+3.04%** of the **538.14M ATOM** in circulation, and the next 90 days should add about **+3.18%**. Other supply trackers show **+2.86%** for the same stretch, a difference of **0.18 points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. The reading is simple: ATOM is inflationary by design, paying stakers in new coins at about **12.7% a year** while almost nothing leaves the supply. A fair label is a high-issuance staking chain with no sink.

## Sell pressure: where new ATOM comes from

Protocol inflation is the whole story. Every block on the Cosmos Hub mints new ATOM and hands it to stakers, with 2% set aside for the community pool. Between Jul 27 and Oct 25 2026 that came to **16.38M ATOM**, about **182K ATOM a day**. The yearly rate floats between a 7% floor and a 10% ceiling, depending on how much ATOM is staked. The target is 67% staked; only **63.1%** is, so the rate has stayed pinned at the 10% ceiling for the whole window.

The real pace is faster than 10%. The rate is set as if a block comes every 7.2 seconds, but Cosmos Hub blocks come about every **5.7 seconds**, and each block pays the same amount. More blocks means more new ATOM, which is why supply grows close to **12.7% a year**. The chain also stood still for about 24 hours on Sep 22 and Sep 23 2026, after the Neutron governance attack, and no ATOM was minted during the halt. Without a repeat, the next 90 days should bring about **17.10M ATOM**.

Vesting unlocks add nothing. The 2017 fundraiser and the genesis allocations unlocked years ago, and every ATOM already counts as circulating. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks also add nothing to the count: the community pool, the Interchain Foundation and the recovered Neutron coins all hold ATOM that is already counted, so when they spend it, no new supply appears. There is no bankruptcy estate or court-held pile of ATOM. A proposal to pay **1.72M ATOM** out as a refund bounty was voted down on Sep 30 2026.

## Buy pressure: where new ATOM goes

Very little ATOM ever leaves the supply. There is no programmatic buyback: no contract or treasury buys ATOM back, and the tokenomics review now under way has not put one to a vote. There is no protocol fee burn either. The base part of each fee is set aside in a module account, which grew from about **58.39K** to **62.01K ATOM** over the window, and nothing left it as a burn.

The Interchain Foundation and the community pool do not buy ATOM on the market, and exchange or broker buying, such as the new listing on a regulated US broker platform approved in September 2026, uses coins that already circulate. Staking is not a lock for this count: about **339.47M ATOM** is staked, and staked coins still count as circulating.

The one real removal is the validator penalty burn. When a validator stays offline too long, a small share of the ATOM staked with it is destroyed. That happened in **56 blocks** over the window and destroyed **1,950 ATOM** in all, with bursts in August, September and October. We expect about the same over the next 90 days. It cancels roughly one new ATOM in every 8,400 minted.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest pile under shared control is the community pool, which held about **11.48M ATOM** on Oct 25 2026, up from 10.60M on Jul 27. It grows from its 2% share of new issuance and from money returned by funded programs, and it spends only after a public on-chain vote. The Interchain Foundation, the nonprofit that backed the Hub at launch, reported **14.93M ATOM** on Jun 30 2026 in its monthly treasury snapshots; in October 2026 it set out a plan to delegate 597,794 ATOM to a staking provider, which moves no coins out of its hands.

The **1.23M ATOM** that the Hub took back from the Neutron attacker on Sep 23 2026 sat in a recovery wallet until a vote passed on Oct 12 2026, then went to Neutron to repay the users it was taken from. The fee module account holds about **62.01K ATOM**. Every one of these balances is already part of the 538.14M ATOM in circulation, so none of them can raise the count. If any of them is sold, that is selling pressure on the market, but it is not new supply, and the count only changes when the mint or the penalty burn moves.

## How ATOM compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most large proof-of-stake chains pay stakers in new coins, but the Cosmos Hub pays far more than most. Ethereum has no cap either, yet its validator pay adds only about 0.2% to supply every 90 days, and part of every fee is destroyed. ATOM adds about fifteen times that, and none of its fees are destroyed. Where Ethereum mixes issuance with a burn, the Cosmos Hub has issuance alone.

Other chains lower their rate on a fixed schedule. Solana steps its staking rate down every year toward a long-term floor, and Celestia, which is built with the same Cosmos tools, also cuts its rate each year. The Cosmos Hub has no such schedule. Its rate only moves when the staked share crosses the 67% target or when holders vote to change the floor and ceiling, as they did in November 2023, when they lowered the ceiling to 10%. Until then, the only thing that lowers ATOM issuance is more ATOM being staked.

Compared with fixed-supply coins such as Bitcoin, which halve their issuance on a known timetable, ATOM has no endpoint at all. Supply keeps rising for as long as the Hub produces blocks, and the faster it produces them, the faster supply rises.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Oct 27 2026:** the vote on the Gaia v29 upgrade ends, with the switch planned at block 33,618,500, around Oct 28 2026. It removes an old settings module and does not change the mint, but past upgrades have come with a burst of validator penalties.

**The tokenomics review:** Cosmos Labs has been testing new reward designs with an outside research team since August 2026 and says the next update comes after its next quarterly report. Any on-chain proposal to lower the 10% ceiling or the 7% floor would change the biggest row on this page, and a forum draft from Sep 21 2026 already asks for a 4% to 8% band.

**The staked share:** 63.1% of ATOM is staked today. If it climbed past 67%, the rate would start to fall from 10% toward 7%, but at the pace seen since July that is unlikely before January 2027.

**Block speed and halts:** blocks every 5.7 seconds keep real issuance near 12.7% a year. A faster chain would mint more, and another long halt, like the one on Sep 22 2026, would mint less.

## Summary

ATOM supply grew **+3.04%** in the 90 days to Oct 25 2026 and should grow about **+3.18%** in the next 90 days. The Cosmos Hub mints new ATOM for stakers every block at its 10% ceiling, and because blocks come faster than the rate assumes, real growth runs near 12.7% a year. Almost nothing offsets it: fees are not burned, there is no buyback, and validator penalties removed only 1,950 ATOM. ATOM has no supply cap, so the main thing to watch is whether holders vote to lower the rate.
