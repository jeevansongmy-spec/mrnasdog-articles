# ATOM Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Cosmos Hub minted **16,151,026 ATOM** of new supply in the ninety days to **Aug 22 2026**, measured directly from on-chain supply at both ends of the window, and removed nothing: ATOM has no buyback, no fee burn and no lockup. The Cosmos Hub publishes a **10.00%** annual inflation rate, but the mint pays a per-block slice of that budget against a governance parameter of **4,360,000** blocks a year, and the chain actually produced **5,509,238** — so realised issuance runs at **12.64%** a year. Framework net **+3.07%** for the trailing window and **+3.16%** forward, against a supply monitor reading **+3.27%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 22 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ATOM at +3.07% net**. Sell pressure totals **16.15M ATOM**, buy pressure totals **zero**, against a circulating base of **526,514,073 ATOM**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+3.27%**, a gap of **0.21 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's half-a-point tolerance — so no monitor-gap chip ships on the ATOM overview page, and no deep walk was required. The residual is the ordinary difference between a supply series derived from market cap and price and a direct chain read. Forward, the framework projects **+3.16%**, slightly higher than the trailing figure because the Cosmos Hub mint compounds off a base that just grew. ATOM is best characterised as **structurally inflationary on a fully circulating float**: the emission is real, it is uncapped, and there is nothing anywhere in the Cosmos Hub design that takes a single ATOM back off the market.

## Sell pressure: where new ATOM comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **16.15M ATOM** and it is the entire ledger. The Cosmos Hub is an uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1: the **x/mint** module creates ATOM every block and hands it to validators and their delegators as staking rewards, with **2%** skimmed into the community pool by the community tax. The rate the Cosmos Hub publishes is **10.00%** a year, which is its hard ceiling — the protocol raises inflation whenever the bonded share of ATOM sits below its **67%** goal, and bonding is currently **63.9%**, so the rate is pinned at the cap and cannot go higher.

The published rate is not the realised rate, and that distinction is the single most important fact on this page. The Cosmos Hub mint divides its yearly budget by a stored assumption of **4,360,000** blocks a year and pays that slice per block. That assumption is a governance parameter last set in **2020**, not a measurement. We counted the blocks across the exact window — **1,358,441** blocks between the block timestamped **May 24 2026** and the block timestamped **Aug 22 2026**, an average of **5.72** seconds per block, or **5,509,238** blocks a year. The Cosmos Hub therefore produces **26%** more blocks than its own parameter assumes, and pays a full block reward for each one, so ATOM issues at **12.64%** a year rather than 10.00%. The measurement confirms it independently: on-chain supply read **510,374,374.68 ATOM** at the opening block and **526,525,400.53 ATOM** at the closing block, from an archive node whose served height was verified on every request and corroborated by two other independent endpoints. The mint model predicts **16,148,602 ATOM** against that measured **16,151,026 ATOM** — agreement to within **0.015%**, which also proves no chain-owned reward reserve is paying stakers outside the supply number.

The other three sell rows are zero for structural reasons. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Cosmos Hub's 2019 fundraiser and early-backer allocations finished vesting in **2021**, and the arithmetic settles it without needing a tracker: chain supply is **526,525,400 ATOM** and the counted tradable float is **526,514,073 ATOM**, a difference of roughly **11,300** coins. Nothing meaningful is locked, so nothing can unlock. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: the Cosmos Hub is a live public network with no estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered ATOM distribution. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero**, and it is the row where things are genuinely being watched.

## Buy pressure: where new ATOM goes

Every buy row on the ATOM ledger is **zero**, and each for a different reason. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero because the Cosmos Hub has never deployed one — there is no buyback contract, no treasury repurchase programme and no revenue stream routed to the open market. A proposal to buy back and burn ATOM using fees from connected chains has circulated on the Cosmos Hub governance forum, but it has never reached an on-chain vote; when we checked, the Cosmos Hub's deposit queue and voting queue were both empty.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero because Cosmos Hub fees are recycled rather than destroyed. Transaction fees route through a fee collector and are paid straight back out — **98%** to validators and delegators, **2%** to the community pool. The Cosmos Hub's newer base-fee module does hold its take instead of distributing it, which is easy to mistake for a burn, but holding is not burning: that module's balance grew from **55,602 ATOM** to **59,370 ATOM** across the window, so those coins still exist and still count. The only genuine ATOM burn on this chain is a deposit forfeited by a vetoed governance proposal, and across the whole window that came to well under **3,000 ATOM** — a rounding error against a 16.15M mint. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero: the Interchain Foundation publishes periodic treasury snapshots and delegates ATOM to validators, but delegation moves custody rather than buying supply, and no dated, sized open-market purchase was disclosed inside the window.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the row most likely to be shipped wrong on a proof-of-stake chain, and it is **zero**. Bonded ATOM rose by **13,071,986** across the window, and unbonding on the Cosmos Hub takes **21 days**, so staked ATOM really is slow to reach an order book. But the framework asks a second question: is the lock inside or outside the number we divide by? Here it is unambiguously inside. With chain supply and counted float differing by about **11,300 ATOM**, the entire **336.58M ATOM** bonded pool is already counted as circulating. Booking the bonding increase as a buy would manufacture an offset out of coins the float never excluded, and would have flattered this page's net from **+3.07%** to roughly **+0.6%**. It stays at zero.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled overhangs are enumerated for ATOM and none of them released supply to the market. The largest readable one is the Cosmos Hub community pool, the protocol's own treasury, which takes **2%** of every minted block; it grew from **10,370,348 ATOM** to **10,693,571 ATOM** across the window, an accrual of **323,223 ATOM** that is exactly the 2% community-tax slice of the measured mint. It accumulated rather than spending, and the one grant that passed governance inside the window was denominated in a stablecoin, not ATOM; it is refreshed by chain read at every rebuild. Sitting in the same distribution module are **15,520,554 ATOM** of staking rewards that have been earned but not yet withdrawn — a real claim on already-minted supply, also refreshed by chain read. The Cosmos Hub's base-fee module holds a further **59,370 ATOM** of collected fees that are neither paid out nor destroyed, and no governance decision has yet named their destination.

The fourth is opaque. The Interchain Foundation, the Swiss foundation stewarding the Cosmos ecosystem, still counts ATOM among its largest crypto positions, but its periodic treasury snapshots publish neither wallet addresses nor an ATOM token count, so the quantum cannot be read on-chain and is watched through that official disclosure by hand rather than by a chain read. Every one of these four pools already sits inside the float this page divides by, which is why none of them can add new supply — they can only change who holds it. That is scope we track rather than pressure we count. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ATOM compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1s

The uncapped proof-of-stake class shares one mechanism — pay validators in new units forever — and separates on what, if anything, comes back the other way. The strongest members of the class run a fee burn: base fees are destroyed rather than recycled, so heavy network usage offsets and occasionally exceeds issuance, and the chain can print a negative net in a busy quarter. The Cosmos Hub does not do this. Its fees are handed back to the same stakers the mint already pays, so there is no usage-linked counterweight at all, and no level of Cosmos Hub activity can pull ATOM's net below zero under the current design. That is the structural difference behind ATOM's reading, not any judgement about the network itself.

Against hard-capped, halving-model chains the contrast is starker still. A capped chain's issuance is a published schedule that steps down on a known date and terminates; ATOM's is a rate with no terminal quantity, governed by a bonded-ratio target that currently holds it at its ceiling. The Cosmos Hub's adaptive mechanism means the rate can fall on its own — if bonding rises past **67%**, inflation drifts down towards the **7%** floor without any vote — but bonding has been moving roughly half a percentage point a quarter, so that relief is not a 90-day event. ATOM also differs from most of the class in one way that flatters it: it is genuinely, fully circulating. There is no vesting cliff ahead, no treasury sitting outside the float ready to be reclassified, and no unlock calendar that can surprise a holder. The pressure ATOM carries is visible, mechanical and evenly spread across every block — which is a better problem than a hidden one, even though the framework scores the size of it rather than its honesty.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the bonded ratio: it stands at **63.9%**, and the moment it crosses the Cosmos Hub's **67%** goal the mint rate unpins from its **10%** ceiling and begins falling on its own, which is the only automatic path to a lower ATOM reading. Second, the Cosmos Hub tokenomics review: a proposal to narrow the inflation band to roughly **2–6%** and add lock-based staking multipliers has been through a research and submission process, but as of **Aug 23 2026** nothing has been put to an on-chain vote, and this reading changes only when a vote passes. Third, whether governance ever revisits the blocks-per-year parameter — a single parameter change from **4,360,000** to the measured **5,509,238** would cut realised ATOM issuance by **26%** overnight without touching the headline rate. Fourth, the fate of the **59,370 ATOM** accumulating in the base-fee module: a decision to burn that balance would be the first genuine buy row this ledger has ever carried. Fifth, the Interchain Foundation's next monthly treasury snapshot, the only window onto the one ATOM overhang that cannot be read on-chain, and any community-pool spend proposal denominated in ATOM rather than a stablecoin.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Cosmos Hub's ATOM at **+3.07%** net supply growth over the trailing 90 days and **+3.16%** projected forward, against a supply monitor reading **+3.27%** — a **0.21 percentage point** gap that needs no explanation beyond derivation noise. The structural mechanism is an uncapped staking mint pinned at its **10%** published ceiling but realised at **12.64%**, because the Cosmos Hub pays a per-block reward against a stored block count it beats by **26%** — **16,151,026 ATOM** created in ninety days and none removed. The key risk is that nothing on the Cosmos Hub can offset this: there is no buyback, no fee burn and no lock, so the only relief is a governance vote or a bonded ratio that climbs past **67%**. There is no ceiling at all — ATOM has no maximum supply — but there is also no hidden overhang, because the float already counts essentially every ATOM that exists.
