The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **Jito's JTO at +9.96%** net supply over the last 90 days and **+9.96%** again over the next 90, and every point of it is one mechanism: the genesis vesting calendar releasing **16.96M JTO** on the seventh of each month. JTO is a Solana SPL token whose mint authority and freeze authority both read null on-chain, so not one new JTO can ever be created — on-chain supply is **986.52M** against a genesis of **1,000M**, the difference being **13.48M** already burned. The offsetting mechanism exists on paper: JIP-38 passed on **Jul 13 2026** and commits the Jito DAO's share of JTX platform fees to buying JTO and burning it, but the realised burn to date is **zero**. Our supply monitor reads **+8.01%** for the same window; the **1.95 percentage point** difference is a smoothing artefact, not a supply conflict.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window running **May 20 2026** to **Aug 18 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **JTO at +9.96% net**: sell pressure of **50.88M JTO** against buy pressure of **0**, on a tradable base of **510.94M JTO**. Our supply monitor reads **+8.01%** for the same period, a gap of **1.95 percentage points**, and the overview page carries a monitor-gap note because that exceeds the framework's half-point tolerance. The two numbers are not describing different events. The monitor's float series climbs by roughly **0.42M JTO** a day and never steps on the seventh, so it interpolates the Jito vesting curve into a smooth line and books **37.87M**; the published Jito calendar releases three whole monthly tranches inside the same window and books **50.87M**. JTO is best labelled a **fixed-supply token still working through its final unlock quarter** — nothing is being created, and a great deal is still being released.

## Sell pressure: where new JTO comes from

Protocol inflation on Jito is **0**, and that zero is permanent rather than merely current. The JTO mint on Solana carries no mint authority and no freeze authority, a state that cannot be reversed once set, so the **1,000,000,000 JTO** issued at the Jito genesis on **Dec 7 2023** are all the JTO that will ever exist. This is worth stressing because Jito is a revenue network: MEV tips, block-engine fees and BAM plugin fees all flow through the protocol, but they are denominated in the chain's own coin, never in freshly issued JTO. There is no validator subsidy, no staking emission and no mint function behind this line at all.

Vesting unlocks are therefore the entire sell ledger, and they are large. The Jito genesis allocation put **245M JTO** with core contributors and **162M JTO** with investors — **407M** together — behind a one-year cliff that expired in December 2024, after which the allocation releases in twenty-four equal monthly tranches of **16.96M JTO** on the seventh of each month, finishing on **Nov 7 2026**. Three of those tranches fell inside this window, on **Jun 7 2026**, **Jul 7 2026** and **Aug 7 2026**, giving the **50.88M** booked here. We tested whether the released-beats-scheduled rule applies by pulling the twenty largest JTO token accounts on-chain: not one of them is a vesting or lock program, the only program-owned account in the set being the Jito governance stake pool holding **21.18M JTO**. With no readable escrow to measure, the published Jito schedule governs, and an independent model of the same release — a linear drip of **542,870 JTO** a day across contributors, investors and ecosystem development — lands at **48.86M** over ninety days, within four percent of the figure booked.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks read **0**, and this one is a measurement rather than an absence of news. The Jito DAO treasury token account held **209.42M JTO** at its last transaction before the window opened and holds **209.47M JTO** now — a move of **46,285 JTO** across three months, which is noise, not a release. The Jito Foundation multisig holds **0 JTO**. Long-term locked or bankruptcy reads **0** for the simplest reason available: JTO has never been part of a bankruptcy estate, so there is no trustee schedule to draw down.

## Buy pressure: where new JTO goes

The headline buy-side mechanism is JIP-38, which the Jito DAO passed on **Jul 13 2026**. It commits the DAO's revenue share from JTX — Jito's self-custody trading platform, of which the DAO takes eighty percent of platform fees — into an on-chain Rev Splitter that buys JTO on the open market and burns every token it acquires, binding through **Q4 2027**. The destination is a burn, which is the strongest form a buyback can take, and it means a realised flow would show up directly as a fall in JTO's on-chain supply. That is exactly the test we ran. JTX opened phased early access on **Jul 14 2026**, and the JTO mint still reads **986,522,976.85** — precisely genesis less the **13.48M** of cumulative burn that a treasury programme completed before this window opened. So the programmatic buyback books **0**: the mechanism is real and binding, the realised burn is nil, and an announced commitment is not a measured flow.

Protocol fee burn reads **0** because Jito has no JTO fee sink at all — tips and fees are collected in the network's own coin, so no transaction on the chain destroys JTO. Foundation buy reads **0**, again on-chain rather than by inference: the Jito DAO treasury's JTO balance grew by **46,285 JTO** across the whole window, which no accumulation programme could hide behind. New long-term lock reads **0**: the **21.18M JTO** in the Jito governance staking pool can be withdrawn at will, which makes it custody rather than a lock, and locking already-circulating coins that can leave freely is not supply removal.

## Foundation and overhang

The JTO overhang is concentrated and readable. The largest single piece is the Jito DAO treasury, whose token account holds **209.47M JTO** — roughly forty-one percent of the tradable float sitting in one governed wallet, watched on-chain every rebuild and confirmed flat across this window. The Jito Foundation multisig, listed in the project's own transparency filing, holds **0 JTO**. Beyond those, **123.2M JTO** of genesis allocation remains locked on the vesting calendar: the final **50.88M** of the contributor and investor tranches through **Nov 7 2026**, and the slower ecosystem development stream that runs into late 2027. The governance stake pool holds a further **21.18M JTO** that is liquid on withdrawal. Each of these carries the same trigger: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How JTO compares to other fixed-supply infrastructure tokens

Against an uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1, JTO has the better long-run shape and the worse quarter. A chain that pays validators from new issuance dilutes forever, and its holders can never point to a date on which dilution ends; Jito cannot mint at all, and its dilution has a hard terminal date of **Nov 7 2026** for the contributor and investor calendar. The catch is that the framework measures the next ninety days, not the next ten years, and inside this particular quarter a finishing unlock schedule is heavier than most live emission curves. That is the honest shape of the trade: JTO is paying its dilution down in a short, loud burst instead of a long, quiet drip.

Against a Solana peer with the same architecture — a fixed SPL supply, mint authority renounced, dilution driven purely by a vesting calendar — JTO differs mainly in rhythm. A token that releases its locked supply in one enormous annual cliff shows a brutal quarter and three quiet ones; Jito's monthly tranches spread the same effect evenly, which is easier for a market to absorb but never gives holders a clean window. Against an exchange token with a quarterly buyback-and-burn, JIP-38 is architecturally the same promise and one step behind in evidence: the destination is a genuine burn rather than a treasury that could sell later, which is the better design, but the mechanism has produced no measurable destruction since JTX launched on **Jul 14 2026**. A burn that is real but empty scores the same as no burn, and it should — the framework counts tokens, not intentions.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the three remaining Jito vesting tranches on **Sep 7 2026**, **Oct 7 2026** and **Nov 7 2026**, each **16.96M JTO** — the last one closes the contributor and investor calendar permanently and is the single most important date on this page. Second, the JIP-38 Rev Splitter's per-epoch reports of fees collected, JTO acquired and JTO burned; the first epoch that moves JTO's on-chain supply below **986.52M** converts the buy side from a zero into a real number. Third, JTX trading volume, since the burn is a fixed share of platform fees and the platform is only weeks old with unproven revenue. Fourth, the Jito DAO treasury balance of **209.47M JTO**, which has been flat all quarter and would be the largest discretionary supply event on the network if it moved. Fifth, any governance proposal that extends buybacks beyond JTX revenue into the DAO's existing reserves, which was floated alongside JIP-37 in **July 2026** and remains the fastest route to a negative reading here.
