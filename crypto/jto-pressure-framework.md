## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Jito at **+7.58% net**: about **38.5M JTO** of vesting release reaching the market against effectively **zero** buy pressure, on a circulating base of **507.62M JTO**. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+8.26%**, a gap of about **0.68 percentage points** that is over tolerance, so this build ships a **monitor-gap chip**. The gap is a denominator convention, not a data conflict: the same ~38.5M of vesting is **+8.21%** measured against the supply 90 days ago (~468.9M) and **+7.58%** against today's larger circulating base, which accounts for nearly all of it, and the small remainder is day-to-day noise in the monitor's price-derived supply. JTO is **structurally inflationary on its unlock calendar** — a fixed-supply governance token that mints nothing, but whose three-year genesis vest still floods the float faster than any buyback yet absorbs.

## Sell pressure: where new JTO comes from

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the entire sell side, at about **38.5M JTO** over the next 90 days. Jito's genesis allocation gave investors **16.21%** and core contributors **24.50%** of supply — **407.1M JTO** combined — on a three-year linear vest with a one-year cliff. That cliff fell in December 2024, and the linear release runs until it completes on **Dec 7 2026**. On the published calendar the release is about **16.96M JTO** a month, but only about **12.8M** a month is actually reaching the tradable float — the rest lands in still-custodied or unclaimed recipient wallets — so roughly **38.5M JTO** arrives over 90 days. The framework books the realised market-arrival rate, not the gross calendar figure, because coins that vested on paper but never left a custody wallet are not sell pressure yet.

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and structurally so. Every one of the **1,000M JTO** was minted at the December 2023 launch; JTO is a governance token with no block subsidy, no staking-reward mint and no other emission path, so total supply can only fall from here. The protocol earns real revenue — MEV tips routed through its Solana block engine, plus fees on the **JitoSOL** liquid-staking token — but that value is earned in SOL and dollars, never by printing JTO. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is also **zero** on this build, though the overhang behind it is large: about **492M JTO**, roughly **49%** of supply, is still locked. Most of that is the Foundation- and DAO-custodied Community Growth (**34.29%**) and Ecosystem Development (**25.00%**) allocations, which vest on a slow six-year linear schedule and did not move off-calendar in the window. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Jito is a live protocol with no bankruptcy estate.

## Buy pressure: where new JTO goes

Every buy row is **zero** on this build, but one of them is about to matter. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is the story: a governance vote, **JIP-38**, passed on **Jul 13 2026** and commits **100%** of the Jito DAO's revenue share from **JTX** — Jito's new self-custodial Solana exchange, which routes the DAO about **80%** of platform fees — into an open-market JTO buyback that is then burned, executed on-chain through a Rev Splitter and verifiable each epoch, running through Q4 2027. This turns JTO from a pure governance token into a value-accrual token. But JTX only launched on **Jul 14 2026**, opening to its first 1,000 users, so no epoch figure for JTO bought or burned has been published yet; with a single source and no dated on-chain quantum, the framework books it at **zero** and monitors it rather than inventing a number.

The other three buy rows are zero by structure. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero** because Jito has no per-transaction burn; JTO is destroyed only through the JIP-38 buyback in Buy #1. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero** because the Jito Foundation discloses no discretionary open-market buying outside that automatic program. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: JTO has no staking lockup, and the buyback burns the coins it acquires rather than locking them.

## Foundation and overhang

The team- and community-controlled overhang on Jito is roughly **492M JTO** — the locked balance behind the **507.62M** circulating out of a fixed **1,000M**. It splits across the genesis allocations still releasing: the remainder of the investor (**16.21%**) and core-contributor (**24.50%**) vest that completes on **Dec 7 2026**, and the Foundation- and DAO-custodied Community Growth (**34.29%**) and Ecosystem Development (**25.00%**) allocations that vest on a slow six-year linear schedule and are governed by JTO holders. These balances are tracked against the published vesting calendar and the on-chain circulating read at every rebuild. If any of them falls between refreshes faster than the schedule allows — an off-calendar Foundation or DAO distribution — the excess outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh rather than being absorbed silently into the vesting row.

## How JTO compares to other fixed-supply value-accrual tokens

JTO belongs to a specific structural class: the fixed-supply governance token whose near-term inflation is set entirely by a genesis vesting calendar rather than by any block subsidy. Compared with an uncapped continuous-emission chain like Solana — the very network Jito is built on — Jito mints nothing at all; Solana issues genuinely new SOL every epoch, whereas JTO's **1,000M** is fixed and its supply can only fall. That makes the comparison with capped-but-vesting tokens like Jupiter's JUP or the newer Solana governance tokens far more apt than any comparison with a mint-based L1: the dominant supply variable is an unlock schedule, and once that schedule ends the inflation simply stops.

The more interesting comparison is with exchange tokens that run a revenue buyback-and-burn, like BNB. With **JIP-38**, Jito has just installed exactly that machinery — **100%** of the DAO's JTX revenue share buying and burning JTO on the open market — the same class of lever that lets BNB post steady deflation from exchange fees. The difference today is entirely maturity: BNB's burn runs on years of exchange volume, while JTX is weeks old and has posted no epoch figure yet. So JTO reads like a fixed-supply token caught between two regimes — the tail end of a large vesting calendar that is still adding supply, and the front end of a buyback that has not yet started subtracting it.

That makes the structural ceiling on JTO's framework score clear and near-term. As long as the 2023 vest runs, no realistic buyback offsets it, so JTO reads firmly inflationary through the end of 2026. Once the investor and core-contributor calendar completes on **Dec 7 2026**, the arithmetic flips fast: the only remaining supply growth is the slow six-year community and ecosystem vest, and the JTX buyback — if JTX volume is real — starts to net against it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The first thing to watch is the **Rev Splitter** epoch dashboard: JIP-38 promises per-epoch on-chain figures for fees collected, JTO acquired and JTO burned, and the first real numbers will show whether the JTX buyback is a rounding error or a genuine buy row — the moment it posts a quantum, it books in Buy #1. Second, watch the monthly vest around **Sep 7 2026** and **Oct 7 2026**: each releases roughly **12.8M JTO** to the float and keeps the net firmly positive. Third, the key dated event is **Dec 7 2026**, when the three-year investor and core-contributor vest completes and the dominant source of JTO supply growth falls away — watch the following build's forward net drop sharply. Finally, watch JTX trading volume itself, since the buyback scales directly with it and is the only path to a deflationary print.
