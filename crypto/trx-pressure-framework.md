# TRX Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/trx/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/trx/inflation).*

<!-- main-page -->
**Main page:** the full TRX coin page — signal, price drivers and the buy/sell questions → [mrnasdog.com/research/trx](https://mrnasdog.com/research/trx)

TRX supply is **roughly steady, edging up**. Every TRON block pays **136 new TRX**, there is no supply cap, and fees paid in TRX are burned. From Aug 20 to Nov 18 2026 the chain created **352.39M TRX** and burned **217.77M TRX**, so supply grew **+0.14%**; other trackers show **+0.12%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 18 2026, TRON paid out **352.39M** new TRX in block and voting rewards and destroyed **217.77M** TRX in fees. That leaves a net rise of about **134.6M TRX**, or **+0.14%** of the 95.05B TRX in circulation. Other supply trackers show **+0.12%** for the same stretch, a gap of about two hundredths of a point, so the coin page carries **no warning note**. The label is **Mixed flows · supply roughly steady**: TRX is slowly growing again, but by a small amount, and the next 90 days look about the same at **+0.14%**.

A year ago the picture was different. From Aug 20 to Nov 17 2025 the burn was slightly larger than the new coins, and TRX supply shrank a little. The change came from two network votes in 2025 that pulled in opposite directions, and the fee cut won.

## Sell pressure: where new TRX comes from

**Block and voting rewards: 352.39M TRX.** TRON is run by 27 elected block producers, and every block they make creates 136 new TRX. Eight TRX go to the producer of the block. The other 128 TRX are shared among the top 127 voted candidates, who pass much of it on to the people who voted for them. A block comes every three seconds, and from Aug 20 to Nov 18 2026 the chain made 2,591,081 blocks, which works out to 352.39M TRX, close to 3.92M a day. A missed block pays nothing, so the count follows the real block count, not the clock.

The pay rate was cut in June 2025, when block producers voted to halve the block reward from 16 to 8 TRX and lower the voting reward from 160 to 128 TRX. No vote since then has touched the rewards. The two upgrades inside this window, on Aug 28 2026 and Nov 2 2026, changed how smart contracts run and left the pay rate alone.

**Vesting unlocks: 0.** TRX has no vesting schedule. The coins from the 2017 sale and the team share were released years ago, and today almost every TRX that exists already counts as circulating.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks: 0.** The foundation's coins are already part of the circulating count, so even a sale would not add new TRX. The two early foundation wallets did not move any TRX in the window.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy: 0.** No court case or bankrupt firm is holding TRX for a later payout. The thief in the Oct 29 2026 Binance hack still held about 475M TRX in early November, but those coins were circulating before the theft, so moving them changes nothing in the count.

## Buy pressure: where new TRX goes

**Fee burn: 217.77M TRX.** To send a transaction on TRON, an account needs bandwidth and, for smart contracts like a USDT transfer, energy. Users can get both by staking TRX, or they can pay in TRX on the spot. TRX paid on the spot is destroyed. From Aug 20 to Nov 18 2026 that burned 217.77M TRX, about 2.42M a day and 62% of the new coins. The burn per day rose through the window, from about 2.35M in late August to about 2.57M in November, with a peak of 3.64M TRX on Nov 2 2026.

Two things keep the burn below the rewards. In August 2025 block producers cut the price of energy by more than half, from 210 to 100 sun, which made every fee cheaper. And more users now pay with staked energy instead of burning TRX: TRON's own Q3 2026 fee review put the staked share above 90% of all energy in September 2026. Busier traffic alone has not been enough to win the burn back.

**Programmatic buyback: 0.** No contract or treasury buys TRX back for the network. The fee burn is the only built-in way TRX leaves the supply.

**Foundation buy: 0.** The foundation does not buy TRX back. Tron Inc., a company listed in the US, keeps buying small lots and held 724.1M TRX on Nov 17 2026. Its buying takes TRX off the market, but those coins were already circulating, so the supply count stays the same.

**New long-term lock: 0.** Staked TRX still counts as circulating, so staking removes nothing. About 45.26B TRX was staked on Nov 17 2026, a little less than the 45.68B on Aug 19 2026.

## Foundation and overhang

TRX has no locked team or investor batch waiting to be released. The two early foundation wallets, opened in June 2018, hold **283.0M TRX** between them and sent no TRX out between Aug 20 and Nov 18 2026. Reports from September 2025, based on a list of holdings his team shared, put about **60B TRX**, more than 60% of the supply, with the founder Justin Sun, a figure he disputes. Tron Inc. holds **724.1M TRX**, most of it staked. The Binance hacker held about **475M TRX** in early November 2026.

None of these holdings adds to the supply count if it is sold, because all of it already counts as circulating. What these large holders could do is move the price: a big sale from any of them would add selling on the market, even though the number of TRX would not change.

## How TRX compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

TRX works like Ethereum's ETH in one key way: both pay their block producers in new coins and burn part of the fees, so supply rises when traffic is quiet and can fall when fees run hot. The difference is how TRON users pay fees. On Ethereum every transaction burns some ETH. On TRON most users stake TRX for energy and pay nothing on the spot, so a large share of TRON's busy stablecoin traffic never reaches the burn.

TRX also differs from chains that set a falling inflation schedule in advance. TRON has no schedule and no cap. Its reward rate changes only when the block producers vote, as they did in June 2025, and they can lower fees the same way, as they did in August 2025. The supply path is set by those votes and by how many users choose to burn instead of stake, not by a timetable.

Compared with coins that face large unlocks, TRX is simple. Nothing vests, and the only two flows are the block pay and the burn. That keeps the net change small: **+0.14%** in 90 days, which is low against most proof-of-stake chains.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**The burn rate.** The burn per day rose from about 2.35M TRX in August to about 2.57M in November 2026. If it climbs toward 3.92M a day, the same as the new coins, TRX supply would stop growing.

**Fee proposals.** A proposal opened on Oct 6 2026 would cut the multi-signature fee from 1 TRX to 0.5 TRX, and another would lower the account permission fee. Neither has a vote date, and both would trim the burn only slightly.

**The next fee review.** TRON's Q3 2026 fee review, posted on Oct 21 2026, warned that net supply had risen eleven months in a row. These reviews come out every quarter, so a Q4 2026 review should follow early in 2027; any change to fees or rewards would still need a new vote by the block producers.

**Large holders.** Tron Inc.'s regular buying and the roughly 475M TRX still with the Binance hacker in early November 2026 can move the price, though not the supply count.

## Summary

TRON creates 136 new TRX with every block, which came to 352.39M TRX from Aug 20 to Nov 18 2026. Fees paid in TRX burned 217.77M TRX over the same days, about 62% of the new coins. Net, TRX supply rose about 0.14% in 90 days and should rise about the same in the next 90. Nothing vests, and the large holdings of the foundation, the founder and Tron Inc. already count as circulating, so only a change in the burn or a new vote on rewards would shift the picture.
