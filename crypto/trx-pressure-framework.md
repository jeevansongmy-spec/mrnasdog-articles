# TRX Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/trx/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/trx/inflation).*

TRX supply grew about **+0.14%** in the 90 days from Aug 10 to Nov 8 2026, and it should grow about the same in the next 90 days: mixed flows, with supply roughly steady. TRON pays the makers of every block and their voters in brand-new TRX, **352.39M TRX** in the window, while the fees users pay for energy and bandwidth burned **219.85M TRX**, so supply rose by about **132.54M TRX**. TRX has no supply cap and no vesting, and other trackers show **+0.14%** for the same 90 days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 8 2026, TRX supply rose a net **+0.14%**, and the next 90 days point to the same **+0.14%**. Other trackers read **+0.14%** for the window too, a gap of **0.00 percentage points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. The TRON fee burn now hands back only about six of every ten new TRX, because most users pay for energy by staking TRX instead of burning it. A year earlier the burn was larger than the new coins and TRX supply was shrinking; today TRON is a slowly inflating payments chain, growing at about 0.6% a year.

## Sell pressure: where new TRX comes from

The only source of new TRX is the block reward. Every TRON block, made every 3 seconds, creates **136 TRX**: 8 go to the Super Representative that produced the block, and 128 go to a pool shared by the top 127 candidates and the TRX holders who vote for them. That rate was set by a vote of the Super Representatives in June 2025 and has not changed since. Between Aug 10 and Nov 8 2026 TRON produced **2,591,087 blocks**, which paid out **352,387,832 TRX**, about 3.92M TRX a day. Because the reward is paid per block, a few missed blocks lower it slightly; the count above is the real one.

Vesting unlocks add **0 TRX**. Total TRX and circulating TRX differ by less than 1M coins, so no team, investor or ecosystem batch is locked and waiting to open. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks also add **0 TRX**: no TRON foundation or DAO wallet sits outside the circulating count, so when a project-linked wallet moves TRX, those coins were already counted. Long-term locked or bankruptcy coins add **0 TRX** as well. No court case or bankrupt firm holds TRX to pay out later, and the roughly **475M TRX** still held by the thief behind the Oct 29 2026 Binance hack was bought on the open market, so it was already in circulation.

## Buy pressure: where new TRX goes

TRON has no buyback, so the programmatic buyback row is **0 TRX**. All of the buy side is the fee burn. On TRON, sending USDT or calling a smart contract costs energy and bandwidth. A user can stake TRX to get those resources for free, or pay for them in TRX, and the TRX paid is destroyed. From Aug 10 to Nov 8 2026 the TRON fee burn destroyed **219,851,213 TRX**, about 2.44M TRX a day, or **62%** of the new coins. The energy price has been 100 sun per unit since Aug 2025, when the Super Representatives cut it from 210 sun, and the busiest burn day in the window was Nov 2 2026, at **3.64M TRX**, in the days after the Binance hack.

Foundation buying is **0 TRX**. No foundation buys TRX back. Tron Inc., a company listed on Nasdaq, buys about $50,000 of TRX every day and held about **722M TRX** on Nov 7 2026, and the first US staked TRX fund has traded since Sep 9 2026. Both buy coins that already circulate, so their buying takes nothing out of the count. New long-term locks are **0 TRX** too: staked TRX still counts as circulating, and the amount staked actually slipped from **45.78B** to **45.31B TRX** between Aug 9 and Nov 7 2026.

## Foundation and overhang

TRON has almost no coins outside the float. Of about **95.03B TRX** in existence, all but about 0.87M count as circulating, so there is no locked reserve that could suddenly reach the market. The large holders we watch are already inside that number. Wallets linked to founder Justin Sun were reported in Sep 2025 to hold around **60B TRX**, roughly six in ten of all TRX, though Justin Sun disputes which wallets are his. Tron Inc. holds about **722M TRX** and became a Super Representative in October 2026, so its block rewards are already part of the new-coin count. The Binance hacker holds about **475M TRX**. We re-check these balances at every rebuild; if any of these wallets ever sits outside the circulating count and its balance falls between checks, the outflow enters the foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next check. Until then, a sale by any of them moves price, not supply.

## How TRX compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

TRX and ETH share the same basic design: no supply cap, new coins paid to the people who secure the chain, and a burn of part of every fee. The difference is the size of the burn. Ethereum's burn handed back only about 2.4% of its new ETH in its latest 90 days, while the TRON fee burn handed back about 62% of new TRX. TRON charges a real price for each smart-contract call, and the USDT payments that run on TRON make those calls all day, so the burn is a large, steady force rather than a rounding error.

Against exchange coins with scheduled burns, TRX looks different again. A coin with a quarterly burn shrinks in steps on known dates; TRX burns continuously, block by block, and the amount follows how many users pay in TRX rather than staking for energy. That makes TRON sensitive to fee policy. When the energy price was cut from 210 to 100 sun in Aug 2025, the burn fell sharply, and within a few months TRX went from shrinking to growing. A TRON community fee review posted on Oct 21 2026 found that staking now covers more than 90% of energy, and that in September 2026 the burn covered only 60% of new TRX.

Against chains that print a fixed percentage a year and burn little, TRX sits in the middle. Its gross issuance of about 1.5% of supply a year is modest, and the burn cuts the net to about 0.6%. The TRX mint is fixed per block, not per percent of supply, so as supply grows the rate slowly falls; the burn, not the mint, decides whether TRX grows or shrinks.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the TRON fee proposals. TIP-938, opened on Oct 6 2026, would halve the fee for multi-signature setups from 1 TRX to 0.5 TRX, and TIP-915, opened on Jul 23 2026, would cut the account-permission fee from 100 TRX to 8 TRX; neither has a vote date, and either would trim the burn only a little. A change to the energy price or the 136 TRX block reward would matter far more, and none is proposed as of Nov 8 2026.

Second, the next quarterly fee review, expected in January 2027 after the Q3 2026 review of Oct 21 2026. If the staking share of energy keeps rising above 90%, the burn will keep slipping and TRX supply growth will edge up from **+0.14%**.

Third, the big holders. Tron Inc. filed on Oct 15 2026 to sell up to $200M of new shares to buy more TRX, and the Binance hacker still held about 475M TRX on Nov 3 2026. Both move price, not supply, because those TRX already circulate. Fourth, USDT traffic on TRON: more stablecoin payments mean more energy, and any of it paid in TRX is burned. ## Summary

TRX supply grew **+0.14%** in the 90 days to Nov 8 2026, with **352.39M TRX** paid to TRON block makers and voters and **219.85M TRX** destroyed by the fee burn, and the next 90 days look the same. TRON has no supply cap, no vesting and almost no coins outside the float, so the mint and the burn are the whole story. The key risk is fee policy: with more than 90% of energy now paid by staking, the TRON burn covers only about six in ten new TRX, and TRX keeps inflating slowly unless USDT traffic paid in TRX grows or the block reward is cut.
