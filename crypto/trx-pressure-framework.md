# TRX Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/trx/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/trx/inflation).*

TRX supply is roughly steady, rising slowly. Over the 90 days to Sep 29 2026, TRON block rewards created **352,388,648 TRX** and the TRON fee burn destroyed **228,386,206 TRX**, so net supply grew by **124.0M TRX**, or **+0.13%** of the **94.98B TRX** in circulation. The monitor reads **+0.14%** over the same stretch. TRX has no supply cap, no vesting left and no locked bucket, so the whole story is one mint against one burn.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads TRX at **+0.13%** net supply over the last 90 days and projects the same **+0.13%** for the next 90 days. The monitor, which measures supply from market data, reads **+0.14%**. The gap is **0.01 percentage points**, well inside the 0.5-point line, so no warning chip is shown and the two readings agree. Put as a yearly pace, TRON supply is growing by roughly **0.5%** a year. The label that fits TRX today: **a mildly inflationary chain where fee burn cancels about two-thirds of new issuance**.

## Sell pressure: where new TRX comes from

Protocol inflation is the only source of new TRX: **352.39M TRX** in 90 days. Every TRON block pays **136 TRX** — 8 TRX to the Super Representative that produced it and 128 TRX into the reward pool shared by the voters of the top 127 candidates. The TRON chain produced **2,591,093 blocks** across the window, a block about every three seconds, and 136 times that block count equals the new supply to the coin. That works out to about **3.92M TRX a day**. The 136-TRX reward was set by a governance vote in June 2025, when it was lowered from 176 TRX, and no vote to change it again is open, so the next 90 days should mint about the same 352.39M TRX.

Vesting unlocks add **0 TRX**. The 2017 token sale, team and TRON Foundation allocations all finished unlocking years ago, no unlock tracker lists a remaining TRX schedule, and the circulating count equals total supply, so there is no locked bucket left to open.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks add **0 TRX**. Because every TRX already sits inside the circulating count, a Foundation wallet, an exchange or a company treasury moving or selling TRX shifts coins that were already counted. Only a mint can add supply on TRON.

Long-term locks and bankruptcy add **0 TRX**. There is no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or unwinding lock releasing TRX. The TRON stake did shrink, from **45.77B** to **45.18B TRX**, but staked TRX was always counted as circulating, so unstaking releases nothing new.

## Buy pressure: where new TRX goes

The programmatic buyback is **0 TRX**. The TRON protocol has no buyback contract, and no treasury is set up to buy TRX off the market on a schedule.

The protocol fee burn is the whole buy side: **228.39M TRX** in 90 days, about **2.54M TRX a day**. On TRON, every fee paid in TRX is destroyed — bandwidth and energy for transfers and smart contracts, new-account fees, memo fees and multisig fees. There is no fee pool and no fee share to validators, so the burn tracks how much TRX people spend on the network. The burn is slowing without any fee change behind it: about **2.69M TRX a day** in July, **2.35M** in September and **2.12M** over the last week. About 90% of the energy used on TRON now comes from staked TRX rather than burned fees, and the energy price was cut from 210 to 100 sun in August 2025, which cut the burn per day by about 40% a year ago. With no new fee change on the books, the framework holds the trailing 228.39M TRX for the next 90 days.

The Foundation buy is **0 TRX**. TRON DAO reports buybacks for other tokens on the TRON chain, but no announcement or on-chain flow this window shows it buying TRX. A Nasdaq-listed company, Tron Inc., keeps adding TRX to its treasury — more than **715.8M TRX** after a purchase on Sep 23 2026 — but it buys coins already in the market and keeps them in a wallet still counted as circulating, so its buying is a move inside the float, not a buy row.

The new long-term lock is **0 TRX**. Nearly half of all TRX is staked for votes and network resources, and a new staked TRX ETF launched on Sep 9 2026, but staked TRX stays in the circulating count and the total stake fell this window.

## Foundation and overhang

TRX has no meaningful team-controlled overhang left outside the float. The old TRON Foundation fund accounts that the chain explorer still lists hold about **283 TRX** in total and have not sent anything since 2020. No TRON DAO wallet is labelled on-chain, and total supply minus circulating supply is below zero today, so there is no excluded bucket that could be released. The large holders that do exist — exchange cold wallets, the Tron Inc. treasury of more than 715.8M TRX, the staked TRX ETF and the 45.18B TRX stake — are all already inside the circulating count. The framework re-reads these balances at every rebuild. If a wallet that sits outside the circulating count ever appears and its balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How TRX compares to other uncapped Layer 1 chains

TRX belongs to the group of uncapped Layer 1 coins that pay a fixed or near-fixed reward per block and burn part of what users pay. Ethereum is the closest mechanism match: ETH also has no cap, pays validators in new ETH and burns the base fee. The difference is the size of the burn. TRON burns every fee, not just a base-fee slice, so on TRX the burn cancels about **65%** of new issuance, far more than a base-fee-only burn usually does. TRON also pays a flat 136 TRX a block, while Ethereum's issuance rises and falls with the size of its stake.

Against capped, halving-model chains like Bitcoin, TRX has no ceiling and no halving: new supply only changes when Super Representatives vote on the reward. Against exchange-chain coins that run a scheduled auto-burn on top of a fee burn, TRX has no second burn engine and no buyback, so there is no quarterly event that can swing the ledger. Its supply path is set by two dials, the block reward and the fee prices, and both are changed only by on-chain governance votes.

That makes TRX unusual in one way. Through 2024 and the first half of 2025, the TRON fee burn was larger than issuance and supply shrank; since the August 2025 energy price cut, TRX has been mildly inflationary. The TRON chain has burned about **16.91B TRX** in fees since launch, which is why supply today, **94.98B TRX**, still sits below the 100B TRX created at genesis.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the burn rate. If the last week's pace of about 2.12M TRX a day holds for the whole next 90 days, the burn would come in near 190M TRX and net supply growth would rise toward **+0.17%**. Second, an open TRON proposal to cut two fees — account-permission updates from 100 to 8 TRX and multisig transactions from 1 to 0.5 TRX — was still under discussion on Sep 29 2026 with no on-chain vote; it would trim the burn only a little. Third, any Super Representative vote on the 136-TRX block reward or the 100-sun energy price, the two dials that move TRX supply; none was open on Sep 30 2026. Fourth, TRON's quarterly fee review for Q3 2026, which tracks burn against issuance and has preceded past fee changes. Fifth, the Tron Inc. treasury and the staked TRX ETF: their buying moves coins inside the float and does not change the ledger, but a large sale by either would be worth a look.

## Summary

TRX supply grew **+0.13%** over the 90 days to Sep 29 2026 and is projected to grow about the same over the next 90 days, with the monitor at **+0.14%**. TRON mints 136 TRX per block, **352.39M TRX** in 90 days, and burns every fee, **228.39M TRX**, so the fee burn cancels about two-thirds of issuance. The key risk is a falling burn: fees are declining as more users stake for energy, which pushes net supply up unless governance changes the reward. TRX has no supply cap, but it still has less than its 100B TRX genesis supply.
