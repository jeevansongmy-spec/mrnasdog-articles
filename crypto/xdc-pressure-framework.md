# XDC Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply shrinking · projected to keep shrinking

XDC Network is an uncapped XDPoS chain that mints almost nothing and still reads as one of the most deflationary assets in the catalog — because of where its coins are going, not because any are being destroyed. Over the last 90 days the XDC protocol minted **18.76M XDC** in masternode block rewards, roughly **0.38%** a year, while node operators locked **890.13M XDC** into the masternode collateral contract and the XDC fee burn destroyed just **14,605** coins. Net supply reaching the market was **−4.37%** of circulating, against **+0.04%** on the inflation monitor — a gap of **4.41** percentage points that comes entirely from the monitor counting staked XDC as circulating. The forward window reads the same **−4.37%**, and the whole reading is reversible: masternode collateral is withdrawable after a delay of about **30** days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The XDC Pressure Framework ledger has one large number on each side and both were read from the XDC Network chain at the two ends of the same 90-day window. Sell pressure is **18.76M XDC** of XDPoS block reward. Buy pressure is **890.14M XDC**, of which **890.13M** is new masternode collateral and **0.015M** is the fee burn. That nets to **−4.37%** of circulating supply over 90 days. The inflation monitor reads **+0.04%** for the same window, a gap of **4.41** percentage points, which is over the framework's half-point tolerance and therefore ships with a warning chip. The gap is not an error on either side: the masternode collateral contract sits inside the classified circulating supply, so **890.13M XDC** can move into it without the monitor's number changing at all. Add the lock back and the two readings agree to within **0.05** percentage points. The cite-able label for XDC in August 2026: **deflationary by absorption, not by burn — and reversible**.

## Sell pressure: where new XDC comes from

There is exactly one source of new XDC, and it is the XDPoS block reward. The XDC Network pays node operators freshly minted coins at every 900-block checkpoint, and this build read the rate off the chain rather than out of a document: the network reports a reward of **5,000 XDC** per checkpoint, which is **5.5556 XDC** a block, not the **5.5** the widely-copied community tokenomics page rounds it to. The rate was confirmed a second way — the wallet entitled to a fixed 10% share of every checkpoint was credited exactly **500 XDC** at 31 consecutive checkpoints, with no exceptions. The XDC chain produced **3,377,002** blocks in the window at a measured **2.30** seconds each, about 15% slower than the 2-second parameter, so Sell #1 is **18.76M XDC**. A longer baseline shows the reward stepped down from a higher level well before the window opened, around **Mar 2026**, so nothing changed mid-window and the forward column runs at the same rate.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each for a different reason. Vesting unlocks are zero because XDC releases on a single annual cliff every **Feb 5** to the ecosystem allocation: the last one fired **Feb 5 2026** and the next is **Feb 5 2027**, so no unlock lands in either the trailing or the forward 90-day window. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero because the XDC reserve did not move: three wallets holding **18,281M XDC** between them read identical balances at both ends of the window, to the last unit, and that is essentially the entire **18,119M** gap between XDC's total supply and its circulating supply. Long-term locked or bankruptcy is zero because there is no bankruptcy estate holding XDC and no trustee disposal schedule anywhere in the asset's history.

## Buy pressure: where new XDC goes

The XDC buy ledger is dominated by one mechanism that is not a burn and not a buyback. Programmatic buyback is zero: the XDC protocol keeps no revenue to buy with — transaction fees split between the block producer and the burn, and neither leg reaches a treasury — and no buyback contract or mandate exists. Foundation buy is zero: no entity behind XDC Network has disclosed an open-market purchase in the trailing year, with no dated quantum and no wallet observably accumulating.

The XDC fee burn is real but immaterial. Since the fee upgrade shipped, a transaction's base fee is destroyed rather than paid to anyone — the node credits the block producer with the tip alone — and that base fee sits pinned at the protocol floor in every one of the 420 blocks sampled across the window, so the burn tracks gas used and nothing else. It came to **14,605 XDC** over 90 days, or **0.015M**, against **18.76M** minted. This build checked the burn from the other side too, because a burn is often a transfer rather than a supply-reducing call: the unspendable addresses on the XDC chain took in **22 XDC** across the entire quarter, which is misdirected sends, not a programme.

New long-term lock is the row that decides the XDC page. Running a node on XDC Network means posting collateral into a single contract, **0x0000…0088**, and getting it back only after resigning and waiting out a withdrawal delay of roughly **30** days. That contract held **2,600.9M XDC** when the window opened and **3,491.0M XDC** when it closed — **890.13M XDC** pulled off the tradable float in 90 days, **4.46%** of circulating supply, and roughly **47** times everything the chain minted. It is many operators rather than one transfer: the deposits arrive in **10M** chunks at the masternode minimum, the registered operator count rose from **257** to **342** between the same two blocks, and 85 new operators at that minimum accounts for **850M** of the balance change on its own. In the six months before this window the same contract added only **50M**, so the wave is new and in-window.

## Foundation and overhang

XDC carries a large team-controlled overhang and it was static this quarter. Three reserve wallets hold **13,446M**, **3,609M** and **1,226M XDC** — **18,281M** together — and each read the same balance at the window open and the window close, refreshed from the chain on every rebuild. Those wallets are the non-circulating bucket, and they release on the annual **Feb 5** cliff rather than on a continuous vest. The XDC Foundation treasury wallet named in the project's own 2021 tokenomics write-up now reads zero and is no longer an overhang. The one live foundation stream is the 10% share of each block reward, already counted inside Sell #1, and it is swept out periodically to a pass-through wallet that held nothing at either end of the window.

The fourth tracked overhang is the mirror image of the buy side: the masternode collateral contract itself now holds **3,491.0M XDC**, dispersed across **342** independent operators, with no schedule — it is withdrawable whenever an operator resigns and waits out the delay. That is the asymmetry a reader has to hold in mind. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and for the collateral contract specifically a falling balance would flip the XDC net reading from deeply negative to positive in a single quarter.

## How XDC compares to other delegated proof-of-stake chains

Against the rest of the delegated proof-of-stake class, XDC Network's mint is unusually small. Most uncapped continuous-emission layer-1s run staking issuance in the low single digits of percent per year and some run far higher; XDC mints about **0.38%** a year, because its reward is a flat **5,000 XDC** per checkpoint rather than a percentage of stake. That flat-quantum design is the structural difference: on a percentage-of-stake chain, more staking means more issuance, so the lock and the mint grow together. On XDC the mint is indifferent to how much is staked, which is exactly why **890.13M** of new collateral can swamp **18.76M** of issuance without any feedback pushing back.

Against hard-capped proof-of-work chains, XDC is the opposite shape. A capped mining chain has a ceiling and a scheduled step-down, and its buy side is usually empty — its deflation, when it has any, has to come from the halving. XDC has no cap at all and a reward that only governance can change, so it can never argue scarcity from the supply schedule. Everything negative in the XDC reading is on the demand-for-collateral side.

Against fee-burn chains, the comparison is the sharpest. A chain that burns its base fee turns usage into permanent supply destruction, and at scale that burn can exceed issuance outright. XDC has the same mechanism — the XDC base fee is destroyed, not paid out — but the base fee is pinned at the protocol floor and the chain's throughput is modest, so the burn came to **14,605 XDC** in a quarter. XDC's negative reading is therefore custody-driven rather than usage-driven, and custody can be undone in a way a burn cannot.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the masternode collateral contract. It is the entire XDC thesis, and the trailing 30 days ran at a pace equivalent to **810M** a quarter while the trailing 45 days ran at **940M** — the wave is still going, but a flat or falling balance would move XDC from **−4.37%** to roughly **+0.09%** immediately. Second, the node reward automation upgrade targeted for **Oct 2026**, which would bring Protector and Observer node rewards fully on chain and, if approved by governance and deployed to mainnet, change the XDC block reward for the first time since around **Mar 2026**. Third, the annual vesting cliff on **Feb 5 2027** — outside this forward window, but the single largest dated event on the XDC calendar and the reason the reserve balance is refreshed every build. Fourth, XDCDAO governance, which holds the authority to change both the burn share and the masternode reward; nothing was voted in this window. Fifth, the three reserve wallets, which have been exactly static and whose first movement would be the strongest signal on this page.

## Summary

XDC Network mints **18.76M XDC** a quarter from its XDPoS block reward — about **0.38%** a year, one of the smallest mints in the uncapped layer-1 class — and burns almost nothing, at **14,605 XDC**. The reason the XDC framework reading is **−4.37%** rather than mildly positive is that node operators moved **890.13M XDC** into masternode collateral over the same 90 days, taking **4.46%** of circulating supply off the tradable float. The key risk is that this is absorption rather than destruction: collateral is withdrawable after roughly **30** days, and a reversal would flip the net reading positive in one quarter, which is also why the framework and the inflation monitor disagree by **4.41** percentage points. XDC has no maximum supply, so there is no ceiling underneath this reading — only the annual **Feb 5** cliff and the **18,281M** reserve still waiting behind it.
