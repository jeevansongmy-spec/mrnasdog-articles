XDC Network is an uncapped chain that barely inflates, and over the last quarter it removed forty times more XDC from the tradable float than it created. The XDPoS masternode reward minted about **18.74M XDC** in 90 days, while **760.1M XDC** was locked into validator collateral and **0.010M** was burned by fees, so the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **-3.72% net** for the trailing window and **-3.72%** forward. Our supply monitor reads **-0.05%** — a gap of **3.67 percentage points** — because it counts masternode-staked XDC as circulating. The constraint to keep in mind is that XDC collateral is a lock, not a burn: it is withdrawable if operators resign, and XDC Network has **no supply cap** above any of it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads XDC Network at **-3.72% net**: about **18.74M XDC** of new supply against **760.11M XDC** absorbed, on a circulating base of **19.95B XDC**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **-0.05%**, a gap of **3.67 percentage points**, which is far outside the framework's half-point tolerance and so this build ships a **monitor-gap flag**. The reconciliation walk closed the gap cleanly rather than leaving it unexplained: the XDC collateral contract at address 0x0000…0088 held **2,600.9M XDC** on **May 18 2026** and **3,361.0M XDC** on **Aug 16 2026**, but staked XDC is still classified as circulating upstream, so a lock of that size leaves no trace in the monitor's number. Projected forward the framework reads **-3.72%** again on the same mechanics. XDC Network is **deflationary by collateral absorption, not by burn** — the cleanest possible label for the reading, and the most reversible one.

## Sell pressure: where new XDC comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the only live source of new XDC, and it is small. The XDC Network protocol mints **5.5 XDC** on every block as the masternode reward — newly created supply, not a payout from a reserve — and the chain produced **3,406,827** blocks over the measured window. That is **18.74M XDC** for the trailing 90 days and the same figure projected forward, roughly **0.38%** a year. The block interval is the whole calculation, so this build measured it rather than trusting the parameter: XDC Network advertises a **2s** block time, but across the 90-day window the chain actually ran at **2.2825s** a block, about **14%** slower. Taking the nominal figure would have over-booked XDC issuance by that same margin. A long-run check confirms both numbers — the project's own dated total supply of **37,694,082,084 XDC** in September 2021 against **38,065,686,425 XDC** today works out to **5.23 XDC** per block realised over 71 million blocks, within five percent of the 5.5 gross reward.

The other three sell rows are **0**, and each for a concrete reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is empty because XDC Network uses a single annual cliff each February rather than a monthly drip; the last release fell on **Feb 5 2026** and the next lands on **Feb 5 2027**, outside both the trailing and the forward window. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is empty because the three reserve wallets that hold essentially all of XDC's non-circulating supply finished the 90 days on exactly the balances they started on — no release, not a partial one. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is empty because no XDC bankruptcy estate or trustee distribution schedule exists.

## Buy pressure: where new XDC goes

Buy #4, new long-term lock, carries the entire reading at **760.1M XDC**. The XDC masternode collateral contract grew from **2,600.9M XDC** to **3,361.0M XDC** across the window, read directly on-chain at both ends. The deposits arrive in **10,000,000 XDC** units — the masternode collateral minimum — from many separate depositor addresses, which is the signature of validator onboarding rather than one holder shuffling funds. Independent confirmation comes from XDC Network's own reporting: the validator set passed **320** operators on **Jul 31 2026**, up **26.6%** on the quarter, while the collateral contract grew **29.2%** over the same 90 days. The decisive question is whether that XDC came out of the market or out of the project's own reserves, and the chain answers it: the three reserve wallets moved by zero, and a traced deposit was funded straight from an exchange hot wallet whose balance fell **305.9M XDC** across the window. This is float leaving the market.

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is real and irrelevant. XDC Network activated an EIP-1559 base-fee burn on mainnet on **Jan 29 2026** in the Cancun v2.6.8 upgrade, on top of an older rule that burns 20% of smart-contract transaction fees. Sampling blocks evenly across the window gives a base fee pinned at **12.5** gwei and a mean gas usage of **224,949**, which is about **9,580 XDC** destroyed over the whole 90 days — **0.010M**, or one XDC in every 1,950 minted. Buy #1, a programmatic buyback, is **0** because XDC Network runs no buyback and none has been announced. Buy #3, a foundation buy, is **0** because no open-market purchase has been disclosed.

## Foundation and overhang

XDC Network's overhang is large and unusually easy to watch, because it sits in a handful of readable wallets. The genesis reserve at 0xd778…e03a holds **13,446.0M XDC**, a second reserve at 0xa488…315b holds **3,609.2M XDC**, and a third at 0x0c98…7d1c holds **1,225.8M XDC**. Together those three come to roughly **18,281M XDC**, which is essentially the entire gap between XDC's **38.07B** total supply and its **19.95B** circulating supply — in other words, these wallets are the non-circulating bucket. All three are read from the chain on every rebuild, and all three were unchanged across this window.

The fourth overhang is the one this build added: the masternode collateral pool itself, now **3,361.0M XDC**. Collateral is locked while a validator is active but withdrawable once an operator resigns, so the 760.1M XDC counted as buy pressure this quarter is a lock rather than a destruction, and the framework treats it that way. If any of those four balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and for the collateral pool specifically, a falling balance would flip this reading from deeply deflationary to mildly inflationary in a single quarter.

## How XDC compares to other proof-of-stake layer-1 chains

Against the usual uncapped proof-of-stake comparison set, XDC Network is a structural outlier on the issuance side. Most delegated or bonded proof-of-stake chains size their staking reward as a percentage of supply, which means issuance scales with the market cap and typically lands somewhere between 4% and 10% a year. XDC Network instead pays a fixed **5.5 XDC** per block regardless of how large the supply grows, so its inflation rate mechanically decays over time; at today's **19.95B** circulating that fixed reward works out to about **0.38%** a year, an order of magnitude below a percentage-of-supply chain. That is closer to a late-stage halving chain than to a typical staking layer-1, without the hard cap those chains carry.

The comparison flips on the demand side. Chains that lean on a fee burn to offset issuance — the EIP-1559 model, or an exchange token running a quarterly buyback out of revenue — need real fee volume for the offset to matter, and XDC Network deliberately does not have it. Its fees are sub-cent by design, aimed at enterprise trade-finance settlement, so both of its burn mechanisms combined remove roughly one XDC in every 1,950 minted. XDC Network's counterweight is therefore not a burn at all but collateral: a tiered validator structure of core, protector and observer nodes, each requiring a large XDC deposit, which absorbs float as the network onboards operators. That distinction matters for durability. A burn is permanent and a buyback held in treasury is semi-permanent, but validator collateral is a claim its owner can withdraw. On the mechanism, XDC Network is one of the few uncapped layer-1 chains whose supply-side pressure is currently negative — and one of the few whose negative reading depends on a flow that could reverse rather than on tokens that no longer exist.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the collateral contract balance itself, which is the single number this reading rests on: it grew 760.1M XDC last quarter, but the pace in the final eleven days of the window implies closer to 409M if extrapolated, so a slowdown is already visible and would soften the forward figure. Second, any validator resignation wave — withdrawal is the mechanism that would turn this row from buy pressure into sell pressure. Third, the annual vesting cliff on **Feb 5 2027**, which sits just outside the forward window but is the largest single dated supply event on XDC Network's calendar. Fourth, XDCDAO, which holds the authority to change the burn percentage and the masternode reward; a governance move on either would reset both sides of this ledger. Fifth, the proposed reward restructuring that would split standby nodes into protector and observer tiers with automated on-chain payouts — announced but not live, and it would change Sell #1 directly if it ships.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads XDC Network at **-3.72%** for the last 90 days and **-3.72%** for the next, against a monitor reading of **-0.05%** and a **3.67 percentage point** gap that resolves to a classification difference rather than a disagreement about the chain. Structurally, XDC is an uncapped delegated proof-of-stake layer-1 whose fixed **5.5 XDC** per-block masternode reward produces only **18.74M XDC** a quarter, while validator onboarding pulled **760.1M XDC** out of the tradable float into collateral over the same period. The key risk is that this absorption is a lock and not a burn: masternode collateral is withdrawable on resignation, so a reversal in validator growth would flip the reading rather than merely slow it. And there is no ceiling above any of this — XDC Network has no maximum supply, so the mint continues indefinitely even while the float shrinks.
