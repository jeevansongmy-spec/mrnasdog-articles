# ZRO Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

LayerZero creates no new ZRO at all — the token lives on seven networks at once and a single simultaneous reading of all seven summed to exactly **1,000,000,000 ZRO**, the hard cap, with nothing held in escrow anywhere — and yet the Pressure Framework reads ZRO at **+21.69%** over the trailing 90 days and **+21.69%** over the next 90. Almost all of that comes from one mechanism: a dated release calendar that hands **25.7M ZRO** to early backers, staff and a bankruptcy-settlement pot on the 20th of every month until **Jun 20 2027**. Sell pressure is **77.1M ZRO**, buy pressure is **0.49M ZRO** of open-market buying, and the ceiling is a hard **1,000M ZRO** that no vote can raise.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **353.3M ZRO**, the LayerZero ledger books **77.1M ZRO** of sell pressure and **0.49M ZRO** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+21.69%** — and projects **+21.69%** for the next 90 days on the same unbroken calendar. The inflation monitor reads **+39.37%** for the same window, a gap of **17.68 percentage points**, far over the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so the ZRO overview ships with a monitor-gap warning. That gap decomposes cleanly and completely: **8.67pp** is base convention, because the monitor divides its supply change by the 90-day-old supply while the framework divides by today's, and the remaining **9.01pp** is a single aggregator step — the counted float jumped from **252.3M** to **353.3M ZRO** on **Jul 8 2026** in one day, a restatement covering releases accrued before this window rather than a flow inside it. The label for ZRO is a **hard-capped token being diluted by a calendar**: LayerZero cannot inflate the supply, but it can, and does, keep enlarging the part of it that trades.

## Sell pressure: where new ZRO comes from

It does not come from minting, and on an omnichain token that claim has to be earned rather than asserted. ZRO is a burn-and-mint omnichain token deployed at the same address on Ethereum, Base, Polygon, BNB Chain, Arbitrum, Optimism and Avalanche, so the first question is whether those seven supplies are mirrored views of one balance or independent slices. They are independent slices, and the proof is arithmetic: read simultaneously, the seven legs sum to **1,000,000,000.000000 ZRO** with a residual of zero. A mirrored deployment would put the sum above the cap with a lockbox holding the difference; there is no lockbox, and no eighth network can be carrying a balance because the identity leaves no room for one. LayerZero pays no block reward and no staking reward — the Zero network that would pay one is announced but not live — so Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, watched rather than closed permanently.

The entire supply story is Sell #2, vesting unlocks, at **77.1M ZRO**. LayerZero's token generation event was Jun 20 2024; strategic partners and core contributors were fully locked for one year and then put on 24 equal monthly steps running from **Jun 20 2025** to **Jun 20 2027**. The arithmetic is exact and checkable: strategic partners hold 32.20% of supply, or 322M ZRO, releasing **13.42M** a step; core contributors hold 25.50%, or 255M ZRO, releasing **10.63M** a step; and a 40M repurchased pot — the tranche recovered by the FTX estate in a settlement and folded back onto the same calendar — releases **1.67M** a step. That is **25.7M ZRO** a month, which is what the unlock trackers publish for Jul 20 2026 and again for Sep 20 2026. Three of those dated steps fall inside the trailing window — **Jun 20**, **Jul 20** and **Aug 20 2026** — and three fall inside the next one: **Sep 20**, **Oct 20** and **Nov 20 2026**. The two columns of this page are identical for that reason and no other: the same dated calendar spans both windows. It is worth being precise about what a LayerZero unlock is. No ZRO is created. The coins already existed and were already inside the 1,000M ceiling; what changes is that they stop being restricted and become sellable — which is exactly what the Pressure Framework measures, and exactly what a hard cap does not protect a holder from.

One honest caveat belongs on this row. The framework prefers a realised on-chain reading over a published calendar whenever the allocation sits in a readable lock contract — and ZRO's does not. The fifteen largest ZRO-holding contracts on Ethereum were resolved this session and every one is an ordinary custody multisig, a bridge swapper or the buyback accumulator; none has vesting logic, so a LayerZero unlock date lifts a contractual restriction rather than moving a coin. Measured as realised flow instead, the 32 contract holders inside the hundred largest fell from **457.2M** to **445.8M ZRO**, a decline of **11.4M**, and 71 of the top 100 — **668.0M ZRO** between them — did not move at all. Shipping the calendar therefore states sell pressure roughly **6.8×** higher than what visibly left identified custody. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**: nothing left the LayerZero multisigs outside the published calendar. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** too — the bankruptcy tranche exists, but it rides the same monthly calendar and is already counted in Sell #2.

## Buy pressure: where new ZRO goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0.49M ZRO** and it is genuinely running. Revenue from LayerZero's bridge is spent buying ZRO on the open market once a month: **141,557 ZRO** in June 2026, **160,271** in July and **183,904** in August. That is 485,732 ZRO, and the accumulation contract those purchases land in rose from **1.89M** to **2.38M ZRO** across the window — a measured delta of **485,732.11**, agreeing with the published figures to a tenth of a coin. The destination matters: the coins are **held, not burned**, and that contract has never sent anything out, so the bought ZRO is tracked as an overhang on the sell side rather than treated as destroyed.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and this is the row most worth explaining, because a great many write-ups state that LayerZero's fee switch was turned on in December 2025 and that message fees are being burned. They are not. An immutable contract puts the fee switch to holders every six months, and LayerZero's own referendum page lists four completed votes — December 2024, June 2025, December 2025 and the one that ended **Jun 27 2026** — with the same result each time: **Off**. Both burn surfaces were read at both ends of the window and both are flat. The dead address on Ethereum held **0.3129 ZRO** on either date, unchanged to four decimals. The seven-network count of ZRO in existence is exactly **1,000,000,000** — meaning not one coin has been destroyed since generation, ever. Those two surfaces are independent: a transfer to a dead address does not reduce this token's supply counter, and an omnichain burn reduces a leg's counter without touching a dead address. Both read zero, so this is a verified zero rather than an opacity. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** — there is no discretionary purchase beyond the monthly programme already counted, and counting it twice would double the only real demand on the page. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: ZRO staking does not exist yet, because the network it would secure has not launched.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on ZRO is very large, and most of it is on a published calendar rather than in anyone's discretion. The dominant item is the remaining release schedule itself: **231.4M ZRO** still waiting behind nine more monthly steps to Jun 2027. The second is a LayerZero Foundation multisig holding **106.1M ZRO**, and the third an unlabelled custody multisig holding **69.5M ZRO** — both unchanged to the wei at both ends of the window, and both refreshed from the chain at every rebuild. The fourth is the buyback accumulation contract at **2.4M ZRO**, which only ever fills. Beyond those, the classified float of **353.3M ZRO** leaves an excluded bucket of **646.7M ZRO** against the 1,000M ceiling, and the difference between that bucket and the release calendar is the undistributed community allocation, which has no schedule of its own. The trigger sentence applies to every item: if the Foundation multisig, the custody multisig or the buyback contract sees its balance fall between refreshes by more than the calendar accounts for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ZRO compares to other hard-capped tokens

ZRO belongs to a small class: tokens with a hard, on-chain ceiling that also issue nothing at all. That is a stricter commitment than a halving schedule — a halving-model chain like Bitcoin still mints on every block, just at a decaying rate, while LayerZero's seven-network sum sits exactly on the cap. On the pure issuance axis ZRO is stricter than any proof-of-work chain and stricter than every uncapped continuous-emission L1. And yet ZRO reads **+21.69%** while a mid-cycle Bitcoin reads a fraction of a percent. That is the whole lesson: a hard cap constrains total supply, not tradable float, and the two move independently. ZRO is far closer in shape to a recently-launched token working through a four-year investor and team vest — at the harsh end of it, because only about a third of the supply trades while the calendar keeps feeding the rest in, and because the release is cliff-shaped rather than a continuous drip.

The other structural comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns. Those offset issuance with a demand-linked removal that scales with usage, and their inflation readings can go genuinely negative. LayerZero has both halves of that mechanism on paper — a revenue-funded buyback and a fee burn — but one parks what it buys and the other has never been switched on. For the buy side to matter here, LayerZero would need to remove on the order of **77M ZRO** a quarter, roughly **158×** what the buyback actually took off the market in these 90 days. That is the distance between a mechanism existing and a mechanism mattering.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the calendar itself, which is the only thing driving the number: **25.7M ZRO** comes off lock on **Sep 20 2026**, again on **Oct 20 2026** and again on **Nov 20 2026**, and the schedule does not end until **Jun 20 2027** — so absent a governance change the next reading lands near **+21.69%** again. Second, the fee switch: the next referendum falls in **December 2026**, just outside this window, and an On result would be the single event most capable of flipping this page's sign. Third, the ATLAS trading engine announced on **Aug 25 2026**, which would route three quarters of its residual fees into buying and burning ZRO and give trading venues a reason to stake it — it is not live, and the day it is, both Buy #2 and Buy #4 stop being structural zeros. Fourth, the Zero network itself, scheduled for autumn 2026, which introduces a delegated-proof-of-stake reward path and is the only thing that could move Sell #1 off zero. Fifth, the two custody multisigs at **106.1M** and **69.5M ZRO**, both motionless so far and both read at every rebuild.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ZRO at **+21.69%** over the trailing 90 days and **+21.69%** projected forward: supply growing, projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is not inflation but release — LayerZero mints nothing, has burned nothing since generation, and holds a hard seven-network ceiling of **1,000M ZRO**, while a dated calendar frees **25.7M ZRO** a month out of **231.4M** still restricted. The key risk is that this is mechanical and cannot be talked down: it fires on the 20th regardless of price, and the buyback that could offset it took **0.49M ZRO** off the market in 90 days against **77.1M** coming on. The ceiling is the one genuine comfort — ZRO's supply cannot be raised by any vote, only its tradable share can, and that share stops growing on **Jun 20 2027**.
