# AERO Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing


AERO supply grew about **2.79%** in the 90 days to Nov 4 2026, and the AERO ledger points to about **8.50%** in the next 90 days. Reward emissions minted **64.05M AERO** and Velodrome holders claimed **84.72M AERO** from the Aero merger, while holders staked a net **127.89M AERO** and the Momentum Fund burned **407,815 AERO**. AERO has no maximum supply, but since the Aero launch on Oct 21 2026 new rewards are capped at **20% a year** and are only minted when they are claimed.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

On the AERO ledger, circulating AERO grew by a net **20.47M AERO** in the 90 days from Aug 6 to Nov 4 2026, or **+2.79%** of the **734.27M AERO** counted as circulating. Other trackers show **−18.29%** for the same 90 days, a gap of **21.08 points**, so the AERO coin page carries a warning note. The gap is not new AERO and not burned AERO: the supply figure those trackers use still counts only the old Aerodrome contract, and since Oct 21 2026 holders have been swapping their old AERO into the new Aero contract, one for one. About **183M** liquid old AERO left the old contract that way by Nov 3 2026 and came back as new AERO the old figure does not see. Looking ahead, about **91.23M AERO** of new supply meets about **28.84M AERO** of staking and burns, for **8.50%**. AERO is a reward-funded exchange token in the middle of a merger: inflationary on the liquid float, with staking soaking up most of it so far but much less from here.

## Sell pressure: where new AERO comes from

Protocol inflation is the main source of new AERO. Until Oct 21 2026 the old Aerodrome contract minted about **4.77M AERO** a week for liquidity providers and for lockers' weekly top-ups, and two smaller weekly mints followed after the switch, for **57.12M AERO** from that contract in the window. The new Aero contract pays rewards by the second and mints AERO only when a liquidity provider claims, plus **5%** on top for the Aero Foundation; that added **6.92M AERO** in the first two weeks. Together that is **64.05M AERO** in 90 days. At the pace of the last seven days, the new contract mints about **57.07M AERO** per 90 days, and the old contract's small weekly tail adds about **1.88M AERO** more, so Aerodrome rewards should add about **58.95M AERO** in the next 90 days. The hard ceiling is higher, about **104.9M AERO** per 90 days, and the gap between the two is reward value the AERO pools did not earn, which is never minted.

Vesting unlocks add **0**: Aerodrome launched in 2023 with no investor round and no team tranche, and the 500M AERO made at launch went to the community, most of it locked as votes. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks also book **0**, because no Foundation wallet released AERO outside its programs in the window. There is no AERO bankruptcy estate, so that row is **0** as well.

The Velodrome merger share is the second source of new AERO. When Aerodrome and Velodrome merged into Aero on Oct 21 2026, Velodrome holders received **5.5%** of the new AERO, **117M AERO** at about 0.044 AERO per VELO, while old AERO swaps one for one. By Nov 4 2026 Velodrome holders had claimed **84.72M AERO**, some of it straight into stakes. The other **32.28M AERO** is booked in full for the next 90 days, because exchanges are swapping their customers' VELO in early November.

## Buy pressure: where new AERO goes

The programmatic buyback is the Momentum Fund, the Aero Foundation's program that buys AERO on the market and burns it, timed to market conditions. Its first two rounds, reported on Oct 29 and Nov 2 2026, destroyed **407,815 AERO** for about $268K. At today's price the same dollars buy about **427,705 AERO**, which is what the AERO ledger books for the next 90 days.

The protocol fee burn is **0**: Aero burns no trading fees, and all exchange revenue goes to AERO stakers in the tokens traders use. The Foundation buy row is **0** because the old Public Goods Fund locked the AERO it bought, so those purchases already sit inside the staking row.

New long-term locks are the largest buyer of AERO. Staked AERO, in old veAERO locks or in the new sAERO stakes, rose by a net **127.89M AERO** in 90 days. Before the merger that came from weekly top-ups paid straight into locks, from new locks and from the Public Goods Fund; after it, mostly from Velodrome holders whose locked VELO became sAERO. Old veAERO that moved into sAERO stays staked and counts once. Staking will not keep that pace: the weekly top-ups ended with the merger, and in the last seven days new stakes took only about **1.77M AERO**. The AERO ledger books about **28.42M AERO** of new staking for the next 90 days, including the part of the last Velodrome swaps that goes straight into stakes.

## Foundation and overhang

The AERO team-controlled overhang is mostly liquid AERO that already counts as circulating, so spending it adds no new supply. On Nov 4 2026 the Aero Foundation's incentives wallet held **28.94M AERO**, set aside to pay pools after the launch; the old Aerodrome team wallet held **4.06M AERO**; the Flight School grants wallet **2.03M AERO**; the Momentum Fund's main wallet **1.68M AERO**; the payout contract for the Foundation's 5% reward share **0.33M AERO**; and the Velodrome Foundation **0.28M AERO**. About **4.04M AERO** minted by the old Aerodrome contract after the switch sits in its old reward contracts. The two Aero swap contracts hold **792.59M AERO** for old AERO not yet swapped, which only replaces AERO already counted, and **32.28M AERO** for Velodrome holders, booked above; either balance can be burned once swaps close, with no date set. The Foundation's programs also hold large sAERO stakes, which stay locked until they end. We re-read these AERO balances at every rebuild, and if a locked or reserved balance falls between rebuilds, the outflow enters the AERO ledger as new supply at the next rebuild.

## How AERO compares to other exchange tokens

AERO is paid out the way a mining reward is: liquidity providers earn new AERO for every hour their money sits in the right pools, and the coin has no maximum supply. That puts AERO closer to reward-funded exchange tokens than to exchange tokens that buy back and burn on a schedule from fees, such as BNB with its quarterly burn. Where a fee-burning exchange token shrinks as volume grows, AERO's supply grows with rewards and leans on staking to keep coins off the market.

The Aero launch changed the shape of that growth. Old Aerodrome emissions followed a fixed weekly curve and paid weekly top-ups into locks; new Aero rewards are capped per pool at a multiple of what each pool earns, minted only on claim, with a hard ceiling of **20% a year**. Uniswap's UNI, by contrast, pays no rewards at all and has no fee-funded lock, so UNI supply stays nearly flat while AERO supply keeps rising.

The Momentum Fund makes AERO a hybrid. Most ve-style exchange tokens send fees to lockers and never burn; AERO now does both, though the burn is tiny next to rewards: the first two Momentum Fund rounds destroyed **407,815 AERO**, while rewards minted about **6.92M AERO** on the new contract in the same two weeks.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Exchange swaps: Coinbase converted old AERO and VELO from Nov 2 to Nov 4 2026 and Kraken runs to Nov 6 2026; once they finish, the rest of the **32.28M AERO** Velodrome share is either claimed or left waiting.

Reward pace: if Aero trading fees rise, the AERO reward cap per pool rises with them; a pace above **650,000 AERO** a day would push new supply past the AERO ledger's forecast.

Burns of unswapped AERO: both swap contracts can burn what is left once swapping closes; a burn of the Velodrome remainder would cut next quarter's AERO supply, and no date has been set.

Momentum Fund rounds: the fund buys more when AERO trades low against its history, so a weak market could bring larger AERO burns than the two rounds so far.

The tracked supply figure: once market trackers follow the new AERO contract, the −18.29% reading should give way to one close to the AERO ledger.

## Summary

AERO supply grew **2.79%** in the 90 days to Nov 4 2026, as **64.05M AERO** of rewards and **84.72M AERO** of Velodrome merger share met **127.89M AERO** of net new staking and a **407,815 AERO** Momentum Fund burn. For the next 90 days the AERO ledger points to about **8.50%**, driven by rewards near 59M AERO and the last 32.28M AERO of the Velodrome share. The key risk is that staking slows while rewards keep coming, because AERO has no maximum supply and only a 20% a year ceiling on new rewards. Other trackers show −18.29% only because they still count the old Aerodrome contract while holders swap to the new one.
