AERO is **structurally inflationary by design, with a voter-controlled emission dial and a single voluntary brake**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 13 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **AERO at +4.04% net**: sell pressure of **60.8M AERO** from the Aerodrome Minter against buy pressure of **21.3M AERO** of net new veAERO locking, on a circulating base of **977.2M AERO**. Our supply monitor reads **+4.12%** for the same window, a gap of **0.08 percentage points** — comfortably inside the half-point tolerance, so no flag is raised on this build. The two agree because the on-chain ledger closes exactly: the AERO contract at **0x940181a94A35A4569E4529A3CDfB74e38FD98631** went from **1,898.6M** to **1,959.4M** total supply, the veAERO escrow at **0xeBf418Fe2512e7E6bd9b87a8F0f294aCDC67e6B4** took in **21.3M**, and total supply minus the escrow's AERO balance lands within **410 AERO** of the classified circulating figure. Aerodrome is best labelled **an uncapped continuous emitter whose inflation is a governance parameter, not a fixed schedule**.

## Sell pressure: where new AERO comes from

Almost all of it comes from one place. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **60.8M AERO** over 90 days, and it is the whole sell side of the AERO ledger. The Aerodrome Minter at **0xeB018363F0a9Af8f91F06FEe6613a751b2A33FE5** mints once per epoch — every Thursday at 00:00 UTC — and the window covered **13** of them, epoch **142** through epoch **155**. What matters for the forward read is that Aerodrome's original emission schedule is finished. The launch design ramped emissions up, then decayed them **1%** per epoch; that decay has now run its course and the Minter has crossed into its tail phase, where the weekly emission is no longer a decaying number but a straight percentage of total supply. The tail rate reads **21** basis points per epoch and it was **21** at every one of the fifteen consecutive epochs sampled around this window — so no emission change landed inside the window, and the trailing rate is a fair predictor. On top of the base emission the Minter adds a rebase paid to veAERO lockers and a **228** basis-point team share, which is why the observed mint at epoch 155 was **4.75M** rather than the **4.10M** base emission alone. That arithmetic reproduces the observed mint to within **0.5%**.

The other sell rows are zero, and each for a concrete reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because AERO has no vesting contract at all: the token's mint function is restricted to the Minter, so the weekly emission is the only path by which an AERO can come into existence, and the launch airdrop was distributed as locked veAERO positions rather than as a dated unlock calendar. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** — there is no public evidence of release in window. The identified team wallet at **0xbde0c70bdc242577c52dfad53389f82fd149ea5a** actually shrank across the window, from **2.23M** to **1.67M AERO**, and in any case its coins were already counted as new supply at the instant the Minter created them, so moving them adds nothing. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: Aerodrome has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution. One extra row is carried at **zero** and watched — the merger conversion allocation, covered below.

## Buy pressure: where new AERO goes

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the entire buy side at **21.3M AERO**, and it is worth being precise about how it is measured. The veAERO escrow contract exposes an internal supply accumulator that reads **1,040.1M**, and using it would be a mistake — the framework instead reads the escrow's actual AERO balance, which moved from **960.9M** to **982.2M** across the window. The check that settles which number is right is simple: total supply minus the escrow's AERO balance gives **977,211,572**, which matches the classified circulating figure of **977,211,982** to **410 AERO**, while the accumulator would give **919.2M** and reconcile with nothing. The **21.3M** is also a net figure — every lock that expired and left the escrow in the same window is already subtracted from it. The important context for the next quarter is direction: over the trailing twelve months the escrow absorbed **148.2M AERO**, an average of roughly **37.0M** a quarter, so the last 90 days ran well below Aerodrome's own established pace. The brake is loosening, not tightening.

The remaining buy rows are all **zero**. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** because AERO has no burn mechanism whatsoever — the burn address held exactly the same balance at both ends of the window, and Aerodrome's fee design routes **100%** of trading fees to veAERO voters in the traded assets rather than using them to destroy AERO. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** for a subtler reason worth stating plainly: where the Aerodrome Foundation does buy AERO, the reported practice is to max-lock the purchased coins, which deposits them into the same veAERO escrow that Buy #4 already measures. Booking those coins again as a buyback would double-count them, and no buyback wallet address or dated quantum could be verified from a primary source in this build, so no separate figure is claimed. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** on the same evidence test. The honest reading is that Aerodrome's buy side is one mechanism wearing two hats, and the escrow balance captures both.

## Foundation and overhang

Aerodrome's overhang is small in wallet terms and large in one specific pending decision. The identified team wallet at **0xbde0c70bdc242577c52dfad53389f82fd149ea5a** holds **1.67M AERO**, down from **2.23M** at the start of the window, and it is refreshed from the chain at every rebuild. The rewards distributor at **0x227f65131A261548b057215bB1D5Ab2997964C7d** holds **1.81M AERO** of unclaimed locker rebase, up from **1.48M**; those coins were minted already and sit inside the circulating classification, so when a locker claims them the effect is a transfer into the escrow, not a new sale. Neither pool is large against a **977.2M** float.

The overhang that actually matters is the merger. Dromos Labs announced in **Nov 2025** that Aerodrome on Base and its sister exchange Velodrome on Optimism would fold into a single unified protocol and a single AERO token, preserving the two protocols' revenue split at **94.5%** to AERO holders and **5.5%** to the sister side. At that ratio the conversion implies roughly **114M** new AERO, about **5.8%** of total supply. The launch was targeted first at Q2 **2026** and then at **Jul 2026**, and as of **Aug 13 2026** it has not executed: the sister token still trades independently with its own circulating supply and live price, and a fifteen-epoch walk of AERO's on-chain supply shows only smooth weekly mints of **4.57M** rising to **4.75M** with no step anywhere. Because no execution date has ever been published, the framework books that allocation at **zero** and carries it as a watched row. The rule is the same for all three pools: if the team wallet's or the rewards distributor's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and if the conversion mint lands, it enters Sell #5 at the next refresh at whatever size the chain shows.

## How AERO compares to other uncapped emission DeFi tokens

AERO belongs to the vote-escrow exchange class — uncapped tokens that pay continuous emissions to liquidity providers and let holders lock the token in exchange for the protocol's fee revenue. Against a hard-capped, halving-schedule asset the contrast is total: a halving chain's issuance is written into code and cannot be voted upward, whereas Aerodrome's tail rate is a governance parameter that veAERO voters can move between **1** and **100** basis points per epoch. At the current **21** basis points AERO issues roughly **11%** of total supply a year; the same dial at its ceiling would issue several times that. That is the structural risk in one sentence — AERO's inflation rate is not a fact about the protocol, it is a standing decision.

Against other DeFi tokens the comparison turns on what sits on the buy side. A capped exchange token that routes revenue into a buy-and-burn shrinks its own float while its unlocks run, so the two effects partly cancel; AERO has no burn address activity and no buyback contract at all, so nothing on the ledger subtracts supply permanently. What Aerodrome has instead is the deepest voluntary lock in its class: roughly half of all AERO ever minted currently sits inside the veAERO escrow, and lockers rather than the treasury receive every trading fee. That is a genuine and unusual brake — but it is a behavioural one. A burn removes coins whether or not holders cooperate; a vote-escrow lock only removes them for as long as holders keep choosing to relock. The **21.3M** absorbed this window against a **37.0M** quarterly average over the trailing year is exactly what that fragility looks like in practice, and it is why AERO reads more inflationary now than its own annual trend would suggest.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the tail emission rate. It has held at **21** basis points across every epoch sampled, and a single governance vote to raise or lower it moves the entire sell side of this page — it is the highest-leverage number in Aerodrome's economics. Second, the net locking pace: the escrow absorbed **21.3M** this window against a trailing-year average near **37.0M** a quarter, and a return to that pace would cut the net reading roughly in half while a further slowdown would push it above **+5%**. Third, the merger conversion — the roughly **114M** allocation implied by the announced **94.5** to **5.5** split has no execution date, and the first on-chain step in total supply would be a supply event of real size. Fourth, the Predictive Allocation upgrade announced **Jul 26 2026** and slated to roll out in **Sep 2026**: it changes how emissions are directed across pools rather than how many are minted, so it should not move this ledger — but any gauge-cap mechanism that skips minting unneeded emissions would, and that is worth reading from the chain rather than from the announcement. Fifth, the four-year locks created at Aerodrome's launch in **Aug 2023**, whose earliest expiries begin arriving from **Aug 2027** and would show up as escrow outflow.

## Summary

AERO is an uncapped, continuously emitting exchange token whose inflation is set by governance rather than by schedule. Over the 90 days to **Aug 13 2026**, Aerodrome minted **60.8M AERO** across **13** weekly emissions at a tail rate of **21** basis points of total supply, against **21.3M** of net new veAERO locking and zero burn and zero buyback, for a net of **+4.04%** on a **977.2M** circulating base — projected to **+4.25%** as the tail emission compounds on a rising supply. Our supply monitor reads **+4.12%**, a **0.08-point** gap that raises no flag because the on-chain ledger closes to within **410 AERO**. The key risk is that AERO's only offsetting mechanism is voluntary: locking absorbed a third of this quarter's mint, well below its own trailing-year pace, and there is no cap, no burn and no buyback beneath it — with a roughly **114M** merger conversion still undated on top.
