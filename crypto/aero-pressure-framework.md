# AERO Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Aerodrome Finance is usually described as a token whose weekly emission decays **1%** an epoch. That phase is finished, and the contract has quietly inverted: the Aerodrome Minter now sits permanently in its tail branch, where each weekly mint is a flat **21 basis points** of total supply — applied to a total the previous mint just enlarged, so every batch is **bigger** than the one before it. Thirteen mints landed in the trailing 90 days and created **61,386,481 AERO**. Nothing was burned, because the AERO token has no burn function at all. Against that, vote-escrow locking absorbed **19,917,431 AERO**, of which **4,583,664** was a traced open-market buyback. The Pressure Framework reads AERO at **+4.19%** over the trailing 90 days and **+4.32%** over the next 90, and there is no supply ceiling anywhere in the design.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **988.9M AERO**, the framework books **61.386M AERO** of sell pressure and **19.918M AERO** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+4.19%** — and projects **+4.32%** for the next 90 days on the same unbroken mechanism. The inflation monitor reads **+3.76%** for the same window, a gap of **0.44 percentage points**, which is inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so this page ships with no monitor-gap warning. The framework's own side of that comparison is not an estimate. Thirteen mint events summed to **61,386,481.210** AERO against a totalSupply change read independently from Base state at both ends of **61,386,481.210** — a residual of **0.000**. A complete sweep of both sides of the vote-escrow contract, **80,362** deposits and **2,346** withdrawals across **3,888,000** blocks with zero range failures, closed against the contract's own balance change to **zero wei**. The forward column sits **above** the trailing one, and the reason is entirely on the sell side: a mint priced as a percentage of supply compounds, while a buyback and a locking rate measured in absolute tokens do not. The label for Aerodrome Finance is **an uncapped ve(3,3) emission whose weekly batch grows with the supply it creates**.

## Sell pressure: where new AERO comes from

All of it is minted, and all of it comes from one weekly call. Every Thursday at 00:00 UTC the Aerodrome Minter runs one epoch, and the framework measured those epochs one at a time instead of trusting a published rate. The live Minter state, read on Base at both ends of the window and at every weekly boundary back to **Mar 26 2026**, holds **tailEmissionRate** at **21** and the legacy **weekly** constant frozen at **8,969,149.54**, just under the **8,969,150** line that switches the contract into its tail branch. Because the frozen constant can never rise again, the tail branch is now permanent, and the emission is **totalSupply × 21 ÷ 10,000** — a share, not a quantity. The measured consequence is visible epoch by epoch: **4,656,371 AERO** minted on **Jun 18 2026** and **4,765,212** on **Sep 10 2026**, thirteen mints in all, summing to **61,386,481 AERO**. Sell #1, protocol inflation, books the whole of it, because one call is one mechanism. Its three destinations were read off the Minter's own outflow log at each flip: about **87%** to the Voter and onward to liquidity gauges, about **10%** to the RewardsDistributor as the anti-dilution rebase paid to veAERO lockers, and about **3%** to a team wallet. The team leg is nearly flat from week to week — **219,839** at the start of the window and **220,427** at the end — because the contract sizes it against that frozen legacy constant rather than against the live emission.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0**, and the proof is stronger than an absent document. Aerodrome Finance distributed its launch allocation in **Aug 2023** as locked vote-escrow positions rather than as a calendar, and today the escrow's permanent-lock balance reads **985,399,644 AERO** against a vote-escrow balance of **989,536,288** — **99.58%** of the escrowed AERO is in permanent locks with no end date at all. The largest quantity that could expire on any schedule, ever, is **4,136,644 AERO**. One vesting tracker publishes a weekly "unlock" line for AERO with a next date of **Sep 17 2026**; that is a Thursday, which is to say an ordinary epoch flip already counted in Sell #1, and booking it would double the mint. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**: every identified project wallet was read at both window ends and none of them sold. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well — Aerodrome Finance has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new AERO goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **0**, and it cannot be anything else. The deployed AERO token is **4,737** bytes and carries a mint function and a setMinter function; the burn selector is **absent from the bytecode** entirely, so the count of AERO in existence has no mechanism by which it can ever fall. Both removal surfaces were read at both ends anyway, because neither is sufficient alone: the unspendable address moved from **2,000.50** to **2,000.77 AERO** across the whole quarter, a change of **0.27 AERO**, and totalSupply rose by an amount the thirteen mints explain to the token, leaving no room for a destruction the supply surface might have hidden. Aerodrome Finance does route **100%** of trading fees to veAERO lockers, but it pays them in the assets that were traded, so the fee engine buys and destroys no AERO. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** for the plainer reason that the only project entity buying AERO is already counted once, in Buy #1.

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **4,583,664 AERO**, and it was traced two hops back rather than read off a balance — which is what changed the number. The Aerodrome public goods fund runs a market-aware buyback and locks what it buys for the maximum term. Of the AERO that reached its wallet in the window, **3,079,784** arrived by way of a relay wallet that received exactly that amount and no more, **directly out of five AERO trading pools**: a volatile USDC/AERO pool and four concentrated pools against WETH, USDC and cbBTC. Coins that come straight out of an automated market maker are a swap, which is to say an open-market purchase. The other **1,503,879** came from the converter that swaps the protocol's non-AERO revenue into AERO on chain. The pace is steady and still running: **1,098,928** in June, **1,167,872** in July, **1,198,228** in August and **453,433** in the first ten days of September. The wallet then pushed **6,224,921 AERO** into maximum-term locks, more than it bought, drawing its own balance down from **2.52M** to **0.88M**.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **15,333,766 AERO**, and it is the row that decides this page. On Aerodrome Finance a lock genuinely removes tradable supply, and that is a measurement rather than an assumption: the count of AERO in existence, **1,978,450,301**, minus the AERO held by the vote-escrow contract, **989,536,288**, reconstructs the published circulating figure of **988,916,672** to within **2,659 AERO**, or **0.00027%**. The excluded bucket is the escrow balance and nothing else. So locking removes float, and the rebase paid to lockers is minted into the float and leaves it again on claim without ever reaching the market. The amount of AERO locked in the protocol rose **19,917,431** over the window, net of every expiry and early exit; subtracting the **4,583,664** already booked as a buyback leaves **15,333,766** here, with no double count. One further quantity was deliberately excluded: a separate **5,236,507 AERO** of already-locked principal migrated between two protocol contracts, out of the managed-relay holders and back into the escrow. A float-only reading counts that as absorption. Nothing was bought or sold, so this page does not.

## Foundation and overhang

The identified overhang on AERO is **32.77M AERO** across four wallets, all read on chain at both window ends and all refreshed from the chain at every rebuild. The largest by far is a static team multisig holding **29,294,313 AERO**, which fell only **254,618** across ninety days — roughly **3%** of the float sitting in one place with no release calendar. The team emissions wallet holds **2,592,543**; it received **2,861,181** of new mint and sent **3,220,000** straight into locks, so its decline is a lock rather than a sale. The buyback wallet holds **878,630** and exists to be drained into locks. The fourth reads **zero at both ends** and is not dormant — it relayed **3,079,784 AERO** through itself, which is why it was resolved by reading its flow rather than its balance.

A second, much larger overhang has no wallet yet. Aerodrome Finance announced a merger with its sister exchange on **Nov 13 2025**, under which AERO holders would take **94.5%** of a unified token and the sister token's holders **5.5%** — an issuance of roughly **115M AERO**, close to two quarters of emission in one step. It was guided to the second quarter of **2026**, then to July **2026**, and this session verified on chain that it has not executed: the window's entire supply change is accounted for by the thirteen epoch mints, and AERO still trades as the same Base contract. It is watched, not booked. Separately, **58,838,014 AERO** of economically locked principal sits in managed-relay contracts rather than in the escrow, which means the circulating figure this page divides by counts it as float. The trigger sentence applies to every item: if any of these balances falls between refreshes by more than the mechanism accounts for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How AERO compares to other uncapped emission tokens

AERO belongs to the ve(3,3) family, and inside that family it is at the far end of one axis: its emission is defined as a **percentage of its own supply**. That is a genuinely different object from a halving schedule or a decay curve. A halving-model chain like Bitcoin mints a fixed quantity per block and cuts that quantity on a known clock, so its absolute issuance only ever falls and its percentage issuance falls faster. A continuous-emission Cosmos chain mints a percentage too, but of a supply where staking is counted as circulating, so the mint has nowhere to hide. Aerodrome Finance mints **21 basis points** of total supply every week, and the tail branch it now sits in is one-way: the frozen constant that gates it can never climb back above **8,969,150**. So the absolute weekly batch compounds at **0.2427%** an epoch forever, unless veAERO voters vote it down.

The other structural comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns, whose inflation readings can go genuinely negative. Aerodrome Finance has the buyback half and not the burn half. Its public goods fund really does spend revenue buying AERO in the open market — **4,583,664 AERO** this quarter, traced to the pools it came out of — but the token contract has no burn function to call, so the bought AERO is locked rather than destroyed. Locking is reversible and destruction is not, which is the whole distance between a **+4.19%** reading and a negative one. For the buy side to match the mint, the fund would need to acquire and lock on the order of **61M AERO** a quarter, thirteen times what it managed in this one.

Where AERO does hold up well is in what it does with the coins it creates. Roughly a third of every mint is locked on arrival, and **99.58%** of the escrow is in permanent locks with no unlock date, so the vast majority of Aerodrome Finance's supply is held by people who chose to give up the ability to sell it. That is a stronger commitment structure than a four-year investor vest. It is also why the framework's reading and the raw emission rate differ: the mint is **1.24%** of total supply a quarter, but only about half the total is float, so it lands as **+4.19%** on the number that matters.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Aero Fed. veAERO voters can nudge **tailEmissionRate** by one basis point per epoch inside a **1**–**100** band, and it has held at **21** for every weekly boundary since **Mar 26 2026**; a sustained run of downward nudges is the only mechanism that can bend this page's number, and thirteen successful nudges would cut the emission by roughly **62%**. Second, the merger. A public audit contest on the upgrade codebase opened **Aug 31 2026** for three weeks, which is the last gate before it ships; execution would issue roughly **115M AERO** in one step and would land in Sell #3 immediately. Third, a burn mandate. The public goods fund has publicly discussed burning rather than locking what it buys, and a transfer-to-dead burn needs no contract change at all — the unspendable address is read at every rebuild, so any movement off **2,000.77 AERO** shows up at once. Fourth, the static team multisig at **29,294,313 AERO**, the one large balance on this chain with a holder rather than a schedule. Fifth, a pool-operator incentive change dated **Sep 21 2026**, which redirects where emissions go without changing how many are minted — worth confirming it stays that way, because the quantum is what this page measures.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads AERO at **+4.19%** over the trailing 90 days and **+4.32%** projected forward: supply growing, projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is a tail emission that is a share rather than a quantity — **21 basis points** of total supply every Thursday, applied to a total the previous mint enlarged, which is why thirteen mints ran from **4,656,371** up to **4,765,212 AERO** and why the forward column sits above the trailing one. The key risk is that nothing on the buy side scales with it: Aerodrome Finance destroyed nothing, because its token has no burn function, and the **19.9M AERO** that vote-locking absorbed is a near-constant absolute quantity against a mint that compounds. There is no ceiling to fall back on either — AERO is uncapped, its mint authority is live, and the one genuine comfort is that **99.58%** of the **989.5M** already locked has no unlock date at all.
