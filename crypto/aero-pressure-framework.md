Aerodrome Finance mints a fresh batch of AERO every Thursday and has no supply cap to stop it. Over the 90 days to **Jul 27 2026** the AERO token supply on Base rose from **1,884.86M** to **1,945.16M** — **60.3M** newly issued across thirteen weekly epochs — while vote-escrow locking pulled back only **12.07M**. Aerodrome runs no buyback and no burn, so the Pressure Framework reads AERO at about **+4.95%** net on a circulating base of **974.23M AERO**, against our supply monitor's **+4.71%** for the same window — a gap of **0.24 percentage points**, comfortably inside tolerance. AERO is **structurally inflationary on the active float**, with vote-locking as the only brake.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Jul 27 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **AERO at about +4.95% net**: sell pressure of **60.3M AERO** against buy pressure of **12.07M AERO** on a circulating base of **974.23M AERO**. Our supply monitor reads **+4.71%** for the same period, a gap of only **0.24 percentage points** — well inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag ships with this page. The two readings agree because the AERO ledger is closed rather than estimated. Aerodrome's circulating figure is defined by exactly two on-chain numbers: the AERO token's total supply and the AERO balance sitting inside the veAERO vote-escrow contract. Subtract one from the other at both ends of the window and the float moved **+48.23M**, which is precisely the **60.3M** minted minus the **12.07M** locked. Nothing in this analysis is modelled. AERO is best labelled **an uncapped emission token whose float grows near 20% a year unless locking accelerates**.

## Sell pressure: where new AERO comes from

It comes from one place, and it arrives on a clock. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **60.3M AERO**, every token of it minted by Aerodrome's own emission contract on a weekly epoch that rolls at Thursday midnight UTC. Aerodrome is a ve(3,3) liquidity marketplace: veAERO lockers vote each week on which pools receive the new AERO, and the pools pay it out to liquidity providers, who overwhelmingly sell it. The protocol has passed out of its original decay schedule into what Aerodrome calls the Aero Fed phase, where the emission is a governance-set share of total supply rather than a fixed decaying number. On-chain that share currently reads **0.21%** of total supply per epoch, and it read the same value at both ends of this window — no vote moved it mid-window, so the trailing rate is an honest forward predictor. Three streams ride on top of each other inside every mint: the gauge emission itself, a rebase paid to existing lockers so their share is not diluted, and a **2.28%** team allocation. The epoch that settled on **Jul 23 2026** minted **4.72M AERO** in total, and those three documented components reproduce that figure to the token — **4.07M** in gauge emission, **0.54M** in rebase, **0.11M** to the team.

Every other sell row on the AERO ledger is zero, and each for a different reason. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero** because Aerodrome never built a cliff calendar. Its launch allocations were handed out as vote-locked veAERO positions on day one rather than as dated unlocks to seed investors, and the vesting trackers confirm no third-party cliff sits inside this window or after it. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** in value but not in importance. Aerodrome's team address holds **0.95M AERO**, down from **1.56M** ninety days ago; it received roughly **1.4M** from the thirteen epoch team mints and sent out roughly **2.0M**. Those tokens were already counted as new supply the moment they were minted, so booking the wallet's outflow again would double-count it. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: AERO has no estate, no trustee distribution and no court-ordered sale. Locks that reach expiry do return AERO to the float, and those exits are netted inside the lock row rather than invented here. Sell #5 covers the one genuinely new item: the pending merger allocation, described below.

## Buy pressure: where new AERO goes

Aerodrome has exactly one buy-side mechanism, and it is voluntary. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **12.07M AERO**, the net increase in AERO held inside the veAERO vote-escrow contract, which rose from **958.87M** to **970.94M** across the window. That is a net figure, not a gross one: four of the last nineteen weeks show the escrow shrinking as expired locks were withdrawn. Locking is what buys AERO its brake. Holders lock for up to four years to earn voting power over emissions, and because **100%** of Aerodrome's swap fees are paid to those lockers, the incentive to lock is the protocol's central design. Roughly half of all AERO ever minted — **970.94M** of **1,945.16M** — currently sits inside that contract, which is why the tradable float is only **974.23M**.

The other three buy rows are zero. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, though not because Aerodrome never buys. Its Public Goods Fund and Protocol Growth Fund do acquire AERO on the open market and max-lock it; a **214,000** AERO purchase and an **87,000** AERO lock are on record. But neither the size nor the timing is published per quarter, and a single undated anecdote is not enough evidence to book a number. What those funds buy is max-locked into the very same vote-escrow contract measured in Buy #4, so their effect on the float already appears on this page. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**: AERO is never destroyed. Swap fees are paid to lockers in the assets that were traded, not in AERO, and the burn address held the same **2,000** tokens at both ends of the window. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**, with no dated treasury purchase landing inside the window.

## Foundation and overhang

Three pools of AERO sit outside ordinary market hands, and all three are watched. The largest by far is the veAERO vote-escrow contract itself, holding **970.94M AERO** with no published release schedule — lockers exit whenever their individual terms expire, and the contract is read on-chain every refresh. Second is Aerodrome's team address, holding **0.95M AERO** and topped up by the **2.28%** team share inside every weekly mint; it has been a net seller over the window, sending out about **2.0M** against about **1.4M** received. Third is the buy-and-lock destination used by the Public Goods Fund and Protocol Growth Fund, whose purchases are max-locked inside the same escrow and are therefore trackable through it rather than as a separate wallet.

The rule that governs all three is the same: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. That matters most for the escrow. Because the vote-escrow contract is the entire difference between AERO's **1,945.16M** total supply and its **974.23M** float, a wave of expiring locks would show up as float growth on top of the weekly mint, not instead of it. Locking is a brake that can be released.

## How AERO compares to other emission-driven DEX tokens

The right comparison class for AERO is not a capped chain coin but the ve(3,3) exchange tokens — Aerodrome on Base and its own sibling Velodrome on Optimism, alongside the older vote-escrow model that Curve popularised. What separates them is not price but three mechanism choices: whether the supply is capped, whether new issuance decays on a fixed schedule or is voted, and whether any fee revenue is used to remove tokens. AERO sits at the loosest end of all three. It has no maximum supply at all. Its issuance is no longer on the fixed **1%**-a-week decay it launched with, but on a governance-set share of supply that voters can raise as well as lower — the on-chain bounds run from **0.01%** to **1%** of supply per epoch, and the current setting of **0.21%** sits closer to the floor than the ceiling. And it removes nothing: no burn, no protocol buyback booked on this ledger.

Contrast that with a fee-burning exchange token, where trading volume mechanically destroys supply and heavy quarters shrink the float outright, or with a halving-model chain, where the issuance path is written into the code and no vote can change it. AERO's equivalent of a burn is a lock — reversible, holder-elected and, over this window, worth only about a fifth of what was issued. The honest way to read AERO is that it converts its considerable fee revenue into a reason to lock rather than into a reduction in supply. That is a legitimate design, and it is why Aerodrome commands the majority of Base DEX volume, but it does not make the token deflationary. On the Pressure Framework's only question — is there more AERO chasing the market at the end of the quarter than at the start — the answer for thirteen straight epochs has been yes.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the merger. Dromos Labs is folding Aerodrome on Base and Velodrome on Optimism into a single cross-chain protocol, with the unified token split **94.5%** to AERO holders and **5.5%** to holders of the other token — on current supply, roughly **113M** of conversion allocation, about **5.8%** of AERO's total. Coverage has pointed at a launch through **2026**, but the weekly supply walk to **Jul 27 2026** shows only smooth epoch mints with no step, so nothing has been issued and the row is booked at zero. If it executes inside the next window it would be the single largest supply event in AERO's history. Second, the emission rate itself: veAERO voters can move it by **0.01%** a week in either direction, so thirteen epochs of consistent votes could shift the run rate meaningfully before **Oct 25 2026**. Third, the liquidity migration to the new MEV-resistant pools, which gates emissions — providers who do not migrate stop receiving AERO, which changes where new supply lands, not how much of it there is. Fourth, the lock balance: a quarter in which expiring veAERO exceeds new locking would flip Buy #4 negative and push the net reading above **+6%**. Fifth, any governance move to route fee revenue into a booked buyback, which is currently the clearest path from a **+4.95%** reading toward a neutral one.

## Summary

AERO is the clearest example on our catalogue of an uncapped emission token whose economics are strong and whose supply is not. Aerodrome Finance mints roughly **4.7M AERO** a week — **60.3M** over the last 90 days — and removes none of it, because **100%** of its swap fees are paid to veAERO lockers in traded assets rather than used to buy or burn the token. The only counterweight is voluntary vote-escrow locking, which absorbed **12.07M** over the same window, leaving a net **+4.95%** increase in the tradable float and a projected **+5.16%** for the next 90 days. The key risk is that the brake is reversible and the ceiling is not fixed: nearly half the total supply sits inside a contract that releases on its own schedule, and a pending merger allocation of about **113M AERO** has been announced but never issued.
