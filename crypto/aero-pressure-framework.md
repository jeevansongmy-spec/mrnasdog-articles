# AERO Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply was shrinking · trend softening

AERO supply shrank slightly over the 90 days from Jul 28 to Oct 26 2026, by **0.77%**, but it is set to grow about **8.01%** in the next 90 days. Aerodrome and Velodrome merged into one exchange, Aero, on Oct 22 2026: AERO rewards for liquidity providers and the Velodrome holders' share of the merger add new coins, while holders who lock or stake AERO take coins out of circulation. In 90 days **72.56M AERO** came onto the market and **78.95M AERO** went into locks; other trackers show **−13.91%** because they still count only the old token, so the coin page carries a warning note. New AERO rewards are capped at a 20% yearly rate, and the merger share is fixed at 117M AERO.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Oct 26 2026, AERO's net supply change was **−0.77%** on a circulating supply of **835.31M AERO**: **72.56M AERO** came onto the market and **78.95M AERO** was locked or staked. For the next 90 days we project **+8.01%**, with **80.84M AERO** arriving and only **13.89M AERO** going into new stakes. Other trackers read **−13.91%** for the same window, a gap of **13.15 points**, so the coin page carries a warning note. The cause is the upgrade itself: since Oct 22 2026, holders moved **116.27M** liquid old AERO into the upgrade, where the old coins stay locked for good while the holders receive the same amount of new AERO. The trackers still follow only the old token, so to them that swap looks like coins disappearing. It removes no supply. The plain label for AERO today: an emissions-funded exchange token in the middle of a merger, flat over the last 90 days and set to grow in the next 90.

## Sell pressure: where new AERO comes from

Row 1, protocol inflation, is the largest steady source. Until Oct 22 2026, the old Aerodrome contract paid about 4.1M new AERO every Thursday to liquidity providers and about 220K to the team wallet; the 12 weekly payouts from Jul 30 to Oct 15 2026 added **51.49M AERO**. A weekly top-up for lockers went straight into locked positions and never reached the market, so it is not counted. The final old payout on Oct 22 2026, about 4.0M AERO, went to reward pools that had already been switched off and now sits unspent in the old contract. Since the launch, Aero mints new AERO only when a liquidity provider claims rewards, plus a 5% team share on top: that came to **1.92M AERO** in the first four days and is still climbing as more providers claim. Together that is **53.41M AERO** for the last 90 days. At the latest pace, the new rewards come to about **41.47M AERO** in the next 90 days, close to the 8% low end of the 8–12% yearly rate the Aero team expects. The hard ceiling is a 20% yearly rate.

Row 2, vesting unlocks, is zero: AERO has no vesting calendar. The 2023 launch allocations were locked from the first day, and the merger added no team or investor schedule. Row 3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero too: the team wallets hold liquid AERO, but those coins already count as circulating, so spending them adds nothing new. Row 4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero: no bankruptcy estate holds AERO, and staked AERO can only leave when its time runs out. A small amount of old locks that had already expired paid out **284K AERO** after the launch; that is netted inside the locking figure on the buy side.

Row 5, the Velodrome holders' share, is the merger itself. Velodrome holders get **0.044 AERO** for each VELO, **117M AERO** in all, which was 5.5% of the new AERO supply at launch. By Oct 26 2026, **19.15M AERO** had arrived as liquid coins and **45.82M AERO** as staked AERO, which stays out of the count. About **39.37M AERO** more can still arrive as liquid coins, and we count all of it in the next 90 days because two big exchanges convert their users' VELO in early November 2026. VELO that is never upgraded never becomes AERO.

## Buy pressure: where new AERO goes

Row 1, programmatic buyback, books zero on purpose. The Momentum Fund, which used to be called the Public Goods Fund, buys AERO with its share of exchange fees and locks it for the longest time allowed. Its buys in this window, such as **343K AERO** on Aug 12, **325K AERO** on Aug 19 and **171K AERO** on Sep 10 2026, were locked right away, so they are already inside row 4; counting them here too would count the same coins twice. No buyback has been announced since the launch, though the fund can now also burn AERO. Row 2, protocol fee burn, is zero: trading fees go to stakers in the coins people trade, and no AERO is destroyed. Upgrade coins that are never claimed can be burned later, but they were never part of the circulating supply. Row 3, foundation buy, is zero, because the Foundation buys only through the Momentum Fund.

Row 4, new long-term lock, is the buy side's whole weight. Locking or staking AERO takes it out of circulation, and in 90 days holders locked a net **78.95M AERO**. Of that, **48.93M AERO** came before the launch, much of it reward pools moving into locks as the old system shut down, and **30.02M AERO** came after it, mostly on launch day. Those reward pools are almost empty now, so they cannot repeat. For the next 90 days we count only fresh staking at the pace seen since launch day, about **13.89M AERO**. That is the main reason the forward reading turns from a small shrink to clear growth.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team wallets hold liquid AERO that already counts as circulating. An incentives wallet holds **28.94M AERO**, and the Aero Foundation has said it will pay out liquid AERO as pool rewards over several weeks to cover rewards that stayed in the old pools during the upgrade. The team emissions wallet holds **4.06M AERO**, the grants wallet **2.03M AERO** and the Momentum Fund **1.70M AERO**. A new team-share wallet collects 5% of every new reward and held **91,576 AERO** at the check. Because these coins are already in the count, spending them is not new supply, but selling them would still add to what the market must absorb.

Larger staked positions sit outside the count. Before the launch, the Momentum Fund had about **154.67M AERO** locked, the grants program about **64.12M AERO**, and the Velodrome Foundation **29.34M AERO**, which is now staked until Oct 2030. Staked AERO can only be withdrawn once its time runs out, and permanent stakes never run out, so none of this can reach the market inside the next 90 days. We read these wallets on-chain at each rebuild: if any of their balances falls between checks, the outflow enters row 3 at the next refresh.

## How AERO compares to other DEX tokens

AERO belongs to the vote-escrow family of exchange tokens that Curve made popular. Like CRV, AERO pays liquidity providers in brand-new coins and asks holders to lock their coins to steer those rewards and earn the fees. The difference is the brake. CRV follows a fixed, slowly shrinking schedule toward a hard cap. AERO now has no fixed schedule: rewards are minted only when claimed, each pool's rewards are capped at a multiple of the revenue it earns, and the total can never pass a 20% yearly rate. That ties new AERO to trading activity, but it also means the supply can grow faster in busy markets.

Against Uniswap's UNI, the contrast is sharp. UNI has a fixed cap, pays no rewards in new coins and now burns part of its fees, so its supply barely moves. AERO pays its liquidity in new coins and returns its fees to stakers in other tokens, not by destroying AERO, so AERO only shrinks when holders lock faster than rewards are paid. PancakeSwap's CAKE sits in between, with small new rewards and weekly fee buybacks that burn coins.

The merger makes AERO unusual for now. Few exchange tokens have added a second community's holders in one step: the Velodrome share adds up to **117M AERO** once, on top of the normal rewards, and most of what is still to come is expected in the next few weeks.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Nov 2–4 2026:** Coinbase upgrades its users' AERO and VELO to the new AERO, a large block of the remaining **39.37M AERO** Velodrome share.

**Nov 3 2026:** Binance ends VELO trading and pauses old AERO deposits for its own upgrade; its old AERO swap is one for one and adds nothing, but its VELO becomes new AERO.

**The reward pace through November 2026:** the first four days minted **1.92M AERO** and the pace was still rising; a sustained move toward the 12% yearly rate the team expects would lift new supply well above our **41.47M AERO** forecast.

**Momentum Fund buybacks or burns:** none has been announced since Oct 22 2026; any burn, or any buy that is locked, would add a new buy-side row.

**Fresh staking:** about **13.89M AERO** per 90 days at the post-launch pace; if new stakers lock faster to earn the fees, the forward growth shrinks.

## Summary

AERO supply shrank **0.77%** over the 90 days to Oct 26 2026 because holders locked **78.95M AERO**, slightly more than the **72.56M AERO** that rewards and the Velodrome merger added. The next 90 days look different: about **41.47M AERO** of new rewards and up to **39.37M AERO** more from Velodrome holders, against only about **13.89M AERO** of fresh staking, for growth of about **8.01%**. The key risk is that new AERO rewards rise toward their 20% yearly ceiling while locking stays slow. The merger share is fixed at 117M AERO and fades after November 2026, so later quarters depend on rewards against staking.
