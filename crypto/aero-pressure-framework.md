Aerodrome Finance minted **61.0M AERO** across exactly **13 weekly epochs** in the last 90 days, and every epoch printed more than the one before it. Against that, the veAERO vote-escrow absorbed **21.1M AERO** — a programmatic buyback of **1.5M** plus **19.6M** of net new locking. Nothing burns. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads AERO at **+4.07% net** over the last 90 days and **+4.33%** forward, against a supply-monitor reading of **+4.22%**. AERO is **uncapped**: the emission is now a flat percentage of a supply that keeps growing, so the mint compounds rather than decays.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Aerodrome Finance at **+4.07% net**: **61.0M AERO** of newly minted supply against **21.1M AERO** of vote-escrow absorption, on a circulating base of **981.4M AERO**. The supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+4.22%**, a gap of **0.16 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and needs no reconciliation walk. The two agree because the framework and the monitor draw the circulating line in the same place: AERO in the vote-escrow is excluded from the float by both, and the subtraction proves it at both ends of the window, with the escrow balance matching total supply minus circulating supply to the token. Forward, the framework reads **+4.33%**. AERO is **structurally inflationary on the active float** — a ve(3,3) exchange whose weekly mint outruns the lock that absorbs it by roughly two to one.

## Sell pressure: where new AERO comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is almost the entire sell side on AERO, and it is the row where the coin's reputation is most out of date. Aerodrome Finance launched with a weekly emission that shrank **1%** every epoch, and that decay phase is finished. The minter contract on Base now sits in its tail regime: each Thursday it mints **21 basis points** of total supply, a rate read directly off the contract at both ends of this window. Because the base it is charged against grows every week, the weekly batch grows too. Measured epoch by epoch rather than multiplied from a nominal figure, the 13 mints inside this window ran **4.60M AERO** in late May to **4.78M AERO** in the week of **Aug 20 2026** — a **3.8%** increase in the weekly print across a single quarter. Most of each batch goes to liquidity providers through the gauge system; a smaller slice is the anti-dilution rebase paid to veAERO lockers. Both are freshly minted AERO, so both sit in this row, giving **58.2M** over the window. The arithmetic closes exactly: the sum of the 13 mint events equals the change in AERO total supply to the token, which also means no AERO was destroyed anywhere in the window.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**, and not by chance. Aerodrome Finance has no vesting escrow, no cliff calendar and no linear release: every AERO in existence was either distributed at launch or minted by the weekly epoch schedule. This is worth stating plainly because an unlock tracker currently advertises a **Sep 3 2026** AERO unlock in an "airdrop" category. **Sep 3 2026** is a Thursday — the ordinary weekly epoch flip, already counted in Sell #1. Booking it again would double-count the mint.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — carries **2.8M AERO**, and it is a rate worth re-measuring rather than reading from documentation. The launch tokenomics describe a team allocation of **5%** of emissions; the minter contract itself reports **2.28%** today, unchanged across all 13 epochs. Applied to the measured mint, the team stream is **2.8M AERO** over the window. What that stream then does is the interesting part, and it cuts against the row: the team wallet vote-locked **3.2M AERO** into veAERO during the window, more than the whole stream, and its liquid balance fell only from **2.45M** to **1.90M**. Alongside it, a protocol safe holds **2.68M** and a separate multisig has sat on **29.40M** all window. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Aerodrome Finance has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new AERO goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **1.5M AERO**, and it is real. Aerodrome Finance routes part of its protocol income into buying its own token on the open market, then vote-locks what it buys for the maximum term. This build traced that flow by **sender** rather than by watching a balance rise, which matters: of the **5.1M AERO** that arrived at the protocol's buyback safe over the window, only **1.5M** came from the converter contract that actually performs market purchases, across **11** firings. The other **3.6M** came from a second team safe — an internal transfer that looks identical to a purchase on a balance chart and is not buy pressure. The project's own weekly buyback posts quote larger figures, but those aggregate several categories including relocked rewards, so this row ships the traced market leg and names the divergence rather than adopting the announced number.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**, and this is the structural fact that defines AERO. Aerodrome Finance pays **100%** of trading fees to veAERO lockers in the tokens that were traded; it never buys AERO with them and never destroys AERO. The chain settles it rather than the documentation: total AERO supply rose over the window by precisely the amount the 13 weekly batches minted, to the coin, which leaves no room for a burn of any size. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero**, with no discretionary accumulation programme beyond the buyback above.

Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **19.6M AERO** and is the offset that carries the whole buy side. veAERO is a genuine term lock of up to four years, and both this framework and the supply monitor exclude escrowed AERO from circulating supply, so what the lock swallows really does leave the tradable float. Reading the escrow contract at both ends of the window, its AERO balance rose from **961.7M** to **982.8M**. A full scan of every transfer in and out of that contract closes on the same number — **33.0M** deposited against **11.9M** withdrawn — which confirms the lock is filling rather than draining. Inside that **21.1M** of net absorption sit the rebase deposits of **5.6M**, the team's own **3.2M** lock, the buyback, and ordinary holders locking more than they withdrew.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled balances are watched on AERO. The team emissions wallet holds **1.90M AERO**, down from **2.45M** at the start of the window even while receiving **2.8M** of fresh mint — the difference went into the vote-escrow, not to market. The protocol buyback safe holds **2.68M**, up from **2.28M**, and forwards what it accumulates into maximum-term locks. A second multisig holds **29.40M AERO** and moved only **0.34M** across the entire window — the largest single team-controlled overhang on the coin, and the one to watch. A fourth safe ran a pure pass-through, holding zero AERO at both ends of the window while relaying **3.6M** onward, which is why a flat balance was checked rather than assumed to mean no flow.

One further bucket deserves naming because it is easy to misread in Aerodrome Finance's favour. Three managed-lock relay contracts hold roughly **47M AERO** between them. That AERO is economically locked, but it sits outside the vote-escrow contract and is therefore counted inside the circulating float. This build does not credit it as absorption — only the escrow's own balance change is booked. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How AERO compares to other ve(3,3) exchange tokens

AERO belongs to the ve(3,3) family — exchange tokens that mint continuously to bribe liquidity and rely on vote-escrow locking to hold the float down. Against a hard-capped, halving asset the contrast is total: a halving chain's issuance is written into code and falls on a schedule nobody can vote on, while Aerodrome Finance's tail rate is a governance parameter that veAERO holders nudge weekly inside a **1** to **100** basis-point band. Uncapped is not the same as unbounded, but it does mean the ceiling is a vote rather than a constant.

Against exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns, the difference is where the fee goes. Aerodrome Finance sends **100%** of trading fees to veAERO lockers in the traded assets — excellent for a locker's yield, and worth nothing as buy pressure, because not one of those fees becomes an AERO purchase or an AERO burn. A burn-and-buyback exchange token converts revenue directly into permanent supply reduction; AERO converts revenue into a reason to lock. That is why its entire buy side is the lock rather than the burn, and why the reading swings on locking behaviour rather than on trading volume.

The comparison that matters most, though, is with its own past. Aerodrome Finance is frequently described as having a decaying emission, which was true through the first phase and is no longer true at all. In the tail regime the weekly mint is a percentage of total supply, so as supply grows the mint grows with it — the opposite of decay. A reader carrying the old model forward will underestimate AERO issuance by a widening margin every quarter.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the merger of Aerodrome Finance and Velodrome into a single unified token. Under the announced terms AERO holders take **94.5%** of the new supply and Velodrome holders **5.5%**, which is a one-time dilution of up to **5.5%** — larger than a full quarter of emissions. This build verified on-chain that it has not executed: the AERO contract's supply change is fully explained by the 13 epoch mints, with no merger issuance. Second, the thirteen weekly emission votes between **Aug 27 2026** and **Nov 19 2026**: the tail rate held at **21** basis points all window, but the epoch governor can move it one basis point per epoch in either direction, so the forward mint could plausibly land anywhere from **8** to **34** basis points by the end of the window. Third, the transition of the Public Goods Fund into the Momentum Fund, which is stated to direct **100%** of proceeds to buybacks — the one change that could move Buy #1 materially. Fourth, whether the **29.40M** multisig stays still. Fifth, the Predictive Allocation change announced **Jul 26 2026**, which redirects where emissions go without altering how many are minted, and so should move the ledger not at all — worth confirming it does not.

## Summary

Aerodrome Finance is structurally inflationary on its active float. Thirteen weekly epochs minted **61.0M AERO** in 90 days and the veAERO vote-escrow absorbed **21.1M**, leaving **+4.07% net** against a monitor reading of **+4.22%**, and a projected **+4.33%** forward. The structural mechanism is a tail emission fixed at **21 basis points** of an uncapped and growing total supply, which means the weekly mint compounds rather than decays — the single most commonly misread fact about AERO. The key risk is that the entire buy side is one behaviour: vote-locking. There is no burn and the fee stream never touches the token, so if locking slows the offset disappears immediately, and the pending merger with Velodrome adds up to **5.5%** of one-time dilution on top. The ceiling is a governance parameter rather than a cap: veAERO holders can set the emission anywhere between **1** and **100** basis points a week, so AERO's scarcity is a vote, not a constant.
