# TEL Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Telcoin cannot create another TEL. All **100B** was minted at the 2017 genesis, and the Telcoin token contract on Ethereum returned exactly **100,000,000,000** at both ends of the 90 days to **Aug 16 2026** — no mint, no burn. Yet TEL still drifts upward, because the Telcoin Association releases pre-minted treasury TEL under an annual governance vote: the Year 3 allocation commits **900M TEL** to calendar 2026, or about **221.9M TEL** per quarter. Against zero buyback and zero burn, the Pressure Framework reads TEL at **+0.23%** net on a circulating base of **98.57B TEL**, versus our supply monitor's **+3.74%** — a **3.51 percentage point** gap that is a supply reclassification, not a flow. TEL is best read as **capped but one-directional**: it can never mint past its ceiling, and it has no mechanism that can ever pull supply back.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 16 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **TEL at +0.23% net**: sell pressure of **221.9M TEL** against buy pressure of **0 TEL**, on a circulating base of **98.57B TEL**. Our supply monitor reads **+3.74%** for the same period, a gap of **3.51 percentage points**, which is over the half-point tolerance and therefore ships with a data-conflict flag on the overview card. The deep walk found the cause and it is not a Telcoin event. The tracked float sat pinned near **95.08B** every single day from **Jan 1 2026** to **Aug 3 2026**, jumped to **100.00B** on **Aug 4 2026** — nearly **5B** in one day, with no transaction anywhere on Ethereum or Polygon to match it — and settled back at **98.54B** on **Aug 14 2026**. That is the Telcoin Association treasury bucket being reclassified, not TEL reaching the market. The framework labels Telcoin **a hard-capped payments token whose only supply lever is a discretionary annual treasury vote**.

## Sell pressure: where new TEL comes from

Strictly speaking, no new TEL comes from anywhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and it is zero at the level of the contract rather than the marketing. The Telcoin token is a fixed-supply ERC-20 minted in full at the 2017 launch; reading its total supply on Ethereum at the first block of the window and again at the last returned the same **100,000,000,000 TEL** to the unit, and the burn address held an identical **8,756 TEL** throughout. The Polygon representation of TEL did grow by **1.37B** over the window, which looks like issuance until you read the Ethereum side: the bridge lock that backs it rose by the matching **1.38B**. Telcoin minted nothing; TEL simply moved chains.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because Telcoin has no unlock calendar left. The 2017 sale and the team allocation released over the years after launch, and no dated cliff falls inside this window. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, carries the entire ledger at **221.9M TEL**. The Telcoin Association votes an annual TEL budget, and the Year 3 allocation for calendar 2026 authorises **900M TEL** out of the roughly **8.1B** the association counted in the TEL Treasury: **320M** for Telcoin Network validator incentives, **350M** for the operations safe covering mainnet launch, operator installations and marketing, **200M** for TELx liquidity-miner incentives, and **30M** for council compensation across 33 members. Snapshot voting opened **Jan 27 2026** and the Platform and Treasury Councils passed it in **May 2026**, inside this window. Pro-rated across 90 days that is **221.9M TEL**, and an association-linked safe on Polygon corroborates it independently: it fell from **331.8M** to **83.3M TEL** in four dated transfers on **Jul 16 2026**, **Jul 23 2026**, **Jul 28 2026** and **Aug 4 2026**, totalling **248.5M**. Two independent readings, twelve percent apart. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: TEL has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new TEL goes

Nowhere, and that is the defining fact about Telcoin's supply. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: no Telcoin treasury programme and no protocol contract purchases TEL on any schedule. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**. Telcoin Network is designed to destroy a share of each block's gas and immediately regenerate the same amount to the TEL Treasury, which nets to zero by construction — and the network is still pre-mainnet as of **Aug 2026**, so the mechanism is not even running. The burn addresses on Ethereum and Polygon held identical balances at both ends of the quarter.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**, with no dated Telcoin Association market purchase in the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**, and this one deserves the explanation. The Telcoin staking contract on Polygon grew from **3.85B** to **4.21B TEL** across the window, a **369M** increase that superficially looks like supply being pulled off the market. It is not. Telcoin staking has no protocol lock-up — a staker can withdraw on demand — so the contract is custody rather than a lock, and the framework refuses to credit a free-exit position as removed supply. It is tracked as an overhang instead. The absence of any burn or buyback is what makes TEL structurally one-directional: nothing in Telcoin's design can make the float smaller.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs sit behind Telcoin's ledger. The first and largest is the TEL Treasury itself, the association's pre-minted reserve. On the market's current classification it holds about **1.43B TEL** — the gap between the **100B** minted cap and the **98.57B** counted as circulating — though the association's own Year 3 filing counted roughly **8.1B** in Treasury inventory before this year's draw. That divergence is itself the reclassification story. The second is the set of council and operations safes on Polygon that hold each Year 3 tranche between authorisation and payout; the one identified this quarter drained **248.5M TEL** between **Jul 16 2026** and **Aug 4 2026**. The third is the Telcoin staking contract holding about **4.21B TEL** in free-exit custody.

The rule that governs all three is the same: if any of these balances falls between refreshes and those tokens reach the tradable market, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The treasury is walked against the Telcoin Association governance forum on a bi-weekly cadence; the safes and the staking contract are read directly from the chain. What makes Telcoin unusually legible is that the overhang is concentrated in one governed pool with one annual vote — you do not have to track dozens of vesting counterparties, you have to read one proposal a year and then watch whether the money actually leaves.

## How TEL compares to other capped, fully-minted tokens

Telcoin belongs to the class of hard-capped, fully-minted tokens whose entire supply already exists — the ICO-era ERC-20s and payments tokens where the mint question was settled at genesis. Within that class, the only variables that matter are how much supply is still held back and whether anything removes supply. TEL scores well on the first and worst possible on the second. Roughly **98.6%** of the cap is already counted as circulating, so the remaining release capacity is small in absolute terms, and it can never mint past **100B** no matter what governance decides.

Compare that to a fee-burning exchange token, where trading volume mechanically destroys units and a busy quarter can push the float outright deflationary, or to an uncapped continuous-emission chain, where a fresh block reward lands every few seconds regardless of demand. TEL sits between those two and shares the weakness of neither extreme but the strength of neither either: it will never inflate the way an uncapped chain does, and it will never deflate the way a burn token does. Its float can only move one direction — upward, through discretionary treasury deployment — which is why Telcoin's ceiling on the framework's inflation metric is structurally limited. A capped supply that is still slowly releasing reserve is a mildly inflationary asset, not a scarce one, and the framework scores it that way rather than rewarding the cap on its own.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the TEL3 token upgrade. A constitutional governance vote opened **Aug 6 2026** to move TEL from 2 decimals to 18, deploy one unified contract address across Ethereum, Polygon, Base and Telcoin Network, and replace today's lock-and-release bridging with bridge-authorised mint and burn. Balances are preserved one for one and the cap is unchanged, so it is supply-neutral on paper — but it would replace a contract that provably cannot mint with one that can, which is precisely why the protocol inflation row is tagged as checked rather than permanent. Second, the Telcoin Network mainnet: it remains pre-launch, and the moment validator issuance begins routing treasury TEL into holders' wallets, Sell #3 stops being a pro-rata estimate and becomes a measurable block-by-block flow. Third, the remainder of the Year 3 spend — roughly **900M TEL** is authorised for the calendar year and the council safes are visibly draining, so any acceleration shows up directly. Fourth, the pending strategic telecom advisory proposal seeking **194M TEL**, which would lift the annual draw materially if it passes. Fifth, whether the tracked circulating figure stabilises near **98.5B** or revises again, since the **Aug 4 2026** and **Aug 14 2026** steps show the classification is currently unsettled.

## Summary

Telcoin's TEL is a hard-capped, fully-minted token that still inflates gently. The contract cannot create new units — we read **100,000,000,000 TEL** on-chain at both ends of the quarter — and there is no burn and no buyback anywhere in the design, so the entire ledger rests on one row: the Telcoin Association's **900M TEL** Year 3 treasury allocation, which releases about **221.9M TEL** per 90 days from pre-minted reserve. That gives a framework reading of **+0.23%** net against our monitor's **+3.74%**, a **3.51 point** gap driven by a supply reclassification on **Aug 4 2026** rather than any Telcoin flow. The key risk is one-directional and structural: TEL has no mechanism that can ever shrink its float, so a mainnet launch that routes treasury issuance into circulation would raise the number and nothing in the protocol could pull it back.
