# HASH Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Provenance Blockchain creates no new HASH at all — the chain's emission settings read zero and the count of HASH in existence was identical at both ends of the last 90 days — and yet the Pressure Framework reads HASH at **+5.92%** over the trailing 90 days and **+5.91%** over the next 90. Every point of that comes from a single mechanism: a governance-imposed lockup, voted in 2025, unwinding in a straight line to **May 22 2029** and moving about **37.8M HASH** a day from locked into tradable. Sell pressure is **3,402.8M HASH**, buy pressure is **0**, and the ceiling is a hard, on-chain **95,000M HASH** that no one can raise without a vote.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **57,526M HASH**, the framework books **3,402.8M HASH** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+5.92%** — and projects **+5.91%** for the next 90 days on the same unbroken schedule. The inflation monitor reads **+6.72%** for the same window, a gap of **0.80 percentage points**, which is over the framework's 0.5pp tolerance and therefore ships with a monitor-gap warning on the overview page. That gap decomposes cleanly and completely: **0.40pp** is base convention, because the monitor divides its supply change by the 90-day-old supply while the framework divides by today's, and the remaining **0.40pp** is the monitor's market-derived supply estimate running above the chain's own account state. The label for HASH is a **capped chain whose lockup is still unwinding**: the token cannot inflate, but its tradable float can, and right now it does.

## Sell pressure: where new HASH comes from

It does not come from minting. Provenance Blockchain's emission module reports an inflation rate of zero, annual provisions of zero, and both an upper and a lower inflation bound of zero; the HASH marker itself is flagged as fixed-supply with no minting permission granted to any account. Read at both ends of the window, the count of HASH in existence was **95,000M** on both dates, identical to the ninth decimal place. Validators and delegators on Provenance Blockchain are paid out of transaction fees, not out of new coins. So **Sell #1, protocol inflation, is 0**. Provenance Blockchain's own older tokenomics writing still advertises a dynamic emission model that scales between 1% and 52.5% depending on how much HASH is staked; that model is not switched on, and the chain, not the document, is what the framework reads. Because a vote could switch it back on, the row is watched rather than closed permanently.

The entire supply story is **Sell #2, vesting unlocks, at 3,402.8M HASH**. A 2025 governance proposal placed large HASH holders on a linear lockup. On chain that is **448** continuous vesting accounts, holding **54,044.7M HASH** between them when the schedule opened on **Jun 22 2025**, releasing without cliffs until **May 22 2029** — 1,430 days of perfectly straight-line unlock. Locked HASH fell from **40,854.3M** at the start of the window to **37,451.5M** at the end, and that fall is the number. It is worth being precise about what this is: no HASH is created by a vesting unlock. The coins already existed and were already counted in the 95,000M ceiling. What changes is that they stop being unspendable and become tradable float — which is exactly the thing the Pressure Framework measures, and exactly the thing a hard cap does not protect a holder from.

**Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is 0.** Everything still held back on Provenance Blockchain sits on the published vesting schedule above, so there is no separate discretionary pool with its own release decision to make. **Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is 0** as well: HASH has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee, and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new HASH goes

Nowhere, this window. **Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is 0** — Provenance Blockchain runs no programme that spends treasury money repurchasing HASH on the open market, and none was announced or executed inside the window. The Provenance Blockchain Foundation was restructured in early 2026, with Figure Technology Solutions taking on the operational role of executing community decisions, but no repurchase mandate travelled with that change.

**Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is also 0**, and this is the row most worth explaining, because a burn mechanism genuinely exists. Provenance Blockchain routes 40% of network fees and 100% of settlement fees into an auction where participants bid in HASH, and the winning HASH bids are destroyed. It did not fire once in these 90 days. Provenance Blockchain destroys HASH in two structurally independent places, and the framework read both at both ends of the window rather than trusting either alone: the account that holds destroyed HASH stood at **21.5M HASH** on both dates, unchanged to five decimals, and the count of HASH in existence stood at **95,000M** on both dates. Neither surface moved, so nothing was burned. The last real removal was a governance vote that destroyed **5,000M HASH** in early **May 2026** — a genuinely large burn, but one that lands 37 days before this window opens and therefore contributes nothing to this reading.

**Buy #3, Foundation buy, is 0.** The Provenance Blockchain community treasury did grow across the window, from **32.0M** to **40.3M HASH**, but that is fee income arriving on its own rather than a market purchase, so the framework tracks it as an overhang on the sell side instead of crediting it as demand. **Buy #4, new long-term lock, is 0**, and staking actually moved the other way: bonded HASH fell from **17,460.5M** to **16,767.2M**. Even had it risen, it would not have counted here, because Provenance Blockchain's own circulating-supply definition already treats staked HASH as tradable float.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on HASH is large, fully enumerated, and unusually well-behaved. The dominant item is the vesting cohort itself: **37,451.5M HASH** still locked across **448** accounts, of which the single largest account holds **14,745.5M HASH** — roughly 15.5% of the entire ceiling in one place. That balance is readable on chain and is refreshed from the chain at every rebuild. The second item is the Provenance Blockchain community treasury, which held **40.3M HASH** at the end of the window against **32.0M** at the start, growing purely on network fee income; it is refreshed from the chain on the same cadence. There is no buyback accumulation wallet to track, because there is no buyback, and there is no bankruptcy estate residual.

What makes this overhang unusual is that almost none of it is discretionary. Every locked HASH sits on a published, on-chain, unbroken straight line to **May 22 2029**, so the release calendar is not a decision anybody gets to make each quarter — it is already written. The trigger sentence still applies to both items: if the vesting cohort's locked balance or the community treasury's balance falls between refreshes by more than the schedule accounts for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HASH compares to other capped, non-minting chains

HASH belongs to a small class: chains with a hard, on-chain supply ceiling that have also switched issuance fully off. That is a harder commitment than a halving schedule. A halving-model chain like Bitcoin still mints on every block, just at a decaying rate, so its inflation reading is positive but shrinking on a known clock. Provenance Blockchain mints nothing at all — its emission reading is a flat zero, not a small number. On the pure issuance axis, HASH is stricter than almost any proof-of-work chain and stricter than every uncapped continuous-emission Cosmos L1, where a staking-linked emission of 5% to 15% a year is normal and the supply ceiling is a policy rather than a number in the code.

And yet HASH reads **+5.92%** while a mid-cycle Bitcoin reads a fraction of a percent. That is the whole lesson of the comparison: a hard cap constrains total supply, not tradable float, and those are different quantities that move independently. HASH is far closer in shape to a recently-launched token working through a four-year investor and team vest than it is to a mature capped chain. The difference from a typical vest is that HASH's is continuous rather than cliff-based, so there is no single dated unlock to trade around — the release is smooth, predictable, and relentless.

The other structural comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns. Those chains offset issuance with a demand-linked removal that scales with usage, and their inflation readings can go genuinely negative. Provenance Blockchain has the same shape of mechanism on paper — an auction burn fed by network and settlement fees — but it is not producing volume: the burn account did not move by a single unit across 90 days. For the burn to matter to this reading, Provenance Blockchain would need settlement activity large enough to burn on the order of **3,402.8M HASH** a quarter, which is more than 5% of circulating supply. That is the gap between the mechanism existing and the mechanism mattering.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the vesting schedule itself, which is the only thing driving the number: it releases roughly **37.8M HASH** a day without interruption and does not end until **May 22 2029**, so absent a governance change the next-90-day reading will land near **+5.91%** again. Second, the auction burn — the single event that could flip this page's sign is a burn large enough to matter, and the burn-holding account is read at every rebuild, so any movement off **21.5M HASH** shows up immediately. Third, Provenance Blockchain governance: proposals passing inside the last window were chain upgrades and a marker-module parameter change, none of them supply-affecting for HASH, but the same vote that switched emission off can switch it back on, and the same vote type that destroyed **5,000M HASH** in **May 2026** can destroy more. Fourth, the community treasury balance, at **40.3M HASH** and climbing on fee income, which is the only pot on this chain with a spender rather than a schedule. Fifth, the largest vesting account at **14,745.5M HASH** — its released portion is now spendable, and where it goes is not on any calendar.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads HASH at **+5.92%** over the trailing 90 days and **+5.91%** projected forward: supply growing, projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is not inflation but unlock — Provenance Blockchain mints nothing, burned nothing, and holds a fixed on-chain ceiling of **95,000M HASH**, while a 2025 governance lockup releases **3,402.8M HASH** per 90 days out of a remaining **37,451.5M** still locked across **448** accounts. The key risk is that this is mechanical and cannot be talked down: it runs at the same rate every day until **May 22 2029** regardless of price, and the auction burn that could offset it produced exactly zero over the window. The ceiling is the one genuine comfort — no vote is needed to keep HASH from being minted, only to start it, and neither the emission module nor the marker has moved.
