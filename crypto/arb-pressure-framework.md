Arbitrum (ARB) is a **fixed-supply** governance token on **Arbitrum One**: ten billion ARB were minted once in March 2023, the Arbitrum protocol has no emission of any kind, and gas on Arbitrum is paid in ETH rather than ARB, so no fee burn exists either. ARB total supply read **9,999,998,977** at both ends of the 90-day window — a move of two hundredths of a coin. And ARB still dilutes: the vesting calendar freed **277.94M ARB** in three monthly cliffs, and a further **145.84M** crossed out of the Arbitrum DAO treasury and the Arbitrum Foundation's locked budget into the counted float. ARB supply pressure reads **+6.35%** against an inflation monitor reading of **+6.76%** — a gap of **0.41 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so the ARB reading ships verified with no data-conflict warning.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Sep 2 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Arbitrum supply pressure at **+6.35%** of circulating ARB, easing to **+5.20%** over the next 90 days. Our inflation monitor reads **+6.76%** for the same window, a gap of **0.41 percentage points** — inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap warning appears on the ARB overview page. The two agree because the flow decomposes exactly: **277.94M** of vesting, **45.12M** released by the Arbitrum Foundation's locked budget and a net **100.72M** out of the Arbitrum DAO treasury sum to 423.78M against a measured float growth of 422.78M, 0.24% apart. The honest label for ARB is **a hard-capped token that is structurally inflationary on its active float**: nothing is created, nothing is destroyed, and the entire reading is a distribution calendar running down against a buy side of exactly zero.

## Sell pressure: where new ARB comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**, and it is worth stating plainly because it is the fact most often mistaken for safety. Arbitrum has never minted a single ARB beyond the genesis ten billion. Read on-chain at both ends of the window, ARB total supply was 9,999,998,977.630224 and then 9,999,998,977.610262 — a fall of 0.019962 ARB, roughly 1,022 below the launch figure and never once above it. The ARB token does carry a live mint function: the Arbitrum DAO upgrade executor may add up to 2% of supply once per year. It has never been used. Because that authority is not renounced, no row on the ARB page can be marked permanently closed.

Vesting unlocks are the whole story, at **277.94M ARB**. The Arbitrum team, contributors and investors hold 44.47% of supply — 4,447M ARB — on a four-year lockup dated from March 2023: a quarter freed at the **Mar 16 2024** cliff, the rest in 36 equal monthly tranches on the 16th. That works out at **92.65M ARB** per tranche, split 56.13M to team and advisers and 36.52M to investors, and three of them landed inside this window on **Jun 16 2026**, **Jul 16 2026** and **Aug 16 2026**. Three more land on Sep 16, Oct 16 and Nov 16 2026. The Arbitrum vesting schedule ends for good on **Mar 16 2027**, after which this row is zero permanently — the single most important date on the ARB page. Arbitrum spreads the tranche across hundreds of per-beneficiary vesting wallets rather than one readable escrow, so the published calendar governs; it was validated against the unvested residual, which comes out at 656.13M ARB against seven remaining tranches worth 648.52M — 1.17% apart.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks carry **145.84M ARB**, and this is where reading Arbitrum on-chain matters most. The Arbitrum DAO treasury fell a net **100.72M**: it sent **230.00M ARB** to the Arbitrum Foundation's operating wallet on **Jun 28 2026** under an approved DAO funding vote, and took **129.28M** back from wound-down programme wallets. The Foundation's own locked budget released a further **45.12M** in three month-end tranches. Both pools sit outside the counted float and the operating wallet sits inside it, so those crossings are genuine supply reaching the market. Long-term locked or bankruptcy is zero: no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution holds ARB, and everything still restricted is already on the calendar above.

## Buy pressure: where new ARB goes

Every buy row on the ARB page is zero, and that is the structural fact behind the reading. The programmatic buyback is zero because Arbitrum has no buyback contract and no approved buyback programme; Arbitrum's core developer company has stated a discretionary corporate plan to add ARB to its own balance sheet, but has never disclosed a wallet, an amount or a date, and capacity is not evidence. Protocol fee burn is zero and was measured rather than assumed: both burn surfaces were read at each end of the window, and the graveyard address held the same **1,533 ARB** at both while ARB total supply moved by two hundredths of a coin. Arbitrum charges gas in ETH, and its sequencer, Timeboost and expansion revenue accrues in ETH and stablecoins, so no fee stream ever touches the ARB token — converting that revenue into ARB purchases would need its own governance vote, and none has carried.

Foundation buy is zero: every large treasury movement traced this window ran outward, from Arbitrum team custody into the counted supply, never the other way. New long-term lock is zero as well. Arbitrum DAO holders approved ARB staking, and it is a real governance win — but the receipt token it hands back is redeemable one-for-one on demand, so staked ARB would never leave the tradable count even once the programme is funded, and the staking contract held **zero ARB** at both ends of the window. A coin with no burn, no buyback and no lock has literally nothing on the buy side of the ledger, which is why the ARB net reading is simply its gross distribution.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled ARB overhangs are tracked. The Arbitrum DAO treasury at **0xF3FC178157fb3c87548bAA86F9d24BA38E649B58** holds **2.56B ARB**, by far the largest ARB holding in existence and outside the counted float, refreshed from chain state. The Arbitrum Foundation's operating wallet holds **330.34M ARB** and is inside the float; it has sent exactly **10.00M ARB** to an exchange deposit relay at the end of every month for twelve consecutive months, most recently **Aug 31 2026** — that transfer is where the booked flow lands, not a second amount stacked on top of it. The Foundation's locked budget wallet holds **109.66M ARB** and drains at roughly 15.04M a month, exhausting around April 2027. The fourth is the unvested team and investor residual of about **656.13M ARB**, which has a published calendar and is already counted as vesting. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ARB compares to other layer-2 governance tokens

ARB belongs to the class of capped, non-emitting layer-2 governance tokens, alongside Optimism's OP and Starknet's STRK, and it is the cleanest example of why a hard cap is not a defence. A halving-model chain with a hard cap, like Bitcoin, dilutes because it mints and shrinks that mint on a fixed schedule. Arbitrum dilutes without minting at all: the ceiling was reached on day one, and the entire supply curve is a custody schedule releasing pre-minted ARB into the float. Structurally that is the opposite risk profile — the pressure has an end date rather than a tail, and for ARB that date is **Mar 16 2027**.

Against exchange tokens that run quarterly buyback-and-burn, ARB is the mirror image. Those tokens have a mechanical bid funded by revenue and a supply that actually falls; Arbitrum generates real revenue but takes it in ETH and stablecoins into the DAO treasury, so the revenue never becomes an ARB bid. Against uncapped continuous-emission layer-1s, ARB looks better on paper and worse in practice this quarter: a 5% annual emission would be a slower drip than the 6.35% ARB moved in 90 days. The difference that matters is direction of travel. An uncapped chain's dilution continues forever; Arbitrum's ends when the vesting calendar does, and what remains after March 2027 is a DAO treasury spending down at a governance-controlled pace rather than a schedule nobody can stop.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The three vesting cliffs are the dated certainties: **Sep 16 2026**, **Oct 16 2026** and **Nov 16 2026**, each freeing 92.65M ARB. The Arbitrum Foundation's locked budget should release again on **Sep 30 2026**, **Oct 31 2026** and **Nov 30 2026** at roughly 15.04M a time, followed within days by the standing 10.00M ARB transfer to an exchange relay — the clearest single signal of Foundation selling on the Arbitrum chain. Watch the Arbitrum DAO treasury balance for any new programme approval: it fell 95.79M over the trailing year, and one governance vote can move a hundred million ARB in a single transaction, as the Jun 28 2026 funding package did. Watch whether ARB staking is actually funded, and whether it is ever given a redemption delay — a liquid receipt is not a lock, and only a locked one would put a number on the ARB buy side. Finally, watch for any proposal to route sequencer revenue into an ARB buyback; that, and not the cap, is the only thing that would change the shape of this page.

## Summary

Arbitrum (ARB) is a fixed ten-billion-supply governance token that has never minted and never burned, and it is nonetheless one of the more inflationary readings in the MrNasdog Pressure Framework at **+6.35%** over the last 90 days and **+5.20%** projected. The mechanism is pure distribution: a **92.65M ARB** monthly vesting cliff running to **Mar 16 2027**, plus DAO treasury and Arbitrum Foundation releases crossing into the float, against a buy side that is zero on all four rows because Arbitrum takes its revenue in ETH and has no burn, no buyback and no lock. The key risk is that the Arbitrum DAO treasury still holds **2.56B ARB** with no schedule at all, so a single governance vote can add pressure the calendar does not predict. The ceiling is real and absolute — ten billion ARB, reached on day one — which means the dilution has an end date, and holders are being paid in patience rather than in scarcity.
