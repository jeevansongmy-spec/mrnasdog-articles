The pressure comes from the vesting calendar instead: **92.65M ARB** is handed to the team and investors on the 16th of every month until **Mar 16 2027**, and the Arbitrum Foundation has sent a further **10M ARB** to an exchange every month for twelve months straight. That is **307.9M ARB** reaching the market over 90 days against a buy ledger of four zeros — no buyback, no burn, no Foundation bid, no new lock. The Pressure Framework reads **+4.66%** net for the last 90 days and **+4.66%** for the next, on a circulating supply of **6.61B ARB**. Our supply monitor reads **+7.30%**, a gap of **2.64 percentage points**, because it also counts a **230M ARB** transfer that never left the project. ARB is a capped, zero-mint token that is still structurally inflationary on its active float.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 13 2026** the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ARB at +4.66% net**, and projects the same **+4.66%** for the 90 days ahead, because both of the mechanisms driving it are monthly and both keep firing. Two rows carry the whole ledger: the published vesting calendar at **277.9M ARB** across the three tranches on **Aug 16 2026**, **Sep 16 2026** and **Oct 16 2026**, and the Arbitrum Foundation's standing monthly exchange transfer at **30.0M ARB**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change in circulating ARB at **+7.30%**, so the gap is **2.64 percentage points** and a **data-conflict chip ships** with the page. That gap has a single identifiable cause, and it is not an error on either side: on **Jun 28 2026** the Arbitrum DAO treasury sent **230M ARB** to the Foundation's own operating safe under the approved 2027 funding vote. The monitor counts that as float; the Pressure Framework does not, because a transfer from one team-controlled wallet to another is not supply reaching the market. The label for ARB is precise: **a hard-capped, zero-mint layer-two token that is still structurally inflationary on its active float**.

## Sell pressure: where new ARB comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and for Arbitrum that is a structural fact rather than a temporary one. The entire **10B ARB** supply was created in a single genesis mint in March 2023, and reading the ARB token contract on Arbitrum One today returns **9,999,998,978 ARB** — fractionally below the original number, because a rounding dust has been burned and nothing has ever been added. Arbitrum does not need to print ARB to pay for itself: the network charges gas in ETH and pays its sequencer out of that revenue, so the token has no issuance role at all. The one caveat worth naming is that the Arbitrum Constitution permits token holders to mint up to **2%** of supply per year by constitutional proposal, first eligible from **Mar 15 2024**. In more than two years of Arbitrum DAO governance, no such proposal has ever executed.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **277.9M ARB** and it is the dominant number on the page. Arbitrum allocated **2.694B ARB** to the team, future team and advisors and **1.753B ARB** to investors, both on a four-year lockup running from **Mar 16 2023**. The first unlock landed a year later on **Mar 16 2024** and released a quarter of the schedule at once; the remaining **3.335B ARB** pays out in thirty-six equal monthly tranches of **92.65M ARB** on the 16th, split **56.13M** to the team side and **36.52M** to investors. Three tranches fall inside every 90-day window, and only eight remain before the final one on **Mar 16 2027**. These tokens are not held in one shared escrow that can be read at both ends of a window — they sit in dispersed per-recipient vesting wallets — so the published Arbitrum calendar is what governs. The accounting cross-check supports it: the unvested team and investor residual reads **705.1M ARB** against **741.2M ARB** still scheduled, a difference of roughly a third of one tranche.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **30.0M ARB**, and this is the row a net-balance test would miss. The Arbitrum Foundation operating safe has sent exactly **10M ARB** into an exchange deposit path every single month for twelve consecutive months, from **Aug 29 2025** through **Jul 31 2026** — **120M ARB** in a year, with no month skipped and no amount varied. Three of those firings fall in each 90-day window. What is deliberately excluded is far larger, and this is where the framework parts company with the raw supply figure: the **230M ARB** the DAO treasury sent to that same Foundation safe on **Jun 28 2026**, and the **43.9M ARB** the Foundation vesting lock released into it over the same window, both stay inside project control and are not booked. So does the **129.3M ARB** that flowed back into the Arbitrum DAO treasury from two wind-down wallets. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: ARB has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution anywhere in its history.

## Buy pressure: where new ARB goes

All four buy rows read **zero**, and the reasons are structural rather than circumstantial. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** because Arbitrum has no buyback contract and no approved buyback programme. The chain does earn real revenue — sequencer fees, the Timeboost priority auction and the Orbit expansion programme all pay the Arbitrum DAO treasury — but that revenue arrives in ETH and stablecoins, and converting it into ARB purchases would require its own governance vote. None has carried. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** for the same reason ARB has no issuance: **ARB is not the gas token**. Users pay Arbitrum in ETH, so there is no ARB fee stream that could be destroyed, and the token supply has never once fallen.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**. Neither the Arbitrum Foundation nor the Arbitrum DAO has disclosed or been observed buying ARB on the open market; across a full year of transfers, the Foundation safe only ever sends ARB outward. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well, and this is the row most often misread. ARB staking is live, but the design is a liquid one — stakers receive a tradable receipt token — and the rewards are funded from chain revenue in ETH rather than from newly minted ARB. Staking therefore neither adds supply nor removes it from the market, and no new escrow or lockup contract was deployed inside this window. The result is an unusually clean asymmetry: every mechanism on the Arbitrum sell side fires on a calendar, and nothing at all fires on the buy side.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled overhangs are tracked for ARB, and together they are much larger than the flow the ledger books. The **Arbitrum DAO treasury timelock** holds **2,556.1M ARB** and is the single largest ARB holder in existence; it is entirely unscheduled, reaching the float only when a governance vote sends it somewhere. The **Arbitrum Foundation operating safe** holds **325.3M ARB** after that **Jun 28 2026** funding transfer, and it is the wallet paying the monthly **10M ARB** already booked in Sell #3. The **Foundation vesting lock** holds **124.7M ARB** and pays roughly **14.6M ARB** a month into that safe on a linear schedule that exhausts during 2027. And **705.1M ARB** of team and investor stock remains unvested, already booked deterministically in Sell #2.

All four are read directly from Arbitrum One on every rebuild. If any of these balances falls to a destination outside the project between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and the distinction that matters is destination, not size. The **230M ARB** move was more than seven times the flow the framework actually books this window, and it counts for nothing here, because it went from the Arbitrum DAO treasury to the Arbitrum Foundation. The **10M ARB** monthly transfer is small by comparison and counts in full, because it goes to an exchange.

## How ARB compares to other layer-two governance tokens

Compared with layer twos that mint, Arbitrum looks structurally superior and is not. Chains whose tokens pay validators or sequencers in new supply run a permanent issuance floor that no schedule ever retires. ARB has no such floor at all — the **10B** genesis supply is fixed, the gas token is ETH, and the only minting authority is a **2%** constitutional ceiling that has sat unused since **Mar 15 2024**. Yet the Pressure Framework still reads **+4.66%** for the next 90 days, higher than many chains that do mint, because inflation here is measured against the active float rather than against total supply. A token that is only **66%** circulating and hands over another **92.65M** every month is diluting its holders on exactly the same arithmetic as a mint, whatever the token contract says.

Compared with its closest structural analogue — the other large Ethereum layer twos with fixed genesis supplies, dispersed monthly investor unlocks and a governance treasury holding the biggest single balance — ARB sits in the middle of the pack on unlock intensity, and near the bottom on offset. The distinguishing feature is what Arbitrum does with the revenue it earns. Exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks convert cash flow into a bid; fee-burning networks convert usage directly into destroyed supply. Arbitrum converts real chain revenue into an ETH-and-stablecoin treasury balance that never touches ARB. That is defensible treasury management and it is also why the buy column is four zeros.

The one genuine advantage over most peers is that the calendar ends. Arbitrum's vesting has a terminal date — **Mar 16 2027** — after which the single largest sell row on this page goes permanently to zero and does not come back. Chains with continuous emission have no equivalent date. For ARB the entire question is what the DAO treasury and the Foundation do once the vesting overhang is gone.

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Arbitrum's ARB at **+4.66%** net over the last 90 days and **+4.66%** over the next, against our supply monitor's **+7.30%** — a **2.64 percentage point** gap caused entirely by a **230M ARB** transfer on **Jun 28 2026** that moved between two project-controlled wallets and never reached the market.
