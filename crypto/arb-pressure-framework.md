## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window opening **Jul 27 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ARB at +4.20% net** forward, matching the **+4.20%** it measured over the trailing 90 days — the vesting calendar is flat, not tapering. Our supply monitor reads **+7.50%**, a gap of **3.30 percentage points**, well outside the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so a monitor-gap chip ships on the ARB overview. The deep walk found the reason on-chain and dated: **230,000,000 ARB** left the Arbitrum DAO treasury timelock on **Jun 28 2026** and landed in the Arbitrum Foundation's own operating safe under the continued-funding vote that closed **Jun 25 2026**. That ARB never left team control, so the framework excludes it while the broader market read counts it as float. Arbitrum is best characterised as **a hard-capped governance token that is structurally inflationary on its active float until March 2027**.

## Sell pressure: where new ARB comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero** on Arbitrum, and the on-chain evidence is unusually clean. The ARB token contract reported **9,999,998,977.630224 ARB** of total supply at the start of this window and the same figure at the end: not one token minted, not one burned, across 90 days. Arbitrum One's sequencer produces no ARB reward, and the network's only ARB creation route is a dormant governance power for token holders to authorise up to **2%** of new supply per year. The Arbitrum DAO has never exercised it and no proposal to exercise it is live, so this row contributes nothing.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the entire ledger at about **277.9M ARB** per 90 days. Team, advisor, contributor and early-investor allocations total **4,447,000,000 ARB**, or **44.47%** of the Arbitrum supply. A one-year cliff on **Mar 16 2024** released a quarter of that stock, and the remaining **3,335,250,000 ARB** unlocks in 36 equal monthly tranches on the 16th, which is **92,645,833 ARB** a month — the figure the market quotes as 92.65M. Three tranches fell inside this window, on **May 16 2026**, **Jun 16 2026** and **Jul 16 2026**, and three more fall inside the next, on **Aug 16 2026**, **Sep 16 2026** and **Oct 16 2026**. The June tranche split **36.52M** to investors and **56.13M** to the team and advisors. Reading the chain independently, the positive 90-day balance changes across the largest ARB wallets, with the Foundation safe and the automated market-maker pools removed, add up to roughly **302.8M ARB** accumulated in beneficiary-type wallets — an upper bound within **9%** of the schedule, which is why the framework ships the schedule figure rather than the wallet sum.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** this window, and the arithmetic behind that zero matters, because a shallower reading would call it large. The Arbitrum DAO treasury timelock fell from **2,656.9M ARB** to **2,556.1M ARB**, a drop of **100.7M**. But the **230M ARB** that left it on **Jun 28 2026** went to the Arbitrum Foundation's own multisig, and the treasury simultaneously took roughly **129.2M ARB** back in from the wind-down of its investment arm, which returns about **143.7M ARB** of surplus capital. Counted as one balance sheet, the three team-controlled wallets — DAO treasury, Foundation operating safe, Foundation vesting lock — held **2,916.9M ARB** at the start of the window and **3,016.1M ARB** at the end. Team-controlled ARB grew by **99.3M**. Nothing crossed into the market, so the row stays at zero and the balances are carried as tracked overhangs instead. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** permanently: no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holds ARB.

## Buy pressure: where new ARB goes

Every buy row on Arbitrum is **zero**, and unlike most tokens that is a structural fact rather than a programme that has lapsed. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is zero because the Arbitrum DAO has never approved one. Arbitrum does earn real revenue: sequencer fees and priority-ordering proceeds accrue to the treasury, and a fee-sharing arrangement routes a share of layer-3 chain revenue back to the DAO. But all of it lands in ETH and stablecoins, and turning any of it into ARB purchases would require its own governance vote, which no proposal has yet carried. Fee income on Arbitrum builds the treasury; it does not bid for the token.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is zero because ARB is not the gas token. Every transaction on Arbitrum One is paid for in ETH, so network activity never consumes a single ARB, and there is no burn address anywhere in the fee path — the identical total-supply reads at both ends of the window are the proof. This is the structural difference between ARB and a layer-1 base asset: on a chain that burns its own base fee, usage growth is deflationary, whereas on Arbitrum usage growth accrues ETH to a treasury and leaves ARB supply untouched. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is zero: no open-market ARB purchase by the Arbitrum Foundation or any ecosystem entity has been disclosed, and no accumulation wallet exists to read; the Foundation is funded in ARB by the DAO rather than buying it. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is zero because staked ARB stays liquid. The staking route issues a transferable receipt token that trades freely and is used as collateral in lending markets, so staking removes nothing from the float, and its rewards are paid out of sequencer revenue rather than in newly issued ARB.

The team-controlled overhang on Arbitrum is the largest single fact about the token, and it is fully enumerable on-chain. The Arbitrum DAO treasury timelock **0xF3FC178157fb3c87548bAA86F9d24BA38E649B58** held **2,556,141,992 ARB** when read this session — more than a third of the entire supply and close to **39%** of the current float — with no release schedule of any kind; it reaches the market only when a governance vote sends it somewhere. The Arbitrum Foundation operating safe **0xD6c8a4E72584f24bd5517AfeD6c01D21477C17f6** held **320,550,890 ARB**, up from **76,988,769** at the start of the window, and that balance is a year of authorised Foundation operating budget waiting to be spent. The Foundation vesting-budget lock **0x15533b77981cDa0F85c4F9a485237DF4285D6844** held **139,449,101 ARB**, down from **183,011,222**, releasing to the operating safe at a steady rate of roughly **43.5M ARB** per quarter and running out during **2027**. Alongside them sits the unvested remainder of the team and investor stock, about **741M ARB** across eight tranches to **Mar 16 2027**, which is already booked deterministically in Sell #2. All three wallets are read directly on-chain every rebuild. If any of these balances falls between refreshes and the ARB leaves team control, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ARB compares to other layer-2 governance tokens

ARB belongs to the layer-2 governance-token class, and that class shares one structural weakness: the token is not the gas asset. On a layer-1 with a base-fee burn, heavy usage removes coins from supply and can flip the asset net deflationary. On Arbitrum, heavy usage produces ETH revenue for the treasury, and ARB supply is completely unaffected — the burn side of the ledger is not small, it is absent. Compared with a rollup token that has already built a fee-to-buyback loop, ARB is a step behind: the revenue exists and the DAO controls it, but no vote has converted it into token demand, so the framework books zero rather than a small positive.

Against a fixed-subsidy halving chain, the comparison flatters Arbitrum on the ceiling and punishes it on timing. A halving chain mints genuinely new coins forever, on a schedule nobody controls; Arbitrum mints nothing at all, so its **10,000,000,000 ARB** is an absolute maximum and the dilution has a defined end date. But it is front-loaded: the entire remaining team and investor unlock, about **741M ARB**, clears in eight tranches to **Mar 16 2027**, which is why ARB reads at **+4.20%** per 90 days today and will read close to zero from the vesting row after that date. Against another uncapped continuous-emission layer-1, Arbitrum is better in the long run and worse right now.

The comparison that actually matters, though, is custody rather than emission. Most large layer-1s hold a foundation reserve worth a few percent of supply; the Arbitrum DAO treasury alone is **2,556.1M ARB**, which dwarfs any single vesting tranche by more than an order of magnitude. The framework books that at zero because it has not moved into the market, and this window it moved the other way. But the honest reading is that Arbitrum's inflation profile after **March 2027** is not set by a schedule at all — it is set by how fast a treasury holding more than a third of the supply decides to spend.
