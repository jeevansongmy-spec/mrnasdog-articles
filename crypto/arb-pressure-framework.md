## Sell pressure: where new ARB comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. ARB has no block reward and no staking reward, because Arbitrum's validators and sequencer are paid in ETH. The ARB token contract does let the Arbitrum DAO mint up to 2% of supply a year, but only by a vote, and no such vote has ever passed. On-chain, total supply sat just under 10B ARB at both ends of the window, about 0.02 ARB lower at the end.

Vesting unlocks are the main source of new ARB: **277.94M ARB** in the last 90 days. Arbitrum's team, contributors and advisors received 2.694B ARB at launch and investors 1.753B ARB, all locked for four years from March 2023. After a one-year cliff on Mar 16 2024, the rest unlocks in equal monthly steps of about **92.65M ARB**, roughly 56.13M to the team and 36.52M to investors. The August, September and October 2026 steps fell inside this window. The November, December and January steps fall inside the next one, so the next 90 days carry the same **277.94M ARB**. After that, two steps remain, in February and March 2027, and then the team and investor schedule is finished.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks add **44.04M ARB**. The Arbitrum Foundation's 700M ARB budget sits in a vesting wallet that releases coins every second from Apr 17 2023 to Apr 17 2027. In these 90 days the Foundation drew 44.04M ARB out of it; about **43.09M ARB** more vests in the next 90 days, and the wallet still holds 95.41M ARB in total.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**: no court estate or seized pile of ARB is waiting to be released. One extra row tracks DAO treasury grants. The treasury paid out nothing in this window, but on Oct 22 2026 an off-chain DAO vote backed adding **100M ARB** to DRIP, the DAO's incentive program, to grow the USDG stablecoin on Arbitrum. The Foundation holds that program's coins, so once the binding on-chain vote passes, expected around Nov 12 2026, the 100M ARB joins the circulating supply. We count it in the next 90 days.

## Buy pressure: where new ARB goes

There is no programmatic buyback of ARB: **0**. Offchain Labs, the company that builds Arbitrum, said in March 2025 that it would buy ARB over time for its own treasury, but it has never published an amount, and coins in a company wallet still count as circulating, so the plan removes nothing from the float.

The protocol fee burn is also **0**. Every Arbitrum transaction pays gas in ETH, and the sequencer and auction revenue flows to the DAO in ETH, so heavy chain use does not destroy ARB. Only about 6 ARB was sent to a dead address in the whole window.

Foundation buying is **0**: the Arbitrum Foundation and the DAO spend ARB, they do not buy it. The last coins to flow back into the DAO treasury were 43M ARB returned by a closed gaming fund on Jul 21 2026, five days before this window opened. A new long-term lock is **0** too. ARB has no staking lock; tokens delegated for governance votes stay in their owners' wallets and still count as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest overhang is the Arbitrum DAO treasury, which holds **2.56B ARB** outside the circulating supply. It moves only when delegates vote. In the past year it sent 230M ARB to the Arbitrum Foundation on Jun 28 2026 to fund another year of work, took back about 129M ARB from a wound-down gaming fund in July, and then stayed flat for the whole window. The next planned outflow is the 100M ARB for the USDG push.

The unvested team and investor stock is about **463M ARB**, five monthly steps from November 2026 to March 2027. The Foundation's vesting wallet holds **95.41M ARB** that finishes vesting on Apr 17 2027. Two more wallets are already part of the float and can sell without adding new supply: the Arbitrum Foundation's main wallet with **324.59M ARB**, and the old gaming fund wallet with 14.59M ARB. We read the treasury, the vesting wallet and the Foundation wallet on-chain at every rebuild; if any of their balances falls between checks, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How ARB compares to other Ethereum Layer 2 tokens

ARB belongs to the class of Layer 2 governance tokens whose chains run on ETH. On Arbitrum, as on Optimism, users pay gas in ETH, so the token gets no fee burn and no automatic buyer from network use. That is the key difference from a Layer 1 coin such as ETH, where every transaction burns part of its fee and offsets new issuance. For ARB, busier blocks raise the DAO's ETH income, not demand for ARB itself.

Inside its own class, ARB's new supply comes from a fixed calendar rather than from a reward stream. Tokens such as STRK on Starknet are also used to pay fees and to stake, so their supply story includes staking rewards. ARB has neither, and its 10B launch supply has never been added to. Its inflation is the slow release of coins minted in 2023: about 92.65M ARB a month to insiders until March 2027, the Foundation's budget until April 2027, and whatever the DAO votes to spend from its 2.56B ARB treasury.

That makes the shape of ARB's supply easier to forecast than most. The insider calendar ends in five months, and from then on the treasury is the only big source of new float, on the DAO's timetable and by public vote.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The binding on-chain vote on the USDG plan, expected to pass around **Nov 12 2026**, decides whether 100M ARB leaves the DAO treasury; if it fails, our next-90-day figure falls to about +4.66%.

The monthly team and investor unlocks of about 92.65M ARB each, around **Nov 16 2026**, **Dec 16 2026** and **Jan 16 2027**, are the steady base of new supply.

The Arbitrum Foundation's draws from its vesting wallet, which still holds 95.41M ARB and runs out on Apr 17 2027, show how fast the Foundation turns its budget into spending.

Any proposal to mint new ARB, start an ARB buyback with DAO revenue, or add ARB staking would change the shape of this ledger; none is on a vote today.
