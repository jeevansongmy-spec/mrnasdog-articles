# GRT Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

The Graph's Ethereum token contract returned exactly **10,800,262,816.048214 GRT** at both ends of the 90 days to **Aug 20 2026** — identical to the eighteenth decimal — which is why most supply counters show GRT as flat. The Graph protocol does not run there. It runs on **Arbitrum**, where indexing rewards minted **69.61M GRT** over the same window, and a token lock on Ethereum that has been described as finished released another **38.75M GRT** in three monthly instalments. Against a protocol burn of just **62,504 GRT** and no buyback of any kind, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads GRT at **+0.99%** net on a circulating base of **10.94B GRT**, against our supply monitor's **+1.23%** — a gap of **0.24 percentage points**, inside tolerance. GRT is best read as **structurally inflationary on a float that its headline contract cannot see**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days ending **Aug 20 2026**, the framework reads **GRT at +0.99% net**: sell pressure of **108.36M GRT** against buy pressure of **0.06M GRT**, on a circulating base of **10.94B GRT**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.23%** for the same window, a gap of **0.24 percentage points**, which is inside the half-point tolerance, so the overview card carries no data-conflict flag. The agreement is worth qualifying rather than celebrating. The tracked float sat pinned near **10.7999B GRT** every single day from **May 22 2026** to **Aug 18 2026** — a range of two hundredths of one percent across three months, while the chain created more than a hundred million GRT — and then stepped up by roughly **142M** in a single day on **Aug 19 2026**. The monitor and the framework land on nearly the same number, but the monitor gets there in one discrete catch-up and the framework gets there by reading the mint. The label the framework gives The Graph is **an uncapped work token whose issuance is invisible on its own headline chain**.

## Sell pressure: where new GRT comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **69.61M GRT**, and every unit of it was created on **Arbitrum**. The Graph pays indexers for indexing subgraphs with newly issued GRT, and since the protocol's migration to Arbitrum the Rewards Manager mints at a fixed rate written into the contract. We read that parameter directly at both ends of the window and it returned **120.73 GRT per block** both times — unchanged, and genuinely the live setting. But a parameter is not a measurement. At **120.73** per block across the **645,531** blocks the window spans, The Graph was scheduled to issue **77.94M GRT**. It actually issued **69.61M**, or **89.3%** of schedule, across **71,714** separate mint events into the protocol's Subgraph Service contract. Indexing rewards only come into existence when an indexer closes and claims an allocation, and a subgraph that nobody has signalled on earns nothing at all, so the shortfall is structural rather than accidental. Shipping the schedule instead of the measurement would have overstated The Graph's issuance by **8.33M GRT** a quarter.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **38.75M GRT**, and this is the row that a supply reader is most likely to get wrong on The Graph. The token lock contract holding the largest single non-circulating position was funded with **1.55B GRT** in December 2020 and is frequently described as spent. It is not. Its balance fell from **723.33M** to **684.58M GRT** across this window, in three releases of **12,916,666.67 GRT** dated **May 28 2026**, **Jun 26 2026** and **Jul 24 2026** — an unbroken monthly rhythm that has run since launch, at half its former quantum since **Dec 18 2025**. Reading the destination matters as much as reading the lock. Each release lands in a safe that holds only about **116,667 GRT**, is forwarded the same day to a second safe holding **0.68 GRT**, and on to a third address holding **0.00 GRT**, which distributes it to market-facing wallets. All three waypoints read essentially flat at both ends of the quarter while **38.75M GRT** passed through them. A balance check alone sees nothing; the transfer log sees the whole thing.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** — no dated release from an identified project wallet landed inside the window. A **97M GRT** project multi-signature wallet held exactly the same balance at both ends and moved nothing. The vesting lock's remaining **684.58M GRT** is the entire supply currently classified as outside the market, and its monthly release rule is already carried in Sell #2 rather than double-counted here. The other very large positions on the Ethereum contract are unlabelled and cannot be attributed to The Graph, so they do not qualify as team-controlled overhang. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** and structurally so: The Graph has never had an estate, a trustee or a court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new GRT goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the only non-zero buy row, and it is **0.06M GRT**. The burn mechanism is real and is written into the protocol in three places: a **0.5%** delegation tax when a delegator delegates GRT, a **1%** curation tax when a curator signals on a subgraph, and **1%** of query fees paid to the network. We swept every burn event on the live Arbitrum token across the window rather than taking the documentation's word for it. The Graph destroyed **62,504 GRT** in 90 days — **48,562** through the payments contract across **24,175** separate events, **13,941** through curation, and under one GRT through staking. That is under **0.1%** of what the protocol minted in the same window. The project's own tokenomics page still says approximately **1%** of GRT supply is burned annually; the measured rate is closer to **0.002%**. At today's query volume the burn is a rounding error against issuance, and the framework follows the measurement.

Buy #1, a programmatic buyback, is **0**. The Graph does not buy GRT back. Its tokenomics documentation describes issuance and burns and never mentions a buyback, no purchase contract exists on either chain, and nothing in governance authorises one. A third-party page advertising a GRT buyback and burn programme is not published by The Graph and has no on-chain counterpart; it earns no value in this ledger. Buy #3, a foundation buy, is **0** — no dated open-market purchase by the project landed inside the window. Buy #4, a new long-term lock, is **0**, and the reasoning here is the one most often fudged elsewhere. GRT delegated to indexers stays inside the counted market supply and can be withdrawn after a thawing period, which makes staking custody rather than a lock. The direction removes any remaining argument: the protocol's staking contract held **2.05B GRT** at the end of the quarter against **2.09B** at the start, a fall of **37.19M GRT**. Delegation shrank; it did not absorb.

## Foundation and overhang

The Graph's tracked overhang is unusually simple, because almost all of it is one contract. The vesting lock holds **684.58M GRT** and is refreshed by direct chain read at every rebuild; on current classification that balance is the entire non-circulating bucket, which the market's own supply figure puts at **671.68M GRT** — a difference of exactly one monthly release, which is what you would expect if the classifier tracks the lock with a short lag. Behind it sits a **97M GRT** project multi-signature wallet, unchanged at both ends of the window and also refreshed by chain read, and two opaque positions: The Graph Foundation's own treasury and the community treasury overseen by The Graph Council, neither of which publishes a wallet address, so both are watched through official disclosure on a fortnightly walk rather than on-chain. Neither disclosed a release inside this window. The bridge escrow on Ethereum holds **2.84B GRT**, but that is not overhang at all: it is the Ethereum-side backing for GRT that already exists and trades on Arbitrum, and counting it would double-count the entire Arbitrum float. If either the vesting lock's balance or the multi-signature wallet's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and in the lock's case, the monthly release it is already known to make is carried in Sell #2 instead.

## How GRT compares to other uncapped work tokens

GRT belongs to the uncapped work-token class: tokens issued continuously to pay the operators who do a network's actual work, with no ceiling written into the protocol. Compared with a hard-capped chain, the structural difference is that there is no terminal supply to converge on and no halving to look forward to — issuance is a governance parameter, and The Graph's parameter has been **120.73 GRT per block** throughout this window. What separates GRT from most of its class is the gap between the parameter and the realisation. A staking chain that mints on a schedule delivers essentially all of its scheduled issuance; The Graph delivered **89.3%**, because rewards are claim-driven and signal-gated. That is a genuine structural discount on paper inflation, and it is only visible if you measure the mint rather than multiply the rate.

The comparison that matters more is with fee-burning networks. The Graph is often grouped with tokens whose usage burns supply, and the mechanism does exist — three separate taxes, all destroying GRT. But a burn only offsets issuance if the underlying activity is large enough. On a base-fee-burning L1, burn scales with block space demand and can exceed issuance in busy periods. On The Graph, burn scales with delegation events, curation signals and query fees, and at current volumes it removed **62,504 GRT** against **69.61M** minted — a ratio of roughly one to eleven hundred. The Graph reads structurally closer to an uncapped emission network with a nominal burn than to a fee-burn deflationary asset, and the framework scores it that way.

The third structural comparison is with multi-chain tokens generally. GRT sits on Ethereum and Arbitrum, and the two legs are neither a mirror nor simply additive. Ethereum's supply was flat to the eighteenth decimal; Arbitrum's supply actually **fell** by **27.61M**; and the Ethereum bridge escrow that backs the Arbitrum float moved by **81.10M**. Reading only Ethereum reports zero inflation. Reading only Arbitrum reports deflation. Summing them double-counts the bridge. Only netting the escrow — and then accounting for the withdrawals burned on Arbitrum but not yet released on Ethereum — produces the true figure of **69.55M GRT** of genuinely new supply. That reconciliation is the single most important thing to get right on this token.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The token lock's next monthly release is expected around **Aug 21 2026**, with two more due around **Sep 18 2026** and **Oct 16 2026**; recent firings have run at roughly 28-day spacing rather than a calendar month, so a fourth **12.92M GRT** release could pull forward into the window. The issuance parameter is the second watch line: **GIP-0076**, the Issuance Allocator, was discussed in March 2026 and would split issuance across distribution targets, which changes where new GRT lands without changing how much is created. Third, watch whether the realised-to-scheduled ratio holds near **89%** — a rise toward full delivery would add roughly **8M GRT** a quarter of sell pressure with no parameter change at all. Fourth, watch query volume, because the burn only becomes material if delegation, curation and query fees rise by two orders of magnitude. Fifth, watch how the market's tracked float behaves after its **Aug 19 2026** step: if it resumes sitting frozen, the monitor and the framework will diverge again inside the next quarter.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads GRT at **+0.99%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 20 2026**: **108.36M GRT** of sell pressure against **0.06M GRT** of buy pressure on a **10.94B GRT** circulating base. The structural mechanism is an uncapped work token that mints **120.73 GRT** per block on Arbitrum and realises **89.3%** of that schedule, alongside a vesting lock that still holds **684.58M GRT** and pays out **12.92M** every month. The key risk is that both sources are live and neither has an offset: The Graph has no buyback, its three-part burn removed only **62,504 GRT** in the quarter, and delegated stake shrank rather than absorbed. There is no supply cap to converge on, so the only ceiling on GRT's inflation is a governance decision to lower the issuance parameter — and that parameter has not moved.
