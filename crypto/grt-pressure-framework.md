# GRT Inflation Analysis · July 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

The Graph mints new GRT every Ethereum block to pay indexers, and almost nothing takes it back. Indexing-reward issuance is fixed in the rewards contract at **120.73 GRT per block** — about **78.2M GRT** over the next 90 days — against a query-fee and curation burn of roughly **0.1M GRT** and no buyback at all, leaving the framework at **+0.72%** net. GRT is uncapped: there is no halving, no supply ceiling and no scheduled end to the issuance.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window opening **Jul 25 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **GRT at +0.72% net** forward, after a measured **+0.87%** over the trailing 90 days. Our supply monitor reads only **+0.04%**, a gap of **0.83 percentage points** — well outside the 0.5-point tolerance, so a **⚠ monitor-gap chip** ships on the overview. The gap has a single, verifiable cause: the monitor's supply series follows the Ethereum GRT contract, whose total supply sat unchanged at **10,800,262,816** from **Apr 25 2026** to **Jul 24 2026**, while every unit of indexing-reward issuance is minted on the Arbitrum deployment instead. Read across both layers — Ethereum supply, minus the tokens locked in the bridge escrow, plus the Arbitrum supply — the real global GRT float grew from **11,479,622,954** to **11,573,364,860** over the same window, an increase of **93.7M GRT**. The Graph is therefore **structurally inflationary on an uncapped work token**, not the neutral asset a single-contract supply read implies.

## Sell pressure: where new GRT comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the whole story, at about **78.2M GRT** per 90 days forward and **93.8M GRT** measured over the trailing window. The Graph pays indexers newly minted GRT for indexing subgraphs, and the rate is set on-chain rather than by discretion: the Arbitrum rewards contract reports issuance of **120.73 GRT per Ethereum block**, which over 648,000 blocks in 90 days is **78,233,040 GRT**, or roughly **317.5M GRT** a year — the protocol's long-stated 3% target. The Ethereum rewards contract now reports zero, confirming that the entire issuance stream has migrated to layer 2. The trailing window ran hotter than the fixed rate because indexers mint accrued rewards in batches when they close allocations, and a batch landed inside it; across 180 days the measured growth of **154.2M GRT** matches the fixed rate to within 2%, which is why the framework projects the contract's 78.2M forward rather than the lumpy trailing figure.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**. The December 2020 genesis tranches covering early team, advisors, backers and the public sale have run out their multi-year schedules, and the unlock tracker shows no scheduled release left for GRT. The Graph Foundation's own allocation still moves on a long calendar, but the circulating denominator already counts that supply, so those transfers hand over existing GRT rather than creating any. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is also **zero**, because no dated outflow from any team-controlled wallet was observed inside the window; the overhang is enumerated and monitored rather than projected. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is zero: no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holds GRT.

## Buy pressure: where new GRT goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**. The Graph has never run a treasury buyback, and no official channel has announced one; claims of a "GRT buyback and burn" circulating on third-party blogs do not originate from the project. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is the only live offset, at roughly **0.1M GRT** per 90 days. Two burns remain: **1%** of every query fee, and a **1%** curation tax when curators signal on a new subgraph. Both are genuinely live but tiny at today's usage — network fees ran about **4.9M GRT** across the window, so the query-fee burn removes near **0.05M**, and curation signal grew only **0.7M GRT**, so its tax adds less again. A third burn, the **0.5%** delegation tax, was removed outright by the Graph Horizon upgrade, cutting the burn side further. Reading supply across both layers bounds the total burn at under **1.2M GRT** per 90 days — under 2% of issuance either way.

Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**: no discretionary open-market purchase of GRT by the Foundation or the core development company was disclosed in the window. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**, and the direction actually runs the other way. Staking GRT is not a lock: indexers and delegators can thaw and withdraw, and they did, with the two staking contracts holding **27.3M GRT** less at the end of the window than at the start. So the newly minted indexing rewards are not being re-absorbed into stake — they are leaving it.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled blocks sit behind GRT. The largest is the **Graph Foundation** allocation, about **19.6%** of the genesis 10B supply, held for grants, core development funding and ecosystem programmes on a decade-long calendar with no published wallet-level release schedule. Second is the **Edge & Node** core-team allocation at roughly **7.7%**, likewise without a public per-wallet calendar. Third are the **Graph Council** multisigs that govern the protocol, read on-chain this session at **116,667 GRT** on Ethereum and **zero** on Arbitrum — signing keys, not a treasury, and far too small to move the ledger. None of the three fired a dated, quantified outflow inside the window, so Sell #3 carries a projected value of zero; the enumeration exists to define what is being watched, not to invent a forward number. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The Foundation and Edge & Node blocks are re-walked on the web roughly every two weeks; the Council multisigs are read directly on-chain.

## How GRT compares to other uncapped work tokens

GRT belongs to the class of **uncapped work tokens with usage-linked burns** — assets whose supply expands to pay a service provider network and contracts only when demand for that service is high. The design intent is self-balancing: issuance pays indexers, and query fees, curation and delegation taxes burn supply back out, so heavy usage is meant to make GRT deflationary. That is the same shape as an uncapped continuous-emission layer 1 with a fee burn, where the burn genuinely can outrun issuance during congestion. The difference is scale of demand. The Graph's burn is fee-proportional at 1%, and its query-fee revenue is currently measured in tens of thousands of dollars a quarter, so the burn recovers well under 1% of what the protocol mints. Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, GRT has neither a ceiling nor a scheduled step-down: the 120.73 GRT per block continues indefinitely until governance changes it.

The closer structural comparison is to an exchange token that buys back and burns from revenue. Those assets convert real cash flow into supply reduction and frequently run net deflationary; GRT has no buyback at all, so the only mechanism pointing down is the fee burn. Compared to a governance token with no emission — where supply growth comes purely from vesting and stops when the calendar ends — GRT is the opposite case: its unlocks are finished, but its emission never ends. That is the key thing an inflation lens should extract. GRT's dilution is not a temporary vesting overhang that runs out in 2027; it is a permanent, protocol-level mint that only shrinks in relative terms if the network's query volume grows by orders of magnitude, or if a governance proposal cuts the issuance rate directly.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the issuance rate itself: proposals filed **Mar 6 2026** would route about **6 GRT per block** — roughly 5% of issuance — from the rewards manager into a recurring-agreement manager funding on-chain indexing agreements, cutting the rewards stream from **120.73** to **114.73 GRT per block**. The on-chain read on **Jul 25 2026** still shows the full 120.73, so nothing has activated yet; if it does, the split changes but the total issuance does not. Watch the Issuance Allocator contract, whose whole purpose is to let governance divide issuance across targets — it is the mechanism through which any future rate cut would land. Watch the chain-deprecation process, which stops paying rewards for networks that no longer show sustained query volume, since that is the one live path to a materially lower effective issuance. Watch query-fee volume as the Horizon-based subgraph service, Substreams and the token data service roll out through 2026: the burn is 1% of fees, so it takes a very large revenue increase to matter. And watch the Foundation and Edge & Node allocations for any dated, quantified deployment, which would move Sell #3 off zero.

## Summary

GRT is an uncapped work token whose supply grows by protocol design and has no scheduled end. Indexing-reward issuance is fixed on-chain at **120.73 GRT per Ethereum block** — about **78.2M GRT** per 90 days, or **317.5M** a year — against a query-fee and curation burn of roughly **0.1M** and no buyback whatsoever, leaving the framework at **+0.72%** net forward and **+0.87%** measured over the trailing window. Our supply monitor reads **+0.04%** because it follows only the Ethereum contract, which has been flat since **Apr 25 2026** while all issuance mints on Arbitrum — a **0.83-point** gap that ships a ⚠ chip and, once corrected, shows real supply across both layers rising **93.7M GRT** in 90 days. The key risk is that the balancing mechanism The Graph was designed around has not engaged: the burn is proportional to query fees, and query fees are currently far too small to offset the mint. Vesting is finished and the Foundation overhang is quiet, so the entire inflation story is the emission — and only a governance change to the issuance rate, or a step-change in query demand, will alter it.
