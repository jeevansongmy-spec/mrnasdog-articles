> Originally published at **[mrnasdog.com/research/tao/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/tao/inflation)** by MrNasdog.

Bittensor (TAO) issued **323.4K TAO** of block emission over the last 90 days and took only **3.7K TAO** back through registration recycling, the single destruction channel the protocol has. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+2.88% net** on an issued base of **11.26M TAO** against a **21M** hard cap that is barely half mined, with **+2.79%** projected forward. Bittensor is a fair-launched, hard-capped chain that is still structurally inflationary, because the cap is a ceiling in the distance rather than a constraint today.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **May 24 2026** to **Aug 22 2026**, the framework reads **Bittensor at +2.88% net inflation**: **327.5K TAO** of new issuance — **323.4K** of block emission plus a one-off **4.2K** accounting step — against **3.7K TAO** destroyed. That sell figure is a direct measurement rather than a model: Bittensor's own issuance counter moved from **10,934,758 TAO** at block **8,251,926** to **11,258,600 TAO** at block **8,898,384**, read at both ends on a public archive node. The independent supply monitor reads **-0.11%** over the same window, a gap of **2.98 percentage points**, wide enough that a monitor-gap flag ships with this page. The cause is identified and cannot be closed at source: the classified public supply figure the monitor consumes has been pinned near **9.60M TAO** every single day since **Aug 2025**, while the chain issued **1.44M TAO** in that time. Bittensor is best labelled a **hard-capped chain that is structurally inflationary on the active float**.

A word on the denominator, because it decides the headline number. This page divides real on-chain flow by the chain's own issued supply of **11,258,600 TAO** rather than the frozen public figure of about **9.60M**. Four conditions justify that, and all four were re-verified this rebuild: the public field has held **9.59M** to **9.60M** every sampled day for thirteen months; the chain moved **1.44M TAO** while the field did not; Bittensor has no non-circulating bucket at all, because it was fair-launched with zero allocations and the chain carries no vesting entries whatsoever, so the network's own definition of circulating supply is free TAO plus staked TAO — exactly what the issuance counter returns; and an independent classifier publishes **11.25M** circulating at **53.61%** of the cap against the chain's **53.612%**. On the frozen figure the same flows would read **+3.37%**, the higher and less honest number.

## Sell pressure: where new TAO comes from

Bittensor has one mint and only one. Sell #1, protocol inflation, booked **323.4K TAO**. The chain pays **0.5 TAO** per block, roughly **3,591 TAO** a day, to the miners and validators of its subnets. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** and always will be: Bittensor launched in **Jan 2021** with no premine, no presale, no ICO and no allocation to founders, investors or a foundation, so no vesting contract exists to unlock — a claim this build checked on-chain rather than assumed, and the vesting storage on the chain is empty. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**: there is no foundation allocation and no reserve pool, because none was ever created. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: no estate, no trustee, no escrow anywhere in the asset's history. Sell #5 carries a one-off **4.2K TAO**, and it is worth a paragraph of its own.

The emission figure above is measured, not scheduled, and on Bittensor those two numbers do not agree. Three parameters look authoritative and are all wrong to trust. The block time is nominally twelve seconds; measured across the whole window from the chain's own block numbers at both ends, Bittensor produced **646,458** blocks in 90 days, an actual **12.03** seconds a block, **99.76%** of the nominal rate. The stored emission setting on the chain still reports **1.0 TAO** a block, but realised issuance sampled block by block clusters at **0.4955** with a hard ceiling of **0.4992** — the halving is live and the parameter is stale. And the halving itself is a supply-height event, not a calendar one: it fired in **Dec 2025** when issuance crossed **10.5M**, cutting daily emission from **7,200 TAO** to **3,600 TAO**, and the next one waits at **15.75M** — years out at the observed pace. No halving falls inside either the trailing or the forward window.

The one-off matters because it looks exactly like supply and is not. At block **8,283,785** on **May 28 2026**, the issuance counter stepped up **4,161 TAO** in a single block, with a steady **0.5** either side of it. The runtime was upgraded at that exact block, and Bittensor's general balance counter — which until then had been tracking only unstaked TAO — was rewritten in the same block to equal the network's issuance total. The subnet pools and the registration locks did not move. It is an accounting correction, booked because the published supply genuinely changed, and excluded from the forward column because it cannot repeat. Reading the general balance counter instead of the network's own issuance counter would have made this window look like **+212%** inflation.

## Buy pressure: where new TAO goes

The Bittensor buy ledger is nearly empty, and what is in it is small. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: the protocol takes no revenue cut and holds no treasury, so nothing could fund a buyback and no contract exists that could execute one. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **3.7K TAO**, and it is the only real destruction channel Bittensor has. Registration fees are recycled: the TAO is subtracted from the issuance total itself and returned to the unissued pool, where later emission can pay it out again, which is why each halving arrives a little later than a straight-line schedule would predict. That destruction leaves no footprint at any dead address — reading one returns zero — so this figure was summed from the chain's own per-subnet recycling counters at both window ends across all **129** subnets. It comes to **0.03%** of supply, and the pace eased slightly after the **Aug 4 2026** upgrade.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**: no entity holds a mandate or a budget to buy TAO on the open market, and the lean non-profit that develops the software has never announced or executed an accumulation programme. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**, and this is the row most likely to be booked wrongly. Staked TAO rose **86.6K** over the window to **7.40M**, which looks like a lock and is not one: unstaking on Bittensor is instant, with no bonding period and no exit queue, and the supply base this page divides by already counts free and staked TAO as one float. Booking it would remove supply that was never added. Subnet registration locks rose **5.2K** over the same window, but the issuance counter never fell when they were paid, so that TAO moved into a subnet's own pool rather than out of supply. Both are carried as overhangs on the sell side instead.

## Foundation and overhang

Bittensor has less overhang than almost any coin the framework covers, because there is nothing to overhang with. There is no team allocation, no investor allocation and no foundation allocation, so the classic sell-side risk — a large identified wallet with a schedule or a discretion — does not exist here. The **9.74M TAO** still unmined beneath the cap is the only unreleased supply, and no entity can hand it out early; block emission is the sole release mechanism, at a rate the protocol fixes. Two smaller overhangs are tracked. The first is subnet registration locks, holding **29.9K TAO** across **128** subnets and up **5.2K** over the window, refundable to a subnet owner if that subnet is deregistered; it is read directly on-chain every rebuild. The second is the non-profit that develops the software, which funds itself from validator operations and has never published a treasury address, so its balance is opaque and monitored through official disclosure on a bi-weekly walk. If either of those balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How TAO compares to other hard-capped halving chains

Bittensor deliberately copies Bitcoin's monetary shape: a **21M** hard cap, a fixed block subsidy, and a halving. The difference that matters for a supply reading is where each chain sits on its own curve. Bitcoin is roughly **95%** mined and four halvings deep, so its issuance is a thin residual — under **1%** a year — and the cap functions as a live constraint. Bittensor is about **54%** mined and one halving deep, so the same monetary design produces something like **12%** a year of new supply. The cap is real; it is simply not yet doing any work. That is the single most misread fact about TAO: a hard cap is often quoted as if it implied scarcity today, when what it implies is a known ceiling on issuance that has not yet been approached.

Against a fair-launched proof-of-work peer such as Kaspa, the structural similarity is close — mining as the only mint, no premine, no treasury, an empty or near-empty buy ledger — but the delivery differs. Kaspa steps its reward down every month, so its forward figure falls continuously; Bittensor holds a flat reward between rare, supply-triggered halvings, so its forward figure is flat until it steps in half. Against an uncapped, continuously-inflating proof-of-stake L1, Bittensor's advantage is that its issuance is bounded and its dilution schedule is knowable to the block; its disadvantage right now is that the rate is comparable to, or higher than, many of them. And against an exchange token running a quarterly buyback-and-burn, Bittensor has no offset at all: registration recycling removes **0.03%** of supply a quarter, roughly a hundredth of the emission it is set against, and it does not permanently destroy anything — recycled TAO returns to the pool to be emitted again.

One genuinely Bittensor-specific mechanism is worth naming, because it makes the realised number diverge from the scheduled one. Emission is split across subnets by their own token prices under the dynamic-subnet rules, and withheld or emission-disabled shares are redistributed rather than paid. Measured across this window, realised gross issuance came to **100.04%** of the scheduled figure overall — but split at the **Aug 4 2026** upgrade it was **100.5%** before and **98.1%** after. Realised emission below the published schedule is a real, measurable property of this chain, not a rounding artefact, and it is why the forward column is built on the post-upgrade run rate rather than on the block reward.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether the post-upgrade issuance rate holds. The **Aug 4 2026** Root Reborn upgrade shipped its headline curation layer gated off, and enabling it in a later runtime would change how root-stake yield is realised — the framework re-bases the forward column on the observed rate, so a second step would move the projection again. Second, subnet registrations: each one recycles TAO out of issuance and adds to the locked pool, and the recycling rate eased after **Aug 4 2026**; a burst of new subnets would push Buy #2 up. Third, the runtime version itself — it moved from **402** to **448** inside this single window, and on an upgradeable Substrate chain the **21M** cap is enforced by code that governance can replace, so every rebuild re-checks it rather than treating it as permanent. Fourth, the frozen public supply figure: if the upstream classifier ever unfreezes it, the monitor gap on this page closes overnight and the headline percentage shifts with it. Fifth, the next halving at **15.75M** issued — nowhere near this window, but the recycling mechanism pushes it further out every quarter.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Bittensor at **+2.88% net inflation** over the 90 days to **Aug 22 2026**, projected at **+2.79%** forward — **323.4K TAO** of block emission against **3.7K TAO** of registration recycling, measured on-chain at both ends of the window rather than taken from a published parameter. The structural mechanism is Bitcoin's: a **21M** hard cap and a supply-triggered halving that fired in **Dec 2025** and cut the block reward to **0.5 TAO**. The key risk is that the cap is doing no work yet — only **54%** of TAO has been mined, the buy ledger removes about a hundredth of what the mint adds, and nothing on this chain permanently destroys a coin. The ceiling is real and knowable; the dilution between here and it is real too, and it is roughly **12%** a year at the current reward.
