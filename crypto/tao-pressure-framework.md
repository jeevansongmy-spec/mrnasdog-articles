Bittensor mined **331.1K TAO** over the last 90 days at the halved block reward of **0.5 TAO** per block, and took only **10.7K TAO** back — **4.6K** recycled through registration fees and **6.1K** newly locked in subnet registrations. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+2.86% net** on an issued base of **11.22M TAO**, with **+2.81%** projected forward. Our supply monitor reads **-0.07%**, a **2.93-point** gap that ships with a warning chip, because the monitor's upstream supply feed has been frozen since **Aug 6 2025**. Bittensor is a fair-launched, hard-capped chain whose float is still being mined open.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **May 12 2026** to **Aug 11 2026**, the framework reads **Bittensor at +2.86% net inflation**: **331.1K TAO** of new issuance against **10.7K TAO** absorbed. The sell figure is a direct measurement, not a model — Bittensor's own issuance counter moved from **10,894,013 TAO** to **11,220,510 TAO** across the window, read at both ends on a public archive node. The monitor reads **-0.07%**, leaving a gap of **2.93 percentage points**, wide enough that a monitor-gap flag ships with this page. The cause is identified and cannot be closed at source: the classified supply figure the monitor consumes has been pinned near **9.60M TAO** every single day since **Aug 6 2025**, while the chain issued **1.62M TAO** in that time. Bittensor is best labelled a **hard-capped chain that is structurally inflationary on the active float**: the ceiling is fixed at **21M**, but barely half of it has been mined, and the mining continues every twelve seconds.

A word on the denominator, because it decides the headline number. This page divides real on-chain flow by the chain's own issued supply of **11,220,510 TAO** rather than the frozen public figure of about **9.60M**. Four conditions justify that, and all four were re-verified this rebuild: the public field has held **9.59–9.60M** every day for a full year after rising normally before that; the chain moved **1.62M TAO** while the field did not; TAO has no non-circulating bucket at all, because the network's own definition of circulating supply is free TAO plus staked TAO, which is exactly what the chain read returns; and an independent classifier publishes **11,220,395.6 TAO** against the chain's **11,220,509.6** — **0.001%** apart. On the frozen figure the same flows would read about **+3.34%**, which is the higher and less honest number.

## Sell pressure: where new TAO comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, booked **331.1K TAO**, and it is the only sell row on the page that carries a number. Bittensor issues TAO exactly one way: block emission. The subsidy started at **1 TAO** per block and halved to **0.5 TAO** per block in **December 2025**, which is roughly **3,600 TAO** a day. Two independent angles agree on the quarter. The published schedule gives **324.0K TAO** across the window's **648,000** blocks; the chain's measured issuance delta, net of recycling, gives **331.1K TAO** gross. The 2% spread is expected, because the amount of TAO actually injected into each subnet depends on that subnet's own token price under the dynamic-subnet rules, and Bittensor reverted to a price-based emission split in **June 2026**. That change landed inside the window, so the forward column is re-based on the run rate observed since: **3,569 TAO** a day of net issuance over the last thirty days, or **325.8K TAO** gross across the next quarter — **+2.81%**.

Every other sell row is structurally zero, and for Bittensor that is a genuine feature rather than a data gap. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because there is nothing to vest: the network was fair-launched with no premine, no team allocation and no investor allocation, so the only bucket in the Bittensor cap table is mining rewards, at **100%**. No cliff exists to fire, this quarter or ever. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** for the same structural reason — there is no foundation allocation and therefore no treasury wallet holding TAO for later release. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: no bankruptcy estate and no trustee distribution is associated with the asset.

## Buy pressure: where new TAO goes

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, booked **4.6K TAO**, and the mechanism deserves precision because it is routinely described as a burn when it is not. TAO spent on neuron registration, subnet registration and transaction fees is **recycled**: it is subtracted from Bittensor's issuance counter and returned to the unissued pool, where the protocol can emit it again later. The effect is not a shrinking supply; it is a delayed halving, because Bittensor's halvings trigger on issuance thresholds rather than block heights. The recycle counters across all subnets moved from **384,610 TAO** to **389,169 TAO** over the window — **4.6K** against a **331.1K** mint, or about one and a half percent.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, booked **6.1K TAO**. Registering a subnet on Bittensor costs a lock, and that TAO sits with the protocol, untradable, until it decays away; the locked balance summed across all subnets rose from **24,074 TAO** to **30,183 TAO** over the quarter. Ordinary staking is deliberately not counted here — Bittensor's own definition puts staked TAO inside circulating supply, and stake can be withdrawn, so booking it would manufacture absorption that no holder experiences. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: no contract buys TAO. Mature subnets do route surplus emission into buying their own subnet token on their own pool, but that pressure lands on the alpha token, not on TAO. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**, because there is no foundation treasury to buy with.

## Foundation and overhang

Bittensor is one of the few assets in this catalogue with no team-controlled overhang at all. There is no Opentensor Foundation allocation, no labs treasury, no DAO wallet and no identified group-controlled multisig holding TAO — the network launched with none, and the core codebase itself moved to the Rao Foundation on **Jul 2 2026** without any accompanying token grant. The **9.78M TAO** that remains unissued is not an overhang either: nobody holds it, and it can only enter existence block by block on the halving schedule, which is already fully counted in Sell #1. Two balances are worth naming, and both are small. The subnet registration lock holds **30,183 TAO** at the last refresh, read directly on chain each rebuild. The foundation's own operating account holds an amount too small to register against an **11.22M** float — it has no allocation behind it, no disclosed release programme, and it has only received across the window. The share subnet owners earn is paid in subnet alpha rather than TAO, and has been auto-locked on arrival since **May 13 2026** under the Conviction rules. If the subnet-lock balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How TAO compares to other hard-capped mined chains

Bittensor belongs to the Bitcoin family of supply designs: a fixed **21M** cap, a fair launch with no premine, and a block subsidy that halves on a rule no vote can quietly rewrite. Against Bitcoin itself, the structural difference is maturity, not design. Bitcoin has mined roughly **95%** of its cap, so its remaining issuance is a rounding line against the float. Bittensor has mined about **53%**, so the same halving mathematics produces **+2.86%** of supply growth a quarter instead of a fraction of a percent. A hard cap tells you where inflation ends; it does not tell you what inflation is today, and TAO is the clearest illustration of that gap in the catalogue.

The second difference is the halving trigger. Bitcoin halves every **210,000** blocks, a date anyone can calculate years ahead. Bittensor halves when issuance crosses the midpoint of the remaining supply, and recycling subtracts from that count — so heavy subnet registration activity literally pushes the next halving further into the future. That makes the TAO emission schedule endogenous to network usage in a way Bitcoin's never is. Against uncapped continuous-emission networks, Bittensor looks conservative: those chains mint indefinitely at a governance-set rate, while TAO's ceiling is protocol-encoded. And against fee-burn chains, the comparison is the sharpest of all — Bittensor has no permanent burn whatsoever. Recycled TAO comes back. There is no path by which this network's supply shrinks.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the supply feed itself. The classified circulating figure the wider market reads has been frozen near **9.60M TAO** since **Aug 6 2025** while the chain reached **11.22M**; the day that feed is repaired, every headline market capitalisation and inflation figure for TAO jumps by roughly **17%** overnight with nothing having changed on chain. Second, the emission split. The price-based subnet allocation shipped in **June 2026** and the **v4.3.1** release followed on **Jul 18 2026**; both change how much TAO is injected per block in practice, and the daily issuance rate is the check. Third, the Conviction rollout — subnet-owner rewards have been auto-locked since **May 13 2026**, and any extension of that locking from alpha to TAO itself would create a genuine Buy #4 line. Fourth, the recycle rate: it currently offsets one and a half percent of the mint, and only a step change in subnet registration activity would make it matter. Fifth, the pending United States spot-product decisions expected around **Aug 2026** — they move demand rather than supply, but a listed vehicle that stakes would lock TAO in size.

## Summary

Bittensor is a fair-launched, hard-capped AI network whose entire supply comes from mining, and the framework reads it at **+2.86% net** over the last 90 days — **331.1K TAO** issued against **10.7K TAO** recycled and locked. The structural mechanism is a halved block subsidy running against an almost empty buy side: nothing on Bittensor destroys a coin permanently, so recycling only postpones the next halving rather than shrinking the float. The key risk is that the cap flatters the picture — with barely half of the **21M** mined, TAO carries Bitcoin's promise on a schedule that still adds nearly three percent of supply a quarter, and the frozen public supply feed has been hiding exactly that from the market for a year. The ceiling is real and permanent at **21M**; the distance still to travel is the whole story.
