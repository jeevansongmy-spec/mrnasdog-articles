# HTX Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

HTX DAO cannot issue another HTX — the deployed TRON contract has no mint, burn, owner or upgrade instruction anywhere in its live code — and the Pressure Framework reads HTX at **−0.83%** over the trailing 90 days and **−0.83%** over the next 90. All of that comes from one mechanism: a quarterly burn funded by half of HTX exchange revenue, which sent **7,474,935,439,560.55 HTX** to an address with no key on **Jul 15 2026**. Sell pressure is **0**, buy pressure is **7,474.94B HTX**, and the ceiling is a hard **999,990,000,000,000 HTX** that no code path can raise — or lower.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **898,232,728,634,017 HTX**, the framework books **0** of sell pressure and **7,474.94B HTX** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **−0.83%** — and projects **−0.83%** for the next 90 days on one dated firing. The inflation monitor reads **−1.02%** for the same window on a single snapshot and **−0.91%** on seven-day medians at both ends, a gap of **0.19** and **0.08** percentage points respectively. Both sit inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so this page ships with no data-conflict warning. The label for HTX is **a fixed-supply exchange token whose float can only fall**: HTX DAO cannot mint, has no unlock calendar to run down, and runs one mechanism that moves the number at all — and that mechanism only ever points one way.

## Sell pressure: where new HTX comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is provable rather than promised. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**. The HTX contract on TRON answers exactly eleven ordinary token calls — name, symbol, decimals, balances, transfers, allowances — and carries no mint instruction, no owner, no upgrade path and no pause switch. A flat supply reading on its own would prove nothing, so the framework checked whether the field could move at all: the ceiling value lives in writable storage rather than being welded into the contract's code, which makes the flat reading a real measurement, and every place the contract writes to storage sits in the balance and allowance paths. There is no delegated call and no self-destruct either, so no proxy hides a mint. HTX is a token on TRON, not a chain with a block reward, so nothing is paid to validators in HTX.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0**. The entire supply was assigned when HTX DAO deployed the token in 2023, across seven allocation buckets, and no cliff date for any bucket has ever been published — not in HTX DAO's own writing, not on any unlock tracker. This is not a schedule that quietly expired; it is a schedule that was never written. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** for a structural reason the overhang section below sets out. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well: HTX has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new HTX goes

Into an address nobody holds the key to. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **7,474.94B HTX**. HTX DAO commits half of the HTX exchange's quarterly revenue to taking HTX off the market, and it fired once inside this window: **7,474,935,439,560.55 HTX**, worth about **$13.60M**, on **Jul 15 2026**. That number is not lifted from the announcement — the announcement and the chain agree to the last decimal. The framework read both destruction surfaces at both ends of the window, and they disagree, which is the finding rather than an error: the dead address rose by the full amount while the count of HTX in existence never moved, because the contract has no destroy instruction and the burn has to be done by mailing coins away instead. A build that watched only the supply figure would have booked this coin's entire buy side at zero and shipped an inflationary page.

Two further checks matter. First, the burn settles on TRON and only on TRON: HTX DAO's published transaction hash returns a successful transfer at TRON block **84,487,580** and returns nothing at all on Ethereum or BNB Chain, where mirrored HTX also exists. Second, every HTX ever sent to that dead address was listed — **sixteen** transfers since 2024 — and they sum to its balance of **101,757,271,365,982.78 HTX** exactly, so nothing is missing from the count and nothing is doubled. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**: HTX is not chain gas, so no transaction anywhere pays a fee in HTX, and the burn most write-ups mean is the one above, counted once. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** — the executor wallet held nothing before the firing and nothing after, so nothing accumulates. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: HTX DAO staking pays about five percent from an internal reserve but its receipt token cannot be traded, its unstaking wait is zero days, and the coins never leave the counted float.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on HTX is enormous and the reason it books at zero is arithmetic, not charity. Subtract the dead address's **101,757,271,365,982.78 HTX** from the **999,990,000,000,000 HTX** that exist and the answer is **898,232,728,634,017 HTX** — the circulating figure this page divides by, to the last decimal. The dead address is therefore the **only** bucket held outside the count. No reserve sits outside it, so no reserve-to-market release can register as inflation here at all.

That same identity settles the harder question, which is where the burned coins came from. Traced by sender rather than by destination, the July firing came out of a reserve wallet holding **230,974,428,784,003.81 HTX** — **25.7%** of circulating — which has funded every quarterly tranche since 2024 and paid **7,480.0B HTX** down the chain that day through an operational wallet and a single-use executor. So the destruction is provable and the open-market purchase is not visible on chain. It still counts, and this is the whole point: because that reserve is inside the circulating figure, burning from it genuinely lowered the tradable float. Had HTX's circulating figure excluded the reserve, the same transaction would have removed nothing and this row would be zero. Also watched: an operational wallet at **397.31B HTX** dispatching roughly **70B HTX** a week to reward programmes, a wallet holding exactly **19,999.80B HTX** — two percent of the ceiling — untouched for over two years, about **16,030B HTX** pledged in a liquidity position whose receipt tokens were destroyed, and **7,014.43B HTX** mirrored on Ethereum and BNB Chain that the circulating figure does not count at all. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HTX compares to other exchange tokens

HTX belongs to the class of exchange tokens that offset supply with a revenue-linked burn, and inside that class it sits at the strict end on issuance. A chain-native exchange token typically mints on every block and burns fee revenue against that issuance, so its reading is a race between two live flows. HTX has no issuance leg at all — not a decaying one, a missing one — which is a harder commitment than a halving schedule and stricter than any continuous-emission layer one, where the ceiling is a policy rather than a number in the code.

The comparison that actually matters is with tokens whose burn is funded by protocol usage rather than by a corporate reserve. A fee burn scales with transactions and is paid by whoever pays the fee; HTX's burn is a discretionary corporate commitment funded from a wallet the project already controls, and its size tracks exchange revenue rather than network activity. That shows in the series: **$23.31M** in January, **$19.22M** in April, **$13.60M** in July — three consecutive declines, and the token counts fell with them, from **13,616.37B** to **10,825.40B** to **7,474.94B HTX**. The mechanism is real and it is delivering, but it is a business result, not a protocol constant, and it can be reduced by a decision in a way a base-fee burn cannot.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the firing itself, dated **Oct 15 2026** by HTX DAO and the only event in the forward window; the framework sizes it at the last measured quantum of **7,474.94B HTX**. Second, the size of that firing, which is the single biggest source of error on this page: holding revenue flat at **$13.60M** and buying at today's price would destroy nearer **8,063.77B HTX** and read **−0.90%**, while another quarter of falling revenue would put it nearer **5,650B HTX** and **−0.63%** — so **−0.83%** is the measured middle of a real range, not a point forecast. Third, the reserve wallet at **230,974,428,784,003.81 HTX**, which is read at every rebuild; it holds roughly thirty more quarters of burn fuel, and where any of it goes other than the dead address is on no calendar. Fourth, the **$10M** ecosystem fund HTX DAO announced on **Sep 3 2026** on a milestone-delivery basis, with no asset named and nothing on chain yet. Fifth, the liquidity-pledge programme, dormant since 2024 and confirmed dormant this window by HTX DAO's own running total, which rose by exactly the size of the July burn and nothing more.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads HTX at **−0.83%** over the trailing 90 days and **−0.83%** projected forward: supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking. The structural mechanism is not scarcity by promise but scarcity by code — HTX DAO holds a contract that can neither mint nor destroy, so the **999,990,000,000,000 HTX** ceiling is arithmetic, the float can only fall, and the one thing that moves it is a quarterly burn funded by half of exchange revenue. The key risk is that the burn is a corporate decision rather than a protocol rule, and it has shrunk three quarters running with revenue. The genuine comfort is the one-way ratchet underneath: nothing HTX DAO can do adds a coin, the reserve it burns from is already counted in the float, and every coin it has sent to the dead address is gone for good.
