HTX, the governance token of HTX DAO and the HTX exchange, is one of the few assets the Pressure Framework covers where the entire sell side is **four zeros**. The token was minted once, at **999,990,000,000,000 HTX**, and its code contains no function that can create another coin. The only moving part is a quarterly buyback funded by half of the exchange's revenue, which destroyed **7,474,935,439,560 HTX** on **Jul 15 2026** — **0.83%** of the **898.23 trillion HTX** still alive. The framework therefore reads **−0.83%** net supply pressure over the last 90 days and projects **−0.83%** forward on the burn due **Oct 15 2026**. Our supply monitor reads **−0.26%** for the same window, a **0.57 percentage point** gap that the deep walk traced to the monitor's own reading noise rather than to any HTX supply event.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 11 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads HTX DAO at **−0.83%** net supply pressure: **zero** new HTX against **7.47 trillion HTX** bought and destroyed. It projects the same **−0.83%** forward, because the buyback is quarterly, the next firing is dated **Oct 15 2026**, and nothing on the sell side can change between now and then. Our supply monitor reads **−0.26%**, so the gap is **0.57 percentage points** and the page carries a monitor-gap flag. The deep walk dated the cause precisely: the graveyard address that receives every HTX burn took in exactly one transfer inside the window, the **Jul 15 2026** burn, and its balance of **101.76 trillion HTX** reproduces the classified circulating figure to six significant figures — while the monitor's own last six readings, from **Aug 5 2026** to **Aug 10 2026**, span **−1.02%** to **−0.26%** around a midpoint of **−0.71%**, moving roughly **0.4%** between snapshots on a supply the chain shows as motionless. HTX is a **revenue-metered deflationary exchange token**: it cannot inflate, and how fast it shrinks is a direct function of how much the exchange earns.

## Sell pressure: where new HTX comes from

Nowhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and unlike most zeros in this framework it is provable in a single read. The HTX token exposes exactly eleven functions — the standard transfer, approval and balance calls, plus name, symbol and decimals — and not one of them creates a coin. There is no mint entrypoint, no owner entrypoint that could add one, and the total supply figure the contract returns has been **999,990,000,000,000** without interruption since the token was issued on **Oct 31 2023**. HTX is not a blockchain and pays no block reward or staking yield; it is a token issued on three general-purpose chains, and it inherits none of their issuance.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because no unlock calendar exists. HTX DAO's published tokenomics divides the genesis supply into seven buckets — Early Contributors and Public at **19%**, a Community Access Program at **19%**, Platform Development at **17%**, Partnership and Collaboration at **15%**, and Developer Grant, Research and Development and Ecosystem Supporter at **10%** each — but attaches no cliff dates to any of them. The migration from the exchange's previous token completed long before this window, and both unlock trackers walked for this build publish no dated release for HTX at all. Nothing is scheduled to hit the market in either the trailing or the forward window.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** on observed flow; the section below walks what is being watched. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** because HTX DAO has no estate. No trustee holds HTX, no court-supervised distribution is running, and nothing is being liquidated into this market on a schedule outside the DAO's control.

## Buy pressure: where new HTX goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, removes **7.47 trillion HTX** over 90 days and is the entire supply story of this asset. HTX DAO commits **50%** of the exchange's quarterly revenue to HTX which it then destroys, firing around the 15th of January, April, July and October. Because the token has no burn function, a burn is executed as a transfer into a graveyard address that nobody holds the keys to — which makes the entire programme auditable to the token. That address held **101.76 trillion HTX** when this build read it on **Aug 11 2026**, and subtracting it from the genesis supply gives **898.23 trillion HTX**, exactly the circulating figure this page divides by. Its inbound log carries one transfer inside the window: **7,474,935,439,560 HTX** on **Jul 15 2026**, worth about **$13.6 million** at the day's price.

One caveat the chain adds to that label. The executing wallet was funded that same morning by two platform wallets and forwarded everything on **61 minutes** later, so this is an internal transfer of revenue rather than a visible sweep of the open market. It ends the same way — the coins are gone — but it is worth knowing that the destruction is the verified part, not the purchase.

The forward projection is the one judgment call on this page, and the direction is what matters. The last three firings step down in a straight line — **13.62 trillion** in January 2026, **10.83 trillion** in April 2026, **7.47 trillion** in July 2026 — so the four-quarter average of **11.26 trillion** would forecast a burn the programme is no longer producing. This build projects **7.47 trillion** for **Oct 15 2026**, the most recent realised quantum, which is the conservative read on the buy side. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**: HTX runs on other people's blockchains and consumes no network fee of its own, so counting a fee burn would double-count the buyback. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**, and the reason is mechanical rather than a lack of activity — HTX routed into the burn reaches the graveyard address the same day, so no treasury balance accumulates that could later be sold. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**: the DAO's liquidity-pledge programme, which pairs HTX with another asset on a decentralised exchange and then destroys the ownership receipts, has been dormant since late 2024.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled positions are enumerated and watched, and none of them moved this window. The first is the **liquidity-pledge position**: roughly **16.03 trillion HTX**, the difference between the **117.79 trillion** HTX that HTX DAO reports as burned and pledged and the **101.76 trillion** actually sitting in the graveyard address. Those coins are inside a trading pair whose ownership receipts were destroyed, which makes the position permanent — but they are still counted inside the circulating figure, so they are worth naming rather than hiding. The second is a wallet holding **20.0 trillion HTX** that was funded in a single transfer on **Feb 7 2024** from the address that executed that year's burn, and has not moved a coin since; more than two years of stillness means no pattern to project, so it carries no forward value. The third is the pair of burn execution wallets, both at **zero HTX** because they are pass-throughs.

Deliberately excluded: the exchange's own custodial balances, including one wallet holding about **230.97 trillion HTX**, because those coins belong to depositors rather than to the DAO, and an unlabelled distribution wallet of about **20.23 trillion HTX** paying out in small chunks, because an unidentified holder cannot be read as a unit. Every watched address is re-read at each rebuild. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh rather than being smoothed into a forecast.

## How HTX compares to other exchange tokens

HTX belongs to the exchange-token class, where supply is set by a company's revenue rather than by a chain's consensus, and inside that class it sits at the purest end. The dominant design in this group is a quarterly buy-and-burn funded by a share of trading fees, and the variable that separates one from another is what happens to the tokens after they are bought. HTX destroys them in the same quarter, which makes the reduction final and leaves no treasury balance to worry about later. Exchange tokens that buy and **hold** instead move coins out of the tradable float without removing them from supply, so a framework measuring supply reaching the market has to treat the held pile as reversible — a distinction that is invisible in a headline burn number and decisive in a ledger.

Against a fee-burning blockchain the contrast is about what meters the burn. A base-fee burn shrinks supply in proportion to blockspace demand, automatically, with no company in the loop and no vote to hold. HTX's burn is metered by exchange revenue instead, which is why the last three quarters step down: the mechanism is working exactly as designed while producing less, because the business behind it produced less. And against a capped proof-of-work chain the difference is direction of travel. A halving-model chain still issues, just more slowly; HTX cannot issue at all, so its supply curve has only one direction and the only open question is slope. That is a genuinely rare property, and it is also the reason this page's risk is a business risk rather than a token risk.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The Q3 2026 buyback-and-burn is scheduled for **Oct 15 2026** and is the single event that decides the next reading; a fourth consecutive step down would drag the projection below **−0.83%**, while a rebound toward the January 2026 quantum of **13.62 trillion** would push it past **−1.5%**. Watch the burn announcement's cumulative figure, currently **117.79 trillion HTX** burned and pledged, because the split between what is destroyed and what is merely pledged is what separates a permanent reduction from a locked position. Watch the **20.0 trillion HTX** wallet dormant since **Feb 7 2024**: any outflow converts a watched overhang into live Sell #3 pressure at the next refresh. Watch HTX DAO governance for any proposal that changes the **50%** revenue share or revives the dormant liquidity-pledge programme — the proposals passed so far in 2026 have governed asset listings, not supply. And watch for the first sign of an unlock calendar appearing where none exists today; there is currently no scheduled release of any size.

## Summary

HTX is a token that cannot grow. Its contract has no mint entrypoint, its **999,990,000,000,000** coins were all created on **Oct 31 2023**, and **101.76 trillion** of them are already in a graveyard address, leaving **898.23 trillion HTX** alive. The only mechanism that moves supply is a quarterly buyback funded by half of exchange revenue, which took **7.47 trillion HTX** off the market on **Jul 15 2026** and is due to fire again on **Oct 15 2026**, giving a framework reading of **−0.83%** now and **−0.83%** projected against a monitor reading of **−0.26%**. The key risk is not dilution, which the contract makes impossible, but deceleration: three consecutive quarters of smaller burns mean the deflation rate is set by exchange earnings, and a weaker quarter simply shrinks the supply more slowly. The ceiling is fixed forever and the floor is whatever the business can fund.
