HTX DAO runs one of the cleanest supply structures in crypto: the HTX token was issued in full at launch against a fixed **999.99T** ceiling, the contract has no mint, and the number on chain has not risen by a single unit since Oct 2023. That leaves exactly one supply event — a quarterly burn funded by half of Huobi HTX exchange revenue. One burn landed in this window, **7.47T HTX** destroyed on **Jul 15 2026**, worth roughly **$13.6M**, against a sell side of **zero**. The Pressure Framework reads HTX at **−0.83% net** per 90 days versus our supply monitor at **−1.04%**. The catch is not the direction, it is the trend: the burn has fallen from 13.62T to 10.83T to 7.47T in three quarters, and it is provably funded from platform-held coins rather than from visible open-market buying.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending Jul 27 2026, the Pressure Framework reads **HTX at −0.83% net**. Sell pressure totals **zero HTX**, buy pressure **7.47T HTX**, against a circulating base of **898.23T HTX**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change at **−1.04%**, a gap of **0.21 percentage points**, which is inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships on the HTX overview. The reconciliation is unusually tight because the HTX supply obeys an exact identity: the fixed ceiling of 999,990,000,000,000 minus the cumulative 101,757,271,365,982.79 HTX sent to the chain's burn address equals the published circulating figure of 898,232,728,634,017 to within a hundredth of a token. Nothing else moves HTX supply. The right label for HTX is a **fully-issued exchange token that is deflationary by revenue burn, at a rate that falls with revenue**.

## Sell pressure: where new HTX comes from

It does not come from anywhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** and the reason is structural rather than discretionary: the HTX contract on Tron carries a fixed supply of **999,990,000,000,000** tokens, all issued at genesis in Oct 2023, and that figure read identically at both ends of this window. There is no block reward, no staking emission curve and no mint function for HTX DAO governance to turn on. Even the burn does not touch it — HTX burns are transfers to the chain's black-hole address rather than contract-level destruction, which is why the raw supply constant never changes while the tradable count falls. HTX DAO launched a staking product on **Mar 16 2026**, issuing a non-transferable receipt called sHTX and quoting yields of up to 10% a year, and it launched governance vote pools worth around 10 billion HTX, roughly 0.001% of supply. Neither creates HTX. Both pay out of coins that already exist and are already counted, so neither changes the denominator this framework divides by.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because HTX DAO never built an unlock calendar. The token reached holders through a conversion from the exchange's previous token plus a six-month airdrop to TRX and USDT holders that ran from Feb 2024 and finished in **Jul 2024**, drawing on a fixed 1.5% pool. There is no cliff, no linear stream and no lock contract with a published release date, so nothing is scheduled to unlock in the next 90 days. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero**, though this is the row that carries the real long-term risk. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: HTX DAO has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution, and the only genuinely locked bucket — the liquidity pledge — can only ever sit still.

## Buy pressure: where new HTX goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the entire ledger. HTX DAO commits **50%** of quarterly Huobi HTX platform revenue to retiring HTX, and it fires mid-month once a quarter. The window Apr 28 to Jul 27 2026 contains exactly one firing: **7,474,935,439,560 HTX** arrived at the burn address on **Jul 15 2026**, worth about **$13.6M** at the prevailing price. The framework books the burn the chain confirms, not the burn the announcement promises — and it deliberately excludes the previous burn of **10.83T HTX** on **Apr 15 2026**, which falls thirteen days outside this window. That discipline matters here, because the two figures differ by 45% and reaching for the larger one would have flattered the reading.

The provenance of those burned coins deserves a straight answer, because for an exchange token it is the difference between a bid and an accounting entry. Tracing the transfers, the wallet that executed the burn held nothing before **Jul 15 2026** and nothing after: it was funded that same morning out of an HTX platform wallet, passed the coins through an operating wallet, sent 7.47T to the black hole and returned to zero. HTX DAO's own announcement surface describes the burn as executed by its governance committee against quarterly revenue rather than through open-market purchases. So the retirement is real against the counted supply — those coins were classified as circulating and are now permanently unspendable — but no order-book buying is visible on chain. The deflation is genuine; the price support implied by the word "buyback" is not evidenced.

The remaining buy rows are **zero**. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero because HTX is a plain token on its host chain with no transaction tax and no automatic fee destruction; the quarterly revenue burn is already counted once in Buy #1 and counting it twice would double the deflation. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero because no HTX DAO entity has disclosed a separate open-market purchase. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero: staking does hold coins behind a waiting period, but the receipt is redeemable and HTX DAO has published no staked total, so there is no defensible figure. A fifth row tracks the dormant liquidity pledge, which contributed nothing this window.

## Foundation and overhang

HTX carries a large, quiet overhang and it is worth naming precisely. The genesis allocation split the fixed supply across programme buckets — a developer grant at 10%, research at 10%, ecosystem support at 10%, partnership and collaboration at 15%, and platform development at 17% — none of which has a published release schedule and none of which discloses a per-bucket wallet address. Because the supply feed counts every non-burned token as circulating, these coins are already inside the denominator, so a release would not change the headline percentage at all; it would simply move coins from a DAO wallet onto the market with no visible signal. That is the single biggest reason to keep watching HTX rather than trusting the burn headline. Two smaller overhangs are readable on chain: the platform wallet that funds each quarterly burn holds around **0.55T HTX** and is refreshed each cycle, and the two wallets that execute the burn ended the quarter at zero. The staking reward pool is undisclosed. Separately, the difference between the announced cumulative figure of **117.79T** burned-and-pledged and the on-chain burned figure of **101.76T** is roughly **16.03T HTX** pledged into a paired liquidity pool whose pool receipts were burned — locked forever, but never destroyed, and still counted in supply. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HTX compares to other exchange tokens

HTX belongs to the exchange-token class, where the defining mechanism is a revenue-funded burn rather than an emission schedule, and within that class it sits at the purest end. Several large exchange tokens run quarterly burns against a fixed or capped supply, and the structural question for all of them is identical: is the burn funded by real revenue, and does it retire coins that were genuinely tradable? HTX answers the first question transparently — the burn is 50% of quarterly platform revenue and the token count prices out to the announced dollar figure to within rounding. It answers the second question less cleanly, because the burned coins arrive from platform-held wallets on the morning of the burn rather than from visible market purchases. An exchange token whose buyback wallet accumulates coins bought off the order book over weeks leaves a very different on-chain trace than one whose burn wallet is funded and emptied in a single day.

The contrast with the DeFi model is sharper still. Protocol tokens that run buybacks typically buy and **hold**, parking the purchased supply in a treasury multisig where it stays re-deployable by a future vote and stays counted as circulating. HTX destroys, which is irreversible and shows up immediately in every supply feed — a strictly stronger claim, and the reason HTX prints a clean negative number while buy-and-hold tokens print a gap against their own supply monitors. And the contrast with issuance-driven chains is the starkest of all: a proof-of-stake network with a 4% emission has to burn 4% of supply a year merely to reach flat, whereas HTX starts at flat by construction because nothing can be created, so every burned coin is pure downside on the supply curve. The trade-off is that HTX has no protocol demand of its own. Its supply reduction is a direct function of one exchange's trading revenue, which is why the burn shrank from 13.62T to 10.83T to 7.47T across three quarters as market activity cooled. A capped chain's scarcity survives a bear market; an exchange token's burn does not.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q3 2026 burn, which HTX DAO has scheduled for **Oct 15 2026** and which is the only supply event inside the forward window; the framework projects it at the latest realised quantum of 7.47T rather than the larger earlier quarters, so a burn above that figure would improve the reading and a fourth consecutive decline would weaken it. Second, the trend line itself — three quarters of falling burns, from 13.62T in Jan 2026 to 10.83T in Apr 2026 to 7.47T in Jul 2026, imply exchange revenue running roughly 40% below where it was at the start of the year, and a fourth data point in October will tell you whether that has bottomed. Third, any movement out of the undisclosed genesis programme buckets, which would be pure sell pressure with no change to the headline percentage. Fourth, whether HTX DAO ever publishes the staked HTX total, which would let a real long-term-lock figure enter the buy column instead of a zero. Fifth, any change to the 50%-of-revenue rule itself, which is a governance decision and the one thing that could switch the mechanism off entirely.

## Summary

HTX is a fully-issued exchange token with no mint function, no unlock calendar and no emission, so its supply can only fall. In the 90 days to Jul 27 2026 it fell once, by 7.47T HTX destroyed on Jul 15 2026 out of a circulating base of 898.23T, leaving the Pressure Framework at −0.83% net against our supply monitor at −1.04% — a 0.21 percentage-point gap that is inside tolerance and needs no explanation beyond derivation noise. The structural mechanism is a quarterly burn funded by half of Huobi HTX platform revenue, and it is genuinely deflationary rather than cosmetic. The key risk is that it is also genuinely cyclical: the burn has shrunk for three straight quarters as revenue has fallen, and the coins it destroys come from platform-held wallets rather than from visible market buying, so the supply effect is real while the demand effect is unproven. The ceiling is the reassuring part — 999.99T can never be exceeded, roughly 10.2% of it is already destroyed, and the only way that number moves is down.
