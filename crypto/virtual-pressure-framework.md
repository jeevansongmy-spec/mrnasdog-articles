# VIRTUAL Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Virtuals Protocol cannot create another VIRTUAL and cannot destroy one either — the token sits exactly on its hard **1,000,000,000** ceiling, the owner role that guards the mint function was renounced to the empty address, and the contract has no burn function at all. The Pressure Framework still reads VIRTUAL at **+0.29%** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.05%** over the next 90, and every point of that comes from one mechanism: a set of 2024 investor streams held in a lock contract, which released **1.93M VIRTUAL** into the market this window and has only **0.36M** left, all of it on streams that end by **Oct 24 2026**. Sell pressure is **1.93M VIRTUAL**, buy pressure is **0**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **658.4M VIRTUAL**, the framework books **1.93M VIRTUAL** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+0.29%** — and projects **+0.05%** for the next 90 days on what is left in the lock contract. The inflation monitor reads **+0.16%** for the same window, a gap of **0.13 percentage points**, which is inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so this page ships with no data-conflict warning. The label for VIRTUAL is **a finished token with one unfinished lockup**: the supply itself is closed, and the only thing still moving is the last of an investor release that runs out inside the next window.

## Sell pressure: where new VIRTUAL comes from

It does not come from minting, and on Virtuals Protocol that is a stronger statement than usual. The Ethereum contract that issues VIRTUAL reported a supply of **1,000,000,000** at both ends of the window, identical down to the last wei, and its own ceiling is set to the same **1,000,000,000** — so the cap is not headroom, it is already fully spent. A mint function does exist in the compiled code, and it is a live function rather than a leftover stub: calling it returns the contract's own unauthorised-account error. But the role allowed to call it is the empty address, which no person can ever hold and no transaction can ever originate from, so that path is closed permanently rather than by policy. The framework also checked that this flat reading is a real measurement and not a compiled-in constant, by overriding the supply storage slot in a simulated call and watching the reported supply change to the overridden value. So Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and it is a measured zero.

The entire supply story is Sell #2, vesting unlocks, at **1.93M VIRTUAL**. A group of two-year investor streams opened in 2024 sits inside a third-party lock contract on Ethereum that pays out continuously as each stream matures. That contract held **2.29M VIRTUAL** at the start of the window and **0.36M** at the end, across 25 separate withdrawals, and the framework books the realised outflow rather than the calendar entitlement — a token that has vested on paper but never left the contract is not sell pressure yet. Enumerating every stream in the contract that holds VIRTUAL returns twelve, and the sum of what they still owe matches the contract's live balance to a single wei, so nothing outside that list is still holding VIRTUAL. Four of those streams reached their end dates and emptied in August 2026, inside the window, which is exactly why the forward number is so much smaller than the trailing one: the mechanism that produced most of the trailing figure has already stopped.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**. Virtuals Protocol's ecosystem treasury is real and it is large — **340.65M VIRTUAL** in a DAO-controlled multi-signature wallet — but it read the same balance at both ends of the window on two independent readings, and its complete history is seven transfers, the last of them in **May 2024**. More than two years without a movement is not a cadence, so the row is enumerated as an overhang and valued at zero. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well: VIRTUAL has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new VIRTUAL goes

Nowhere, this window, and the reason is the single most misread thing about Virtuals Protocol. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. The protocol does run a buyback-and-burn funded by trading fees, and it is what most write-ups lead with — but it buys the agent tokens launched on the platform and destroys those. VIRTUAL is the currency being spent, not the asset being bought. No wallet anywhere accumulates repurchased VIRTUAL, and none was announced or executed inside this window.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is also **0**, and the framework verified it from both directions rather than trusting either alone. The addresses where tokens go to be destroyed were read at both window ends: the main Ethereum dead address held **1,498 VIRTUAL** on both dates, the zero address held none at all, and the Base copy moved by roughly a tenth of one coin. The count of VIRTUAL in existence was unchanged to the wei. On Ethereum those two surfaces genuinely are independent, because the contract has no burn function at all — sending to a dead address is the only visible form a burn could take, and it did not happen at any size that matters. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**: the treasury bought nothing, and its balance did not move by a single unit in either direction.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** twice over. The vote-locking contract that issues veVIRTUAL held **23.56M VIRTUAL** at both ends of the window, so no net new locking happened. And it would not have counted in any case, because VIRTUAL is the pair asset for every agent token launched on Virtuals Protocol, and all of that liquidity — the agent pools, the vote-lock, the bridge pools, the exchange wallets — sits inside the classified tradable float. Only two addresses sit outside it, both of them on the sell side of this page. Locking more VIRTUAL moves it between pockets inside the float rather than out of it.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on VIRTUAL is short, completely enumerated, and unusually easy to check, because the non-circulating bucket reconciles exactly. Total supply minus the ecosystem treasury's **340.65M VIRTUAL** minus the lock contract's remaining **0.36M** reproduces an independent classifier's published circulating supply to the wei. That arithmetic is what makes the rest of this page measurable rather than arguable: there is no third hidden bucket, so every other VIRTUAL balance on any chain is already float.

The dominant item is the treasury itself — roughly a third of the entire ceiling in one multi-signature wallet, governed by a rule that caps releases at a tenth of the pot a year and requires a governance vote first, and refreshed from the chain at every rebuild. The second is the investor lock contract, which is draining and finishes in October 2026. There is no buyback accumulation wallet to track, because the buyback does not buy VIRTUAL, and there is no bankruptcy estate residual. The trigger sentence applies to both items: if the treasury's balance or the lock contract's balance falls between refreshes by more than the schedule accounts for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How VIRTUAL compares to other fixed-supply application tokens

VIRTUAL belongs to a small class: application tokens with a hard on-chain ceiling that is already fully minted and an owner role that has been given away. That is a stricter commitment than a halving schedule. A halving-model chain like Bitcoin still mints on every block, just at a decaying rate, so its inflation reading stays positive on a known clock. Virtuals Protocol mints nothing at all, and cannot start — the difference between a small number and a structural zero. It is also stricter than the typical uncapped Layer-1, where a staking-linked emission of several percent a year is normal and the ceiling is a policy rather than a number in the code.

And yet VIRTUAL reads **+0.29%** while a mid-cycle Bitcoin reads a fraction of a percent, which is the whole lesson of the comparison: a hard cap constrains total supply, not tradable float, and those are different quantities. In shape VIRTUAL is much closer to a recently-launched token working through an investor vest than to a mature capped asset — with one difference that matters more than the others. This vest is about to run out. After **Oct 24 2026** there is no unlock schedule left at all, and the only supply that could ever reach the market again is a treasury that has not moved since May 2024.

The other structural comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns, whose readings can go genuinely negative because a demand-linked removal offsets issuance. Virtuals Protocol looks like it belongs in that group and does not. Its buyback is real and its burn is real, but both are pointed at the agent tokens rather than at VIRTUAL, so the mechanism that would make this page deflationary is structurally aimed somewhere else. For VIRTUAL to read negative, Virtuals Protocol would have to add a burn function to a contract that has none, or start spending the treasury on open-market repurchases — a governance decision, not a fee flow.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the lock contract, which is the only thing driving the number: it holds **0.36M VIRTUAL** and the last of its streams ends on **Oct 24 2026**, after which the mechanism is finished and the forward reading should fall to zero. Second, the ecosystem treasury at **340.65M VIRTUAL** — the one pot on this token with a spender rather than a schedule, and the only thing that could make a future reading large. Third, Virtuals Protocol governance, because the treasury's own ten-percent-a-year release cap and its vote requirement are the constraint, and a vote is what would change them. Fourth, the buyback: if a proposal ever redirects fee revenue into repurchasing VIRTUAL instead of agent tokens, Buy #1 stops being zero. Fifth, the cross-chain legs — the Base, Solana and Robinhood-chain copies are mirrors backed by locked collateral, so they move supply between chains rather than creating it, and the escrow balances are read at every rebuild to confirm that stays true.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads VIRTUAL at **+0.29%** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.05%** projected forward: mixed flows, supply roughly steady. The structural mechanism is not inflation but unlock — Virtuals Protocol mints nothing, burned nothing, and sits exactly on a fixed on-chain ceiling of **1,000,000,000 VIRTUAL** with the mint role renounced, while one 2024 investor lockup released **1.93M VIRTUAL** this window and has **0.36M** left. The key risk is not the lockup, which ends on **Oct 24 2026**; it is the **340.65M VIRTUAL** ecosystem treasury, which has not moved since May 2024 but is released by a vote rather than by a calendar. The ceiling is the genuine comfort: no decision is needed to keep VIRTUAL from being minted, and there is no decision available that would start it.
