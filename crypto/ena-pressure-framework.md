# ENA Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Ethena never mints ENA — the token contract on Ethereum held total supply at exactly **15,000,000,000 ENA** at both ends of the 90 days to **Aug 20 2026** — and yet ENA is one of the most inflationary assets the Pressure Framework tracks. The reason is the vesting calendar: **801.6M ENA** crossed from locked allocation into tradable float over the window, in three monthly steps of **267.2M** on the 5th, shared between core contributors, investors, the Ethena Foundation and the ecosystem development fund. Against that, Ethena runs no burn, executed no buyback, and the staking wrapper that receives the protocol's revenue share pays out **zero ENA**. On a circulating base of **9,828.1M ENA** the framework reads **+8.16%** net against our supply monitor's **+8.88%** — a gap of **0.72 percentage points** that resolves entirely to the base date, so no monitor-gap flag ships. ENA is **structurally inflationary by unlock calendar rather than by issuance**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 20 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ENA at +8.16% net**: sell pressure of **801.6M ENA** from the Ethena vesting calendar against buy pressure of **0**, on a circulating base of **9,828.1M ENA**. Our supply monitor reads **+8.88%** for the same window, a gap of **0.72 percentage points**. That gap is fully accounted for and raises no flag: both sides are measuring the identical **801.6M** release, but the monitor divides it by the supply as it stood 90 days ago (**9,026.6M**), giving **8.88%**, while the framework divides it by today's larger float (**9,828.1M**), giving **8.16%** — a difference of **0.72** points, equal to the observed gap to four decimal places. Nothing is unexplained. ENA is best labelled **a fixed-supply token that is structurally inflationary on the active float, with an empty buy side**.

## Sell pressure: where new ENA comes from

It does not come from issuance. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and that is measured rather than assumed: the ENA contract at **0x57e114b691db790c35207b2e685d4a43181e6061** returned a total supply of exactly **15,000,000,000** at block **25,147,818** and again at block **25,793,378**, the two ends of this window. There is one caveat worth stating plainly, because a flat total supply is not the same thing as no mint function. The verified ENA source carries a live **owner-only mint**, capped at **10%** of total supply and callable once every **365 days**, and the contract explicitly refuses to let the owner renounce ownership. The mint timestamp still reads the deployment date of **Mar 5 2024**, so it has never been used — but it has been callable since **Mar 5 2025**, and up to **1.5B ENA** a year could be created without a single line of new code. That is why this row ships as a checked row rather than a permanent one, and why the widely quoted **15B** maximum supply is a soft ceiling rather than a protocol cap.

The real sell side is Sell #2, vesting unlocks, at **515.6M ENA**. Core contributors hold **30%** of ENA and investors hold **25%**; both were locked for a year, released **25%** at the cliff, and have been vesting the remaining **75%** in equal monthly instalments over **36 months** ever since. That arithmetic gives **171.9M ENA** a month — **93.8M** to core contributors and **78.1M** to investors — and the tranche reported for **Aug 5 2026** carried exactly that split. Three of those dates fell inside this window: **Jun 5 2026**, **Jul 5 2026** and **Aug 5 2026**. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, adds **285.9M ENA** — the Ethena Foundation's **15%** allocation and the ecosystem development fund release on the same monthly date, at a combined **95.3M** a step. That figure is derived from the schedule, not from a supply chart: **5,171.9M ENA** is still locked, nineteen remaining contributor and investor tranches account for **3,265.6M** of it, and the residual **1,906.3M** spread over the twenty monthly steps to the documented **Apr 2028** vesting end is **95.3M** a month. A separately reported foundation tranche of about **40.6M** on **Jul 2 2026** is the independent cross-check on that number. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: Ethena has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

One structural detail decides how these unlocks are counted, and it is the opposite of what an unlock calendar usually implies. A full sweep of every ENA transfer over the 90-day window found **no distribution event whatsoever** on **Jun 5**, **Jul 5** or **Aug 5 2026** — on the Aug 5 unlock day the largest single sender moved **43.1M ENA** and that wallet is an exchange hot wallet, not a vesting contract. Ethena has no on-chain vesting escrow at all: the allocations already sit in recipient custody and each month a tranche becomes contractually sellable, so nothing needs to move when the calendar fires. Where a readable escrow exists, the framework books what actually left it rather than what the calendar promised — but there is no escrow here to read, so the published schedule governs. Exchange balances are consistent with that reading: the two largest exchange wallets held **729.4M** and **78.6M ENA** at the start of the window and **650.0M** and **54.8M** at the end, so the unlocks were not dumped onto order books inside the window.

## Buy pressure: where new ENA goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **0**, and each has been checked against the chain rather than against an announcement. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0** because ENA has no burn: the burn address held the same **2.63 ENA** at both ends of the window and no fee is routed into destroying the token. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** — no dated purchase and no buying wallet could be confirmed from a primary source, so no figure is claimed. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the one that looks like it should be non-zero and is not. Staked ENA rose from **1,085.2M** to **1,132.3M** over the window, a net increase of **47.2M** — but staking ENA mints a receipt token redeemable after a short cooldown, so those coins never leave the tradable float. Staking is custody, not a lock, and the framework does not book it as buy pressure.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is the row that carries the most widely repeated claim about ENA, and it is **0** on two independent reads. The first is the revenue share to staked ENA. If protocol revenue were being used to buy ENA and hand it to stakers, the staking wrapper's payout rate would rise — the value of one share in ENA terms. It read **1.0182693524** ENA per share at the start of the window and **1.0182693524** at the end, identical to the last digit across 90 days. Whatever Ethena distributes to sENA holders, it is not denominated in ENA, and it removes no ENA from the float. The second read is the buyback destination. The listed treasury vehicle that holds ENA on the market's behalf keeps its position in three custody wallets, all migrated to fresh addresses on **Jul 8** and **Jul 9 2026**; measured across both old and new addresses, those wallets held **3,031.4M ENA** at the start of the window and **3,029.0M** at the end. That is **2.4M less**, not more. Tracing the inflows by sender rather than reading the balance chart explains why: the roughly **3.03B ENA** stake was contributed by the Ethena Foundation and its private investors at the **Jun 25 2026** business combination, a treasury-to-treasury transfer that looks identical to a market purchase on a chart and is nothing of the kind. No ENA was bought back inside this window.

## Foundation and overhang

ENA's overhang is the largest single fact about the token. **5,171.9M ENA** — a third of everything that exists — is still unreleased, and it is scheduled to arrive at **267.2M** a month until roughly **Apr 2028**. That pool splits across the Ethena Foundation's **15%** allocation, the ecosystem development fund, and the remaining core contributor and investor tranches. It is refreshed on every rebuild from the published calendar and from the independent unlock classifier, which agrees with the circulating figure to the unit at **9,828,125,000**.

Two further overhangs sit inside the counted float rather than outside it. The listed treasury vehicle holds roughly **3,029.0M ENA** — about **31%** of the circulating supply — under a corporate mandate with no published sell schedule, disclosed through regulated filings and confirmed in its second-quarter **2026** report on **Aug 14 2026**. Because that stake is already classified as circulating, the effective tradable float is closer to **6.8B** than to **9.83B**, which makes every monthly step heavier in practice than the headline percentage suggests. Separately, one unnamed wallet funded with **1,260.0M ENA** by Ethena's original distributor in **Oct 2024** sent **170.0M** to a trading desk on **Aug 18 2026**, after sending **157.4M** on **Mar 16 2026**; it cannot be attributed to a named entity from a primary source, so it is watched rather than booked. The rule is the same for all three: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and the same applies to the owner-only mint, which would enter Sell #1 at whatever size the chain shows.

## How ENA compares to other fixed-supply governance tokens

ENA belongs to the fixed-supply, calendar-vested governance class — tokens whose entire supply was minted at launch and whose inflation is not issuance at all but the steady reclassification of locked allocation into tradable float. Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap the contrast is instructive: a halving chain genuinely creates new coins, but at a rate written into code that nobody can accelerate, and its float is essentially complete. ENA creates nothing, yet its float grows at roughly **33%** a year at the current step, because a third of the supply is still queued behind a schedule. On the framework's own measure — how much supply reaches the market — the fixed-supply token is by far the more inflationary of the two. The label "fixed supply" describes the ceiling, not the pressure.

Against other governance tokens with revenue, the comparison turns on whether the revenue touches the token. An exchange token that routes quarterly profit into buying and burning its own supply shrinks its float while its unlocks run, so the two effects partly cancel and the net reading lands near zero. Ethena generates real revenue — the protocol is one of the larger fee-producing systems in the sector — but that revenue reaches ENA holders only through a staking wrapper whose ENA-denominated payout rate has not moved at all over 90 days. There is no burn address activity, no buyback contract, and no purchase by the foundation in the window. So ENA sits at the extreme of its class: a full unlock calendar on one side of the ledger and a genuinely empty other side. The comparison that matters for a reader is not against another synthetic-dollar protocol, it is against any token where revenue converts into token demand — because the mechanism that would make ENA look different has been approved in principle and, as measured on-chain, has bought nothing.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the three monthly release dates — **Sep 5 2026**, **Oct 5 2026** and **Nov 5 2026** — each adding roughly **267.2M ENA** to the float. They are the whole sell side of this page and they do not slow down before **2028**. Second, the staking wrapper's payout rate: the moment it rises above **1.0182693524** ENA per share, the revenue share has started paying in ENA, and Buy #1 stops being zero. That single number is the cheapest test of whether Ethena's fee switch has become a buy-side mechanism rather than a stablecoin distribution. Third, the listed treasury vehicle's wallets — an increase means real market purchases and a genuine buy row; a decrease means **3.03B ENA** starting to move the other way. Fourth, the owner-only mint, which has been callable since **Mar 5 2025** and would allow up to **1.5B ENA** in one transaction; a call would be the largest single supply event in the token's history. Fifth, any governance decision that changes the release calendar itself, since the entire reading on this page is a function of three dates a quarter.

## Summary

ENA is a fixed-supply governance token that is nonetheless strongly inflationary in the only sense that matters to a holder: over the 90 days to **Aug 20 2026**, **801.6M ENA** moved from locked allocation into tradable float across three monthly steps of **267.2M**, while the token's on-chain total supply never moved off **15,000,000,000**. The buy side is empty — no burn, no executed buyback, and a staking wrapper whose ENA payout rate was byte-identical at both ends of the window — giving a net of **+8.16%** on a **9,828.1M** circulating base, projected at the same **+8.16%** for the next 90 days on an unchanged calendar. Our supply monitor reads **+8.88%**, a **0.72-point** gap that resolves entirely to which base date the same **801.6M** is divided by. The key risk is that **5,171.9M ENA** — a third of the total — is still queued to arrive by **Apr 2028** against a buy side that has yet to buy anything, and the **15B** ceiling itself is soft, because the contract keeps a live owner-only mint of up to **10%** a year that can never be renounced.
