# ENA Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Ethena minted **zero** new ENA in the last 90 days — the token is capped at **15,000,000,000** and the chain read exactly that figure at both ends of the window. The tradable float still opened by **803.9M ENA**, because Ethena's vesting calendar releases **267.2M ENA** on the 5th of every month and nothing on the buy side took a single coin back. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ENA at **+8.18% net** for the last 90 days and **+20.90%** forward, against a supply-monitor reading of **+8.879%** that reconciles to the same measured flow. The forward number is the story: on **Oct 5 2026** every remaining investor token is released in a single event.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Ethena at **+8.18% net**: **803.9M ENA** of supply reaching the market against **zero** absorbed, on a circulating base of **9,828.1M ENA**. The supply monitor reads the same window at **+8.879%**, a gap of **0.70 percentage points**, which is over tolerance and therefore triggered a five-source reconciliation walk. That walk **closed the gap**, and closed it on the denominator rather than on a missing flow, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip**. The two measurements of the flow itself agree to within **0.007%** — the vesting calendar releases **801.6M ENA** across the three cliff dates in the window, and the monitor's implied change in circulating supply is **801.5M**. The framework divides that flow by today's circulating supply and the monitor divides it by the supply of 90 days ago; re-based onto the monitor's denominator the framework reads **8.906%**, and the remaining **0.027** percentage points is exactly the **2.4M ENA** a corporate treasury moved inside the float, which a circulating-supply measure cannot see. ENA is a **hard-capped token with an unfinished release calendar**: the dilution is entirely a custody question, never an issuance one.

## Sell pressure: where new ENA comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero** on Ethena, and it is measured rather than assumed. ENA has no staking issuance, no block reward and no emission curve; all **15,000,000,000** ENA were created at launch, and the total in existence read the same to the token at both ends of the window. The one caveat this build records rather than hides: a mint function is still present in the deployed ENA contract, so this row is re-checked on every rebuild instead of being closed forever. It has never fired.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **515.6M ENA** and is the largest single row on the coin. Ethena's core contributors hold **30%** of supply and its investors **25%**, both on the same terms: a one-year cliff releasing a quarter of the allocation, then three years of linear monthly vesting from the launch date of **Mar 5 2024**. That arithmetic gives **93.75M ENA** to core contributors and **78.125M ENA** to investors every month, **171.9M** together, on the 5th. Three of those cliffs — **Jun 5 2026**, **Jul 5 2026** and **Aug 5 2026** — fell inside this window, and each contributes its full quantum rather than a smoothed average. The derivation is confirmed independently: dated ENA unlock reporting quotes **171.88M** with exactly that split. There is no readable escrow contract holding these tokens — every large ENA holding read this session sits in ordinary custody, not in a claim contract — so nothing moves on the chain when a cliff fires, the published calendar governs, and there is no realised outflow figure that could replace it.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **288.3M ENA** and has two parts. The larger is the foundation and ecosystem allocation, which rides the same monthly calendar as the team. Its size is derived rather than published: **5,171.9M ENA** is locked today, **3,265.6M** of that is the nineteen remaining team and investor cliffs, and the **1,906.3M** residual spreads over the twenty monthly steps to the documented **Apr 2028** vesting end — **95.3M ENA** a month, or **285.9M** across this window. A dated foundation release of **40.6M ENA** on **Jul 2 2026** sits inside that step and confirms the rate. The smaller part is **2.4M ENA** sold out of a Nasdaq-listed treasury company that holds **3.03B ENA** — about **20%** of every ENA in existence — whose filing states the holding to the hundredth of a token at **3,031,404,421**, matching three on-chain wallets exactly. That match is what identifies them, and it also disarms a trap: in July 2026 all **3.03B** moved from three wallets into three fresh ones in single transfers, which on a balance chart looks like the largest supply event on the coin and is worth nothing at all. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Ethena has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new ENA goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, and this is the number most likely to surprise a reader who saw the headlines. On **Aug 27 2026** the Ethena Foundation opened a governance vote to route **95%** of the net revenue reaching it into open-market ENA buybacks, and the token rallied on the news. The vote closes **Sep 2 2026**. Even a unanimous yes buys nothing: the mechanism only activates once Ethena's synthetic dollar reaches its first milestone of **$7.5B** in circulating supply, and the on-chain read this session puts it at **$4.08B** — the buying switches on roughly **84%** of growth from here, and the share of revenue captured then steps up in stages as that supply grows. An announced programme is not a purchase, so this row stays at zero until ENA is actually bought.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**, and this build checked both places a burn could show rather than one. The number of ENA in existence held at **15,000,000,000** across the window, and the unspendable address that would collect discarded tokens held **2.6311 ENA** on **Jun 2 2026** and the same **2.6311 ENA** on **Aug 31 2026**. Ethena has never destroyed ENA. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is also **zero**, and the reason is a distinction that matters. The Foundation did buy ENA on **Aug 27 2026**, from seed investors that had been selling since the **Oct 10 2025** peak — but it bought their **locked** tokens in private deals, not coins on the open market. Nothing left the tradable float, so nothing is credited on the buy side; the purchase moved a future seller into Foundation custody, which is an overhang, not an absorption.

Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**, and this is the judgement call on ENA. Staking ENA deposits it into a vault contract whose ENA balance rose from **1,074.8M** to **1,220.9M** across the window, an absorption of **146.1M ENA** that is real and measured. The rise is deposits rather than rewards, and one ratio proves it: the vault's assets-per-share read **1.018269** at both ends of the window, identical to six decimal places, so no ENA was distributed into it as yield. It is still not booked, for two reasons that both have to hold before a lock counts. First, the exit is a **7-day** cooldown before ENA can be withdrawn, with another **7 days** on top for the wrapped position — a queue, not a multi-year term lock. Second, staked ENA remains inside the counted circulating supply: total minus circulating is **5,171.9M**, which is exactly the remaining vesting calendar and leaves no room for the **1.22B** sitting in the vault. Nothing left the market, so nothing is credited. Ethena's buy side is empty on all four rows.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled overhangs are tracked on ENA. The first is the whole undistributed allocation — **5,171.9M ENA**, **34.5%** of maximum supply, releasing at **267.2M** a month on the 5th until roughly **Apr 2028**. The second is the Ethena Foundation's own **15%** allocation, **2,250M ENA**, which feeds around **40.6M** into each of those monthly steps. The third is the Nasdaq-listed treasury company's **3.03B ENA**, held under a lockup released over up to **48 months** and unsellable without Ethena Foundation approval — yet held by a company whose own filing names its ability to monetise unlocked ENA as a condition of continuing operations. The fourth is a **839.3M ENA** wallet that sent **170.0M** out on **Aug 19 2026** and had all of it back by **Aug 26 2026** — a round trip, which is why balances were read at both window ends rather than at a single date.

One further overhang has no number yet and deserves naming for that reason: the block of locked investor ENA the Foundation bought back on **Aug 27 2026**. Ethena has not disclosed how much it bought, and those coins unlock with everything else on **Oct 5 2026** — into Foundation custody rather than onto the market. Ethena has no DAO treasury separate from the Foundation, no buyback accumulation wallet, because no buyback has run, and no bankruptcy estate. Unidentified large holders are deliberately excluded from this enumeration rather than guessed at. If any tracked overhang's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ENA compares to other capped governance tokens

ENA belongs to a class that is easy to misjudge: hard-capped tokens whose entire supply already exists, where dilution is a calendar rather than a mint. Against a halving chain the difference is total. A halving chain's new coins are genuinely new, but its issuance is written into code, falls on a fixed schedule, and nobody can vote it higher. Ethena creates nothing — and still opened more of its own float in one quarter, as a share of circulating supply, than most proof-of-work chains issue in a year. A cap tells you the ceiling; it tells you nothing about the speed of the climb.

The closer comparison is other capped tokens on unfinished vesting calendars, where the monthly cliff, not the emission curve, is the number that matters. ENA sits at the heavy end of that group, and for a specific reason: instead of tapering, its calendar is being compressed. Replacing eighteen remaining monthly investor releases with a single **Oct 5 2026** event does not create one extra token, but it moves roughly **1.41B ENA** of investor supply out of the next year and a half and into the next ten weeks. Against exchange tokens that run quarterly burns, or uncapped protocols whose fee switch is live and buying every day, the structural gap is that Ethena's buy side is a proposal with an unmet activation condition rather than a mechanism with a balance you can read. Until that condition is met, ENA has issuance of zero, absorption of zero, and only a soft staking queue in between.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The fee-switch vote closes **Sep 2 2026**; passing it changes the buy row only when Ethena's synthetic dollar supply reaches **$7.5B**, so watch that supply figure, not the vote result. The last ordinary monthly step lands **Sep 5 2026** at **267.2M ENA**. The decisive date is **Oct 5 2026**, when core contributors take **93.8M**, the foundation and ecosystem step takes **95.3M**, and every remaining investor token — **1,406.3M ENA** on the published terms — is released at once. A quieter step of **189.1M** follows on **Nov 5 2026**, with investors finished by then. And the open question this build could not settle: Ethena has not said how much locked investor ENA the Foundation bought back, so the share of that **1.41B** that reaches the market rather than Foundation custody is unknown, and any disclosure of it would move this reading materially.

## Summary

Ethena's ENA is capped at **15,000,000,000** tokens, mints nothing, burns nothing and buys back nothing — and is still one of the most dilutive tokens in the framework, because **803.9M ENA** came off the vesting calendar in the last 90 days and **2,053.9M** is scheduled for the next. Every buy row on the coin reads zero, including staking, where **146.1M ENA** went into a vault with a **7-day** exit that never leaves the counted float. The framework reads **+8.18%** last quarter and **+20.90%** next, reconciling cleanly with the supply monitor's **+8.879%** once both are put on the same denominator. The key risk is **Oct 5 2026**, when the investor calendar collapses into a single release. The ceiling is fixed and can never move — but on the framework's own numbers, roughly **40%** of the remaining locked supply is scheduled to become tradable inside ten weeks.
