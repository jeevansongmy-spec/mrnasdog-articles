# PUMP Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply was growing · trend cooling

PUMP supply grew fast over the last 90 days and should now shrink. Between Aug 4 and Nov 2 2026, **83.38B PUMP** left team and investor vesting wallets while the Pump.fun buyback burned **18.93B**, so the circulating supply rose a net **+14.02%**. No new PUMP can ever be made, the next year of insider vesting already counts as circulating, and the buyback should burn about **20.09B** against only **1.88B** of new releases, for a projected **−3.96%** over the next 90 days. Other trackers read the last 90 days at **+16.30%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 2 2026, PUMP's circulating supply grew a net **+14.02%**, and the Pump.fun ledger projects **−3.96%** for the 90 days to Jan 31 2027. Other trackers show **+16.30%** for the past 90 days. The **2.28-point** difference is not a missing flow: both readings count the same releases and the same burns on the same days, so the coin page carries no warning note. The short label is a **post-unlock token turning deflationary on its buyback**: the big insider releases of September are behind it, and the PUMP buyback now burns more each quarter than the vesting adds.

## Sell pressure: where new PUMP comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**, and it can never be anything else. PUMP is a Solana token whose creation key was switched off for good, so the 1,000B PUMP made at launch in July 2025 is the most that will ever exist. Burns have already brought the total down to about **824.65B** PUMP.

Vesting unlocks were **83.38B PUMP** over the last 90 days, the whole of PUMP's new supply. The main Pump.fun custody wallet sent **20B** on Sep 8 2026 and **54B** on Sep 13 2026 to two payout wallets that serve team members and early investors. A separate 30B allocation wallet, on a four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, released **8.125B** on Aug 26 2026 and then **625M** on Sep 12 and again on Oct 12 2026. On Sep 14 2026 the payout wallets put a full year of team and investor vesting into 82 timed-release escrows. Those escrows already count as circulating, still hold **43.82B PUMP** and hand out about 4.4B a month, so their monthly payouts add nothing new. For the next 90 days the PUMP vesting row is just the allocation wallet's **1.88B**: 625M each on Nov 12 2026, Dec 12 2026 and Jan 12 2027.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks book **zero**. The custody wallet released PUMP in two big lumps this year, on Jun 25 2026 and in September, but it has no public schedule and nothing points to another release before February 2027. The long-term locked or bankruptcy row is also **zero**: no court estate or legal freeze holds PUMP.

## Buy pressure: where new PUMP goes

The programmatic buyback is PUMP's one buyer, and it took **18.93B PUMP** out of supply in the 90 days to Nov 2 2026. Since Apr 28 2026, half of Pump.fun's net revenue from its launchpad, its PumpSwap exchange and its Terminal app goes into wallets that buy PUMP on the open market and burn every coin at once, under a plan Pump.fun locked for one year. That spending came to about **$78.3M** in the window. The buyback wallets hold nothing between buys, so nothing piles up that could come back. At today's PUMP price, the same dollars would burn about **20.09B PUMP** in the next 90 days, more than the 18.93B of the last 90, because PUMP is cheaper now than it was on average.

The protocol fee burn is **zero** because Pump.fun has no separate fee burn: every PUMP destroyed comes through the buyback, and it is counted there once. The Foundation buy row is **zero**, since Pump.fun keeps the other half of its revenue to run the business and buys no PUMP to hold. New long-term locks are **zero** as well: PUMP has no staking, and the vesting escrows already count as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

Four wallets sit outside the circulating count, and together they hold **364.89B PUMP**, about 44% of all PUMP left. The largest is the main Pump.fun custody wallet with **229.26B**. It holds the rest of the team and investor allocation plus other reserves, and it releases in lumps rather than on a calendar: 62.2B on Jun 25 2026, then 74B on Sep 8 and Sep 13 2026. Two more wallets, with **80B** and **35B**, were filled on Jul 14 2025 and have not moved since. The fourth is the 30B allocation wallet, now down to **20.62B** and paying out 625M a month.

Inside the circulating count, Pump.fun's two payout wallets hold **13.86B** and **5.48B**, and the 82 team and investor escrows hold 43.82B. If the custody wallet or any of the other three outside wallets falls between checks, that outflow enters the PUMP sell ledger at the next check.

## How PUMP compares to other revenue-funded buyback tokens

PUMP belongs to a small group of app tokens whose main buyer is the app's own revenue. Within that group, PUMP's design is one of the bluntest. The supply can only fall, because no PUMP can ever be created, and the buyback burns its coins on the spot instead of holding them in a treasury. Some other platforms buy their token with fees and keep it in a fund or a locked trust. Those coins are out of the market for now but can come back, while burned PUMP cannot.

Where PUMP differs from older exchange tokens with scheduled burns is the size of its insider overhang. About 44% of all PUMP still sits in four wallets outside the circulating count, and the main custody wallet alone holds 229.26B. A token whose team allocation is fully spent can rely on the burn alone. PUMP's burn has to keep pace with whatever the custody wallet decides to release. In the last 90 days it did not: the burn matched only about 23% of the released coins. For the next 90 days the ledger has the burn ahead about ten to one.

PUMP's buyback also rests on a busier revenue base than most. It is a fixed share of what Pump.fun earns, so it rises and falls with memecoin trading. A coin with a fixed burn schedule does not carry that risk. PUMP does.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Nov 12 2026, Dec 12 2026 and Jan 12 2027: the 30B allocation wallet is due to release **625M PUMP** each time, the only dated new supply in the window.

The main custody wallet and its **229.26B PUMP**: it released lumps on Jun 25 and Sep 8 to 13 2026, and any new release would swing the next reading back toward growth.

Pump.fun's revenue: the burn is a fixed half of net revenue, so the perpetual futures launched on Oct 16 2026 and the new chains added from Oct 24 2026 matter only if they lift the revenue that pays for the buyback.

The mid-month escrow payouts, next around Nov 14 2026: they already count as circulating and add nothing to the count, but they put about 4.4B PUMP a month into team and investor hands.
