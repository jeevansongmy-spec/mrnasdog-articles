Pump.fun burns half of its revenue, yet the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **PUMP at +9.7%** net supply over the last 90 days — the tradable float is growing. The first big insider vesting cliff put roughly **62B PUMP** onto the market, outrunning a **24B** buyback-and-burn. PUMP is a **token deflating its ceiling while inflating its float** — a fixed 1T cap being burned down, but vesting unlocks reaching the market faster than the burn removes coins.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **Apr 30 2026** to **Jul 29 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **PUMP at +9.7% net**: sell pressure of **~62B PUMP** against buy pressure of **~24B PUMP** on a tradable base of **397.1B PUMP**. Our supply monitor reads **+10.67%** for the same period — a gap of about **1.0 percentage point**. That gap is not a disagreement about what happened; it is a denominator base. The same roughly **38.4B** rise in the float is **+10.67%** measured against the Apr 30 float of **359.85B** and **+9.7%** measured against the current float of **397.1B**, so the page ships that difference as a monitor-gap note rather than a conflict flag. Pump.fun is best labelled **deflationary on the ceiling, inflationary on the float**: the 1T cap is being burned down, but the vesting schedule is releasing coins to the market faster than the burn destroys them.

## Sell pressure: where new PUMP comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and it cannot turn on. PUMP is a Solana token with a fixed **1,000,000,000,000** max supply, no staking rewards and no emission curve; the protocol creates no new PUMP, and total supply only ever falls through the burn. New supply reaches the market only when the vesting schedule releases already-minted coins, which is Sell #2. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **~62B PUMP**, and it is the entire story of this page. The first major insider vesting cliff, one year after the July 2025 ICO, distributed **57.279B PUMP** to **121** wallets on **Jul 15 2026**; with roughly **5B** of community and ecosystem vesting, about **62B** reached the float. The scheduled calendar figure for the one-year cliff was near **82.5B**, so around **20B** was not drawn and remains in vesting — the framework books the realised release, not the gross calendar entitlement.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**. About **448B PUMP** sits outside the float in treasury, foundation, ecosystem and not-yet-vested team and investor allocations, and that is a large tracked overhang, but no discretionary sale beyond the scheduled vesting was observed in the window, so the row books zero and stays monitored. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: Pump.fun is a going concern and no estate or trustee schedule touches PUMP.

## Buy pressure: where new PUMP goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **~24B PUMP**, and it is one of the most aggressive buyback-and-burn programmes in crypto. Pump.fun buys PUMP on the open market and destroys it, funded by **50%** of net revenue from the bonding curve, PumpSwap and its terminal, enforced by an irreversible one-year smart contract. At the disclosed rate of about **3.7M** dollars a week that is roughly **48M** dollars over the window, or about **24B PUMP** burned, matching the steady on-chain float decline of about **0.26B** a day. This sits on top of a one-time **370M**-dollar burn on **Apr 28 2026** that removed roughly **36%** of the float in a single transaction. Crucially the tokens are burned, not held, so this is genuine deflation working against the unlocks.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**: PUMP has no automatic, fee-driven burn, only the discretionary revenue buyback above. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**, with no disclosed Pump.fun purchase of PUMP beyond that programme and no wallet pulling PUMP out of the float to hold. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: PUMP has no staking and no new lockup contract was created in the window. So the buy side is a single very large burn, and it still was not enough to offset the vesting cliff.

## Foundation and overhang

PUMP carries a large team-controlled overhang: the roughly **448B PUMP** that is not yet circulating, the gap between the on-chain total of about **845.3B** and the circulating **397.1B**. That bucket holds the treasury allocation, the foundation and ecosystem fund, the unvested team and investor tokens, and the roughly **20B** of the July cliff that was scheduled but not drawn. It is not booked as sell pressure today because it is releasing on a known schedule, not through discretionary sales, and the framework books the scheduled portion under Sell #2 as it actually vests. If any of these balances leaves through a discretionary sale between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The date that matters is not a single cliff anymore — the biggest one has passed — but the steady monthly drip, because from here the remaining **32%** of supply vests linearly across about **35** monthly events into 2029.

## How PUMP compares to other buyback-and-burn tokens

The natural peer group is revenue-funded buyback-and-burn tokens, the model exchange tokens made famous: take a slice of platform profit, buy the token on the open market, and destroy it. On the burn alone, PUMP is more aggressive than most — **50%** of all revenue, an irreversible contract, and a headline **370M**-dollar single burn — where a typical exchange token burns a smaller quarterly quantum. But PUMP has a second force those mature tokens no longer do: a heavy, still-early vesting schedule. A mature exchange token is largely fully circulating, so its burn lands on a stable float and reads cleanly deflationary. PUMP is only about **40%** circulating and just cleared its first insider cliff, so its burn is fighting a rising tide of unlocks.

Against a high-FDV, VC-backed launch token with a multi-year unlock, PUMP looks more disciplined, because most of those tokens have no offsetting buyer at all — the unlocks simply dilute. PUMP at least burns half its revenue against the dilution, which is why its net float growth of **+9.7%** is far below the gross unlock rate. So PUMP is neither a clean deflation story nor a pure dilution story. It is a fixed-cap token in a tug of war: an unusually strong burn on one side, an unusually large vesting overhang on the other, with the unlocks currently winning but by a shrinking margin.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the monthly linear vesting — with the big cliff behind it, PUMP now releases roughly **9.3B** a month, so the next window carries about **28B** rather than another **62B**; watch the **Aug 12 2026**, **Sep 12 2026** and **Oct 12 2026** unlocks. Second, the buyback rate — the burn is tied to revenue, and Pump.fun revenue has cooled from its 2025 peak, so a further drop in weekly fees means fewer PUMP burned and a higher net. Third, the irreversible buyback contract itself, which runs about a year from April 2026 — any signal about what happens when it expires would reset the buy side. Fourth, the undrawn cliff remainder of roughly **20B**, in case it reaches the market outside the linear schedule. Fifth, total supply on-chain — as long as the burn runs, the **845.3B** total keeps falling even while the float rises.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Pump.fun at **+9.7%** net supply over the last 90 days, in close agreement with our monitor's **+10.67%** once the denominator base is accounted for. PUMP is fixed at a **1T** cap and is being burned aggressively — **50%** of revenue and a one-time **370M**-dollar burn — yet its tradable float still grew because the first insider vesting cliff released about **62B PUMP** against a **24B** burn. The key risk is the vesting overhang: only about **40%** of supply circulates, and the remaining **32%** keeps dripping out monthly. Projected forward, the one-time cliff is gone and the monthly rate of about **28B** against a steady **24B** burn narrows the net to roughly **+1.0%** — still growing, but far more slowly.
