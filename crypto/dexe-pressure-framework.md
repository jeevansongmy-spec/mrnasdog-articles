## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending Jul 31 2026, the Pressure Framework reads **DEXE at 0.00% net**. Sell pressure is **zero** and buy pressure is **zero**, against a circulating base of **46.75M DEXE** out of a fixed **96.5M total**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change at **+0.17%**, a gap of **0.17 percentage points** — well inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships. That small drift is the circulating feed reclassifying roughly **80,000** already-minted reserve DEXE into circulating over the quarter, which is distribution of existing supply rather than new issuance. DEXE is flat by structure: a fixed supply with nothing minting and nothing burning this window.

## Sell pressure: where new DEXE comes from

The short answer is that no new DEXE comes from anywhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** because DEXE is a fixed-supply token: the on-chain total is a flat **96.5M DEXE**, read directly from the Ethereum contract at **96,504,599** — the same figure the market data reports as total supply. There is no mint function and no inflationary emission. This is the point most often misread about DAO tokens with staking: DEXE's staking rewards and ecosystem incentives are paid out of a pre-allocated, already-minted pool, not by issuing fresh coins, so staking does not add supply. The token can only ever get smaller, through burns.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because DEXE's vesting is finished. All eight original allocations have fully released: the team's 20% unlocked in **Apr 2023**, the partnership, trading-school, staking-reward, marketing and sale tranches all released across 2021 and 2022, and the final and largest cliff — the Foundation's **36.5%** — unlocked on **Oct 18 2025**. No unlock calendar remains, and none falls inside or after this window. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** for value but carries the real overhang, discussed below. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: there is no DeXe estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new DEXE goes

There is no buy pressure this window either, and that is a structural fact rather than a temporary lull. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: DeXe runs no automatic buyback. Its documentation says a share of the fees generated by DAOs built on the protocol may be used to buy back and burn DEXE, but that is a discretionary governance action, not a preset on-chain program, and no buyback executed between May and July 2026. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** for the same reason: DEXE has no continuous fee burn. About **3.5M DEXE** has been permanently burned over the token's life, but those burns are proposed one at a time by DAO vote, and none was executed in this window — circulating supply edged up slightly rather than falling.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**: no foundation or DAO entity disclosed an open-market DEXE purchase, and the treasury exists to fund the ecosystem rather than to bid on the token. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is also **zero** for the framework, and this needs a careful distinction. DEXE can be staked in **1, 3, 6, 12 and 24-month** lock tiers, which does pull tokens out of active trading — but staked DEXE is still counted as circulating supply. Locking is not redemption, so it does not shrink the denominator the framework divides by and cannot register as a removal. No new external lockup contract with an announced quantum was deployed in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is the one thing to keep an eye on, because it is large. Only about **46.75M** of the fixed **96.5M** DEXE is circulating, which leaves roughly **49.7M** — more than half the supply — held by the project and its reserves. The biggest single pool is the **DeXe Foundation treasury**, the 36.5% allocation of about **35.2M DEXE**, which is now fully unlocked but sits in treasury with no published release schedule. Alongside it are a staking-rewards reserve of roughly **9.7M DEXE** and other undistributed buckets. None of these made an identified market release this window; a single **625,000 DEXE** transfer to an exchange on **Jul 23 2026** came from an unattributed holder wallet, not an identified Foundation address, so it does not enter the ledger. These pools are re-checked every rebuild, and if any of their balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How DEXE compares to other fixed-supply governance tokens

DEXE sits in the same structural class as fixed-supply utility and governance tokens whose only supply lever is a discretionary burn — closer to an exchange token that burns from revenue than to an emission-driven Layer 1. Unlike a continuous-emission chain, where a protocol mints new units every block and the framework reads a steady positive inflation, DEXE issues nothing: its supply is capped in code at 96.5M and can only move down. That makes its baseline reading flat rather than inflationary, which is a stronger starting point than any token that must out-burn its own emission to stay neutral.

Where DEXE differs from the strongest tokens in its class is that its deflation is not automatic. A token like BNB or a fee-burning exchange asset runs its buyback-and-burn on a schedule tied to revenue, so supply shrinks whether or not anyone votes for it. DEXE's burns are proposed and approved one at a time through DAO governance, so the deflationary pressure only exists when the DAO chooses to act. The result this window is a clean flat: no emission to push the reading up, and no scheduled burn to pull it down. It also carries a heavier overhang than most peers of its age — more than half of supply is still project-held — which is the main structural risk against the otherwise clean supply picture.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, any DAO governance vote to execute a buyback-and-burn: that is the only mechanism that would turn the framework reading negative, and it is entirely discretionary, so a passed burn proposal is the main event to watch. Second, the DeXe Foundation treasury of about **35.2M DEXE** — any distribution from that pool would open Sell #3, so its balance is the primary watch line. Third, staking-reserve movements: while staking itself does not change circulating supply, distributions from the roughly **9.7M** staking-rewards reserve would show up as small circulating drift like the one the monitor already sees. Fourth, there are no scheduled token unlocks in the window at all — vesting concluded in October 2025 — so absent a governance action the forward reading stays flat near **0.00%**.

## Summary

DeXe Protocol's DEXE is a fixed-supply governance token whose supply is flat this window: no mint and no emission, all vesting finished on Oct 18 2025, and no burn or buyback executed between May and July 2026. That leaves the Pressure Framework at 0.00% net, in line with our supply monitor at +0.17%, a gap of just 0.17 percentage points. The key structural strength is that DEXE issues nothing — its 96.5M total is capped in code and can only shrink through governance burns. The key risk is the overhang: more than half of supply, led by a roughly 35.2M DEXE Foundation treasury, is project-held and unlocked with no published schedule, so the flat reading depends on those pools staying put.
