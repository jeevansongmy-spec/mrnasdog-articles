## Sell pressure: where new PIEVERSE comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. Pieverse created all 1B PIEVERSE at launch in late October 2025, 900M on Ethereum and 100M on BNB Chain, and the two chains together still hold exactly 1B. Coins that cross the bridge between the chains are removed on one side and added on the other, so they never add to the count.

Vesting unlocks are the only real source of new PIEVERSE, booked at **~10M** for the last 90 days. The Pieverse ecosystem wallet sent **5M PIEVERSE** on Sep 18 2026 and another **5M** on Oct 20 2026, and both receiving wallets still held them on Nov 24. The published schedule called for far more over the same months, but the project has been paying out much more slowly than its own unlock chart.

The one-year lock on team and investor coins ended on Nov 14 2026. From that day the team's 200M PIEVERSE unlock over 24 months and the investors' 150M over 18 months, about **16.67M** a month together. The Nov 14 2026 tranche had not left the team and investor wallets by Nov 24, so it is not counted as new supply yet. The next 90 days hold three tranches, on Dec 14 2026, Jan 14 2027 and Feb 14 2027, worth **50M** together, plus about **15M** more if the ecosystem wallet keeps its monthly 5M pace. That is the **~65M** behind the next-90-day reading.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are **0**. The 100M Pieverse foundation wallet has never sent a coin, and the operations wallet, which last paid 8.5M on Aug 17 2026, paid nothing in the window and keeps no set dates. Long-term locks and bankruptcy are also **0**: no court or estate holds PIEVERSE.

## Buy pressure: where new PIEVERSE goes

Every buy row for PIEVERSE reads **0**. Pieverse has no programmatic buyback: its paper lists payments, fee discounts, staking and voting as uses of the coin, but no share of revenue buys PIEVERSE back. There is no fee burn either; supply across Ethereum and BNB Chain stayed at exactly 1B from Aug 26 to Nov 24 2026, and the dead addresses hold almost nothing.

No foundation or company buying of PIEVERSE turned up; the project only pays coins out. Staking does exist: holders can lock PIEVERSE for 90, 180 or 365 days to earn points, and about **10.87M PIEVERSE** sits in staking across both chains. Staked coins still count as circulating, so staking takes nothing out of the supply count.

## Foundation and overhang

About **723.65M PIEVERSE**, almost three quarters of the supply, still sits in Pieverse project wallets. The largest is a **166M** community wallet that has never paid out. The team and investor coins sit in three wallets of **150M**, **150M** and **50M**, untouched since launch. The foundation wallet holds **100M**. Four ecosystem payout wallets hold about **97.5M** together, and an operations wallet holds about **10.15M** across the two chains.

None of these Pieverse wallets has a public payout date beyond the team and investor schedule. If any of them pays coins out, that outflow counts as new PIEVERSE supply at the next check.

## How PIEVERSE compares to other young unlock-schedule tokens

PIEVERSE belongs to the large group of young tokens with a fixed cap and a multi-year unlock schedule: no new coins are ever created, but most of the supply starts locked and reaches the market over three years. That sets PIEVERSE apart from proof-of-stake chains such as Ethereum, which pay validators in brand-new coins every day and offset part of it with a fee burn. For PIEVERSE the cap is hard, but the share already on the market is small, so each unlock moves the supply count much more than any emission would.

Among unlock-schedule tokens, the big differences are how the coins are held and whether anything buys them back. Some projects use vesting contracts that release coins to the minute; Pieverse holds each allocation in a multi-signer wallet and pays out by hand, which is why its real releases have run well behind its own chart. Many exchange and trading-platform tokens also run buybacks or burns that take coins back; PIEVERSE has none, so nothing offsets the unlocks.
