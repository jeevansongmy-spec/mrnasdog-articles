Celestia now runs one of the smallest mints of any uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1 — a measured **6.62M TIA** over the last 90 days, **2.30%** a year — and it barely moves the answer, because the 2023 launch distribution released **31.01M TIA** into the tradable float over the same window, roughly five times as much. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads TIA at **+3.89%** over the trailing 90 days and **+3.23%** over the next 90. The buy side is **0** on all four rows: Celestia runs no buyback, and it has no burn mechanism and no burn address at all.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **966.3M TIA**, the framework books **37.63M TIA** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+3.89%** — and projects **+3.23%** for the next 90 days as one of the two unlock streams runs out. The inflation monitor reads **+3.92%** for the same window, a gap of **0.03 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so this page ships with no monitor-gap warning. The label for TIA is a **chain whose issuance problem is already solved and whose distribution problem is not**: the mint is small, disciplined and still shrinking, while the float keeps growing because coins that were minted in 2023 are still arriving.

## Sell pressure: where new TIA comes from

Two places, and the smaller one is the mint. **Sell #1, protocol inflation, is 6.62M TIA**. We measured it rather than quoting it: the count of TIA in existence rose from **1,170.39M** to **1,177.01M** between the two ends of the window, and Celestia's yearly issuance allowance read the same **26.87M TIA** at every height in between, so nothing changed mid-window. That works out at **2.30%** a year. It is worth being precise about this number, because the published one is different. Celestia's documentation and the upgrade announcement both say inflation was cut "to 2.5%"; the rate the chain actually applies is **2.32%**. The reason is in the protocol constants: Celestia sets a starting rate of **2.67%** and steps it down **6.7%** every year, and the step counter runs from the 2023 launch rather than from the change — so the network was already two steps in when the new starting rate took effect. Measured against today's larger supply, the same fixed allowance reads **2.28%**. The next step lands on **Oct 30 2026** and takes the rate to **2.17%**.

A second measurement is worth recording because it is the one most builds get wrong. Celestia's block time more than halved inside this window — the v9 network upgrade cut the target from six seconds to about three on **Jul 1 2026**, and the realised interval went from **6.08 seconds** to **2.84 seconds**. Issuance did not move. Celestia pays its yearly allowance pro-rata to elapsed wall-clock time, not per block, so TIA per second read **0.851322** before the upgrade and **0.851322** after it. Correcting Sell #1 for the faster blocks would have roughly doubled it and invented supply that does not exist.

The real engine is **Sell #2, vesting unlocks, at 31.01M TIA** — about five times the mint. Celestia's 2023 genesis distribution of one billion TIA still has two streams running: the research and ecosystem allocation, which releases continuously until **Oct 30 2027**, and the core-contributor allocation, which ends on **Oct 30 2026**. Together they move about **345,000 TIA a day** from locked into tradable. Locked research and ecosystem TIA fell from **92.86M** to **76.35M** across the window, and the core-contributor pot from **22.71M** to **8.21M**; those two falls are the number. No TIA is created by an unlock — the coins already existed and were already inside the 1,177M count. What changes is that they stop being excluded from the float, which is exactly the quantity this framework measures. The early-backer allocations finished their release in **Oct 2025** and contribute nothing.

**Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is 0** — everything still held back is either on the published release rules above or in a pot that needs a vote, and no such vote has been submitted. **Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is 0**: TIA has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new TIA goes

Nowhere. **Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is 0** — Celestia runs no programme that spends treasury money repurchasing TIA on the open market. The Proof-of-Governance design that would fund open-market buying out of data-availability revenue is still a written proposal; it has never been submitted to a vote on the chain, and Celestia's improvement-proposal repository carries no draft for it.

**Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is 0**, and Celestia is unusual in that there is not even a place to look. The chain exposes no burn address and no destroy path — fees pay the block proposer and stakers, with 2% routed to the community treasury — so the only surface is the count of TIA in existence. That count rose at every height we read, and the rise matched the issuance allowance to within **1.2 TIA** over 90 days, which leaves no room in the counter for anything to have been destroyed. The zero is a measurement, not an absence of documentation.

**Buy #3, Foundation buy, is 0.** The Celestia Foundation did buy TIA once, off-market: **43.45M TIA** from an early backer, announced **Jul 24 2025** and passed to new investors on a release schedule that finished in **Nov 2025**. That is fourteen months before this window opens, it was an assignment rather than open-market demand, and nothing accumulated from it. **Buy #4, new long-term lock, is 0**, and staking moved the other way: bonded TIA fell from **506.98M** to **480.72M**. Even had it risen it would not count here, because Celestia's own definition already treats staked TIA as circulating — staking rewards are spendable the moment they are received.

## Foundation and overhang

The non-circulating side of Celestia reconstructs exactly, which is rare. At the end of the window **1,177M TIA** existed and **966M** counted as circulating; the **210.50M** difference is **76.35M** of still-locked research and ecosystem allocation, **8.21M** of still-locked core-contributor allocation, and **125.94M** of a public allocation reserved at launch for future initiatives. Those three add to the difference to the last decimal, so nothing is unaccounted for. The first two are on published release rules and are already booked in Sell #2. The third is the genuine overhang: it sits on no calendar and enters the float only if an on-chain governance vote spends it, and the newest proposal on the chain is from **Dec 2025**. Alongside it, the community treasury grew from **3.41M** to **3.54M TIA** on network fee income, but that pot is already counted inside the tradable float, so spending it would move coins rather than create them.

Both items are refreshed from the chain at every rebuild. If the **125.94M TIA** future-initiatives allocation or the community treasury balance falls between refreshes by more than the published rules account for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How TIA compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1s

On the issuance axis alone, TIA is now near the front of its class. A typical uncapped Cosmos-family Layer 1 mints somewhere between 5% and 15% a year, sized off how much of the supply is staked, and the rate moves up when staking falls — the opposite of a commitment. Celestia does not work that way. Its yearly allowance is a fixed quantity of TIA set once a year and reduced **6.7%** every year on a path to a **1.5%** floor, so the rate cannot be pushed back up by market conditions and does not depend on the staking ratio at all. At **2.30%** measured, Celestia mints less than almost any uncapped proof-of-stake chain and roughly on par with a mid-cycle halving chain, without needing a halving.

And the page still reads **+3.89%**. That is the whole lesson of the comparison, and it is the mirror image of the lesson a hard-capped chain teaches: a cap constrains total supply and an emission schedule constrains new supply, but neither one constrains the tradable float, and the float is what a holder is diluted against. TIA is structurally much closer to a recently-launched token working through a multi-year investor and team vest than to a mature Layer 1. The difference from a typical vest is that Celestia's is continuous rather than cliff-based, so there is no dated unlock to trade around — the release is smooth, published, and relentless.

The third comparison is to exchange tokens and revenue-sharing chains that offset issuance with a demand-linked burn or buyback. Those readings can go genuinely negative. Celestia has none of that machinery — no burn function, no burn address, no repurchase mandate — so its buy side is not small, it is structurally absent. For the mint alone to be offset, Celestia would need to retire about **6.62M TIA** a quarter; to offset the whole reading it would need **37.63M**, close to 4% of circulating supply every 90 days. That is the distance between a proposal and a mechanism.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, **Oct 30 2026**, which carries two events at once: the core-contributor release finishes, dropping the daily unlock from about **345,000 TIA** to about **184,000**, and the mint-year rolls over, stepping issuance from **2.32%** to **2.17%**. Both are already in the **+3.23%** forward reading. Second, the research and ecosystem stream, which is the only unlock left after that date and runs until **Oct 30 2027** — that is when this page's headline finally collapses toward the mint alone. Third, Proof-of-Governance: it is the single largest forward mechanism risk, it would cut issuance dramatically and add a revenue-funded buyback and burn, and it has still never been submitted to a vote. Fourth, the **125.94M TIA** future-initiatives allocation, which is the only pot on this chain with a spender rather than a schedule. Fifth, Celestia's own circulating-supply service does not yet adjust for staking rewards earned on locked accounts being pulled into the lockup schedule, which its maintainers put at up to 1% of the circulating figure — if that correction lands, this page's numerator gets slightly smaller, not larger.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads TIA at **+3.89%** over the trailing 90 days and **+3.23%** projected forward: supply growing, projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is not the mint — at a measured **6.62M TIA** a quarter, **2.30%** a year, Celestia issues less than almost any uncapped proof-of-stake chain, and the rate steps down again on **Oct 30 2026**. It is the 2023 launch distribution, which is still releasing about **345,000 TIA a day** and contributed **31.01M** of the quarter's **37.63M**. The key risk is that nothing on the buy side can offset it: Celestia has no buyback and no burn mechanism of any kind, so the only relief on the calendar is the unlock streams running out — one on **Oct 30 2026** and the last on **Oct 30 2027**.
