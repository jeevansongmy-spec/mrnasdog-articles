The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads TIA, the staking and blockspace token of the **Celestia** modular data-availability network, at **+3.93%** net supply over the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026**, with **+3.64%** projected forward. Almost none of that is the mint: Celestia issued only **6,619,961 TIA** over the window, about **2.33%** a year and falling. The pressure is the genesis unlock, which released roughly **31.0M TIA** in the same period — five times the mint — against a buy side of exactly **zero**, because Celestia runs no buyback and burns no fees. Our supply monitor reads **+4.15%**. TIA is the case where watching the inflation rate tells you almost nothing about the inflation.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026**, the framework reads TIA at **+3.93%** net supply — **37.64M TIA** of sell pressure against **0** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **957.1M TIA** — and projects **+3.64%** forward. Our supply monitor reads **+4.15%** over the same window, a gap of **0.22 percentage points**, which sits inside the framework's tolerance, so TIA ships with no data-conflict chip; the seven-day median of the monitor's own reading is **+4.149%**, so the endpoint is not a spike. Celestia is best characterised as **structurally inflationary by unlock, not by emission** — an uncapped chain whose actual monetary policy has been tightened twice and is now a minor term, while the genesis distribution schedule set in 2023 does nearly all of the work.

## Sell pressure: where new TIA comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, carries **6.62M TIA**, and getting that number required measuring rather than reading. Celestia's mint module is not exposed over the chain's public data service — every inflation-rate and annual-provisions route on every public node returns **Not Implemented** — so the emission was taken off the bank supply counter at both ends of the window on an archive node: **1,168,776,986 TIA** at height **11,168,359** and **1,175,396,947 TIA** at height **13,218,200**. That is **73,554 TIA** a day, and it held to five significant figures across three separate stretches of the window, so no rate change happened inside it. Annualised, that is **2.33%** of total supply, which reconciles to CIP-41's specified **2.67%** initial rate compounded by two years of **6.7%** disinflation. Note the base: Celestia strikes annual provisions against total TIA supply, not against the **488.7M TIA** that is bonded — using the staked figure would have reported **5.50%** and more than doubled this row.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, carries **10.22M TIA**, and it was read out of the escrow rather than off an unlock calendar. Celestia's mainnet genesis file created **231** delayed-vesting accounts and **229** continuous-vesting accounts; all the delayed ones released on **Oct 30 2024** and are spent, and six of the seven continuous cohorts have already run out. Exactly one is still paying: **20** accounts holding **82,866,647 TIA** released evenly from **Oct 30 2024** to **Oct 30 2026**, which is **113,516 TIA** a day. Four of the twenty were re-read on-chain this session and their terms are unchanged, so no vote has re-cut the schedule. On a Cosmos continuous-vesting account the vested portion becomes spendable automatically, with no claim transaction that could lag, so what is scheduled is what is released and no unclaimed backlog can hide behind the calendar. This block empties on **Oct 30 2026** and then pays nothing.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is the largest row on the page at **20.80M TIA**, and no chain read can see it. Celestia's own tokenomics documentation sets the release rule for each genesis bucket, and two of them are still running through Foundation-administered accounts rather than through on-chain locks: the research and ecosystem allocation of **200,925,000 TIA** releasing evenly from **Oct 30 2024** to **Oct 30 2027** — **183,493 TIA** a day — and the portion of the core-contributor allocation that sits outside the vesting accounts, another **47,580 TIA** a day. That rule is dated and published, which is what makes the row non-zero; it is not sized from any aggregator and not sized from the monitor. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: Celestia Labs is a going concern that acquired another engineering company in **Jul 2026**, and no estate holds TIA.

## Buy pressure: where new TIA goes

All four buy rows on TIA read **zero**, and the page is unusual in that three of the four zeros are measured rather than merely undocumented. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** because Celestia has no repurchase contract, no treasury buying mandate, and nothing announced in the window — the chain's own governance log shows the newest on-chain proposal is from **Dec 2025** and concerns bridge clients, not supply. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and the proof is the supply counter itself: it rose by the identical amount every day of the quarter, which is impossible if any part of it were being offset by a fee burn scaling with blockspace demand. Celestia routes fees to stakers with a **2%** community tax, and that community pool grew from **3.38M TIA** to **3.51M TIA** over the window — redistributed, never destroyed.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** for the plainest reason available: the Foundation is on the other side of this ledger, releasing a scheduled allocation quarter after quarter, and discloses no programme of buying TIA back. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** and moved the wrong way. Staked TIA is counted as circulating by the denominator and the unbonding wait is only **14 days** after CIP-37, so bonding is not supply removal in any case — but bonded supply also fell over the window, from **508.2M TIA** to **488.7M TIA**, which is **19.5M TIA** becoming liquid rather than being locked away.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs are enumerated on TIA and re-read on every rebuild. The first is the on-chain vesting escrow: **20** genesis continuous-vesting accounts still holding roughly **8.29M TIA** of undelivered principal, on a fixed schedule that ends **Oct 30 2026**. The second is the research and ecosystem allocation, of which about **80.4M TIA** remains unreleased after this quarter, running to **Oct 30 2027** through Foundation-administered accounts with no readable lock contract. The third is the un-escrowed remainder of the core-contributor allocation, roughly **3.47M TIA** still to come before it too stops on **Oct 30 2026**.

There is a fourth balance that is worth naming precisely because it is invisible on any schedule. Celestia's published release rules imply that about **1,083M TIA** should be unlocked by now, while the counted float stands at **957.1M TIA** — a difference of roughly **126M TIA** that has technically unlocked but is not counted as circulating. That is the size of the Foundation's undeployed pile, and it is the single largest discretionary overhang on the page: it is already unlocked, so nothing has to vest for it to move. If any of these four balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and in the fourth case it would arrive without a calendar entry to warn anyone.

## How TIA compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake L1s

Against its structural peers, Celestia's **monetary policy** is now among the tightest in the uncapped proof-of-stake class. It launched at **8%** with **10%** annual disinflation, was cut to about **5%** in **Jul 2025**, and cut again to **2.67%** under CIP-41 with a **1.5%** floor — so its live rate of **2.33%** sits below most continuous-emission L1s and below several chains that market themselves on scarcity. On the emission axis alone, TIA would read as a mild, predictable, steadily easing dilution.

What separates TIA from those peers is that emission is not where its supply comes from. A mature uncapped L1 whose genesis distribution finished years ago sees its inflation rate and its supply growth converge; Celestia is not there yet, and the gap is a factor of six — **2.33%** of policy inflation against roughly **15%** a year of actual float growth once the unlock is included. That places TIA closer to a recently-launched token still working through its cap table than to a settled L1, and it is why an investor reading Celestia's **2.33%** headline would be understating the real dilution by about five percentage points a quarter.

The third axis is the buy side, and here Celestia is at the weak end of its class by design. Chains that burn a base fee convert usage into supply destruction automatically, so demand for blockspace tightens the token; Celestia instead pays all blockspace revenue to stakers, which converts usage into yield rather than into scarcity. The practical consequence is that no amount of rollup adoption in the next quarter can offset a single TIA of the unlock — the only forces that can change this page's reading are calendar events, and both of the important ones land on the same day.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch **Oct 30 2026**, twice over. It is the day the last genesis vesting cohort empties, ending a **113,516 TIA**-a-day stream that has run for two years, and it is also the year-3 anniversary that steps the mint down by another **6.7%**, from **2.33%** to about **2.17%**. Both are already carried in the **+3.64%** forward reading, which is why the forward figure is lower than the trailing one; the quarter after that is where the real drop shows up, because the un-escrowed core-contributor stream stops on the same date. Watch the research and ecosystem release, which is the only large stream that survives past **Oct 30 2026** and runs on to **Oct 30 2027** at **183,493 TIA** a day. Watch the bonded-supply line, which fell **19.5M TIA** over this window — a continued slide means more of the float is liquid than the circulating figure alone suggests. And watch the governance forum for any CIP that would add a fee burn: Celestia has discussed burning fees for non-staked activity, and that is the one mechanism change that could put a real number in Buy #2 for the first time.

## Summary

TIA, the staking and blockspace token of the Celestia modular data-availability network, reads **+3.93%** net supply over the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026** and **+3.64%** forward under the MrNasdog Pressure Framework. The structural mechanism is a genesis unlock, not an emission: Celestia minted **6.62M TIA** in the window at a measured **2.33%** annual rate, while the vesting escrow and the Foundation's scheduled allocations released **31.0M TIA** between them, and nothing on the chain buys or burns a single token. The key risk is that this reading is entirely calendar-driven and therefore blind to discretion — roughly **126M TIA** has already unlocked into Foundation control without being counted as circulating, and it needs no vesting event to reach the market. The constraint, such as it is, is real but distant: Celestia has no supply cap at all, and the only ceiling on this page is the **1.5%** floor that the mint is slowly walking down toward.
