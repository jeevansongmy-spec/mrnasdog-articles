# ICP Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/icp/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/icp/inflation).*

**ICP** supply is growing: the Internet Computer created **3,173,860 ICP** between Aug 24 and Nov 22 2026 and burned **150,835 ICP**, a net rise of **+0.54%** in 90 days, with about **+0.57%** expected over the next 90. New ICP comes from two mints run by the network's own governance system, staking rewards cashed out and monthly pay for node operators, while the burn comes from apps turning ICP into computing fuel. There is no supply cap; the market tracker reads **+0.51%** for the same 90 days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 22 2026, ICP supply grew a net **+0.54%**: **3.17M ICP** created against **150.83K ICP** burned, on a circulating supply of **559.08M ICP**. The supply figure most trackers use rose **+0.51%** over the same days, a gap of only **0.03** points, so the coin page carries no warning note. For the next 90 days the Internet Computer is set to grow a little faster, about **+0.57%**, because the latest monthly payment to node operators was larger than the two before it. The short label is: an uncapped network that pays for staking and hardware in new ICP, where usage burns back about one coin in twenty.

## Sell pressure: where new ICP comes from

The first source is **protocol inflation** from staking. People lock ICP in voting accounts called neurons and earn rewards for voting on proposals. Those rewards build up as maturity inside each neuron, and new ICP is only created when the owner cashes it out. From Aug 24 to Nov 22 2026 cash-outs created **1,593,185 ICP**, a little under half of the **3.31M ICP** in rewards credited over the same days. The reward pot paid out each day sat near 53,000 ICP for the whole window; it was cut by roughly a third in April 2026 under the Mission 70 plan and has not moved since, so the next 90 days use the same pace.

There are no **vesting unlocks**. Every ICP from the 2021 launch has already been created. Seed backers still keep about **71.4M ICP** staked and early contributors about **12.6M ICP**, but those coins already count as circulating, so a release of them adds nothing to the ICP count.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks** book zero. The DFINITY Foundation moved coins in the window, taking **2.50M ICP** out of its own stake and sending **1.15M ICP** onward, but all of it was already circulating ICP. There is also no **long-term locked or bankruptcy** pool: no court or failed firm holds ICP for a later payout.

The second mint is **node provider rewards**. The companies that run the Internet Computer's machines are paid once a month in new ICP. The payments on Sep 13, Oct 14 and Nov 13 2026 came to **1,580,675 ICP**. Their size is set in a basket of major currencies and turned into ICP at the market rate, but never at less than a floor price. ICP has traded below that floor, so the Nov 13 2026 payment of **579,569 ICP** is the best guide to the next three. Those land around Dec 14 2026, Jan 13 2027 and Feb 13 2027 and add about **1.74M ICP**, which lifts the next-90-day sell side to about **3.33M ICP**.

## Buy pressure: where new ICP goes

The only working buy-side mechanism is the **protocol fee burn**. Apps on the Internet Computer pay for compute and storage with cycles, and cycles are made by destroying ICP. Small ledger transfer fees are destroyed too. Between Aug 24 and Nov 22 2026 the burn removed **150,835 ICP**, all but about 262 ICP of it as app fuel. That is roughly **4.8%** of the ICP created in the same days, and the next 90 days assume the same pace.

There is no **programmatic buyback** today. The new Cloud Engines service, released in mid-November 2026, plans to spend 20% of its revenue buying ICP and burning it, but no burn from it is on record yet; when it starts it will show up in the burn figure. The DFINITY Foundation runs no **foundation buy**. Staking is not a **new long-term lock** either: about **287M ICP** sits in neurons, and staked ICP still counts as circulating, so more staking removes nothing from the ICP count.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest overhang is not held by any team. It is the staking reward that has been earned but not yet cashed out: about **96.9M ICP** of unspent maturity plus **17.2M ICP** of maturity that owners have staked again, read from the network's governance data on Nov 22 2026. Around **46.3M ICP** of that maturity sits on neurons that no longer vote. None of it is ICP yet; it becomes new supply only when owners cash it out, which is what the protocol inflation row measures each rebuild.

The DFINITY Foundation's main account held about **3.16M ICP** on Nov 20 2026, after taking in 2.50M ICP from its own neurons and sending out 1.15M ICP during the window. This is one account, not a full list of Foundation holdings, and it is already part of circulating supply. Seed backers keep about **71.4M ICP** staked with another 7.9M ICP in staked maturity, early contributors about **12.6M ICP**, and the neurons' fund about **15.4M ICP** staked plus 5.8M ICP of maturity. We read these balances at every rebuild. If any of them falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the foundation row at the next refresh, and cashed-out maturity enters the protocol inflation row.

## How ICP compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains pay their validators from a fixed or slowly falling issuance rate and burn part of each transaction fee. The Internet Computer splits the job in two. Stakers who vote earn maturity, and node operators, who are paid companies rather than token holders, receive a monthly salary in new ICP. That makes ICP issuance a mix of a steady staking stream and a lumpy monthly payment, and the monthly part grows when the ICP price falls, because the salary is fixed in currency terms down to the floor.

The burn side also works differently. On Ethereum, users burn a base fee on every transaction. On the Internet Computer, users usually pay nothing; the app developer converts ICP into cycles in advance, and that ICP is destroyed. So the ICP burn tracks how much computing apps buy, not how many people send coins. Over these 90 days that burn covered about one coin in twenty that the Internet Computer created.

Like Ethereum and Solana, ICP has no hard supply cap, which sets it apart from halving-model coins with a fixed maximum. Unlike those two, ICP's reward rates are set by on-chain votes of the network's governance system, so cuts such as the April 2026 staking change and the node-pay reductions take effect without a client upgrade.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The next monthly node provider payment, due around **Dec 14 2026**, shows whether payments stay near 579,569 ICP; the next two follow around Jan 13 2027 and Feb 13 2027.

The governance vote on renewing the first-generation node providers, opened on **Nov 20 2026**, sets their machine counts and a 24-month term, which can move future node pay up or down.

The first **Cloud Engines** buy-and-burn, promised as 20% of the service's revenue after its mid-November 2026 release, would be the first new buy-side mechanism since the cycles burn.

The ICP price against the node-pay floor matters too: if ICP rises above the floor price, each monthly payment shrinks in ICP terms; while it stays below, the payment stays near its November size.

## Summary

ICP supply grew **+0.54%** in the 90 days to Nov 22 2026 and is set to grow about **+0.57%** over the next 90, so the Internet Computer is inflationary on its whole float. New ICP comes from staking rewards cashed out (**1.59M ICP**) and monthly node operator pay (**1.58M ICP**), while apps burned **150,835 ICP** as computing fuel. The key risk is the **96.9M ICP** of earned but uncashed staking rewards, which can turn into new ICP whenever owners choose. ICP has no supply cap, and only higher app usage or the planned Cloud Engines burn can close the gap between what is created and what is destroyed.
