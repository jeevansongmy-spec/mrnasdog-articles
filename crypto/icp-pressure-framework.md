## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 22 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ICP at +0.54% net**: sell pressure of **3.09M ICP** against buy pressure of **0.11M ICP**, on a circulating base of **556.05M ICP**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.46%** for the same window, a gap of **0.08 percentage points** — deep inside the half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships on this build. That tight agreement is not luck: the Internet Computer publishes its own cumulative burn counter and its own supply series, and the two reconcile against each other to the eighth decimal, which is rare. The forward reading of **+0.49%** is lower for one specific, dated reason rather than a trend guess. The honest label for ICP is **structurally inflationary, but with the only real usage-linked burn in its size class and a governance body that keeps voting the mint down**.

## Sell pressure: where new ICP comes from

ICP has no supply cap and no halving. New coins arrive on two independent schedules, and reading them as one number is the single easiest way to get the Internet Computer wrong. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1.18M ICP**: neuron voting rewards. The subtlety is that voting rewards are not minted when they are earned. They accrue as **maturity**, a claim recorded inside the governance canister, and ICP is only created when a holder chooses to convert that maturity into coins. Over this window the network credited roughly **5.41M ICP** of maturity and only **1.18M** of it turned into actual supply — a realisation rate of **21.9%**. Every framework that quotes the Internet Computer's headline reward rate as its inflation rate is quoting the accrual, not the mint, and overstates ICP's issuance by roughly four times.

The second mint runs on a calendar rather than a stream, which is why it sits in its own row. Sell #5, node provider rewards, is **1.91M ICP** — payments to the data-centre operators who run the machines, issued once a month. Exactly three landed inside the window: **696,678 ICP** on **Jun 14 2026**, **668,529 ICP** on **Jul 15 2026** and **541,884 ICP** on **Aug 14 2026**. Counting events rather than dividing the window by a nominal monthly interval matters here, because the payment either side of the window (**May 15 2026**) falls outside it and a naive division would smear it back in.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each is zero for a different reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Internet Computer's 2021 genesis allocations finished their token-sale vesting in **Jun 2025**; what is left dissolves out of eight-year neurons, and that bucket did drain **8.78M ICP** into liquid form over these 90 days, falling from **29.02M** to **20.24M**. It is booked at zero anyway, and that is the load-bearing judgement on this page: the Internet Computer counts staked neuron ICP as circulating supply, so those coins were already inside the denominator before they dissolved. Releasing them changes who can sell, not how many coins exist. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** because no watched wallet released anything. Sell #4 is **zero** because there is no bankruptcy estate anywhere in the Internet Computer's history.

## Buy pressure: where new ICP goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **0.11M ICP** and it is the only real buy-side mechanism the Internet Computer has — but it is real in a way most burns are not. There is no burn address to read. ICP converted into cycles, the fuel that pays for storage and computation, simply ceases to exist at protocol level, which means anyone looking for a dead wallet finds nothing and concludes the burn is fictional. The chain instead keeps its own cumulative burn counter, and across the window it ran from **2,762,074 ICP** destroyed since genesis to **2,872,064 ICP**. Of the **109,989 ICP** burned in the window, **109,859** was cycles conversion and **130** was ledger transaction fees. The pace is rising — **36.5K**, then **29.9K**, then **43.4K ICP** across the three months — which is what a usage-linked burn should look like when usage grows. It is also, still, under **4%** of the mint.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: the Internet Computer has no buyback contract and its governance record from **May 2026** onward contains no repurchase proposal of any kind. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** — the DFINITY Foundation publishes no accumulation programme and no wallet. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, and this is where a lot of ICP analysis quietly goes wrong. Neuron staking looks like absorption and is not: staked ICP counts as supply whether it is locked or free, so locking more of it removes nothing from the market. Over this window the neuron pool went the other way anyway, shrinking from **296.99M** to **288.67M ICP**.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest overhang on the Internet Computer is not a treasury at all. It is **95.79M ICP** of accrued neuron maturity — voting rewards that have been credited but never converted into coins. That is **17%** of the entire supply sitting as an unscheduled claim, convertible at any holder's discretion, and it is the reason ICP's realised mint can jump without any governance decision at all. Behind it sits the Neurons' Fund, the network-controlled pool that seeds new applications, holding **15.26M ICP** of stake plus **5.70M** of maturity, deployable only by a passing vote. Then the genesis bucket: **71.88M ICP** in seed neurons and **12.62M ICP** in early-contributor neurons, dissolving on eight-year schedules that run to roughly **May 2029**. The DFINITY Foundation's own holding is undisclosed and sits inside that genesis bucket; its governance neuron is a voting instrument holding a token amount, not a treasury. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ICP compares to other uncapped smart-contract chains

The Internet Computer belongs to the uncapped continuous-emission class — chains with no hard cap, where issuance is a governed parameter rather than a halving schedule. Against Ethereum, the mechanism comparison is instructive: both burn fees at protocol level with no burn address, and both fund security from new issuance. The difference is what the burn is paying for. Ethereum's base-fee burn scales with blockspace congestion; the Internet Computer's burn scales with compute and storage actually consumed, because ICP must be converted into cycles before a canister can run at all. That makes ICP's burn a cleaner proxy for real usage — and, at **4%** of issuance, a much smaller one.

Against Solana, the contrast is governance. Solana's emission follows a fixed disinflation curve written into the protocol; the Internet Computer's is voted, and this window contains the proof. NNS proposal **142681**, executed **Jul 6 2026**, set a minimum exchange rate of 2 XDR per ICP in node-reward computation. Node providers are owed a fixed amount in a basket currency, converted to ICP at the market rate; the floor means the network refuses to treat ICP as worth less than 2 XDR when settling that bill. ICP has traded between **1.48** and **1.93 XDR** all window, so the floor binds, and the **Aug 14 2026** payment came in **19%** below July's as a result. That is a governance body actively cutting its own issuance — the opposite of the exchange-token model, where a quarterly buyback is funded by revenue the issuer controls and can quietly stop.

Where ICP sits worst is against hard-capped chains. There is no ceiling, no halving, and no date after which issuance stops. What it has instead is a demand-linked destruction mechanism that a capped chain simply cannot have: if compute demand on the Internet Computer ever grew four-fold with issuance flat, ICP would turn deflationary by arithmetic rather than by promise.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The node-provider reward mints on **Sep 14 2026**, **Oct 14 2026** and **Nov 14 2026** are the three dated supply events in the window; each should land near **0.54M ICP** while the exchange-rate floor binds, and each will land higher the moment ICP trades above 2 XDR. NNS proposal **143190**, executed **Jul 31 2026**, cut the target network topology from 764 nodes to 612, which mechanically shrinks the node-provider bill as the reduction is implemented — watch whether the September and October payments step down again beyond the floor effect. The maturity conversion rate is the one number that can move without any announcement: a shift from **21.9%** realisation toward the accrued **5.41M** would double ICP's inflation with no vote at all. And the burn line is the upside case — three more months at the **43.4K ICP** pace of the last month rather than the **36.7K** average would be the first evidence that compute demand is compounding.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ICP at **+0.54%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 22 2026** and **+0.49%** forward, against a monitor reading of **+0.46%**. The structure is an uncapped chain minting on two clocks — node-provider rewards and neuron maturity conversion — with a genuine, protocol-level burn that destroys ICP as compute is bought. The key risk is the **95.79M ICP** of accrued maturity sitting unconverted and unscheduled, which can become supply at any holder's discretion. The key support is that the Internet Computer has no ceiling but does have a governance body that has now voted twice in one year to cut its own issuance, most recently by refusing to value ICP below 2 XDR when paying the people who run the machines.
