# RENDER Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/render/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/render/inflation).*

Render Network runs RENDER, a Solana token, on a burn-mint equilibrium: customers pay for rendering and AI jobs, RENDER is destroyed to settle each job, and node operators are paid from a fixed monthly emission set by governance. Over the 90 days to **Sep 25 2026**, RENDER jobs burned **333,356 RENDER** while **1.49M RENDER** of new emission reached the market — **240,000** minted straight to the node-reward wallet and **1.25M** released from the emission reserve. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads RENDER at **+0.22% net** over the last 90 days, against a supply-monitor reading of **+0.13%** — a gap of **0.09 percentage points**, inside tolerance. The RENDER emission schedule caps issuance at **5.9M RENDER** for 2026, and the RENDER burn is still a small fraction of it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 25 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **RENDER at +0.22% net**: **1.49M RENDER** of emission entered the counted float and **333,356 RENDER** was burned to settle jobs. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+0.13%**. The gap is **0.09 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so RENDER ships with **no data-conflict flag** — with one caveat recorded in our notes: the classified circulating figure the monitor divides by has barely moved for over a year while the chain kept minting, so its agreement with us says less than usual. The forward column reads **+0.21%**: three monthly mints of **60,000** each, one more quarterly release from the reserve, and burns held at the trailing rate. The label for RENDER is **mildly inflationary under burn-mint equilibrium**: issuance on a declining schedule, only partly offset by real usage burns.

## Sell pressure: where new RENDER comes from

All new RENDER comes from one place — the monthly RENDER emission set by governance vote. For 2026 the Render Network community approved **5.9M RENDER** under RNP-022, and the chain shows it minted on about the 23rd of each month as two legs: **432,132 RENDER** into an emission reserve and **60,000 RENDER** into the vault that pays node operators. Four monthly mints fell inside this window, on Jul 1, Jul 23, Aug 23 and Sep 23. The framework counts a coin once, when it enters the market. The node-reward leg enters at once, so Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **240,000 RENDER**; that vault passed every coin on to the operator reward pools and ended the window at exactly the balance it started with.

The reserve leg is not counted until it pays out, because the project's own supply accounting keeps the reserve outside circulating supply, and that accounting reconstructs the published circulating figure exactly. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is therefore the reserve's outflow: **1.0M RENDER** to a Foundation vault on **Aug 10 2026** and **250,000 RENDER** to the Foundation's payments wallet on **Aug 27 2026**, **1.25M RENDER** in total. The reserve received **1.73M** and released **1.25M**, and its balance rose by exactly the difference. It has paid out about once a quarter for a year, so the next 90 days carry one more release of the same size. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: the original RENDER allocations were distributed long ago and no cliff remains. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**. One more flow was tested and left out on purpose: the upgrade from the old Ethereum RNDR token. When a holder upgrades, the old coin is locked and a new RENDER leaves a pre-funded escrow, so the same coin simply changes chains; the **1.0M RENDER** minted into that escrow on **Sep 24 2026** is migration, not new supply.

## Buy pressure: where new RENDER goes

RENDER leaves the market one way: the RENDER burn. Under burn-mint equilibrium, every job is settled by destroying RENDER. Two burn wallets did this in the window, one for rendering jobs and a newer one for the Dispersed AI-compute marketplace, and every one of their transactions was read from the chain rather than taken from a report. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, came to **333,356 RENDER** over 90 days — **321,101 RENDER** from rendering jobs and **12,255 RENDER** from Dispersed — about **3,700 RENDER a day**, worth roughly **$0.49M** at the window's average price. Where the burned RENDER came from is also on the chain: about **112,000 RENDER** was bought with stablecoins inside the burn transactions themselves, and **250,000 RENDER** was sent to the rendering burn wallet by a Foundation vault on **Aug 26**, **Sep 18** and **Sep 22 2026**. Both are real burns — the coins were in the market and are now gone. We checked the burn two ways: the total supply of RENDER fell by exactly the burns we could see over a timed interval, and a sample spread across the whole window found no other wallet burning anything but dust. One project export of the same burns showed thousands of rows at zero, so it was not used.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** as a separate line: the open-market purchase exists only to feed the RENDER burn, so it is counted once, as the burn. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero** — the Foundation is funded from the emission reserve and does not buy RENDER to hold. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**: RENDER has no staking, and node operators are paid for work, not for locking coins. The forward column holds the burn at its trailing rate. A larger burn leg is written into RNP-023, which brings the Salad GPU network onto RENDER payments, but its burn wallets were not active on-chain in this window, so nothing is booked for it yet.

## Foundation and overhang

Three RENDER balances are watched. The emission reserve holds **3.00M RENDER**; it is the only one outside the counted float, it refills by **432,132 RENDER** every month, and each payout from it enters Sell #3. The node-reward vault holds **0.93M RENDER** and is already counted as circulating, so its payouts add nothing. A vault run for OTOY, the studio behind Render Network, holds **81.9M RENDER**, about **15.8%** of circulating supply; it moved less than four RENDER in 90 days and is already inside the counted float, so it is an overhang to watch rather than a flow. The upgrade escrow at **10.1M RENDER** only swaps old coins for new ones. Every one of these is an on-chain address re-read on each refresh. If the emission reserve's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and if the OTOY vault starts to sell, that is recorded as a change in the overhang.

## How RENDER compares to other DePIN and burn-mint networks

RENDER belongs to the class of DePIN tokens that pay suppliers from a schedule and try to cancel that issuance with usage burns. The design only turns deflationary when paid usage outgrows the emission. RENDER is not there: in this window the emission reaching the market was about **4.5 times** the RENDER burn. That is the common position for compute networks today — the one independent study of the whole DePIN category finds real revenue at only a few networks and compute revenue falling across most of that sector in late 2025, while one positioning network had already lifted its burn to about three-quarters of its issuance.

Against general-purpose Layer 1s, the contrast is in what the issuance pays for. A proof-of-stake chain mints to pay validators whether or not anyone uses it, and a fee burn offsets part of that. RENDER mints on a declining, capped schedule that governance resets each year, and every burned coin settles a job, so the burn line tracks how much work the network sells. Against exchange tokens that burn from a treasury, RENDER sits in between: part of its burn is bought on the open market inside the burn transaction, which is a real bid, and part is RENDER the Foundation sends to the burn wallet. Either way the burn is small, about **0.2%** of market value a year at current prices.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the monthly RENDER emission mints due on **Oct 23 2026**, **Nov 23 2026** and **Dec 23 2026**, each **60,000 RENDER** to the node-reward vault and **432,132 RENDER** to the reserve; a late mint, as happened in June, would slip one out of the window. Second, the next release from the emission reserve, expected around November on its quarterly rhythm; its size moves Sell #3 directly. Third, the Year 4 emission allocation: the 2026 allocation was voted in December 2025, so a vote on the 2027 schedule is likely before year-end and would reset the rate from January. Fourth, Salad's move onto RENDER payments under RNP-023 — once its burn wallets go live, the burn line could grow several-fold, which is the one change that could turn the reading toward flat.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads RENDER at **+0.22% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.21%** over the next 90. The mechanism is burn-mint equilibrium on Solana: a governance-set emission of **5.9M RENDER** for 2026 funds node operators and the Foundation, and jobs are settled by burning RENDER — **333,356 RENDER** this window against **1.49M** of emission reaching the market. The key risk is the gap between the two: until paid usage grows several times over, issuance keeps adding to the float, and a larger emission vote for 2027 would widen it. The ceiling is the capped emission schedule, which shrinks the room for new RENDER every year the network grows into it.
