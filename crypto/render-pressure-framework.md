**TL;DR.** RENDER, the payment token of Render Network, still grows faster than it burns. The Render Network emission program minted three monthly tranches of **492,132 RENDER** inside the window — **1.48M RENDER** in all — and a Render Network Foundation wallet left off the project's published list released **0.36M RENDER** more from older reserves. Against that, the burn-and-mint escrows destroyed **0.31M RENDER** to settle rendering and compute jobs. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads RENDER at **+0.29% net** over the last 90 days against a supply-monitor reading of **−0.12%** — a gap of **0.41 percentage points**, inside tolerance. RENDER has no hard stop on issuance: a Foundation multisig controls the mint, and the yearly emission budget is set by a community vote.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 14 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **RENDER at +0.29% net**: **1.83M RENDER** reached the market through emission and treasury releases, while **0.31M RENDER** was destroyed, about one RENDER burned for every six that reached the market. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **−0.12%**. The gap is **0.41 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so RENDER ships with **no data-conflict flag** — though the monitor reading moves around from one day to the next. The forward column reads **+0.25%**, slightly softer, because the treasury payment schedule behind most of this window's reserve release has only one tranche left. The label for RENDER is **mildly inflationary under burn-and-mint**: a fixed emission budget that job burns have not yet caught.

## Sell pressure: where new RENDER comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1.48M RENDER**, and it follows a published budget. RNP-022, the Year-3 burn-and-mint allocation, set a budget of **5.9M RENDER** for Dec 20 2025 to Dec 19 2026, and the Render Network emission program mints it as a fixed **492,132 RENDER** tranche each month, split between a network rewards vault and a Foundation operations vault. Every mint in the project's history was read and decoded this session: three tranches landed in the window, on **Jul 1 2026**, **Jul 23 2026** and **Aug 23 2026**. The mint is not renounced — a Render Network Foundation multisig controls it, with an on-chain ceiling of **24M RENDER** a day — so RENDER supply is a policy, not a constant. Mints that replace legacy RNDR one-for-one during the Ethereum-to-Solana upgrade add nothing: **1,136,569 RENDER** left the Solana upgrade reserve this window while the same amount of RNDR arrived in the Ethereum bridge custody, so they cancel.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: no RENDER vesting cliff falls inside this window or the next 90 days. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0.36M RENDER**, and it is the finding of this rebuild. The Render Network Foundation publishes a list of **18** wallets, and that list ended the window heavier. But one Foundation multisig is missing from it — the operating wallet that pays monthly staff in RENDER and USDC and funds both burn escrows — and it fell from **1.69M RENDER** to **0.16M RENDER**. Reading every Foundation wallet together, **1.83M RENDER** left the treasury against **1.48M RENDER** of fresh emission, so **0.36M RENDER** came out of reserves minted before the window. Most of it matches the Salad investment agreed in RNP-023: a US$1M convertible note paid in RENDER at about **US$166,667** a month, five payments of which have now gone to an exchange deposit address. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero** — no estate and no long-dated lock exists.

## Buy pressure: where new RENDER goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **0.31M RENDER**, and it is the only buy row with a figure. Render Network settles work through burn-and-mint: when a rendering or compute job is paid for, the escrow buys RENDER and destroys it. Two Foundation escrows ran **14,896** burns inside the window — the main job escrow destroyed **293,504 RENDER** and the Dispersed AI-compute escrow **11,669 RENDER**. Both were checked for the pass-through failure, where tokens move through an escrow without being destroyed. They are true burns, not transfers: the main escrow's balance fell from **88,326** to **19,658 RENDER** while it burned, every transfer in and out reconciles to its opening and closing balance, and RENDER total supply, read several times this session, fell by exactly the escrows' burns between reads, with nothing else burning or minting. The dollars behind the purchases, **US$210,337**, arrived from the Foundation's operating wallet, and a treasury vault also handed the main escrow **100,000 RENDER** to burn on **Aug 26 2026**.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: the escrows do bid for RENDER on the open market, but only inside the same transaction that burns it, so that bid is counted once, in Buy #2. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero** — the treasury is spent down, and the USDC it sends ends in burns. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**: Render Network has no staking, bonding or lock contract, so no RENDER can be taken off the market that way. The forward column holds the trailing burn pace, because no documented change to the burn rules landed in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest team-linked RENDER overhang is a partner vault on the Foundation's own list holding **81.90M RENDER**, about **15.8%** of circulating supply; it barely moved this window. The emissions vault holds **2.57M RENDER**, the operations vault **0.90M**, a second treasury vault **0.48M**, and the reward escrows smaller balances. The off-list operating multisig now holds **0.16M RENDER**, and a transfer wallet that has passed earlier Foundation batches to a large custody account is holding **0.60M RENDER** that has not moved on yet. The Ethereum-to-Solana upgrade reserve, at **9.57M RENDER**, is inventory for coins already counted and is not an overhang. Every one of these balances is re-read on each refresh; if any of them falls between refreshes and the RENDER does not end in a burn, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How RENDER compares to other burn-and-mint compute tokens

RENDER sits in the burn-and-mint class of DePIN tokens, where a network pays its hardware providers with newly minted coins and removes coins when customers pay for service. The design only turns deflationary once usage burns more than the emission schedule mints, and on RENDER that point is still far off: the fixed monthly emission alone is roughly five times this window's burn. That contrasts with uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1s, whose issuance is tied to staking and paid to validators rather than set as a yearly budget, and with capped proof-of-work coins, whose issuance falls on a halving schedule no vote can change. RENDER's supply is steered by governance, which cuts both ways — the community can shrink the budget, and it has also approved pulling future emission forward.

Against exchange and fee tokens that burn from reserves or buy back from revenue, the distinguishing feature of RENDER is that its burn is tied job by job to paid work, so the burn line moves with demand for GPU time rather than with a treasury decision. Measured at the escrows' own purchase prices, this window's RENDER burns were worth roughly **US$0.47M**. RENDER's other structural difference is custody. A large share of non-circulating RENDER sits in Foundation and partner wallets rather than in a vesting contract with a public calendar, so releases show up as treasury movements, not scheduled unlocks.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads RENDER at **+0.29% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.25%** over the next 90, with **1.83M RENDER** reaching the market against **0.31M RENDER** destroyed. The structural mechanism is burn-and-mint: a fixed monthly emission of **492,132 RENDER** set by governance, offset by job-by-job burns that reconcile exactly on-chain but remain a fraction of issuance. The key risk is treasury custody — this window's reserve release came from a Foundation wallet missing from the published list, and a partner vault of **81.90M RENDER** sits outside any schedule. There is no hard ceiling: the Foundation-controlled mint can issue far more than the budget in a single day, so the RENDER supply path depends on the next emission vote.
