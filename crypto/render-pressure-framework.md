**TL;DR.** Render Network runs a burn-and-mint equilibrium, and the mint side is still well ahead of the burn side. Over the 90 days to **Sep 1 2026** the network created **1.78M RENDER** — three fixed emission tranches of **492,132** RENDER each, plus **306,361** RENDER released from Foundation treasury wallets — and destroyed **0.21M RENDER** across **11,503** GPU rendering and AI compute job payments. That is **7.0** RENDER minted for every one burned. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads RENDER at **+0.30% net** supply growth against a supply monitor reading of **−0.29%**, a gap of **0.59 percentage points** that carries a data-conflict flag.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Render Network is a real business with a real burn, and a burn that is still far too small to matter to its own supply. The Pressure Framework reads the 90 days to Sep 1 2026 at **+0.30%** net new RENDER against a circulating base of **518.77M**, and projects **+0.24%** for the next 90 days on the same emission schedule. The supply monitor reads **−0.29%** over the same window — a disagreement of **0.59 percentage points**, past the framework's half-point tolerance, so the page carries a data-conflict chip. The deep walk on Sep 1 2026 traced the direction of the disagreement to the forced retirement of RENDER's legacy contract announced **Jul 28 2026**, which moves coins between chains rather than destroying them, and narrowed the gap without closing it. On the chain that actually settles every mint and every burn, the token's supply rose **1.27M** over the window. RENDER is structurally inflationary on a fixed, governance-voted schedule that does not care whether anyone rents a GPU.

## Sell pressure: where new RENDER comes from

Almost all of it is protocol emission. The Render Network mint pays out a fixed tranche of **492,132** RENDER roughly every 30 days, and three of them landed inside the window — on **Jul 1 2026**, **Jul 23 2026** and **Aug 23 2026** — for **1,476,396** RENDER of brand new supply. The tranche is identical every time because it is set by the Year-3 burn-and-mint equilibrium budget of **5.9M** RENDER voted for the period **Dec 20 2025** to **Dec 19 2026**: twelve tranches, a fixed annual total, and no sensitivity whatsoever to how much GPU work the network actually sells. The interval drifts — measured gaps between tranches this year ran from 19 to 40 days — but the annual budget absorbs that drift, so a fast month changes when the coins arrive and never how many.

Vesting unlocks contribute nothing. RENDER's 2017 public sale and 2018 private sale carried no vesting at all, and the original release schedule is finished — there is no cliff anywhere in the window and none in the next 90 days. Everything still unissued is the emission budget already counted above. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks contributed **306,361** RENDER: a single **300,000** RENDER transfer out of a Foundation treasury vault on **Aug 26 2026** to a wallet that does not appear on the project's own published address list, plus **6,361** RENDER in three small transfers from the partner treasury on Jun 26 and Jul 16 2026. That release is sporadic and has no published calendar, so the framework projects none of it forward. The long-term-locked and bankruptcy row is empty: there is no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to this token.

## Buy pressure: where new RENDER goes

The burn is the whole buy side, and it is genuine. Over the window **210,806** RENDER were destroyed across **11,503** separate job payments, funded by **$320,964** of customer credit spent on rendering and AI compute. These are real destroy instructions on the token itself, not transfers to a wallet nobody holds the key to — and that distinction is load-bearing on this coin. The holding account the burns are drawn from ended the window at **89,150** RENDER against **88,395** at the start, a rise of just **755**. Anyone measuring RENDER's burn by watching that balance would have understated it **279** times over. It is a pass-through, topped up by the treasury — including a **100,000** RENDER refill on **Aug 26 2026** — and drained by job payments at roughly two thousand coins a day.

The other three buy rows are zero, and each for a structural reason. There is no programmatic buyback: the open-market purchase that sits inside the burn-and-mint loop is the same flow as the burn, and counting it separately would count one flow twice. There was no open-market Foundation buy in the window — the only treasury movement was outward, and it already sits on the sell side. And there is no new long-term lock, because Render Network has no staking contract and no bonding requirement: node operators are paid out of emissions rather than by locking RENDER up, so nothing is removed from the tradable float that way.

## Foundation and overhang

The project publishes its own wallet registry, and it is unusually complete: 18 addresses holding **96.64M** RENDER between them, about **18.6%** of circulating supply. The dominant pool is a partner treasury vault holding **81.90M** RENDER with no published release schedule; it moved just **6,361** RENDER in 90 days, which is why its row value is zero rather than a projection. Next is a bridge escrow holding **9.88M** RENDER, which drew down **987,122** RENDER over the window — supply-neutral, because it only releases one-for-one against legacy RENDER retired on the old chain. Then a network emissions vault at **2.57M**, an operations vault at **0.90M**, a second treasury vault at **0.60M** and a rewards escrow at **0.57M**, with a long tail of small escrows under a hundred thousand each.

Outflows from the emissions and operations vaults are deliberately not counted twice: those coins were already booked as sell pressure when they were minted, and the vault payout is the second half of the same flow. The one movement that does count is the Aug 26 2026 release out of the registry entirely. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled-unlocks row at the next refresh — and on the partner treasury vault in particular, an **81.90M** overhang is roughly **55** tranches of emission sitting in one wallet.

## How RENDER compares to other burn-and-mint compute networks

The instructive comparison is not to a fixed-cap chain but to any network whose burn is tied to demand while its mint is not. On a hard-capped proof-of-work coin the issuance schedule is written into consensus and cannot be voted; on RENDER the annual emission is a governance decision renewed every December, which cuts both ways — it can be reduced when the community chooses, and it also stayed at **5.9M** a year through a window in which the network burned barely a fifth of one tranche. On a fee-burning smart-contract chain the burn scales with every transaction on the network, so heavy usage can flip supply negative outright. RENDER's burn scales only with paid GPU jobs, and those jobs generated **$320,964** in 90 days against a market capitalisation near **$739M**. That is the comparison worth writing down carefully, because the direction is easy to invert: the market capitalisation is roughly **570** times the annualised value of the fees that fund the burn, not the other way round.

Against exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks, the difference is who pays. An exchange burns out of corporate profit, so its burn is as large as its business and entirely discretionary. Render Network burns out of customer job payments, so its burn is smaller but fully mechanical — every rendering job destroys coins whether the Foundation wants it to or not. That makes RENDER's burn a clean, honest usage meter, which is exactly why it is worth reading: at **210,806** RENDER a quarter it says the compute business is real and still early. And against uncapped continuous-emission layer-one networks, RENDER at least has a ceiling — **644.2M** RENDER, of which **518.77M** already circulate, leaving roughly **125M** to be issued across the remaining schedule.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the next emission tranche, due around **Sep 22 2026** on the roughly 30-day cadence — another **492,132** RENDER of new supply, with two more expected before the window closes. Second, the Year-3 emission budget expires on **Dec 19 2026**, and the successor budget has to be proposed and voted before then; the vote itself will likely land just outside this window, but the proposal and its debate will not, and a cut would be the single largest change available to this ledger. Third, the partner treasury vault holding **81.90M** RENDER — static for months, and the only wallet on the registry large enough to change the reading on its own. Fourth, whether the sporadic treasury release of **Aug 26 2026** repeats; one firing is an event, two would be a cadence, and a cadence would be projected forward. Fifth, the retirement of the legacy contract forced on **Jul 28 2026**, which is what pushed the cross-chain supply reading and the on-chain reading apart in the first place, and which should stop distorting the comparison once migration finishes.

## Summary

Render Network is one of the few crypto networks whose burn is a direct, verifiable meter of paying customers — **11,503** job payments and **$320,964** in 90 days, destroyed coin by coin on chain. It is also a network whose mint runs **7.0** times that burn on a schedule that is set once a year by vote and is completely indifferent to demand. The result is **+0.30%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+0.24%** projected for the next 90 — small in absolute terms, but growth rather than scarcity, and it will stay growth until either the compute business gets roughly seven times larger or the emission budget is cut. The key risk is the **81.90M** RENDER partner treasury vault, unscheduled and larger than four years of emission. The ceiling is **644.2M** RENDER, and the mint authority is still live, so that ceiling is a governance promise rather than a contract-level impossibility.
