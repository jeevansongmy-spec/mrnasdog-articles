# BCH Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Bitcoin Cash has the simplest supply story the Pressure Framework tracks: mining is the only thing that makes a BCH, and nothing at all destroys one. Over the last 90 days the Bitcoin Cash chain minted **40,137.5 BCH** across **12,844** blocks at a **3.125 BCH** subsidy, against zero buyback, zero burn and zero new locked supply — a net **+0.200%** of circulating supply reaching the market, against **+0.194%** on the inflation monitor. With roughly **96%** of the **21M** hard cap already mined and the next halving not due until around **Apr 2028**, that figure is about as low as a chain that removes nothing can go.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The Bitcoin Cash ledger nets to **+0.200%** of circulating supply over the trailing 90 days and projects the same **+0.200%** forward, because the mechanism that produced it does not change until the halving at block height **1,050,000**. The inflation monitor reads **+0.194%** over the same window, a gap of **0.006** percentage points — the tightest agreement in the catalog, and far inside the tolerance that would trigger a data-conflict flag, so no flag ships. The reason the two agree so closely is structural rather than lucky: on Bitcoin Cash there is exactly one supply mechanism to disagree about. Block subsidy in, nothing out. The label to carry away is a **quiet, capped, single-mechanism chain** — mildly inflationary by design, with the rate stepping down on a schedule nobody controls.

## Sell pressure: where new BCH comes from

Sell pressure on Bitcoin Cash is one row and one row only. The block subsidy has been **3.125 BCH** since the halving at height **840,000** in **Apr 2024**, and the chain produced **12,844** blocks between the window's opening block and its closing block — a figure counted from two height reads, not divided out of an annual number. That matters more than it sounds. Bitcoin Cash blocks arrived **605.4 seconds** apart against the protocol's **600-second** target, so the chain ran **0.89%** slow. The subsidy is paid per block and the difficulty algorithm adjusts difficulty rather than the reward, so nothing in the protocol absorbs that drift — assuming a calendar-perfect 144 blocks a day would have claimed **40,500 BCH** instead of the **40,137.5 BCH** actually minted, an over-statement of **362.5 BCH**. The mined figure closes exactly against the chain's own issuance record, to the satoshi.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each is zero for a different structural reason. Vesting unlocks are **0** because Bitcoin Cash had no premine, no sale and no investor allocation — the chain's issuance record shows every coin in existence arrived as a block reward, so there is no schedule left to release. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are **0** because no foundation, treasury or team wallet was ever created, and the 2020 proposal to divert part of the block reward to developers was rejected outright. Long-term locked and bankruptcy is **0** because the one genuine estate — Mt. Gox — moved no Bitcoin Cash inside the window, and its trustee's most recent Bitcoin Cash repayment notice still dates from **Mar 2025**.

## Buy pressure: where new BCH goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **0**, and on Bitcoin Cash that is a statement about the protocol rather than about effort. There is no programmatic buyback because there is no protocol income to fund one: every satoshi of transaction fee is swept into the coinbase and paid to the miner who found the block, so the network never accumulates a balance of its own. There is no foundation buy because there is no foundation entity. There is no new long-term lock because Bitcoin Cash is pure proof-of-work — no staking, no bonding, no protocol lockup exists to put a coin into.

The fee burn row deserves the detail, because a zero there is the easiest number on any page to get wrong. It was checked on both surfaces this build, not asserted. Total issuance rose across the window by exactly the mined amount and has never fallen, so nothing was destroyed at the protocol level. Separately, the two unspendable addresses Bitcoin Cash inherited from Bitcoin were read at both ends of the window: neither has ever spent a coin in its life, which proves each is a genuine sink rather than a wallet passing coins through, and between them they took in **0.001 BCH** over 90 days against **40,137.5 BCH** mined. Two stray sends by users. Reconciled against a mechanism rather than a label, that is dust — so the burn row books at zero as a measured result.

## Foundation and overhang

Bitcoin Cash has no team-controlled overhang in the ordinary sense — no unscheduled allocation, no Labs treasury, no buyback wallet, no DAO. On a chain this old the largest addresses are almost entirely exchange cold storage holding depositors' coins, which is precisely why they are excluded from the count rather than booked as project supply. One real overhang remains: the **Mt. Gox** rehabilitation estate, whose Bitcoin Cash tranche was sized at roughly **143K BCH** in the trustee's 2023 plan and has been drawn down by payouts since, with no current balance disclosed. The estate is opaque — no Bitcoin Cash address has been published — so it is tracked by trustee disclosure on a fortnightly walk rather than by a chain read, and the repayment deadline now stands at **Oct 31 2026**. If that balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the bankruptcy row at the next refresh. A separate wrinkle worth naming: these coins already sit inside the circulating figure, so a payout moves float rather than supply — it can press on price without moving the inflation reading at all. Pointing the other way, a Nasdaq-listed company has been building its own BCH position since **Dec 2025** and has not disclosed the size; that is an outside buyer, not an overhang, and it is not booked in either direction.

## How BCH compares to other capped halving-model chains

Bitcoin Cash belongs to the small family of coins whose issuance is a fixed subsidy halving on a block interval under a hard cap — Bitcoin itself and Litecoin are its closest structural relatives. Against Bitcoin the mechanism is not merely similar but shared: both are capped at **21M**, both halve every **210,000** blocks, and both are mined with SHA-256, which means the same mining pools point hashpower at whichever chain pays better at that moment. That is a genuine mechanism link, and it is why the block interval on Bitcoin Cash wanders further from its target than a chain with a dedicated miner base would — the **0.89%** slow run this window is normal behaviour, not a defect, and it is why the block count has to be measured every build.

The sharper contrast is on the buy side, where the capped family splits. Bitcoin Cash removes nothing, so its net supply change equals its gross issuance exactly; a chain with a fee burn or a buyback can net toward zero or below without touching its emission schedule. Bitcoin Cash cannot, and the reason shows up in the fee economy. Transaction fees across the whole 90-day window came to **23.3 BCH** — around **$67** a day, or roughly **$24.6K** a year against a **$5.23B** market capitalisation, about **0.00047%**. That is the lowest of the four major proof-of-work coins on this measure, below Dogecoin at roughly **0.0022%**, Litecoin at roughly **0.0035%** and Bitcoin at roughly **0.006%** — and the leg that is small is the fee side, not the market value, since Bitcoin Cash sits around the **20th** largest coin by capitalisation. The consequence is mechanical rather than rhetorical: at that level of fee income, the coinbase is almost entirely subsidy, so issuance on Bitcoin Cash is a near-pure function of how many blocks got found.

Against uncapped emitters the comparison inverts. A chain with continuous or tail emission has no terminal date for new supply; Bitcoin Cash has **915.6K BCH** left to mine before the cap, about **4.4%** of maximum, and each halving cuts the rate in half again. Supply growth here is a decaying series with a known end, and that is the whole of its inflation case.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The **Mt. Gox** repayment deadline of **Oct 31 2026** falls inside the forward window and is the single dated item that could add a row; it has already been pushed back three times, so nothing is booked against it, but a Bitcoin Cash-denominated estate movement would land in the bankruptcy row immediately. Second, the realised block interval: because the reward is paid per block and nothing compensates for pace, a sustained shift in SHA-256 hashpower toward or away from Bitcoin Cash changes issuance directly, and the current **0.89%** slow run is worth re-measuring rather than assuming. Third, the annual protocol upgrade cycle — the **May 15 2026** upgrade shipped scripting changes and the node release inside this window declared no network changes at all, and none of the proposals now under discussion for the next cycle touches the subsidy, the emission schedule or the cap; a proposal that did would be the first monetary change in the chain's history. Fourth, the undisclosed corporate Bitcoin Cash position: a filing that finally names a quantum would make it measurable as demand. Fifth, the halving at block height **1,050,000**, still around **Apr 2028** and far outside this window, but the event that eventually halves this page's only sell row.

## Summary

Bitcoin Cash mints **40,137.5 BCH** a quarter from block rewards and removes none of it, for a net **+0.200%** supply growth that the inflation monitor independently reads at **+0.194%**. The structural mechanism is Bitcoin's own monetary policy inherited unchanged and never modified since the **Aug 2017** fork: a **21M** hard cap, a subsidy halving every **210,000** blocks, no premine, no treasury and no burn. The key risk is not dilution but the absence of any offset — with a fee economy of about **$67** a day, there is no burn or buyback mechanism that could turn this page deflationary, so the only path lower is the next halving. The ceiling is the number that anchors everything else: **915.6K BCH** remain to be mined, about **4.4%** of the maximum that will ever exist.
