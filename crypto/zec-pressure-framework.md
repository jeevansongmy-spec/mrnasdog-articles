# ZEC Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Zcash mints new ZEC only through proof-of-work mining, at a block subsidy of **1.5625 ZEC** that halves roughly every four years against a hard **21 million** cap. Over the 90 days to **Sep 3 2026** the Zcash chain produced **103,198** blocks and issued **161.2K ZEC**, of which **19.3K ZEC** was routed by consensus straight into a lockbox that holds no keys and cannot move without a network upgrade. With no buyback and no fee burn anywhere on the Zcash ledger, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.84%** net for the window and **+0.84%** for the next 90 days, against our supply monitor at **+1.44%** — a gap of **0.60 percentage points**, wide enough that a data-conflict flag ships with the page.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 3 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ZEC at +0.84% net**, with the forward view also at **+0.84%**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+1.44%**, so the gap is **0.60 percentage points** and a monitor-gap flag is warranted. The framework side of that number is measured, not assumed. The Zcash chain's own supply meter, read from node state at both ends of the window, moved from **16,750,456.42 ZEC** at block **3,366,927** to **16,911,703.29 ZEC** at block **3,470,125** — a rise of exactly **161,246.875 ZEC**, which is **103,198** blocks multiplied by the **1.5625 ZEC** Zcash block subsidy, with nothing left over. Of that gap, **0.11** points is the locked 12% the monitor counts as supply and the framework counts as removed; the rest is the monitor's own supply series, which moved **78.1K ZEC** more than the Zcash chain actually minted over the same span — a reclassification into circulating, not new issuance. Zcash is **mildly inflationary on a hard-capped halving schedule**, with an unusual twist: 12% of every block subsidy is created and instantly locked away.

## Sell pressure: where new ZEC comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **161.2K ZEC**, and it is the only non-zero sell row on the entire Zcash ledger. Zcash is proof-of-work, so mining is the sole mint: no staking emission, no treasury issuance, no bridge minting. The block count is measured rather than assumed. Node reads at both window ends show the chain moving from block **3,366,927** to block **3,470,125** in **7,775,981 seconds**, a realised interval of **75.35 seconds** against a **75 second** consensus target. The target would have implied **103,680** blocks; the chain actually produced **103,198**. Because the Zcash block subsidy is indexed to block height rather than to elapsed time, and no protocol constant rescales it when the interval drifts, that difference is real: trusting the stored parameter would have over-stated Zcash issuance by **482** blocks, or about **753 ZEC**.

The **1.5625 ZEC** subsidy splits three ways, and the split was verified against the coinbase transaction at both ends of the window: **1.25 ZEC** to the miner, or **80%**; **0.125 ZEC** to the Zcash community-grants funding stream, or **8%**; and **0.1875 ZEC** into the deferred development-fund lockbox, or **12%**. The miner leg and the grants leg are two outputs of the same coinbase transaction, created by the same consensus rule at the same height, so neither can be paid without the other — one flow, booked once, inside Sell #1. The grants address is a pass-through rather than a store: it has received **92,965.38 ZEC** over its life and spent **91,997.38** of it, holding **968.01 ZEC** today, so its receipts are ordinary new supply reaching the market.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each for a different structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because Zcash has no vesting: the founders' share of the block reward ended at the first halving in **Nov 2020**, and the company and foundation funding streams ended at the second halving in **Nov 2024**. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because nothing fired in the window — the grant multisig that holds the disbursed dev-fund coins has not moved since **Apr 14 2026**, and no public schedule covers the next 90 days. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero because Zcash launched by mining in 2016 with no investor allocation to unwind and has no bankruptcy estate distributing coins.

## Buy pressure: where new ZEC goes

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **19.3K ZEC**, and it is the only non-zero buy row. The lockbox is a Zcash value pool that holds no keys: **12%** of every block subsidy accrues into it, and nothing can leave until a network upgrade defines a spending path. Read from node state at both window ends, the lockbox grew from **41,349.00 ZEC** to **60,698.625 ZEC** — a rise of **19,349.625 ZEC**, which is exactly **103,198** blocks at **0.1875 ZEC**. The identity closing to the decimal also proves the outflow side was zero: no lockbox disbursement fired inside the window. The pool has been opened once, at the network upgrade of **Nov 2025**, when its contents were paid out to a 2-of-3 multisig for grant funding.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero: Zcash earns no protocol revenue to deploy, because block fees are paid to whoever mines the block and nothing in Zcash consensus buys ZEC on the open market. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero, and it was checked on both surfaces the framework requires. On the supply side, the Zcash chain supply meter rose by exactly the mint, so nothing was destroyed anywhere. On the dead-address side, Zcash has no burn opcode, no burn funding stream and no dead-address convention — the coinbase at both window ends carried the full **1.375 ZEC** paid-out subsidy plus that block's fees, confirming fees reach miners rather than disappearing. Removing a share of Zcash fees from circulation is on the current coinholder ballot, not in consensus. Buy #3, foundation buy, is zero because both the grants stream and the lockbox are funded out of the block subsidy itself, so neither absorbs coins that are already trading.

## Foundation and overhang

Four Zcash overhangs are tracked, totalling about **225.3K ZEC**, or roughly **1.3%** of circulating supply. The largest single pool is the foundation's own treasury at **85,412.34 ZEC** on its last published count of **Mar 31 2026**, which is disclosed in periodic reporting rather than at a published address, so it is monitored through official disclosure. Next is the 2-of-3 grant multisig that received the one-time lockbox disbursement, holding **78,183.41 ZEC** and untouched since **Apr 14 2026**. Third is the keyless lockbox itself at **60,698.63 ZEC**, which is capacity without a schedule — it cannot move at all under current Zcash consensus. Fourth is the community-grants stream address at **968.01 ZEC**, which is a conduit rather than a reserve.

Each of these is read fresh on every rebuild — the on-chain wallets from node state, the foundation treasury from its published reporting. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. Corporate ZEC treasuries and exchange wallets are deliberately excluded from this count: they are third-party holders, not Zcash team-controlled supply, and the framework only enumerates what an identified group could choose to release.

## How ZEC compares to other capped halving-model privacy chains

The privacy sector splits cleanly on one mechanism question, and Zcash sits on the scarce side of it. A tail-emission privacy chain never stops issuing: after its main emission curve exhausts, a fixed per-block reward continues forever, so supply grows without limit and the inflation rate merely decays toward zero as the denominator swells. A capped halving-model privacy chain like Zcash does the opposite — the subsidy steps down by half on a fixed block height, the last coin is eventually mined, and the schedule is knowable years in advance. Zcash has issued about **80.5%** of its **21 million** cap; the third halving arrives at block **4,406,400**, roughly **Nov 2028** at the measured interval, and cuts the subsidy to **0.78125 ZEC**.

Zcash differs from the other capped chains in one respect that matters to this ledger: the dev-fund lockbox. Most capped proof-of-work chains route **100%** of the block subsidy to miners, so gross issuance and market-facing issuance are the same number. Zcash routes **12%** into a pool that is minted and then immobilised, which is why the framework books a sell row of **161.2K ZEC** and a buy row of **19.3K ZEC** where a plain capped chain would show one row and no offset. That is also the mechanical source of most of the monitor gap, since an aggregator counting supply cannot see the difference between a coin a miner can sell and a coin no key controls.

On fee economics, Zcash sits at the subsidy-funded extreme of the field rather than the fee-funded one. Measured from the Zcash coinbase, block fees run about **0.0011 ZEC** per block, roughly **480 ZEC** a year, or about **$392K** against a market capitalisation near **$13.8B** — around **0.003%** of market value a year, and under **0.1%** of the roughly **654K ZEC** the Zcash protocol pays miners over the same year. Security is essentially all subsidy, which is exactly what makes the halving schedule and the current issuance ballot consequential. Shielded usage, measured the same way, held **5.03M ZEC** or **30.1%** of chain supply on **Jun 5 2026** and **4.88M ZEC** or **28.9%** on **Sep 3 2026**, with **3.87M ZEC** migrated into the new shielded pool opened in **Jul 2026** — movement between pools, not issuance, so both sides of the ledger book zero for it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The Zcash coinholder ballot closes **Sep 14 2026**, and it asks whether to replace the halving schedule with a smoother issuance curve, when fee reissuance should begin, and whether to cut block time from **75** seconds to **25** — any of which would re-base Sell #1 if a later network upgrade enacts it. The lockbox will cross roughly **80,000 ZEC** during the window at the current accrual rate, which raises the stakes on the disbursement question that has no specified mechanism yet. The 2-of-3 grant multisig has been still since **Apr 14 2026**; a spend from it is the single most likely trigger for a non-zero Sell #3. The foundation's next quarterly report is the only way its **85,412.34 ZEC** figure refreshes. And the third halving at block **4,406,400** remains far outside this window, so nothing changes the **1.5625 ZEC** subsidy before **Nov 2028**.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Zcash at **+0.84%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Sep 3 2026** and the same again forward — mild, steady, and fully explained by one mechanism, because ZEC mints only through mining and has no vesting, no buyback and no burn. The structural feature that distinguishes Zcash from a plain capped proof-of-work coin is the **12%** lockbox: **19.3K ZEC** of the **161.2K ZEC** issued was created and immediately immobilised, and that split is the source of most of the **0.60 point** gap against our supply monitor. The key risk is governance rather than emission — a coinholder ballot closing **Sep 14 2026** is asking whether Zcash should abandon halvings for a smoother curve, and Zcash security is almost entirely subsidy-funded, with fees at about **0.003%** of market value a year. The ceiling is unchanged: **21 million ZEC**, roughly **80.5%** already mined, next halving **Nov 2028**.
