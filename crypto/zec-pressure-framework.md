# ZEC Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Zcash mints new ZEC only through proof-of-work mining, at a block subsidy of **1.5625 ZEC** that halves roughly every four years against a hard **21 million** cap. Over the last 90 days the Zcash chain produced **103,181** blocks and issued **161.2K ZEC**, of which **19.3K ZEC** was routed by consensus into a deferred development fund that cannot be spent without a network upgrade. With no buyback and no fee burn anywhere on the Zcash ledger, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.84%** net for the window and **+0.84%** for the next 90 days, against our supply monitor at **+0.98%** — a gap of **0.14 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no data flag ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ZEC at +0.84% net**, with the forward view also at **+0.84%**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change at **+0.98%**, so the gap is **0.14 percentage points** — comfortably inside the framework's tolerance, and no monitor-gap flag is warranted. The reconciliation is measured rather than assumed: a block explorer read at both ends of the window shows the Zcash chain moved from block **3,347,218** to block **3,450,399**, a span of **103,181** blocks. The target block time of **75 seconds** would have implied **103,680** blocks, so Zcash ran about half a percent slow and the target would have overstated the mint. Multiplying observed blocks by the observed block subsidy gives the issuance directly. Zcash is **mildly inflationary on a hard-capped halving schedule**, with an unusual twist: an eighth of every block subsidy is created and immediately locked away.

## Sell pressure: where new ZEC comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **161.2K ZEC** and it is the only non-zero sell row on the entire Zcash ledger. Zcash is proof-of-work, so mining is the sole mint: no staking emission, no treasury issuance, no bridge minting. The split is what makes Zcash distinctive. The full block subsidy is **1.5625 ZEC**, and the Zcash development fund rules route **80%** of it to miners — **129.0K ZEC** over the window — and **8%** to the Zcash Community Grants committee, which is **12.9K ZEC**. The remaining **12%**, or **0.1875 ZEC** a block, produces no coinbase output at all and instead accrues to the deferred development fund lockbox. Those coins are nonetheless created: the Zcash specification describes the lockbox as a pool of issued funds tracked by the protocol, a drafted disbursement would pay **78,750 ZEC** out of it, and the chain's own supply series grows by the full subsidy rather than the **1.375 ZEC** that appears as coinbase outputs. So the whole subsidy belongs in Sell #1, and the locked leg is answered on the buy side instead of quietly deducted from the mint.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** and permanently so. Zcash never ran a token sale with investor vesting. The original Founders' Reward was a block-subsidy carve-out that ran from launch in 2016 until the first halving in **Nov 2020**, and the successor company and foundation streams expired in **Nov 2024**. No schedule remains. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** for the window because no team-controlled balance was observed reaching the market, though real overhangs sit behind that zero and are covered below. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: Zcash has no bankruptcy estate and no identified long-term locked seller. It is worth stating plainly that the shielded pool — about **4.36M ZEC**, roughly a quarter of supply — is not a lock and not a burn. Shielded coins are ordinary spendable ZEC whose owners have chosen privacy, so the shielded pool changes nothing in this ledger.

## Buy pressure: where new ZEC goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**. Zcash has no revenue stream and no contract that acquires ZEC from the market. Corporate treasuries accumulating ZEC on exchanges are open-market demand, not a protocol mechanism, so they do not enter this row. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**: Zcash transaction fees are paid to miners rather than destroyed. A drafted proposal would remove **60%** of transaction fees from circulation as part of the Network Sustainability Mechanism, and it sits on a coinholder ballot opening **Aug 25 2026** — but it is unadopted, so it burns nothing today. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** because the Zcash Community Grants committee is funded straight out of the block subsidy rather than by bidding for ZEC on an exchange.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **19.3K ZEC** and it is the row that makes Zcash read correctly. Consensus sends **12%** of every block subsidy into the deferred development fund, which over **103,181** blocks is **19.3K ZEC**. This is not a discretionary treasury decision that could reverse next quarter; it is a lock written into the Zcash consensus rules, and the fund cannot be spent without a network upgrade adopting a disbursement mechanism. Booking the mint gross in Sell #1 and the lock explicitly in Buy #4 leaves the net identical to netting the lockbox out of issuance, but it states the mechanism honestly on both sides and it makes the overhang visible rather than invisible.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled pools are tracked for Zcash. The first and largest is the **deferred development fund lockbox**, which accrues **0.1875 ZEC** every block. Two different numbers get quoted for it, and the difference matters. Since the fund started in **Nov 2024** it has accrued about **135,881 ZEC** in total — that is simply the block count multiplied by the per-block share, and a dev-fund dashboard reports the same running total. But the fund has been drawn once: a one-time disbursement of **78,750 ZEC** went to a two-of-three multisig held by the Zcash Foundation, the Electric Coin Company and Shielded Labs. The balance sitting in the lockbox today is therefore about **57.1K ZEC**, not the lifetime total.

The second pool is the **Zcash Community Grants operating treasury**, holding about **82.5K ZEC** plus a cash balance, funded by the **8%** subsidy leg that is already counted inside Sell #1 — so grants paid from it move already-issued coins rather than creating new ones. The third is the **Zcash foundation treasury**, which discloses a dollar figure but no coin-denominated balance, and is therefore tracked as an opaque overhang through official disclosure. For all three the trigger is the same: if any balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ZEC compares to other hard-capped proof-of-work chains

Zcash belongs to the Bitcoin structural family: proof-of-work, a fixed **21 million** cap, and a block subsidy that halves on a fixed block-height schedule rather than drifting with usage or participation. Against Bitcoin itself, the differences that matter for inflation are block pace and carve-out. Zcash targets **75-second** blocks against Bitcoin's ten minutes, producing roughly eight times as many blocks a day, and the third Zcash halving lands at block **4,406,400**, projected for around **Nov 2028**. More importantly, Bitcoin pays **100%** of its subsidy to miners, while Zcash routes **20%** away from them — **8%** to grants and **12%** into a lock. Among halving-model chains that carve-out is close to unique, and it is why the Zcash float grows more slowly than its headline subsidy implies.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains the contrast is sharper. A proof-of-stake L1 paying validators a percentage-of-stake yield has an emission rate that responds to participation and can be retuned by a governance parameter, with no terminal ceiling. Zcash issuance is a step function written into consensus: it cannot be raised, it halves on schedule, and it terminates. Against other privacy chains the divergence is structural rather than cosmetic — chains shipping a permanent tail emission accept a small perpetual inflation floor in exchange for a permanent security budget, whereas Zcash accepts the Bitcoin trade-off of a hard cap and an eventual fee-only security model. Against exchange tokens and fee-burn L1s, Zcash sits at the opposite pole: those assets generate revenue and route it into quarterly buybacks or a base-fee burn that can push net supply negative in a busy quarter, while the Zcash net supply change is simply its mint less its lock. That makes the reading unusually predictable, but it also means the framework will not score ZEC as deflationary while the subsidy runs.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First and most important, the **NU7 coinholder vote**: the snapshot is taken **Aug 24 2026**, the ballot opens **Aug 25 2026** and runs about eighteen days to **Sep 12 2026**, and it is only valid if **1,000,000 ZEC** participates. Coinholders will decide whether to replace halvings with a smoother issuance curve, when transaction-fee reissuance should begin, whether to remove **60%** of transaction fees from circulation, and whether to cut block times from **75 seconds** to **25 seconds**. Any one of those would rewrite this ledger; none of them is adopted, so none of them moves a row today. Second, any further disbursement from the **57.1K ZEC** lockbox, which would move a large block from overhang into Sell #3 in a single step — it has been drawn once already. Third, the aftermath of the **Ironwood** upgrade, which activated **Jul 28 2026** at block **3,428,143** after an emergency fix on **Jun 3 2026**; it sealed the older Orchard shielded pool behind a turnstile that bounds what can leave it, a supply-integrity event that left emission untouched. Fourth, drawdown of the Zcash Community Grants treasury as grants are paid. Fifth, the third halving at block **4,406,400** around **Nov 2028** — far outside this window, but the only scheduled event that changes the mint rate itself.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Zcash at **+0.84%** net over the last 90 days and **+0.84%** forward, against a supply monitor at **+0.98%** — a **0.14 percentage point** gap that needs no flag. The structural mechanism is a hard-capped proof-of-work block subsidy of **1.5625 ZEC**, split **80%** to miners and **8%** to community grants, with the final **12%** created and locked into a development fund that consensus will not release without a network upgrade — and with no buyback and no fee burn anywhere on the buy side. The key risk is governance rather than emission: the **57.1K ZEC** lockbox has already been drawn once and is one decision away from another release, while the **NU7** ballot opening **Aug 25 2026** could replace the halving schedule outright. The ceiling bounds everything else — **21 million** ZEC, about **80%** already mined, halving again around **Nov 2028**.
