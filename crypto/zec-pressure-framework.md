# ZEC Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

ZEC, the coin of the Zcash privacy chain, is inflationary at a steady, known pace: every Zcash block creates **1.5625 ZEC**, and the **103,184 blocks** of the last 90 days created **161,225 ZEC**, while nothing was burned or bought back. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Zcash supply up **+0.95%** over 90 days and the same for the next 90 days, against a monitor reading of **+0.97%**. The 21M ZEC cap and the next halving, due around **Nov 2028**, are what slow it down.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 29 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **ZEC at +0.95% net**: the sell side added **161,225 ZEC** of block rewards and the buy side removed **0 ZEC**, measured against **16.95M ZEC** in circulation. The inflation monitor, which reads the supply count day by day, shows **+0.97%** for the same period, a gap of only **0.02 percentage points** — well inside the 0.5-point band, so no warning chip is shown. Zcash’s own supply count rose by exactly the block rewards across the window, to the last decimal. The next 90 days look the same: **+0.95%**. The label that fits: **a capped proof-of-work chain still in its high-issuance years, with no burn**.

## Sell pressure: where new ZEC comes from

Protocol inflation is the only sell row with a number, and it is large: **161,225 ZEC** in 90 days, about **1,791 ZEC a day**. Zcash pays **1.5625 ZEC** for each mined block, and since the Nov 2025 network upgrade that reward is split three ways: **80%** to the miner (128,980 ZEC this window), **8%** to the Zcash Community Grants fund (12,898 ZEC) and **12%** to the Zcash lockbox (19,347 ZEC), the pot that coinholders now direct. The chain produced a block every **75.4 seconds** on average, slightly slower than its 75-second target, so we count the blocks that really happened rather than the target — the target would have claimed about 775 ZEC too many.

Vesting unlocks are **0**, and permanently so. Zcash never sold coins to investors; its founders were paid through a founders’ reward taken from each block, which ended in Nov 2020. There is no vesting contract and no locked team bucket left to open.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are **0**. The team-linked balances exist — they are listed in the overhang section below — but every one of them is already counted as circulating, so spending or selling them moves coins inside the market rather than adding new ones. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is also **0**: no estate or trustee holds ZEC for release, and the US exchange-traded fund that holds ZEC buys its coins on the open market.

## Buy pressure: where new ZEC goes

All four buy rows are **0**. There is no programmatic buyback: no contract, treasury or company program buys ZEC for the project. There is no protocol fee burn yet either. Zcash fees are tiny — about **2.8 ZEC a day** in recent weeks — and today all of them go to the miner, which is why the supply count rose by exactly the block rewards and not a coin less.

The Zcash Foundation reports no ZEC purchases; it pays for its work from its own reserves. And there is no new long-term lock: Zcash has no staking, and the lockbox that keeps 12% of each block reward sits inside the circulating count, so those coins never leave the float. Coins moved into Zcash’s private pools — about **4.9M ZEC**, close to 29% of supply — are still counted as circulating too. Privacy hides who holds a coin; it does not take the coin off the market.

## Foundation and overhang

We track four team-linked balances on Zcash. The largest single pot is the **grants multisig** controlled by three Zcash organisations, which received a one-time **78,750 ZEC** from the lockbox in Nov 2025 and now holds **78,183 ZEC**; it did not move in this window, and coinholders vote each quarter on grants paid from it. Next is the **Zcash Foundation**, with **78,986 ZEC** at its latest quarterly report. The **lockbox** itself holds **66,298 ZEC** and grows by 0.1875 ZEC every block; it can only pay out through a network upgrade. The Zcash Community Grants address holds about **699 ZEC** and pays grants out as the block rewards arrive.

We read the lockbox and the two on-chain addresses from the chain at every rebuild, and the Foundation from its quarterly reports. All four are already inside the circulating count, so a release from any of them would move coins within the market rather than add new supply. Even so, we watch them: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow is reviewed as Foundation and unscheduled supply at the next refresh.

## How ZEC compares to other capped proof-of-work chains

Zcash copied Bitcoin’s shape: a hard cap of **21M** coins, rewards cut in half roughly every four years, and mining as the only way new coins appear. The difference is timing. Zcash launched in 2016 and has had only two halvings, so about **0.95%** new supply every 90 days is roughly five times Bitcoin’s current pace. Bitcoin is further down the same curve; Zcash will get closer to it after its next halving, due around **Nov 2028**, when the block reward drops to 0.78125 ZEC.

Against Monero, the other big privacy coin, the design is the opposite. Monero has no cap and pays a small fixed tail reward forever, so its new supply keeps shrinking as a share of the total but never stops. Zcash issues faster today but stops at 21M. And unlike Monero or Bitcoin, Zcash sends **20%** of every block reward to development funds rather than to miners — the coins still enter the market, just through grants instead of mining sales.

Neither Zcash nor these peers has a fee burn that matters. Ethereum-style fee burning can offset new supply on busy chains; on Zcash the planned fee removal would take out only about **90 ZEC** by late December — a rounding error next to 161,225 new coins.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Nov 5 2026 — the NU7 upgrade (target).** Blocks move from 75 seconds to 25 seconds, and the reward per block is cut to a third, so new ZEC per day stays the same. The final start block is due to be fixed on **Oct 20 2026**, after a test-network launch on **Oct 6 2026**.

**The new fee removal.** NU7 also starts taking 60% of transaction fees out of circulation into a reserve that is paid back out from 2031. At today’s fee levels that is about 90 ZEC by late December; we will book it once it is live and measurable.

**Old private coins become frozen.** NU7 ends the oldest transaction format, so about **22,300 ZEC** still in the original 2016 private pool becomes unspendable. Those coins stay in the circulating count, so the Pressure Framework number does not change, but it is supply that can no longer trade.

**Coinholder grant votes.** The third-quarter grant vote closes on **Sep 29 2026**; payouts come from the grants multisig and move coins already in the market. A new lockbox payout would need its own network upgrade, and none is scheduled.

## Summary

ZEC is inflationary on a fixed, predictable schedule: Zcash creates **1.5625 ZEC** per block, **161,225 ZEC** over the last 90 days, and nothing burns or buys any of it back, so supply grows about **+0.95%** every 90 days. The Pressure Framework and the inflation monitor agree within **0.02 percentage points**. The main risk is simply that steady flow of new coins, 80% of it to miners who usually sell to cover costs, while the team-linked pots are already counted in the market. The ceiling is hard: ZEC stops at **21M**, and the next halving, around **Nov 2028**, cuts the flow in half.
