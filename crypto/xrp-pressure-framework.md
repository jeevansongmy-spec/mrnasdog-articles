# XRP Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

XRP has no protocol inflation at all — every one of the **100B XRP** was created in the XRP Ledger's first ledger in 2012, and the network has no way to issue more. The supply pressure comes from Ripple's escrow instead: **1.00B XRP** unlocks on the first of each month, but reading the locked escrow contracts directly shows the balance falling only from **32.60B** to **31.70B** across this window, so the realised release was **900M XRP**, not the **3.00B** the calendar promised. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.43%** net against our supply monitor at **+1.31%** — a gap of **0.12 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 3 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **XRP at +1.43% net**. The sell side is the Ripple escrow release, measured on-chain as a realised outflow rather than as a calendar entitlement: the locked escrow balance fell by **900M XRP** across the window, even though **3.00B XRP** unlocked on paper across three monthly firings and **2.10B XRP** was written straight back into fresh escrow contracts. The buy side is a mere **30.0K XRP** of transaction-fee burn — real, permanent, and a few thousandths of one percent of the release. Our supply monitor, which tracks the realised change in circulating XRP, reads **+1.31%** over the same window. The gap is **0.12 percentage points** and needs no explaining away. XRP is best described as **non-inflationary by protocol but schedule-inflationary on the active float** — the total supply of XRP can never grow, yet the monthly escrow unlock keeps expanding the tradable share of a fixed pie.

## Sell pressure: where new XRP comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and it is zero for a reason no governance vote can undo. The XRP Ledger has no minting operation in its transaction set. The entire 100B XRP supply was created in a single genesis ledger in 2012, and every read of the ledger's total-supply field since has been lower than the one before it. Two direct on-chain reads bracket this window: **99,985,651,053 XRP** at the start and **99,985,621,078 XRP** at the end. Supply fell. There is no emission curve, no block reward, no staking issuance, and no inflation parameter to tune — XRP is one of the rare assets where the protocol-inflation row is permanently empty.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the whole XRP supply story, and it is where the published schedule and the tradable float pull apart. Ripple wrote 55B XRP into dated on-ledger escrow contracts in December 2017; **1.00B XRP** expires out of escrow on the first of each month. Three such unlocks fell inside this window, on **Jul 1 2026**, **Aug 1 2026** and **Sep 1 2026**, which is **3.00B XRP** of calendar entitlement. But the escrow contracts are readable on-chain, and reading them settles it: total escrowed XRP was **32.60B** at the start of the window and **31.70B** at the end. Ripple re-escrowed **2.10B XRP** into fresh contracts within days of each unlock, in six separate escrow-creation transactions. The realised release — the number the Pressure Framework ships — is therefore **900M XRP**, or roughly **300M XRP** a month. Booking the gross 3.00B would have invented 2.1B of XRP sell pressure that never touched a market.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** for this window, and it is zero on measurement rather than on assumption. Ripple's identified non-escrow wallets on the XRP Ledger held **3.79B XRP** at both ends of the window; the balance rose by about **4M XRP** rather than draining, so no unscheduled XRP release happened outside the escrow calendar. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy distributions — is **zero** too, and permanently: no bankruptcy estate holds XRP and no trustee schedule distributes it, unlike several of the exchange-linked tokens the framework also tracks.

## Buy pressure: where new XRP goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, and this is the single most common misreading of XRP. Ripple ran a repurchase programme reported at **$750M** in March 2026, but that programme buys Ripple company shares from early investors and employees. It does not buy XRP, and it removes no XRP from the float. There is no fee-funded buyback contract, no revenue-to-token pipe, and no protocol mechanism that converts XRP Ledger activity into XRP purchases.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is the only genuinely deflationary force XRP has, and it is real but tiny: **30.0K XRP** over the window, about **333 XRP** a day. Every XRP Ledger transaction destroys its fee outright; the coins are subtracted from the ledger's own supply meter and are not swept to any address. That matters for how the burn is verified. The XRP Ledger has no dead address to read — the four unspendable accounts on the chain moved a combined **0.8 XRP** across the whole window — so the burn is proved by identity instead. Over a sample of 400 ledgers carrying **49,355 transactions**, the sum of every fee field came to **6.843207 XRP** and the total supply fell by exactly **6.843207 XRP**. Had those fees merely moved somewhere, the supply figure would not have changed at all. It did, to the drop. The reason the burn is small is fee design, not absent usage: the XRP Ledger prices a payment at ten drops — a fraction of a US cent — as an anti-spam floor rather than as revenue, on a chain clearing millions of transactions a day. Annualised, the XRP fee burn is worth roughly **0.0002%** of XRP's market capitalisation, which places XRP at the very bottom of every fee-economy reading the framework has taken.

Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**. Ripple is a structural seller of XRP, not a buyer, and it has never run an accumulation programme. A listed treasury company is building an XRP position of roughly **473M XRP** and faces a shareholder vote on **Sep 30 2026**, but open-market buying by a third party moves existing XRP between holders rather than absorbing new supply, so it does not enter the buy ledger. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** by construction: the **2.10B XRP** Ripple re-escrowed this window is already netted inside the realised release on the sell side, and counting the same flow twice would double the coin's entire buy column.

## Foundation and overhang

Two XRP overhangs are identified and watched. The first is the on-ledger escrow itself, holding **31.70B XRP** at **Sep 3 2026** across 100 escrow objects on Ripple-published accounts — roughly a third of all XRP in existence, released at 1.00B a month with about 70% re-locked each cycle. Its undrawn remainder from this window alone is the **2.10B XRP** that went back into escrow rather than to market. The second is Ripple's non-escrow operating wallets, holding **3.79B XRP** at the same date, sold through private and institutional channels on no published calendar. Both are read directly from the XRP Ledger on every refresh. Exchange custodial wallets and unidentified large XRP holders are deliberately excluded: the first belong to depositors and the second cannot be read as a unit. If either enumerated overhang's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How XRP compares to other payment-settlement chains

Against hard-capped, halving-model chains, XRP looks unusual in one direction and ordinary in the other. Bitcoin and Litecoin have a fixed cap enforced by a decaying block subsidy, so their supply grows slowly and permanently until the cap is reached; XRP has no subsidy at all and its total supply has been falling since 2012. On the total-supply axis, XRP is stricter than any halving chain. But XRP's float is governed by a corporate release calendar rather than by miner issuance, and that is the axis where the pressure lives — the fixed 100B XRP is real, and so is the 31.70B XRP that has not yet been let out of escrow.

Against uncapped continuous-emission layer-1s such as Solana or Cardano, XRP is the mirror image. Those chains mint new units every epoch to pay validators, so their sell pressure is protocol-generated, predictable and never ends. XRP mints nothing, so its sell pressure is finite by construction: the escrow will eventually run dry. The trade is that a proof-of-stake chain's emission is transparent and fixed in code, while XRP's realised release depends on one company's monthly decision about how much to re-escrow — which is why the framework reads the escrow contracts rather than the calendar.

Against exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks and burns, XRP has no offsetting buy leg whatsoever. Those tokens route real revenue into destroying supply, which can make a token net-deflationary even while units unlock. XRP's only burn is the anti-spam transaction fee, worth about **0.0002%** of market cap a year — thinner even than the fee economies of the memecoins and forked payment chains the framework has measured, and far too small to offset a **900M XRP** quarterly release. Among payment-and-settlement assets, XRP's profile is a hard cap with a corporate float schedule and effectively no burn.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The **Oct 1 2026**, **Nov 1 2026** and **Dec 1 2026** escrow unlocks are the three dated events that set the next reading — each releases **1.00B XRP** gross, and the number that matters is how much Ripple re-escrows within the following days. A re-escrow ratio below the recent ~70% would push realised XRP release above 900M and lift the framework's net reading; a ratio above it would tighten the quarter. The listed XRP treasury company's shareholder vote on **Sep 30 2026** is worth tracking as a demand signal, though it moves float rather than supply. On the protocol side, the XRP Ledger amendment votes running through late 2026 — the 3.3.0 tokenisation bundle, and the lending amendments still well short of the validator support they need — cannot mint, burn or unlock XRP, so none of them can move this metric; they are watched only so a supply-affecting proposal would be caught if one ever appeared. Finally, any regulatory change that alters Ripple's ability to release additional XRP from escrow ahead of schedule is the one event that would break the monthly release rhythm the whole reading rests on.

## Summary

XRP is a fixed-supply asset with a scheduled float. No XRP has been minted since the genesis ledger in 2012 and none can be, so the coin's protocol inflation is permanently zero and its total supply only falls. The pressure is Ripple's escrow: **3.00B XRP** unlocked across this window, **2.10B XRP** was re-locked within days, and the realised release read directly off the escrow contracts was **900M XRP**, which is **+1.43%** of circulating XRP over 90 days against a monitor reading of **+1.31%**. The key risk is that the release rate is a corporate decision rather than a protocol rule, and the offsetting buy pressure is negligible — a fee burn of **30.0K XRP** per quarter, worth about **0.0002%** of market cap a year. The ceiling is genuine and near: **31.70B XRP** remains in escrow, and when it is exhausted XRP becomes a fully circulating fixed supply with no issuance at all.
