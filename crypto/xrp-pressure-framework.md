# XRP Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads XRP as inflationary on the traded float: **+1.33% net** over the last 90 days and **+1.33%** projected for the next 90. No XRP is ever minted — the XRP Ledger has no block reward and no staking reward — so the new supply is Ripple paying XRP out of its own wallets: **838.5M XRP** reached outside accounts in 90 days, against a fee burn of only **34,648 XRP**. The ceiling is fixed: Ripple still holds **31.7B XRP** in escrow and several billion more in its wallets, and every coin it pays out adds to the float until that stash runs out.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the window from Jul 1 2026 to Sep 29 2026 the XRP float grew by a net **+1.33%**: **838.5M XRP** of new sell pressure against **34,648 XRP** destroyed in fees, on a circulating supply of **62.88B XRP**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.90%** over the same 90 days, a gap of **0.43 percentage points**. That gap is small, so no warning chip ships: the monitor's supply count is updated only every few weeks and lags the ledger, while our number is read straight from the ledger at both ends of the window. The next 90 days project the same **+1.33%**, because Ripple pays out almost every day and the escrow refills its wallets every month. XRP is a fixed-supply coin that behaves like a steadily inflating one: **a company-funded float, growing about 1.3% a quarter**.

## Sell pressure: where new XRP comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. All 100B XRP were created when the XRP Ledger started in 2012; there is no mining, no block reward and no staking payout, and no transaction type can create XRP. The ledger's own supply only falls: it read **99,985,641,396 XRP** at the start of the window and **99,985,606,749 XRP** at the end.

Vesting unlocks are **0** on this page, even though XRP has the best-known vesting schedule in crypto. Ripple's escrow opens up to **1B XRP** on the 1st of every month, with 32 monthly dates left from Oct 1 2026 to May 1 2029. In this window the escrow opened 1B XRP on Aug 1 2026 and again on Sep 1 2026, and Ripple locked **700M XRP** back each time — plus 700M on Jul 1 2026 from the July opening — so the escrow actually grew, from **31.6B** to **31.7B XRP**. The coins an opening releases land in Ripple's own wallets, which are not counted as circulating. They reach the market only when Ripple pays them out, which is the next row.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks carry the whole sell side: **838.5M XRP**. We read every Ripple-labelled account and every Ripple escrow at both ends of the window, then every transaction those accounts made in between; the payouts to outside accounts match the fall in Ripple's total holdings to within a fraction of one XRP. By month, Ripple paid out **352M XRP** in July, **259M XRP** in August and **228M XRP** in September, almost all of it from one operating wallet in transfers almost every day. Ripple's own distribution count rose at a similar pace, about 816M XRP per 90 days. With no dated end in sight and three more escrow openings ahead, the next 90 days hold the same **838.5M XRP**.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy is **0**. No court, trustee or estate is releasing XRP. Spot XRP funds and listed XRP treasury companies bought on the open market, and the roughly 103M XRP stolen from an exchange on Sep 24 2026 was already circulating, so none of these adds new supply.

## Buy pressure: where new XRP goes

Programmatic buyback is **0**. Ripple's 2026 buyback bought back company shares, not XRP, and no contract takes XRP off the market.

The protocol fee burn is **34,648 XRP** over 90 days, about **385 XRP a day**. Every XRP Ledger transaction pays a small fee in XRP that is destroyed rather than paid to anyone, and deleting an account destroys its reserve too. A second count from explorer totals, day by day, came to about 30,459 XRP for the same days, close enough to confirm the size. The burn offsets about 0.004% of Ripple's payouts.

Foundation buy is **0**: no announcement and no on-chain flow shows Ripple buying XRP this window. New long-term lock is **0** as well. Ripple re-locked **2.1B XRP** into fresh escrows, but those coins never left Ripple's hands and were never part of the float, so locking them again takes nothing off the market. XRP has no staking.

## Foundation and overhang

Ripple is the one team-controlled overhang, and it is large. The on-ledger escrow holds **31.7B XRP** across eight Ripple accounts, opening up to 1B XRP a month until May 2029. Ripple's publicly labelled wallets hold about **3.6B XRP** more, down from about 4.5B at the start of the window; the largest single wallet holds about 1.33B XRP. The gap between total supply and the circulating count, **37.1B XRP**, is about 1.8B XRP larger than escrow plus the labelled wallets, which points to Ripple wallets without a public label; Ripple's own count put its undistributed XRP at about 5.1B on Aug 30 2026.

We re-read the escrow and every labelled Ripple wallet from the ledger at each rebuild. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the foundation row at the next refresh.

## How XRP compares to other pre-mined payment chains

XRP belongs to the small group of payment chains whose whole supply was created on day one and handed to an issuer to distribute. That sets it apart from mined coins such as Bitcoin or Litecoin, where new supply comes from a block reward that falls on a fixed halving schedule, and from proof-of-stake chains, where validators are paid in newly minted coins. XRP has neither. Its supply cap is real — 100B XRP, shrinking slowly through the fee burn — but the float still grows, because the issuer releases its stash on its own timetable.

Stellar is the closest mechanism match: all XLM exists already and a foundation holds and distributes a large share. Hedera works similarly, with a treasury releasing pre-minted HBAR over time. Against those, XRP stands out for two things: its release ceiling is written on the ledger itself, as time-locked escrows anyone can read, and the monthly opening is visibly larger than what Ripple keeps, since most of each 1B returns to escrow. The pace of new supply is therefore set by Ripple's spending, not by a fixed protocol rate.

The fee burn is also far smaller than the burns on busier smart-contract chains. At about 385 XRP a day it is a rounding error next to the payouts, so unlike a chain where heavy use can make supply shrink, XRP's float grows regardless of how busy the ledger gets.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The escrow opens up to **1B XRP** on Oct 1 2026, Nov 1 2026 and Dec 1 2026; a re-lock smaller than the usual 700M would leave Ripple more to pay out. The pace of the operating wallet's payouts matters most: it slowed from 352M XRP in July to 228M XRP in September, and a return to the July pace would lift the next reading. Watch for any Ripple wallet without a public label starting to pay out, which would show up as a jump in the circulating count. The pending ledger amendments that hold majority votes, including batch transactions, change no supply rule. The roughly 103M XRP taken in the Sep 24 2026 exchange hack is already circulating and does not change the count, whatever happens to it next.

XRP is a fixed-supply coin whose traded float still grows: the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.33%** over the last 90 days and projects the same for the next 90. Nothing is minted; the growth is Ripple paying out **838.5M XRP** a quarter from wallets refilled by a monthly escrow, against a fee burn of only **34,648 XRP**. The key risk is the size of the stash — **31.7B XRP** in escrow plus several billion in Ripple wallets — and the fact that its pace is Ripple's choice. The ceiling is the 100B XRP created at launch; supply can only reach the market from that fixed pool.
