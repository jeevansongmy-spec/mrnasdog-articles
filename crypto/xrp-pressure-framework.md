XRP has no protocol inflation at all — every one of the **100B XRP** was created in the XRP Ledger's first ledger in 2012, and the network has no way to issue more. The supply pressure comes from Ripple's escrow instead: **1.00B XRP** unlocks on the first of each month, but reading the locked escrow contracts directly shows the balance falling only from **32.90B** to **32.00B** across this window, so the realised release was about **900M XRP**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.44%** net against our supply monitor at **+1.04%** — a gap of **0.39 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 17 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **XRP at +1.44% net**. The sell side is the escrow release, measured on-chain as a realised outflow: the locked escrow balance fell by **900M XRP** across the window, even though **3.00B XRP** unlocked on paper across three monthly firings and **2.10B XRP** was written straight back into fresh escrow contracts. The buy side is a mere **31.3K XRP** of transaction-fee burn — real, but a few thousandths of one percent of the release. Our supply monitor, which tracks the realised change in circulating XRP, reads **+1.04%** over the same window, about **646M XRP** of float growth. The gap is **0.39 percentage points**, inside tolerance, and its residual has a mechanical explanation: part of each month's released escrow is still sitting in Ripple's own wallets that the circulating measure has not yet counted as float. XRP is best described as **non-inflationary by protocol but schedule-inflationary on the active float** — the total can never grow, yet the escrow release keeps expanding the tradable share of a fixed pie.

## Sell pressure: where new XRP comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and it is zero for a reason no governance vote can undo. The XRP Ledger has no minting operation in its transaction set. The entire 100B XRP supply was created in a single genesis ledger in 2012, and every read of the ledger's total-supply field since has been lower than the one before it. Two direct on-chain reads bracket this window: **99,985,658,819 XRP** at the start and **99,985,627,524 XRP** at the end. Supply fell. There is no emission curve, no block reward, no staking issuance, and no inflation parameter to tune — XRP is the rare asset where the protocol-inflation row is permanently empty.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — carries the entire sell side at **900M XRP**, and it is Ripple's escrow read as a realised flow. Under contracts written in December 2017, exactly **1.00B XRP** is released from a dated escrow on the first of every month; three of those firings fall inside this window, on **Jun 1 2026**, **Jul 1 2026** and **Aug 1 2026**, for **3.00B XRP** gross. But an escrow unlock is a mechanical ledger event, not a decision to sell, and most of each billion is written back into new dated escrow within days. So the framework does not ship the 3.00B headline. It reads the locked balance at both ends of the window and ships the difference — **900M XRP** — because XRP that unlocked and was immediately re-locked never actually left escrow. Per-wallet, the arithmetic is exact: **3.00B XRP** left two of Ripple's escrow wallets and **2.10B XRP** reappeared as freshly written escrow objects in four others. That is a steady drain of roughly **300M XRP a month**, unchanged across the last several escrow cycles.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** as a flow. Ripple made no unscheduled release inside the window; every billion that moved, moved on the published monthly escrow schedule. The overhang it represents is real and is enumerated below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is also **zero**: no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution applies to XRP, and the escrow contracts, the only long-dated lock on the asset, are already accounted for in Sell #2.

## Buy pressure: where new XRP goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **31.3K XRP**, small but genuinely deflationary. Every XRP Ledger transaction destroys its fee outright rather than paying it to a validator, at a base of 0.00001 XRP per transaction. Across this window that burned about **348 XRP a day**, roughly **127K XRP** a year. The burn was designed to make transaction spam expensive, not to shrink supply, and at this rate it offsets a few thousandths of one percent of the escrow release. It is the only buy-side flow the framework books, because it is the only mechanism on the XRP Ledger that removes XRP for good.

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**. XRP has no protocol-level buyback, no fee-funded repurchase and no burn engine. The **750M dollar** repurchase Ripple announced in Mar 2026 buys the company's own equity from investors and employees; it does not touch the token. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero** for the same structural reason: Ripple is the token's seller, not its accumulator. A listed treasury company is assembling an XRP reserve through open-market purchases, but buying XRP from another holder moves coins sideways rather than removing them from the tradable float, so the framework does not book it as buy pressure. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — reads **zero** not because no lock happened but because it is already netted: the **2.10B XRP** re-escrowed this window is what turns the 3.00B gross unlock into the 900M realised release, and counting it a second time here would cancel the same coins twice.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs sit behind the XRP ledger, and all three are read on-chain rather than taken from disclosure. The largest is the escrow itself: **32.00B XRP** still locked across the twenty escrow accounts Ripple publishes, refreshed by chain read, releasing 1.00B on the first of each month with the unused remainder re-escrowed. The second is Ripple's non-escrow float: the issuer's labelled XRP Ledger wallets held **3.66B XRP** at the end of this window, down about **23M XRP** from the start — meaning the released escrow does not settle into the labelled set but moves out through unlabelled distribution wallets, which is exactly why the classified circulating figure lags the escrow read. The third is external rather than internal: a listed XRP treasury company disclosed roughly **388M XRP** and targets at least **473M XRP** at launch, but its merger had still not closed inside this window. All three carry the same trigger: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How XRP compares to other fixed-supply payment chains

Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, XRP looks stricter on paper and looser in practice. Bitcoin's cap is 21M and its issuance is still positive — new BTC is minted every block, decaying on a fixed schedule. XRP's cap is 100B and its issuance is already finished; the only thing the protocol does to supply is destroy it through the fee burn. On the pure protocol-inflation row, XRP scores what almost no other major asset can: a permanent zero. But the framework measures supply reaching the market, not supply being created, and on that measure XRP behaves like a chain with a large scheduled unlock calendar rather than one with none.

The closer structural analogue is a foundation-custodied payment chain such as Stellar, where a large share of the total supply sits with the issuing organisation and enters the float on a discretionary or scheduled basis. In both cases the interesting number is not the emission rate — there isn't one — but the release rate, and whether the custodian's calendar is mechanical or discretionary. Ripple's escrow is unusually mechanical: the 1.00B monthly unlock is written into dated on-ledger contracts rather than decided quarter by quarter, and the re-escrow behaviour has been consistent enough that the net rate has held near 300M a month for several cycles running. That predictability is a genuine structural advantage over a chain whose treasury deploys on announcement.

Compared with an uncapped continuous-emission Layer-1, the contrast is sharper still. A staking chain issuing 4–7% a year adds supply forever and can raise the rate by governance vote. XRP cannot add a single unit under any vote, because ledger amendments are validator decisions about features and none of them can create XRP. The framework reading of **+1.44%** is therefore a ceiling that shrinks: every month of escrow release leaves less escrow to release, and once the schedule is exhausted the sell side of this ledger goes to zero permanently while the fee burn keeps grinding.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The escrow unlocks on **Sep 1 2026**, **Oct 1 2026** and **Nov 1 2026** are the whole story — each releases **1.00B XRP** gross, and the number that matters is how much is re-escrowed within the following days. A month where Ripple re-locks materially less than the recent pattern would push the realised release above 300M and lift the framework reading; a month where it re-locks more would cut it. Second, watch whether the listed XRP treasury company's merger finally closes: closing would trigger a contributed XRP transfer from Ripple, which moves already-issued XRP and would show up as a Sell #3 outflow from the issuer's wallets rather than as new supply. Third, watch the gap between the escrow read and the classified circulating figure — the residual is released XRP awaiting reclassification, and a reclassification would close it without any coin moving. Fourth, the fee burn: a step change in XRP Ledger transaction volume is the only thing that could make the burn row material, and at current levels it would need to rise by orders of magnitude.

## Summary

XRP is a fixed-supply asset with a scheduled release problem. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.44%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026**, driven entirely by the **900M XRP** realised escrow release and offset by only **31.3K XRP** of transaction-fee burn, against a monitor reading of **+1.04%** — a 0.39-point gap inside tolerance. The structural mechanism is unusual and worth stating plainly: no XRP has been minted since 2012 and none ever will be, so every unit of pressure on this page comes from moving existing XRP out of a lock rather than from creating any. The key risk is a change in Ripple's re-escrow behaviour, which is discretionary even though the unlock is not — a month at a lower re-lock rate would raise the release without any protocol change. The ceiling is absolute: **100B XRP**, of which **32.00B** is still escrowed, and the sell side of this ledger ends for good when that escrow runs out.
