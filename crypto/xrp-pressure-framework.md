## Part 1 · Supply: XRP Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

The XRP Ledger never mints a single new XRP, yet the amount of **XRP** in the market grew by **+1.14%** over the last 90 days, and the MrNasdog Pressure Framework projects about the same for the next 90. The source is Ripple, not the protocol: its wallets paid **718.47M XRP** to the market, fed by the monthly escrow, while fee burning removed only **34,862 XRP**. The inflation monitor reads **+1.20%**, so the two readings agree. The hard ceiling is the fixed 100B XRP made at launch, of which **31.7B** still sits in escrow.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Net supply pressure on XRP is **+1.14%** of circulating supply over the last 90 days (Jul 2 to Sep 30 2026), and the forward reading for the next 90 days is the same **+1.14%**. The inflation monitor, which measures the change in circulating supply directly, reads **+1.20%** for the same window. The gap is **0.06 percentage points**, well inside the 0.5-point line, so no monitor-gap warning is shown. XRP is a **zero-issuance coin with a company-paced float**: the protocol adds nothing, and almost every new coin in the market is a coin Ripple chose to release.

## Sell pressure: where new XRP comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. The XRP Ledger has no block reward and no staking reward, and no transaction type can create XRP. The ledger keeps its own running coin count, and across this window that count only went down, from about 99,985.64M to 99,985.61M XRP, because fees are destroyed.

Vesting unlocks book **0**, even though the escrow is active. Ripple's escrow opens up to 1B XRP on the first day of each month. Inside this window it opened 1B XRP on Aug 1 2026 and 1B XRP on Sep 1 2026. Each time, Ripple locked 700M XRP straight back into new escrow and kept 300M, so the escrow balance fell from 32.3B to **31.7B XRP**. The kept coins go to Ripple's own wallets, and those wallets are not counted as circulating yet. An escrow opening therefore moves XRP from one Ripple balance to another; the XRP counts once, when it leaves Ripple.

That leaving is the one row that matters. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks come to **718.47M XRP** over 90 days. We read every labelled Ripple wallet and every escrow at both ends of the window: together they fell from about 36.01B to 35.30B XRP, and the payments between them add up to the same figure almost to the last coin. Nearly all of it, **713.97M XRP**, followed one path: the 300M kept from each escrow opening went to an operating wallet, then to a paying wallet, then out in weekly batches of up to about 50M XRP to wallets that pass it on. The other **4.49M XRP** was a single payment from another Ripple wallet on Aug 6 2026. Ripple's own distribution figures point the same way, at roughly 730M XRP per 90 days. The payouts ran every week of the window, so the forward figure holds the same **718.47M XRP**.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy releases are **0**. No estate, trustee or court payout holds XRP, and the large holders that bought on the market, such as spot funds and listed treasury companies, hold coins that were already counted as circulating.

## Buy pressure: where new XRP goes

The only buy-side mechanism is the protocol fee burn: **34,862 XRP** destroyed in 90 days, about 387 XRP a day. Every XRP Ledger transaction pays a tiny fee in XRP, and that fee is removed from supply for good rather than paid to anyone. Activity rose in September, when the burn ran at about 536 XRP a day, but no fee change sits behind that, so the forward figure keeps the trailing **34,862 XRP**. The burn is about 20,000 times smaller than Ripple's payouts, which is why it barely moves the net.

A programmatic buyback is **0**: nothing buys XRP back. Ripple's buybacks this year were of its own company shares, and the tokens Ripple burns in the news are its dollar stablecoin, not XRP. A foundation buy is also **0**. Ripple re-locks 700M XRP a month into escrow, but that moves coins between Ripple accounts that are all outside the circulating count, so it takes nothing off the market. A new long-term lock is **0** as well: XRP has no staking, and fund or treasury holdings stay inside the circulating count.

## Foundation and overhang

Ripple is the one team-controlled overhang, and it is large. The escrow holds **31.7B XRP** across eight Ripple accounts, scheduled to open up to 1B XRP on the first of every month into 2029. Ripple's labelled wallets hold another **3.60B XRP**; the largest single wallet holds 1.33B XRP, and four more hold between 195M and 812M XRP each. Ripple's own figures show about 5.2B XRP not yet distributed, so roughly 1.6B XRP sits in Ripple wallets that carry no public label. Together that is about 37% of all XRP, all outside the circulating count today.

We re-read the escrow and every labelled Ripple wallet on the ledger at each rebuild. If any of these balances falls between refreshes and the coins leave Ripple, that outflow enters the foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How XRP compares to other fixed-supply payment chains

XRP belongs to a small group of payment chains that created their whole supply at launch and no longer mint more, like Stellar's XLM and Hedera's HBAR. On these chains the protocol adds nothing, and the real question is how fast the company or foundation that holds the unreleased coins lets them out. XRP is the clearest case, because Ripple's release runs through an escrow anyone can read on the ledger, and the monthly 1B XRP schedule is written into the escrow entries themselves rather than into a promise.

Against mined or staked chains the difference is the source, not only the size. Bitcoin adds new coins through mining on a fixed halving path; Ethereum and Solana pay validators in newly created coins. For XRP, a 1.14% quarter comes entirely from a company treasury, so the rate can change with one business decision, in either direction, while a mined or staked chain's rate is set by code. XRP's fee burn works like Ethereum's base-fee burn in spirit, but on a much cheaper ledger it removes only a few hundred XRP a day, so it does not offset the float growth.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The escrow opens 1B XRP on **Oct 1 2026**, **Nov 1 2026** and **Dec 1 2026**; the key number each month is how much Ripple locks back up, which was 700M XRP in both August and September. A smaller re-lock would push the float up faster. The weekly payouts from Ripple's paying wallet are the live signal: if they slow down or stop, the forward reading comes down with them. A fund prospectus says Ripple may release extra escrow if US market-structure law passes; the Senate vote on that bill failed on Sep 15 2026, but a return after the November elections would reopen the question. The Batch upgrade to the XRP Ledger can go live on **Oct 9 2026** at the earliest; it changes how transactions are bundled, not how XRP is created, so it could only nudge the fee burn.

## Summary

XRP supply is growing at about **+1.14%** per 90 days, and projected to keep growing at that pace, even though the XRP Ledger mints nothing. The growth comes from Ripple's wallets, which paid **718.47M XRP** to the market in 90 days while fees burned only **34,862 XRP**. The key risk is that Ripple still controls about 37B XRP, and the pace of release is its own choice. The ceiling is fixed: 100B XRP were created at launch, no more can ever be made, and every fee makes the total a little smaller.
