# LTC Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

Litecoin (LTC) supply is growing slowly and on a fixed schedule: the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.42% net** over the last 90 days and **+0.42%** for the next 90. Proof-of-work miners received **323,838 LTC** of new coins, while only **7.5 LTC** left the supply, sent by users to outputs nobody can spend. LTC has no vesting, no team treasury, no fee burn and no buyback, and its hard cap of **84M LTC** sets the ceiling; the inflation monitor reads **+0.43%**, so the two agree.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Between Jul 3 2026 and Oct 1 2026 the Litecoin ledger shows **323,837.5 LTC** of sell pressure against **7.5 LTC** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **77.67M LTC**, a net of **+0.42%** in 90 days. The next 90 days project the same **+0.42%**, because the block reward does not change until the next halving in late July 2027. The inflation monitor, which reads supply from market data instead of the chain, shows **+0.43%**, a gap of about **0.01 percentage points** — well inside the half-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown. In one line: LTC is a quiet, capped proof-of-work coin whose only new supply is the miner reward.

## Sell pressure: where new LTC comes from

Protocol inflation is the whole Litecoin sell side. Every Litecoin block pays its miner a subsidy of **6.25 LTC**, the rate in force since the third halving at block 2,520,000 in August 2023. In the 90-day window the network found **51,814 blocks**, one every 150.08 seconds on average, a hair slower than the 150-second target. That makes **323,837.5 LTC** of new coins, or about **3,598 LTC a day**. A supply series from a chain indexer counts the same 51,814 blocks and the same issuance to the coin, and a second block indexer agrees within 0.02%. Because the Litecoin subsidy is paid per block, a slightly slow chain pays slightly less; the next 90 days project **323,834 LTC** at the measured block pace.

Vesting unlocks are **zero** and always have been. Litecoin launched in October 2011 as a fair launch with no premine, no founder allocation and no investor round, so there is no unlock calendar and nothing for a cliff to release. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are also **zero**: the protocol never set aside a treasury, and the Litecoin Foundation runs on donations. The long-term locked or bankruptcy row is **zero** too, because no court estate, trustee or long lock is paying LTC out; funds and trusts that hold Litecoin bought it on the open market.

Two in-window software changes did not touch supply. Soft forks at block 3,154,440 (Aug 4 2026) and block 3,172,640 (Sep 5 2026) tightened checks on the MWEB privacy layer only; the block reward and the halving schedule were left unchanged.

## Buy pressure: where new LTC goes

Litecoin has no programmatic buyback: no contract, foundation or treasury buys LTC back for the network, so that row is **zero**. A listed company that holds LTC as its main reserve bought coins for itself and sold some to fund a buyback of its own shares, but both legs moved coins already counted as circulating. The protocol fee burn is **zero** as well: Litecoin transaction fees go to the miner, about 0.002 LTC per block, and nothing is destroyed by rule. A well-known keyless burn address holds 0.184 LTC and received nothing new in the window.

There is no foundation buy (**zero**) and no new long-term lock (**zero**). Litecoin has no staking, so coins cannot be locked for yield. Coins moved into the MWEB privacy layer — about **410,351 LTC** now, up 75,176 LTC over the 90 days — can move back at any time and stay part of circulating supply. The only real buy-side flow is small: users sent **7.5 LTC** to 480 unspendable outputs. The chain supply count rose by exactly 7.5 LTC less than the coins mined, so those coins are gone for good, about 43,000 times less than was created.

## Foundation and overhang

Litecoin carries almost no overhang. The gap between total and circulating supply is only about **3,231 LTC**, so any wallet bigger than that already sits inside the float, and moving it creates no new supply. The Litecoin Foundation, a non-profit, has published no LTC wallet or balance and holds no protocol allocation; it is checked by hand every two weeks. The **85,034 LTC** recovered after the March 2026 MWEB bug was pegged back into the privacy layer and frozen by the developers before this window; it is already counted as circulating and is checked at each rebuild. The largest listed company holding LTC reported **832,716 LTC** on Jun 30 2026, more than 1% of all coins mined, bought in the market.

None of these piles adds new supply if it moves, because each is already part of the float. If the Foundation ever discloses a wallet and its balance falls between refreshes, or the frozen output is released outside the float, that outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How LTC compares to other proof-of-work halving chains

Litecoin copies the Bitcoin model with different numbers. Bitcoin pays 3.125 BTC per 10-minute block under a 21M cap; Litecoin pays 6.25 LTC per 2.5-minute block under an 84M cap, and both halve every four years. Because Litecoin halved one year before Bitcoin in each cycle, its next halving lands in July 2027 while Bitcoin's lands around 2028. Today Bitcoin grows about 0.21% per 90 days and Litecoin about 0.42%, so LTC adds supply at roughly twice Bitcoin's pace until its next halving cuts the reward to 3.125 LTC.

Dogecoin, the other big Scrypt coin and Litecoin's merge-mining partner, has no cap at all: it pays a fixed 10,000 DOGE per one-minute block forever, so its percentage inflation only falls slowly as supply grows. Litecoin's capped, halving schedule means its inflation steps down sharply every four years instead. None of these chains burns fees, so in all three the miner reward is the whole story.

Against smart-contract Layer 1s, Litecoin is simpler: no staking rewards that rise with participation, no foundation or venture unlocks, and no burn that changes with network use. That makes LTC supply one of the easiest to forecast in crypto — the open question is demand, not supply.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The block reward stays at 6.25 LTC through Dec 30 2026; the next halving at block 3,360,000 is expected around late July 2027, outside this window. The Grayscale Litecoin Trust filed on Sep 11 2026 to become an exchange-traded fund; approval would bring a new way to buy LTC but would not change supply. Any new Litecoin Core release that touches consensus, as on Aug 4 2026 and Sep 5 2026, is worth reading for supply effects. The frozen 85,034 LTC recovery output stays on the watch list in case it is ever released. The listed LTC treasury company's next filings will show whether it keeps buying or selling.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Litecoin at **+0.42% net** over the last 90 days and **+0.42%** over the next 90: **323,838 LTC** of proof-of-work mining rewards against **7.5 LTC** sent to unspendable outputs, with no vesting, no team treasury, no burn and no buyback. LTC's supply follows a fixed schedule of 6.25 LTC per block that halves to 3.125 LTC in late July 2027. The key risk on the supply side is simply that new coins keep arriving every 2.5 minutes with nothing taking coins back out. The ceiling is the 84M LTC hard cap, and with 77.67M already mined, supply growth only gets smaller from here.
