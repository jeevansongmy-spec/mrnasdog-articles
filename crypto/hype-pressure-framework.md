# HYPE Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

HYPE, the native gas and staking asset of the Hyperliquid L1, runs a hard **1,000M** cap and a fee-funded buyback that took **2.15M HYPE** off the market over the last 90 days. Against it, staking issuance added **0.74M HYPE** to the tradable float, the core-contributor escrow handed out **1.42M** — a twentieth of the **29.76M** its published vesting calendar implies — and the Hyper Foundation moved **0.10M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **-0.13% net** for the trailing window and **-0.30%** forward, because a second buyback stream funded by reserve yield begins paying in on **Oct 3 2026**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework books **2.26M HYPE** of sell pressure against **2.54M HYPE** of buy pressure over the last 90 days, a net of about **-0.13%** of the **222.45M** circulating float, and **-0.30%** forward once the new reserve-yield buyback lands. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at about **-6.92%**, a gap of roughly **6.79 percentage points**, which triggers a **monitor-gap warning**. Rebuilding the float day by day showed exactly where that came from: the whole move is one 24-hour step on **May 26 2026**, when the reading dropped from **238,832,075** to **221,874,756**, while every other day of the window sits inside ordinary price noise. That step now sits on the very first day of the measured window, so it ages out within days and the monitor should snap back to about **+0.20%**. Nothing on chain matches it — Hyperliquid's own total supply is falling by only about **4,341 HYPE** a day, the buyback address only gained, and the core-contributor escrow's outflows are smaller than the buyback's inflows — so the step is an upstream supply-model revision, not a market flow. Hyperliquid is best labelled **deflationary on its own revenue**: a chain whose fees now more than pay for its own issuance.

## Sell pressure: where new HYPE comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **0.74M HYPE** over 90 days, and the structural point is that Hyperliquid does not mint it. Staking rewards are drawn from an un-issued future-emissions reserve, allocated at genesis and sitting at roughly **412.15M HYPE** today, so total supply can never cross the **1,000M** cap no matter how long the chain runs. Reading that reserve at both ends of a six-and-a-half-day baseline showed it draining at **26,724 HYPE** a day, which is **2.41M** gross over 90 days; the published reward curve, which sets the rate inversely to the square root of total HYPE staked and gives roughly **2.37%** a year at **400M** staked, independently implies about **27,130** a day at the **436.43M** currently delegated, and the largest delegator's own reward stream implies **26,125**. Three angles inside four percent of each other. But gross issuance is not float: **301.78M** of that staked base belongs to the core-contributor escrow and the Hyper Foundation wallet, and both of those are settled below at what they actually released. Only the **134.65M** of genuinely market-held stake belongs in this row, which is the **0.74M** booked here; counting the escrow's and the Foundation's accrual as well would book the same tokens twice.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **1.42M HYPE**, and it is the number the aggregators get most wrong. The published vesting calendar implies about **9.92M HYPE** a month reaching core contributors on the sixth. The escrow contract says otherwise, and this build followed the tokens all the way out of the address rather than stopping at the paper entitlement: **533,752** left on **Jun 6** and **Jun 7 2026**, **452,000** on **Jul 7 2026**, and **433,024** on **Aug 6 2026**. A dated public report priced the August claim at **22.65M** dollars, which at that week's HYPE price is the same figure from a completely different direction. The reason the realised number is so much smaller is structural: the escrow was granted **238M HYPE** in December 2024 and still holds **241.40M** today, so not one token of the original core-contributor grant has moved. What the escrow distributes each month is the staking yield that grant earns — and it distributes essentially all of it, keeping a spot balance of about three HYPE.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **0.10M HYPE**: the Hyper Foundation budget wallet transferred **50,000** out on **May 28 2026** and another **50,000** on **Aug 4 2026**, with a further **50,000** already unstaked and sitting liquid since **Aug 11 2026**. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**, and it cannot be otherwise: HYPE launched by airdrop with no venture round, no creditor estate and no trustee schedule. The Nasdaq-listed HYPE treasury vehicles are not a Sell #4 either. They bought their HYPE on the open market, which means those coins were already inside the **222.45M** float; this ledger counts what leaves a project-controlled reserve, not who happens to be holding.

## Buy pressure: where new HYPE goes

Buy #1 — the programmatic buyback — is the dominant force at **2.15M HYPE** over 90 days, and its design is unusual enough to matter. Platform fees are converted to HYPE inside Hyperliquid's own execution layer and parked at a system address created without a private key, so nobody can ever spend the balance; a validator vote in December 2025 formally recognised it as burned. That address held **44,536,854** at the window open and **46,688,928** at the close. The framework reads the balance, never the marketing: Hyperliquid's quoted capture rate is **97–99%** of fees, but pricing the realised accumulation leg by leg at the window's own HYPE prices gives **135.50M** dollars against **192.18M** dollars of platform fees — a realised share of **70.5%**. Fee tiers, staking discounts and builder-deployed market economics intercept the stream before it arrives, and the gap between quoted and realised is the single most useful thing to know about this buyback.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **0.39M HYPE**, a genuine supply destruction rather than an accumulation. Gas on the HyperEVM execution layer and the HYPE side of HyperCore spot trading fees are destroyed outright, so Hyperliquid's own total supply figure erodes continuously rather than in visible burn events. Since the November 2024 genesis, **1,000,900 HYPE** have been destroyed, an average of **1,581** a day; the current rate of **4,341** a day runs far above that mean, which is what a record August of trading and execution-layer activity looks like in the supply data. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**, because no discretionary buying outside the automatic fee conversion has ever been disclosed. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is also **zero**: builder-deployed perpetual markets each bond **500,000 HYPE** for at least 183 days, and a rule announced on **Jul 20 2026** would extend the same bond to permissionless outcome markets, but no deployment inside the window carries a dated quantum and the extension still needs a network upgrade.

Buy #5 is the new one. A validator vote carried by **19 of 26** validators and **69.08%** of stake, above the **66.67%** bar, routes about **90%** of the yield Hyperliquid earns on its idle dollar reserves into that same keyless buyback address. Yield begins accruing on **Aug 26 2026** in 30-day blocks, each paid out eight days after its block closes, so nothing at all landed inside the window just measured — the row is zero for the trailing side by structure, not by judgement. Two payments fall inside the next 90 days, on **Oct 3 2026** and **Nov 2 2026**. Published projections put each at **11M to 13M** dollars; this build's own arithmetic, run against the **6,785M** dollars of stablecoins independently measured sitting on the chain today, gives **13.6M to 17.8M**. Two estimates that disagree by less than half, so the low end of the published range ships: **0.27M HYPE** forward. Worth saying plainly — the trailing window is already net negative without it.

## Foundation and overhang

Roughly **761M HYPE**, about three quarters of the cap, sits behind the tradable float in four identified buckets, and the framework tracks each on a 24-hour on-chain refresh. The largest is the un-issued future-emissions reserve at about **412.15M HYPE**, which nobody can reach discretionarily — it drains only along the staking-reward curve. Next is the core-contributor escrow at **241.40M HYPE** against a **238M** grant, principal untouched, distributing only yield on the sixth of each month. Third is the Hyper Foundation budget wallet at **60.43M HYPE** against a **60M** grant, which accrues **3,602 HYPE** a day on its stake and draws far less, so it genuinely compounds. Fourth is the buyback address itself at **46.70M HYPE**, an overhang only in the bookkeeping sense: it has no key. One more balance deliberately excluded is the **56.44M HYPE** system escrow backing user-held HYPE on the EVM side, which belongs to depositors rather than to any team. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and the same trigger covers the reserve-yield buyback, because it pays into the same keyless address.

## How HYPE compares to other exchange-revenue chains

HYPE belongs to the small class of assets whose issuance is fully pre-funded rather than minted. Ethereum and most uncapped proof-of-stake L1s create new units to pay validators, so their fee burn has to outrun a live mint; Hyperliquid pays validators out of a genesis reserve, which means the **1,000M** cap is arithmetically unreachable and every burn is a permanent, one-directional reduction. Against Bitcoin's halving model the difference is direction of travel: Bitcoin's issuance falls on a fixed calendar regardless of usage, while Hyperliquid's buyback scales with fee revenue, so HYPE's net supply gets more deflationary when the exchange is busy and less so when it is quiet.

The closer comparison is the exchange-token class — BNB and its quarterly auto-burn, or any venue that recycles trading revenue into its own float. Two mechanism differences separate HYPE. First, the conversion is continuous and executed inside protocol code rather than announced quarterly by a company, so there is no discretionary gap between policy and execution. Second, the destination is a keyless address rather than a burn opcode, which means the tokens are economically removed but still visible on chain — an honest reader can audit the balance rather than trust a press release. The cost of that design is the one this page keeps flagging: because the address balance is a public number, aggregators disagree about whether to count it, and the resulting classification steps are exactly what produced the **6.79 percentage point** monitor gap above.

Against vesting-heavy L1s, HYPE's escrow behaviour is the outlier worth naming. Most chains in year two are distributing principal on a cliff calendar, and their realised sell pressure tracks the published schedule closely. Hyperliquid's core-contributor escrow has released none of its **238M** principal and distributes only the yield it earns, which is why the realised **1.42M** is roughly five percent of the **29.76M** the calendar implies. Anyone reading the headline unlock number is reading a number that has not been true for a year.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Aug 26 2026** — the first 30-day reserve-yield accrual block opens; from this date the buyback has two funding sources instead of one. **Sep 6 2026** — the next core-contributor escrow distribution, which the framework projects at about **0.43M HYPE** rather than the headline **9.92M**; a step materially above that would be the first sign the escrow is touching principal. **Oct 3 2026** — the first reserve-yield payment, and the single most important observation in this whole reading, because it converts a projection into a measurement. **Nov 2 2026** — the second payment, which establishes whether the run rate matches the projection. Running alongside all of them: the realised fee-capture share, currently **70.5%** against a quoted **97–99%**, which is falling as builder-deployed markets retain a growing slice of the fees their venues generate — the clearest threat to Buy #1 in the ledger.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **-0.13%** net supply over the trailing 90 days and **-0.30%** over the next 90, a chain whose fee-funded buyback of **2.15M HYPE** now more than covers **0.74M** of staking issuance and a **1.42M** core-contributor distribution. The structural mechanism is that HYPE is never minted — staking rewards come out of a pre-allocated **412.15M** reserve, so the **1,000M** cap is a genuine ceiling and every burn is permanent. The key risk is that the buyback's realised fee capture is **70.5%**, not the quoted **97–99%**, and is being eroded by builder-deployed markets keeping more of their own fees; the offsetting force is the reserve-yield stream starting **Oct 3 2026**, which adds a funding source that scales with dollars parked on the platform rather than with trading volume. Behind all of it sits **761M HYPE** of identified overhang, of which the **238M** core-contributor principal has never moved.
