# HYPE Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

HYPE, the native gas and staking asset of the Hyperliquid L1, runs a hard **1,000M** cap and a fee-funded buyback that removed about **2.25M HYPE** from the float over the last 90 days. Against it, staking issuance released **2.42M HYPE** from the un-issued emissions reserve and the core-contributor escrow let go of **1.42M** — a fraction of the **29.76M** its published vesting calendar implies, because the escrow still holds **241.18M** against a **238M** grant and has never net-released a token. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **+0.66% net** across both the trailing and the forward 90-day window: structurally near-neutral, with a buyback large enough to cancel most of the issuance but not the vest.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework books **3.84M HYPE** of sell pressure against **2.39M HYPE** of buy pressure over the last 90 days, a net of about **+0.65%** of the **222.45M** circulating float, and **+0.66%** forward because every mechanism involved is continuous rather than lumpy. Our supply monitor reads the same window at about **−6.63%**, a gap of roughly **7.29 percentage points**, which triggers a **⚠ monitor-gap chip**. Rebuilding the float day by day showed exactly where that came from: the whole move is one 24-hour step on **May 26 2026**, when the reading dropped from **238,832,075** to **221,874,756**, while every other day of the window sits inside ordinary price noise. Nothing on chain moved to match it — the buyback address only gained, reaching **46.30M**, the escrow released nothing that day, and on-chain supply only burned — so the step is an upstream supply-model revision, not a market flow. Hyperliquid is best labelled **structurally near-neutral with a self-funding buyback**: a chain whose own revenue very nearly pays for its own issuance.

## Sell pressure: where new HYPE comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **2.42M HYPE** over 90 days, and the important structural point is that Hyperliquid does not mint it. Staking rewards are drawn from the un-issued future-emissions reserve, a bucket that was allocated at genesis and sits at roughly **428M HYPE** today, so total supply can never cross the **1,000M** cap no matter how long the chain runs. The reward rate is set inversely to the square root of total HYPE staked, which the Hyperliquid documentation pegs at about **2.37%** a year at **400M** staked. With **436.58M HYPE** currently delegated across **34** validators, that curve gives **2.268%** a year, or **2.44M HYPE** per 90 days; the stake-weighted rate the validators themselves are publishing, grossed back up for commission, gives about **2.24%** and **2.42M**. Two independent angles inside one percent of each other, so the row ships at **2.42M**. Worth noting for anyone reading the float rather than the ledger: roughly two thirds of the staked base is the core-contributor escrow plus the Hyper Foundation wallet, so most of this issuance accrues straight back into balances that are already counted as locked.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is where this coin is most often misread, and it is **1.42M HYPE**, not the **29.76M** that unlock trackers advertise. The published calendar releases **9.92M HYPE** a month to core contributors on the 6th. The escrow's own transfer ledger tells a completely different story: it unstaked and distributed **533,752 HYPE** on **Jun 5**, **452,001** on **Jul 6** and **433,025** on **Aug 5 2026**, a total of **1,418,778**, spread across about ten contributor wallets, with zero inflows. The arithmetic closes the case. If the escrow had really been paying out **9.92M** a month since its cliff, it would be far below its **238,000,000** grant by now. It holds **241,179,823 HYPE** — more than the full allocation, the excess being staking rewards it earned on the way. The published calendar is an entitlement; the escrow is the flow, and the framework books the flow.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**, and for once that zero is provable rather than merely unobserved. Both large team wallets currently hold more HYPE than they were granted: the core-contributor escrow at **241.18M** against **238M**, and the Hyper Foundation wallet at **60.33M** against **60M**. Neither has net-released anything beyond the vesting claim already counted in Sell #2. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** as well: Hyperliquid has no insolvency estate, no trustee distribution and no court-ordered release schedule anywhere in its supply.

## Buy pressure: where new HYPE goes

Buy #1 — the programmatic buyback — is **2.25M HYPE**, and it is the single most consequential number on this page. Hyperliquid converts platform fees into HYPE automatically as part of L1 execution and parks the result at a keyless system address, **0xfefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefe**, that nobody holds a key to. On **Dec 24 2025** a stake-weighted validator vote carried by **85%** of stake formally recognised that balance as burned — binding social consensus never to authorise a protocol upgrade that could reach it. The framework measures the row by reading the address balance rather than by inferring it from a fee split: it holds **46,295,844 HYPE** today and does not stake, so the balance is the whole story, and it absorbed roughly **2.25M** over this window. This distinction matters, because an earlier build of this page sized the row from gross fee revenue and overstated it by roughly half. One caution for the forward read: the pace is easing, running near **19,000 HYPE** a day lately against about **26,000** earlier, tracking a falling fee run-rate.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is a genuinely separate and much smaller **0.14M HYPE**. Gas on HyperEVM and the HYPE side of spot trading fees are destroyed outright, so the on-chain supply figure falls continuously rather than in visible burn transactions. Measured against the cap, that has removed **953,947 HYPE** since the **Nov 29 2024** genesis, an average of about **1,544** a day or **0.14M** per 90 days, and it is deliberately shipped as a floor. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** today but is the row most likely to change. Validators have approved **AQA v2**, which turns idle-USDC reserve yield into a second buyback stream funded via Coinbase as the USDC treasury deployer; yield accrues from **Aug 26 2026** and the first Assistance Fund payment lands **Oct 3 2026**. It is genuine new buy pressure inside the forward window, but with no published quantum it is watched, not booked. HIP-4 deployer locks — **500,000 HYPE** locked per market deployer for six months since **Jul 20 2026** — remain unquantified for the same reason.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled pools sit behind the Hyperliquid ledger, and together they are about **733M HYPE**, close to three quarters of the entire cap. The largest is the un-issued future-emissions reserve at roughly **428M**, drawn down only by the continuous staking-reward stream in Sell #1. Second is the core-contributor escrow at **0x43e9abea1910387c4292bca4b94de81462f8a251**, holding **241,179,823 HYPE** — all of it delegated — against a **238M** grant, with a published calendar of **9.92M** a month that the ledger shows running at roughly **0.43M**; the undrawn remainder of about **240M** is the single biggest unmodelled risk in this coin. Third is the Hyper Foundation wallet at **0xd57ecca444a9acb7208d286be439de12dd09de5d**, holding **60,325,565 HYPE** against a **60M** budget, also fully delegated and never drawn down. Fourth is the community grant pool at about **3.09M**. Alongside them sits the buyback address at **46,295,844 HYPE**, an overhang only in the technical sense — keyless, accumulate-only, and validators have committed never to reach it. All four are refreshed from chain every rebuild. If the balance of any of them falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HYPE compares to other application-layer L1s

Hyperliquid sits in an unusual structural class: an L1 whose issuance is capped and pre-allocated rather than perpetual. A conventional proof-of-stake L1 mints new supply to pay validators, so its inflation is a permanent tax on holders that no amount of usage can retire. Hyperliquid pays validators out of a fixed **428M** reserve carved from a hard **1,000M** cap, which means its staking issuance has an end date built in and its total supply strictly falls over time as gas and spot fees destroy tokens. Against a chain with uncapped continuous emission, that is a materially stronger supply guarantee — and against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, Hyperliquid trades the fixed schedule for a usage-linked one.

The closer structural analogue is the exchange token with a revenue-funded buyback, and Hyperliquid is arguably the purest expression of that model because the buyback is not a quarterly corporate decision but an execution-layer operation the chain performs on itself. An exchange token's buyback depends on a company choosing to run it and can be paused; Hyperliquid's runs inside consensus, and the destination address has been made permanently unreachable by validator vote. The trade-off is that the buyback's size is a direct function of trading volume, so a quiet quarter shrinks it — which is precisely what the fee run-rate is showing right now, even as AQA v2 prepares to add a second, TVL-linked funding source.

Where Hyperliquid diverges from both classes is float maturity and custody concentration. With only **222.45M** of a **1,000M** cap freely trading, Hyperliquid's buyback fights a schedule rather than a steady state, and its supply behaviour is dominated by what a small number of identified wallets choose to do. The mitigating evidence is unusually strong: those wallets have consistently chosen to stake rather than sell, and the escrow's realised release has been running at roughly one twentieth of its entitlement for months. The deciding variable for HYPE is whether the escrow's restraint holds.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the core-contributor escrow releases on **Sep 6 2026**, **Oct 6 2026** and **Nov 6 2026**. The number that matters is not the headline **9.92M** but what actually leaves the escrow; a batch materially above the recent **0.43M** would move this page's reading immediately. Second, the first **AQA v2** buyback payment on **Oct 3 2026** — idle-USDC yield becomes a second HYPE buy stream, and a published size could flip the net negative. Third, watch the buyback address at **0xfefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefe**: its accumulation rate has fallen by roughly a third, and a further slide would keep the net positive. Fourth, HIP-4 deployer locks — a published count of permissionless deployers would open Buy #4 as a real row for the first time. Fifth, the upstream float definition: the **May 26 2026** step showed that a single reclassification can move the reported number by **16.96M** overnight, so a second revision would widen or close the monitor gap without any HYPE changing hands.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **+0.66% net** supply growth over 90 days — **3.70M HYPE** of staking issuance and realised team vesting against **2.24M HYPE** of buyback and burn on a **222.45M** float. The structural mechanism is a chain that funds its own buyback from trading fees and pays its validators from a fixed reserve inside a hard **1,000M** cap, so issuance has an end and total supply only falls. The key risk is custody rather than code: about **733M HYPE** sits in four team-controlled pools, and the core-contributor escrow is releasing roughly one twentieth of what its published calendar entitles it to — restraint that is voluntary and could stop. The ceiling is absolute at **1,000M** and cannot be raised, which is why Hyperliquid's supply question is entirely about the pace of distribution, never about dilution beyond the cap.
