*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/hype/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/hype/inflation)*

Hyperliquid issued about **2.42M HYPE** in staking rewards over the last 90 days and its core-contributor escrow actually released **1.41M** — a fraction of the **29.76M** its published vesting calendar implies — against a fee-funded buyback of **2.37M HYPE** and an on-chain burn of **0.14M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.59% net** on a 1B fixed cap: the buyback absorbs most of the new supply, but not all of it. Our monitor reads **−6.66%**, and this build traced that entire difference to a single-day revision in the upstream supply series on **May 26 2026**, not to any HYPE flow.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework books **3.83M HYPE** of sell pressure against **2.51M HYPE** of buy pressure over 90 days, a net of about **+0.59%** of the **222.45M** circulating float, and the same figure forward because every mechanism involved is continuous rather than lumpy. Our supply monitor reads the identical window at about **−6.66%**, a gap of roughly **7.25 percentage points**, which triggers a **monitor-gap flag**. Rebuilding the float day by day showed exactly where that came from: the whole move is one 24-hour step on **May 26 2026**, when the reading dropped from **238,832,075** to **221,874,756**, while every other day of the window sits inside ordinary price noise. Nothing on chain moved to match it — the buyback address only gained, the escrow released nothing that day, and on-chain supply only burned — so the step is an upstream supply-model revision, not a market flow. Hyperliquid is best labelled **structurally near-neutral with a self-funding buyback**: a chain whose own revenue very nearly pays for its own issuance.

## Sell pressure: where new HYPE comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **2.42M HYPE** over 90 days, and the important structural point is that Hyperliquid does not mint it. Staking rewards are drawn from the un-issued future-emissions reserve, a bucket allocated at genesis that sits at roughly **428M HYPE** today, so total supply can never cross the **1,000M** cap no matter how long the chain runs. The reward rate is set inversely to the square root of total HYPE staked, which the Hyperliquid documentation pegs at about **2.37%** a year at **400M** staked. With **436.37M HYPE** currently delegated across **34** validators, that curve gives **2.269%** a year, or **2.441M HYPE** per 90 days; the stake-weighted rate the validators themselves publish, grossed back up for commission, gives **2.2441%** and **2.415M**. Two independent angles inside one percent, so the row ships at **2.42M**. Roughly two thirds of the staked base is the core-contributor escrow plus the Hyper Foundation wallet, so most of this issuance accrues straight back into balances already counted as locked.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is where HYPE is most often misread, and it is **1.41M HYPE**, not the **29.76M** that unlock trackers advertise. The published calendar releases **9.92M HYPE** a month to core contributors on the 6th, which across **May 6**, **Jun 6** and **Jul 6 2026** would be nearly thirty million tokens. The escrow's own transfer ledger tells a different story: it unstaked and distributed **421,879 HYPE** on **May 4**, **533,752** on **Jun 5** and **452,001** on **Jul 6 2026**, a total of **1,407,632**, spread across about ten contributor wallets, with zero inflows. The arithmetic closes the case. If the escrow had really been paying out **9.92M** a month since its cliff, it would be far below its **238,000,000** grant by now. It holds **241,425,188 HYPE** — more than the full allocation, the excess being staking rewards it earned on the way. The published calendar is an entitlement; the escrow is the flow, and the framework books the flow.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**, and for once that zero is provable rather than merely unobserved. Both large team wallets currently hold more HYPE than they were granted: the core-contributor escrow at **241.43M** against **238M**, and the Hyper Foundation wallet at **60.33M** against **60M**. Neither has net-released anything beyond the vesting claim already counted in Sell #2. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** as well: Hyperliquid has no insolvency estate, no trustee distribution and no court-ordered release schedule anywhere in its supply.

## Buy pressure: where new HYPE goes

Buy #1 — the programmatic buyback — is **2.37M HYPE**, and it is the single most consequential number here. Hyperliquid converts platform fees into HYPE automatically as part of L1 execution and parks the result at a keyless system address, **0xfefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefe**, that nobody holds a key to. On **Dec 24 2025** a stake-weighted validator vote carried by **85%** of stake formally recognised that balance as burned — binding social consensus never to authorise a protocol upgrade that could reach it. The framework measures the row by reading the address at both ends of the window rather than inferring it from a fee split: **43,675,321 HYPE** on **Apr 27 2026** and **46,049,682** on **Jul 27 2026**, a realised accumulation of **2,374,361**. That distinction matters, because an earlier build of this page sized the row from gross fee revenue and overstated it by roughly half. As a cross-check only, platform fees over the same window ran **$145.9M**, and **2.37M HYPE** at the window's average price is **$133M** to **$145M** — consistent with substantially all fees funding the buyback. Every fortnightly sample inside the window rises, so nothing has ever left the address. One caution for the forward read: the pace is easing, running near **17,950 HYPE** a day over the last 30 days against about **26,120** across the full window, tracking a fee run-rate that fell from **$2.06M** a day in May to **$1.27M** in July.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is a genuinely separate and much smaller **0.14M HYPE**. Gas on HyperEVM and the HYPE side of spot trading fees are destroyed outright, so the on-chain supply figure falls continuously rather than in visible burn transactions. Measured against the cap, that has removed **917,628 HYPE** since the **Nov 29 2024** genesis, an average of about **1,516** a day or **0.14M** per 90 days. Live sampling of the supply counter during this build showed it falling faster than that long-run average, so the shipped figure is deliberately a floor rather than an extrapolation. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**: no discretionary open-market buying outside the automatic fee conversion has ever been disclosed. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** today but is the row most likely to change. On **Jul 20 2026** HIP-4 market deployment went permissionless, and every deployer must now lock **500,000 HYPE** for at least six months, slashable by validator vote. Seven days in there is no published deployer count and no published locked balance, so there is no quantum to book — it is watched, not estimated.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled pools sit behind the Hyperliquid ledger, and together they are about **733M HYPE**, close to three quarters of the entire cap. The largest is the un-issued future-emissions reserve at roughly **428M**, which has no release schedule beyond the continuous staking-reward stream already counted in Sell #1. Second is the core-contributor escrow at **0x43e9abea1910387c4292bca4b94de81462f8a251**, holding **241,425,188 HYPE** — all of it delegated — against a **238M** grant, with a published calendar of **9.92M** a month that the ledger shows running at roughly **0.47M**; the undrawn remainder of about **240M** is the single biggest unmodelled risk in this coin. Third is the Hyper Foundation wallet at **0xd57ecca444a9acb7208d286be439de12dd09de5d**, holding **60,328,785 HYPE** against a **60M** budget, also fully delegated and never drawn down. Fourth is the community grant pool at about **3.09M**. Alongside them sits the buyback address at **46,049,682 HYPE**, an overhang only in the technical sense — keyless, accumulate-only, and validators have committed never to reach it. All four are refreshed from chain every rebuild. If the balance of any of them falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HYPE compares to other application-layer L1s

Hyperliquid sits in an unusual structural class: an L1 whose issuance is capped and pre-allocated rather than perpetual. A conventional proof-of-stake L1 mints new supply to pay validators, so its inflation is a permanent tax on holders that no amount of usage can retire. Hyperliquid pays validators out of a fixed **428M** reserve carved from a hard **1,000M** cap, which means its staking issuance has an end date built in and its total supply strictly falls over time as gas and spot fees destroy tokens. Against a chain with uncapped continuous emission, that is a materially stronger supply guarantee — and against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, Hyperliquid trades the fixed schedule for a usage-linked one.

The closer structural analogue is the exchange token with a revenue-funded buyback, and Hyperliquid is arguably the purest expression of that model because the buyback is not a quarterly corporate decision but an execution-layer operation the chain performs on itself. An exchange token's buyback depends on a company choosing to run it and can be paused; Hyperliquid's runs inside consensus, and the destination address has been made permanently unreachable by validator vote. The trade-off is that the buyback's size is a direct function of trading volume, so a quiet quarter shrinks it — which is precisely what the fee run-rate is showing right now.

Where Hyperliquid diverges from both classes is float maturity and custody concentration. With only **222.45M** of a **1,000M** cap freely trading, Hyperliquid's buyback fights a schedule rather than a steady state, and its supply behaviour is dominated by what a small number of identified wallets choose to do. The mitigating evidence is unusually strong: those wallets have consistently chosen to stake rather than sell, and the escrow's realised release has been running at roughly one twentieth of its entitlement for months. But a comparison against a fair-launch chain with dispersed supply is not the right frame — the deciding variable for HYPE is whether the escrow's restraint holds.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the core-contributor escrow releases on **Aug 6 2026**, **Sep 6 2026** and **Oct 6 2026**. The number that matters is not the headline **9.92M** but what actually leaves the escrow; a batch materially above the recent **0.47M** would move this reading immediately. Second, watch the buyback address at **0xfefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefefe**: its accumulation rate has fallen by roughly a third over the last month, and a further slide would flip the net decisively positive. Third, HIP-4 deployer locks — if a published count of permissionless deployers appears, each one adds **500,000 HYPE** to a six-month lock and would open Buy #4 as a real row for the first time. Fourth, the upstream float definition: the **May 26 2026** step showed that a single reclassification can move the reported number by **16.96M** overnight, so a second revision would widen or close the monitor gap without any HYPE changing hands. Fifth, the Hyper Foundation wallet at **0xd57ecca444a9acb7208d286be439de12dd09de5d**, which has never been drawn down; its first outflow would be a new Sell #3 with no precedent to size it against.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **+0.59% net** supply growth over 90 days — **3.83M HYPE** of staking issuance and realised team vesting against **2.51M HYPE** of buyback and burn on a **222.45M** float. The structural mechanism is a chain that funds its own buyback from trading fees and pays its validators from a fixed reserve inside a hard **1,000M** cap, so issuance has an end and total supply only falls. The key risk is custody rather than code: about **733M HYPE** sits in four team-controlled pools, and the core-contributor escrow is releasing roughly one twentieth of what its published calendar entitles it to — restraint that is voluntary and could stop. The ceiling is absolute at **1,000M** and cannot be raised, which is why Hyperliquid's supply question is entirely about the pace of distribution, never about dilution beyond the cap.
