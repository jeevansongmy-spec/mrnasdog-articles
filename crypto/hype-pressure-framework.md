# HYPE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

HYPE, the native gas and staking asset of the Hyperliquid L1, runs a hard **1,000M** cap and a fee-funded buyback that absorbed **2.04M HYPE** over the last 90 days. Against it, Hyperliquid staking issuance added **0.76M HYPE** to the tradable float, the core-contributor vesting vault released **0.89M** — **2.97%** of the **29.75M** its published unlock calendar authorised — and the Hyper Foundation moved **0.10M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **-0.19% net** for the trailing window and **-0.10%** forward. The decisive finding is what the Hyperliquid buyback does with the HYPE it buys: it parks it, permanently and visibly, but it does not destroy it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework books **1.75M HYPE** of sell pressure against **2.18M HYPE** of buy pressure over the last 90 days, a net of about **-0.19%** of the **222.45M** circulating HYPE float, and **-0.10%** forward as three vesting claims and three Hyper Foundation transfers land against a buyback running at an unchanged rate. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at about **-0.08%**, a gap of roughly **0.11 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so no data-conflict chip is raised. Hyperliquid is best labelled **deflationary on its own revenue, by accumulation rather than by burn**: the chain's trading fees more than pay for its own issuance, but the HYPE those fees buy is moved into a keyless address, not removed from the supply counter. The distinction is the whole story of this page.

## Sell pressure: where new HYPE comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **0.76M HYPE** over 90 days, and the structural point is that Hyperliquid does not mint it. HYPE staking rewards are drawn from an un-issued future-emissions reserve allocated at genesis and sitting at **411.82M HYPE** today, so total supply can never cross the **1,000M** cap no matter how long the chain runs. Hyperliquid's published reward curve sets the rate inversely to the square root of total HYPE staked and gives roughly **2.37%** a year at **400M** staked; at the **438.70M** currently delegated that is **2.45M** gross over 90 days, and the stake-weighted validator rate grossed up for commission independently gives **2.41M** — the two land **1.5%** apart. The reward is quoted as an annual rate, accrued every minute and paid out each day, so it is time-indexed rather than block-indexed and the consensus interval never enters the arithmetic. Gross issuance is not float, though: **301.51M** of that staked base belongs to the Hyperliquid core-contributor vault and the Hyper Foundation wallet, whose rewards compound inside balances the chain itself classifies as non-circulating, and both are settled below at what they actually released. Only the **137.19M** of genuinely market-held stake belongs in this row.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **0.89M HYPE**, and it is the row where the published HYPE unlock calendar and the chain disagree most violently. The calendar authorises roughly **9.92M HYPE** a month to Hyperliquid core contributors, which would be **29.75M** across this window. The vesting vault's own event log says otherwise: **452,001 HYPE** finalized out of staking on **Jul 6 2026** and **433,025** on **Aug 5 2026**, with **433,416** put into the seven-day queue on **Aug 30 2026** to land **Sep 6 2026**. That is **2.97%** of the authorised ceiling. The framework books released, not scheduled, and the reason is visible in the balance: the vault was granted **238,000,000 HYPE** on **Dec 28 2024** and holds **241.57M** today, because compounding staking yield has outrun every claim ever made against it. The principal has never been touched.

Sell #3 — Hyper Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **0.10M HYPE**, and it doubled inside this window rather than being re-derived: the Hyper Foundation wallet sent **50,000 HYPE** on **Aug 4 2026** and a second **50,000** on **Aug 31 2026**, both to the same receiving address, with a third **50,000** already unstaking. Its spot balance closes each time at effectively zero, so nothing is retained. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **0**, because Hyperliquid has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution anywhere in its supply; the listed company holding HYPE bought it on the open market, which makes it a buyer, and those coins were already inside the float.

## Buy pressure: where new HYPE goes

Buy #1 — the programmatic Hyperliquid buyback — is **2.04M HYPE**, the largest single number on the page, and it needs to be described precisely. Trading fees are converted into HYPE on the protocol's own order book and sent to an address nobody holds a key to. That address held **44,975,814 HYPE** when this window opened and **47,015,994** when it closed, and across eight readings going back to **Dec 31 2024** it has risen every single time and never once fallen. It stakes nothing. The purchases are real and they come out of the tradable float — **2,040,180 HYPE** bought at a window average of roughly **$63.92** is about **$130.4M** of buying against **$146.2M** of protocol fees over the same 90 days. But the HYPE is **parked, not destroyed**. Hyperliquid validators voted in **December 2025** to treat the balance as permanently burned, and that vote is a social commitment, not a supply-reducing call: the coins are still a named bucket of Hyperliquid's total supply today, and public trackers that have already subtracted them publish a total supply of **955.31M** against the chain's own **998.96M**.

Buy #2 — the protocol fee burn — is the row that is genuinely destroyed, and it is small: **0.14M HYPE** over 90 days. Hyperliquid burns execution-layer gas in full, base fee and priority fee alike, and burns the HYPE paid to launch new spot and perpetual markets. Enumerating those three paths gives **142,930 HYPE** across the window, or **1,588** a day; reading the supply counter against the fixed genesis ceiling gives **1,037,557 HYPE** destroyed over the chain's **645** days of life, or **1,609** a day. The two agree to **1.3%**. Note what did not happen: the conventional burn address held exactly **2.72 HYPE** at both ends of the window and has never been used, so a build that watched only that address would have reported no burn at all, while a build that watched only the supply counter would have missed the entire buyback. Both surfaces have to be read, and on Hyperliquid they say different things.

Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **0**: no discretionary purchase programme is disclosed, and the Hyper Foundation is a net sender this quarter. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is also **0**, and that is a change of direction rather than an absence. Hyperliquid's builder-deployed perpetual markets each have to bond **500,000 HYPE** for at least six months, which is exactly the kind of lock this row exists to capture, but the bonded pool shrank: four venues exited inside the window against one new bond and some top-ups, a net release of **1,372,524 HYPE**, leaving **3.11M** bonded across five live venues. One address looks like a fifth exit and is not — it is withdrawing **500,000** of surplus while keeping **500,217** bonded above the maintained minimum. Buy #5, a second Hyperliquid buyback funded by the yield on platform dollar reserves, was approved by validators with **69.08%** support and activated **Aug 26 2026**, but has paid nothing yet; its first transfer is **Oct 3 2026**, so it adds **0.26M** to the forward column and nothing to the trailing one.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled Hyperliquid overhangs are tracked, and together they are more than three times the traded float. The largest is the un-issued future-emissions reserve at **411.82M HYPE**, which has no published release schedule beyond the continuous staking-reward stream already counted in Sell #1. Second is the core-contributor vesting vault at **241.57M HYPE**, on a partial schedule whose realised draw is running at roughly three percent of its authorised ceiling and whose next claim of **433,416** is already queued for **Sep 6 2026**. Third is the Hyper Foundation's own **60.37M HYPE** of staked balance, firing at about **50,000** a month. Fourth is the buyback address itself at **47.02M HYPE** — the one overhang that is simultaneously counted as a buy, because the coins were bought out of the float, and tracked as an overhang, because they still exist and no code prevents a future protocol change from reaching them. Exchange-custodial wallets, dispersed venture holdings and unidentified large holders are excluded by rule. All four are read on every rebuild, and the commitment is simple: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HYPE compares to other exchange-revenue chains

The right comparison class for HYPE is not general-purpose L1s but tokens whose supply story is dominated by a revenue-funded buyback — exchange tokens with quarterly burns, perpetual-DEX tokens routing fees into open-market purchases, and app-chains that fund a buyback from their own product. Against that class Hyperliquid's distinguishing feature is the size of the fee economy relative to the token. Annualising the **$146.2M** of protocol fees measured across this window gives about **$591.1M** a year, against a market capitalisation of **$18.69B** on the classifier float or **$25.08B** on the chain's own circulating figure. That is a fee-to-market-cap ratio of **2.4%** to **3.2%** — deliberately a range, because the two supply classifications for HYPE disagree by **34%** and the denominator is genuinely contested. Either end of that range is an order of magnitude above chains whose fee economies round to hundredths of a percent of their capitalisation.

The second mechanism difference is destination. An exchange token that runs a quarterly burn reduces its own supply counter, and the reduction is irreversible in code. Hyperliquid does not do that with the bulk of its buying: it accumulates. The economic effect on the float is the same today — the coins are off the market and have been for twenty months without a single outflow — but the guarantee is different in kind, resting on a validator commitment rather than on a burn function. Hyperliquid also differs from halving-model chains with hard caps in a way that flatters it: because HYPE staking rewards are paid from a pre-allocated reserve rather than minted, the **1,000M** cap is not a policy target that governance could raise but an arithmetic ceiling the chain cannot reach. And unlike uncapped continuous-emission L1s, Hyperliquid's issuance is already smaller than the buying its own product generates.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, **Sep 6 2026**: the **433,416 HYPE** core-contributor claim already sitting in the unstaking queue finalizes, and the size of the claim after it will show whether the team's three-percent draw rate is holding or rising toward its authorised ceiling. Second, **Oct 3 2026**: the first reserve-yield buyback payment settles, and its actual size against the published **$135M** to **$160M** annual range is the single largest swing factor in the forward reading. Third, the buyback address: it has never had an outflow, and the first one would change this page's entire character — watch it at every refresh. Fourth, the builder-deployed market bonds, which net-released **1,372,524 HYPE** this window; a second quarter of venue exits would turn a one-off into a trend. Fifth, whether Hyperliquid's validators ever convert the December 2025 burn recognition into an actual supply-reducing call, which would move roughly **47.02M HYPE** from parked to destroyed.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads HYPE at **-0.19%** net supply over the trailing 90 days and **-0.10%** forward, against a monitor reading of **-0.08%** and a gap of **0.11 percentage points**. Hyperliquid's structural mechanism is a fee-funded buyback that absorbs **2.04M HYPE** a quarter out of a **222.45M** float, comfortably outrunning **0.76M** of reserve-paid staking issuance and **0.89M** of realised core-contributor vesting. The key risk is that the buyback parks rather than burns: **47.02M HYPE** sits in an address whose permanence rests on a validator commitment rather than on code, and a further **700M** of reserve, vault and foundation balances stands behind the traded float. The ceiling is genuinely hard — Hyperliquid pays rewards from a pre-allocated reserve rather than minting, so total supply has only ever fallen, from **1,000,000,000** to **998.96M**.
