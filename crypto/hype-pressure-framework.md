# HYPE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

HYPE, the coin of the Hyperliquid perpetual-futures exchange and its own Layer-1 chain, is close to balance. Over the last 90 days **2.19M HYPE** of new supply reached the market — staking rewards, monthly team payouts and Hyper Foundation spending — while the Assistance Fund buyback and the HYPE fee burns took **2.25M HYPE** back out. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads that as **−0.03%** over the last 90 days and **+0.13%** over the next 90, against a supply-monitor reading of **+0.02%**. HYPE has a fixed **1B** cap, but **411.18M** of it is an unissued reserve that pays stakers every day, and **241.48M** more sits in a staked team vault.
## The verdict, in one paragraph
Measured against the **222.45M HYPE** in circulation, HYPE supply changed by **−0.03%** in the last 90 days, and the forward projection is **+0.13%**. The supply monitor, which reads the circulating count over the same 90 days, shows **+0.02%**. The gap is **0.05 percentage points**, inside the 0.5-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown. The forward number is higher than the trailing one for one reason: the Hyperliquid buyback is set in dollars, and at today's price of about **$87.83** the same fee income buys fewer coins than it did at the window's average of about **$71.30**. HYPE is a fee-buyback token whose buyback roughly cancels its new supply.
## Sell pressure: where new HYPE comes from
Protocol inflation added about **764,100 HYPE**. Hyperliquid pays stakers from its unissued reserve at a rate that falls as more HYPE is staked; with **440.63M HYPE** staked the rate is about **2.26% a year**, so the reserve pays out about **27,300 HYPE a day** in total. Most of that goes to holders that are not counted as circulating — the team vault, the Hyper Foundation and its grants wallet — and only the rewards on the other **135.45M** staked HYPE count here. We watched the reserve fall by about **300 HYPE** in 16 minutes on Sep 29 2026, the same pace.
Vesting unlocks added **1.32M HYPE**. The core-contributor allocation sits in one staked team vault holding **241.48M HYPE**. On the published calendar about **9.92M** unlocks each month, but what actually left the vault was far smaller: **452,010 HYPE** on Jul 7, **433,033** on Aug 6 and **433,430** on Sep 6 2026, mostly in 50,000-coin lots to about 30 wallets. That is close to the **1.30M HYPE** the vault itself earned from staking in the same 90 days, so the team is paying out roughly its staking income and leaving the principal locked. The next three payouts fall on about Oct 6, Nov 6 and Dec 6 2026, projected at **433,430** each, **1.30M** in all.
Foundation and unscheduled unlocks added about **103,500 HYPE**: the Hyper Foundation's main wallet sent **50,000 HYPE** on Aug 4 and again on Aug 31 2026 through a transfer wallet to the chain's smart-contract side, and a community-grants wallet paid about **3,500 HYPE** in small grants. The forward figure repeats that pace. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is zero: there is no estate, no trustee and no time-lock releasing HYPE on a schedule.
## Buy pressure: where new HYPE goes
The programmatic buyback took **1,988,110 HYPE** off the market. Almost all Hyperliquid trading fees flow to the Assistance Fund, which buys HYPE on the open market around the clock. Its address has no private key, and in December 2025 validators voted, with **85%** of stake in favour, to treat its HYPE as burned. Its balance rose from **45.57M** on Jul 1 to **47.56M HYPE** on Sep 29 2026. The exchange collected about **$153.6M** in fees in the window, and the fund's buying works out to about **$141.8M** at the window's prices. Spent again at today's price, those dollars buy about **1.61M HYPE**, which is the forward figure.
The protocol fee burn removed about **265,000 HYPE**. On the exchange side, HYPE paid as spot-trading and network fees and HYPE bid in token-launch auctions is destroyed, and the chain's total supply fell by **235,024 HYPE** across the window, from about 999.14M to 998.90M. On the smart-contract side every gas fee is burned: about **29,990 HYPE**, estimated from 8,100 randomly sampled blocks. The forward figure holds that trailing rate. A Foundation buy is zero — the Hyper Foundation spends HYPE and does not buy it back — and a new long-term lock is zero too: HYPE staked with validators, including the **500,000 HYPE** each market builder must stake, stays in the circulating count, so a larger stake removes nothing from the float.
## Foundation and overhang
Four team-controlled pools could send HYPE to market. The largest is the unissued reserve, **411.18M HYPE**, which releases only through staking rewards. The team vault holds **241.48M HYPE**, all staked; its paper schedule runs through 2027 and it has released only its staking income so far. The Hyper Foundation holds **60.51M HYPE** in its main wallet (**60.46M** staked) plus **0.37M** across its five validator accounts, and the community-grants wallet holds **2.97M**. The Assistance Fund's **47.56M HYPE** is not an overhang: it has never sent a coin out and cannot. All of these balances are read from the chain at every rebuild, and if any of them falls between rebuilds, the outflow is added to the sell side at the next rebuild.
## How HYPE compares to other exchange tokens
HYPE's closest structural peer is BNB, another exchange coin that removes supply. The difference is in how: BNB burns a calculated amount once a quarter and aims at a fixed supply target, while Hyperliquid buys HYPE with trading fees every day, so its buyback rises and falls with trading volume. When the exchange is busy the HYPE buyback grows; in a quiet market it shrinks, while staking rewards and team payouts keep running.
Against perpetual-futures DEX tokens such as dYdX or GMX, HYPE sends a much larger share of its fees to its own coin: close to all of it, against revenue-sharing or partial buybacks elsewhere. That is why the HYPE buyback, **1.99M** in 90 days, is big enough to cancel almost all new supply.
Against proof-of-stake chains such as Ethereum or Solana, HYPE's staking reward is modest, about **2.26% a year**, and is paid from a pre-minted reserve rather than new minting, so the **1B** cap still holds. The trade-off is a large overhang: the reserve, the team vault and the foundation together still hold about **713M HYPE**, more than three times what circulates today.
## What to watch in the next 90 days
**Oct 3 2026**: the first payment from a second buyback stream, most of the yield on the exchange's USDC reserves, is due to reach the Assistance Fund, then every 30 days. Its size has not been published, so it is not counted yet; if it pays, it lands in the buyback. **Oct 6, Nov 6 and Dec 6 2026**: team vault payouts — any month well above about 433,000 HYPE would mean the vault has started releasing principal. Trading volume after the **Sep 24 2026** Binance listing moves the fee-funded buyback directly. And the staked total: if more HYPE is staked, the reward rate falls, but more coins earn it.
## Summary
HYPE supply is roughly steady: **−0.03%** over the last 90 days and **+0.13%** projected for the next 90, measured against **222.45M HYPE** in circulation. New supply of about **2.19M HYPE** — staking rewards, team payouts and foundation spending — is almost fully offset by a fee-funded Hyperliquid buyback of **1.99M HYPE** plus fee burns, with the bought coins held at a keyless address that validators treat as burned. The key risk is the overhang: about **713M HYPE** sits in the reserve, the team vault and the foundation, and the buyback only keeps pace while trading stays busy. The 1B cap is fixed; the float is not.
