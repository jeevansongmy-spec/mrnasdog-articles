# HYPE Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

HYPE, the native gas and staking asset of the Hyperliquid L1, runs a hard **1,000M** cap and a fee-funded buyback that took **2.06M HYPE** off the market over the last 90 days. Against it, staking issuance added **0.74M HYPE** to the tradable float, the core-contributor escrow handed out **1.42M** — a twentieth of the **29.76M** its published vesting calendar implies — and the Hyper Foundation moved **0.10M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid at about **-0.01% net** for the trailing window and **-0.23%** forward, because a second buyback stream funded by reserve yield begins paying in on **Oct 3 2026**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework books **2.26M HYPE** of sell pressure against **2.27M HYPE** of buy pressure over the last 90 days, a net of about **-0.01%** of the **222.45M** circulating float, and **-0.23%** forward once the new reserve-yield buyback lands. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at about **-6.64%**, a gap of roughly **6.63 percentage points**, which triggers a **monitor-gap warning**. Rebuilding the float day by day showed exactly where that came from: the whole move is one 24-hour step on **May 26 2026**, when the reading dropped from **238,832,075** to **221,874,756**, while every other day of the window sits inside ordinary price noise and the figure has barely moved since. Nothing on chain matches it — Hyperliquid's own total supply is falling by only about **2,364 HYPE** a day, the buyback address only gained, and the core-contributor escrow's outflows and the buyback's inflows roughly cancel — so the step is an upstream supply-model revision, not a market flow. Hyperliquid is best labelled **structurally neutral, turning deflationary on its own revenue**: a chain whose fees now pay for its own issuance with a little change left over.

## Sell pressure: where new HYPE comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **0.74M HYPE** over 90 days, and the structural point is that Hyperliquid does not mint it. Staking rewards are drawn from an un-issued future-emissions reserve, allocated at genesis and sitting at roughly **412.32M HYPE** today, so total supply can never cross the **1,000M** cap no matter how long the chain runs. Sampling that reserve live this session showed it draining at **26,388 HYPE** a day, which is **2.38M** gross over 90 days; the published reward curve, which sets the rate inversely to the square root of total HYPE staked and gives roughly **2.37%** a year at **400M** staked, independently implies about **27,190** a day at the **437.27M** currently delegated. Two angles within three percent of each other. But gross issuance is not float: **301.65M** of that staked base belongs to the core-contributor escrow and the Hyper Foundation wallet, and both of those are settled below at what they actually released. Only the **135.62M** of genuinely market-held stake belongs in this row, which is the **0.74M** booked here; counting the escrow's and the Foundation's accrual as well would book the same tokens twice.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **1.42M HYPE**, and it is the single number where Hyperliquid is most widely misread. Every unlock tracker publishes a uniform **9.92M HYPE** a month to core contributors on the 6th, which across three firings inside this window would be **29.76M**. This build followed the tokens all the way out of the escrow instead. It unstaked, waited out the seven-day queue, and then transferred **533,752** to 18 outside wallets on **Jun 6 2026**, **452,000** to 10 wallets on **Jul 7 2026** and **433,025** to 9 wallets on **Aug 6 2026** — **1,418,777** in all. The balance arithmetic settles what that money is. The escrow was granted **238M HYPE** in Dec 2024 and holds **241.30M** today, so not one token of the original grant has vested out. What leaves every month is the staking yield the grant earns, roughly **14,430 HYPE** a day, and the escrow harvests essentially all of it — its spot balance today is about **3 HYPE**. The vesting overhang is untouched; the yield on the vesting overhang is what reaches the market.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **0.10M HYPE**. The Hyper Foundation budget wallet sent **50,000 HYPE** out on **May 28 2026** and another **50,000** on **Aug 4 2026**, and has a further **50,000** sitting liquid after an unstake settled on **Aug 11 2026**. Unlike the escrow, this wallet genuinely does compound: it earns about **3,597 HYPE** a day on a **60.35M** stake and drew barely a third of that across the whole window, so its balance keeps growing. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **0**: there is no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution attached to HYPE anywhere. What is locked in Hyperliquid is ordinary vesting and an un-issued reserve, not an insolvency claim.

## Buy pressure: where new HYPE goes

Buy #1 — the programmatic buyback — is **2.06M HYPE**, and it is the mechanism the whole Hyperliquid supply story turns on. Platform fees are converted to HYPE automatically inside the chain's own execution and sent to a system address created without a private key, so the tokens cannot be spent, moved or reclaimed without a hard fork the validators have committed never to authorise; a validator vote in **Dec 2025** formally recognised that balance as burned. This build read the address directly at both ends of the window: **44,350,737 HYPE** at the open and **46,408,362 HYPE** at the close, so the realised absorption is **2,057,625**. That is deliberately not derived from the published fee split, and the reason to check the split is what the check shows. Hyperliquid's buyback is routinely quoted at **97% to 99%** of fees. Priced against the window's own daily prices, the address absorbed **$127.36M** of HYPE against **$182.24M** of total platform fees — a realised capture of **69.9%**, because fee tiers, staking discounts and builder economics intercept the stream before it arrives. What the venue charges and what reaches tokenholders are two different numbers.

Buy #2 — the protocol fee burn — is **0.21M HYPE**. Gas on the execution layer and the HYPE side of trading fees are destroyed outright rather than routed anywhere, so Hyperliquid's on-chain supply figure falls a little every hour instead of in visible burn events; measured against a multi-day baseline that is about **2,364 HYPE** a day, and the **972,746** destroyed since launch is consistent with a rate that has been rising. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **0**, because no discretionary buying outside the automatic fee conversion has been disclosed. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is also **0**, though not for want of a mechanism: anyone deploying their own perpetual market must stake **500,000 HYPE** for at least **183** days, and nine such markets are live; a rule announced on **Jul 20 2026** would extend the same requirement to permissionless outcome markets, but it needs a network upgrade that has not shipped. No dated in-window quantum, so nothing is booked.

Buy #5 — the reserve-yield buyback — is the change that reshapes the forward reading, and it contributed **0** to the trailing window by design. A validator vote on **Jun 12 2026** passed with **69.08%** of stake, 19 validators of 26, and routes about **90%** of the yield earned on idle dollar reserves sitting on Hyperliquid into the same keyless buyback address. Yield begins accruing on **Aug 26 2026** in 30-day blocks, each paid 8 days after its block closes, so the first payment lands on **Oct 3 2026** and the second on **Nov 2 2026**. Against the **6,176M** of stablecoins measured on the chain today, published projections put each payment between **$11M** and **$13M**; the low end is what this reading carries, and it is the whole of the difference between a level trailing window and a mildly deflationary forward one.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled balances sit behind the Hyperliquid ledger and together hold roughly **760M HYPE**, about three quarters of the cap. The un-issued emissions reserve holds **412.32M** and has no discretionary access at all — it drains only along the staking-reward curve, at the **26,388** a day measured above. The core-contributor escrow holds **241.30M** against a **238M** grant, entirely delegated, releasing its yield monthly on the 6th and leaving the principal alone. The Hyper Foundation budget wallet holds **60.40M** against a **60M** grant and fires sporadically — four draws in the trailing year, on **Feb 8**, **Feb 25**, **May 12** and **Aug 11 2026**. The buyback address holds **46.42M** and is the one balance that cannot move at all, having been created without a key and voted burned. All four are read on chain at every rebuild, and the rule is the same for each: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HYPE compares to other exchange-revenue chains

The natural comparison class for HYPE is exchange tokens with revenue-funded buybacks — the venue earns fees, converts a share to its own token, and removes it. Most of that class runs the buyback off-chain and quarterly: a company decides how much to spend, announces it afterwards, and holders verify nothing. Hyperliquid's runs inside protocol execution and settles to a keyless address, which changes what can be audited. The buyback quantum is a balance you can read rather than a press release you have to trust, and this analysis books **2,057,625 HYPE** precisely because that balance moved by that much. The trade-off is that a protocol-level buyback cannot be paused when revenue falls — it simply shrinks with fees, which is exactly what happened across this window as the buy rate stepped down month by month.

Against uncapped continuous-emission L1s, Hyperliquid sits in a different category again. Its staking rewards are a distribution from a pre-allocated reserve, not new issuance, so the **1,000M** cap is arithmetic rather than policy — no governance vote can raise it because there is no mint function to raise. That makes HYPE structurally closer to a fixed-cap chain than to a proof-of-stake L1 with a perpetual issuance rate, even though its float grows a little each quarter as the reserve pays out.

The comparison that matters most, though, is against tokens with large team allocations still to vest. HYPE has **241.30M** sitting in a core-contributor escrow — enormous relative to a **222.45M** float, and larger than the float itself. What separates Hyperliquid from the usual pattern is that in twenty months not one token of the **238M** grant has left; only its yield has. The overhang is real and it is the largest single risk in this reading. But the framework prices behaviour, not entitlement, and the behaviour so far is that the principal has never moved.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The core-contributor escrow fires again on **Sep 6 2026**, **Oct 6 2026** and **Nov 6 2026**; the number to watch is not the headline **9.92M** but whether the monthly draw stays near **0.43M** — the yield — or steps up into the principal for the first time. The first reserve-yield buyback payment lands on **Oct 3 2026** and the second on **Nov 2 2026**; the dollar size of that first payment is the single biggest unknown in this reading, because the forward figure above uses a published estimate rather than a settled one. Hyperliquid trading volumes drive the fee-funded buyback directly, and they fell through this window — the buy rate stepped from roughly **30,900** HYPE a day in late May to **17,300** a day in August — so a further slide would shrink Buy #1 without shrinking any sell row. The Hyper Foundation budget wallet has **50,000 HYPE** already unstaked and liquid, which has usually preceded a deployment. And the permissionless outcome-market rule announced on **Jul 20 2026** becomes a bookable Buy #4 the moment the network upgrade ships and deployers start locking **500,000 HYPE** each.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Hyperliquid (HYPE) at about **-0.01%** net supply over the last 90 days and **-0.23%** over the next 90, on a circulating float of **222.45M** inside a hard **1,000M** cap. The structural mechanism is a fee-funded buyback that converts platform revenue into HYPE inside the protocol and parks it at a keyless address validators voted to treat as burned — **2.06M HYPE** absorbed in the trailing window, with a second stream funded by reserve yield starting **Oct 3 2026**. The key risk is that the buyback scales with exchange volume, which fell across this window, while the **241.30M** core-contributor escrow does not: a quiet quarter would weaken the buy side without weakening the vest, and the escrow has so far released only its staking yield, never its principal. The ceiling is absolute — supply cannot exceed **1,000M**, staking rewards are a distribution from a pre-allocated reserve rather than new issuance, and the burned buyback balance can only move if the validators break a commitment they have already voted to keep.
