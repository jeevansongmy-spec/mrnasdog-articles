NEAR Protocol minted **7.99M NEAR** over the last 90 days — an annualised **2.49%** against the **2.5%** ceiling NEAR has run since the emission halving landed on **Oct 30 2025**. Against that mint, the NEAR Intents buyback bought **0.63M NEAR** on the open market and parked it, and the gas burn destroyed **59.9K NEAR** outright. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.57% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.56%** forward, against a supply-monitor reading of **+0.29%**. NEAR is **uncapped and effectively 100% circulating**: there is no unlock calendar left, so the epoch mint is the entire sell story.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads NEAR at **+0.57% net**: **8.08M NEAR** of sell-side supply against **0.69M NEAR** of buy-side offset, on a circulating base of **1,304.5M NEAR**. The supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+0.29%**, a gap of **0.28 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and no reconciliation walk was required. The residual is mechanical rather than contested: the framework credits the NEAR Intents buyback as buy pressure, while the monitor still counts the buyback multisig's NEAR as circulating. Forward, the framework reads **+0.56%**, because every mechanism in the NEAR ledger — the epoch mint, the Intents buyback, the gas burn — ran for the whole window and is still running. NEAR is **mildly and persistently inflationary by design**: an uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1 whose halved emission is being chipped at, not cancelled, by its own buy side.

## Sell pressure: where new NEAR comes from

Almost all of it is one mechanism. NEAR Protocol mints a validator reward at every epoch boundary, sized at **2.5% a year** of total supply, and that produced **7.99M NEAR** across the window. The figure is measured rather than quoted: NEAR's own block header carries total supply, and reading it at both ends of the 90-day window gives a net rise of **7,929,742 NEAR**, which added to the measured burn recovers the gross mint. Annualised that is **2.4898%** — a fraction under the 2.5% constant, because validators that miss the online threshold earn proportionally less and the unearned reward is never minted at all.

One detail matters more than it looks. NEAR blocks are not one second apart: across the window the chain produced **12,684,617 blocks** in 90 days, a realised interval of **0.613 seconds** — roughly **63% more blocks** than the protocol's nominal one-per-second figure. That does not raise the mint by a single NEAR, because the epoch reward is charged per unit of **time**, not per block: the protocol multiplies the annual rate by the epoch's elapsed wall-clock duration. Treating the fast blocks as extra issuance would have implied **13.05M NEAR** minted instead of 7.99M — a 63% overstatement of NEAR's single largest sell row. The same logic disarms the SPICE architecture preview and its 200-millisecond target: faster NEAR blocks would not mean more NEAR.

Of that mint, **0.80M NEAR** went to NEAR's protocol treasury account rather than to validators — about a tenth of the reward, arriving once per epoch. It is still new supply, so it sits inside the same row. The remaining three sell rows are quiet. **Vesting unlocks are zero**, and permanently so in practice: NEAR's circulating supply and total supply are the same number, the genesis lockup factory account held an unchanged **171,054 NEAR** at both ends of the window, and there is no release calendar left to run. **Foundation and unscheduled unlocks came to 88.6K NEAR** — a NEAR Foundation payments account drained from 82.0K to 709 NEAR, and an ecosystem swap-fund account fell 7.3K. **Long-term locked or bankruptcy is zero**: NEAR has no bankruptcy estate and no trustee distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new NEAR goes

The larger of the two live buy mechanisms is the buyback. Revenue captured by NEAR Intents — the cross-chain swap layer that dominates NEAR's fee economy — is used to buy NEAR on the open market and park it in a published multisig. That wallet went from **360.6K NEAR** to **989.7K NEAR** across the window: **0.63M NEAR** bought and held. Reading the balance at both ends is not enough on its own, so this build sampled it on **91 consecutive days**: the minimum across the whole series is the starting balance, which rules out the failure mode where an escrow drains mid-window and is refilled before anyone reads it. Exactly one outflow appears, **6.3K NEAR**, and it is already netted off. The destination holds — the coins are not burned and not staked — so the buyback's own balance becomes an overhang on the sell side.

The second mechanism is the gas burn, and NEAR has had one since day one. NEAR burns by destroying coins outright rather than by sending them to a dead address, so total supply itself falls; there is no burn address to read. Users paid **75.9K NEAR** of gas across the window, and **59.9K NEAR** of it was destroyed — about **79%**. The split is documented and live on-chain: a smart contract keeps **30%** of the gas spent running it as a developer rebate, and everything else — plain transfers, account creation, key management — burns in full. That the fees were destroyed rather than moved is provable by arithmetic: had they merely changed hands, total supply would have risen by the full 7.99M mint; it rose 59.9K less, exactly the burn.

**Foundation buy is zero** — the Intents buyback is the only open-market purchase anyone runs, so counting it twice would be double-booking. **New long-term lock is zero**, and directionally negative: NEAR's governance vote-escrow contract held **5.25M NEAR** locked at the start of the window and **4.93M** at the end, 0.33M less. Staked NEAR — **622.9M** across 421 validators — is not a lock either, since it comes free within two or three epochs.

## Foundation and overhang

NEAR's team-controlled overhang is small relative to a 1,304.5M circulating base, but it is real and it is growing. The protocol treasury account holds **1.01M NEAR**, up from 0.21M at the start of the window, and across 91 daily readings it never once paid anything out — it only receives. The Intents buyback multisig holds **0.99M NEAR**. Two ecosystem fund accounts hold **1.67M** and **0.50M NEAR**; the first took in 0.26M over the window and the second is the one that released 7.3K into the Sell row. The NEAR Foundation payments account that funded most of this window's Sell row is now effectively empty at **709 NEAR**, which is why the forward projection for that row is zero rather than a repeat.

The largest item is not on-chain yet. On **Aug 4 2026** NEAR co-founder Illia Polosukhin floated a Sovereign Fund seeded with roughly **30M NEAR** drawn from the protocol treasury, intended to wean the chain off inflation funding. It opened a two-week comment period and has had no vote and no execution, so under the framework's evidence rules it carries a value of zero — capacity is not cadence — while being enumerated here as the single biggest thing that could change NEAR's sell side. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How NEAR compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

The cleanest comparison is with uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1s that also burn fees. NEAR's **2.5%** emission is low for the class — many peers run 4–7% — and it is charged on total supply per unit of time, which makes it stable and predictable in a way per-block schedules are not. Where NEAR differs from the fee-burn chains it is most often compared to is scale: NEAR generated **75.9K NEAR** of gas fees over 90 days, which annualises to roughly **0.024% of NEAR's market capitalisation** — annual fee revenue measured against what the market pays for the whole coin, and near the low end of the chains the framework tracks. The point is not that the number is small in the abstract. It is that the burn it produces, **59.9K NEAR**, is **0.75%** of the 7.99M NEAR minted: two orders of magnitude short of offsetting emission. A fee-burn chain only turns deflationary when the burn approaches the mint, and NEAR's does not.

Against hard-capped chains the contrast is structural rather than numerical. A halving-model chain with a fixed cap has an emission schedule that ends; NEAR's does not, and its supply grows indefinitely unless governance changes the rate again — which it has already done once, cutting 5% to 2.5%. Against Layer 1s that still carry heavy vesting calendars, NEAR looks unusually clean: circulating equals total, so there is no cliff risk and no reserve waiting to hit the market. The whole NEAR supply question reduces to two live numbers — how fast the chain mints, and how much revenue the buyback can convert.

The buyback is what makes NEAR interesting against its class. Most Layer 1s have no buy side at all beyond a fee burn. NEAR routes swap-layer revenue into open-market NEAR purchases, and that line grew from nothing in **March 2026** to **0.63M NEAR** in a single 90-day window. It is still roughly a twelfth of the mint. But unlike the emission, which is fixed by protocol constant, the buyback scales with usage — and it is the only line in the NEAR ledger that can.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the developer gas rebate. NEAR governance ratified proposal **HSP-027** on **Jul 3 2026**, removing the 30% rebate so that **100%** of gas fees burn. It ships with nearcore v2.14, and this build verified it is **not live yet** — the chain still reports the 30% rebate, and the newest node release is 2.13.4 from **Sep 3 2026**. When it activates, NEAR's burn row rises by roughly **16K NEAR** per 90 days at current volumes. Second, the **30M NEAR** Sovereign Fund proposal: watch for it entering a formal House of Stake vote, which would be the first thing capable of moving NEAR's treasury overhang.

Third, the buyback multisig's balance. It has only ever accumulated; a sustained drain would flip **0.63M NEAR** of buy pressure into sell pressure at the next refresh. Fourth, NEAR Intents volume, since the buyback is funded by revenue and nothing else — the buy row moves with usage, not with policy. Fifth, any further move on the emission rate itself: Polosukhin has publicly argued for a fixed maximum supply, and a second cut would do more to NEAR's net figure than every other item on this list combined.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads NEAR Protocol at **+0.57% net supply growth** over the last 90 days and **+0.56%** projected forward, from **8.08M NEAR** of sell pressure against **0.69M NEAR** of buy pressure on a **1,304.5M NEAR** circulating base. The structural mechanism is a time-indexed **2.5%** epoch emission on an uncapped supply, halved from 5% in **October 2025**, offset by a revenue-funded open-market buyback that holds rather than burns and by a gas burn worth **0.75%** of the mint. The key risk is that both offsets depend on usage while the mint does not: NEAR Intents revenue can fall, and the emission will not. The ceiling is that NEAR has no maximum supply at all — the only thing capping dilution is a governance-set rate that has been changed once and could be changed again.
