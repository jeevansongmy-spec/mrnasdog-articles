NEAR Protocol mints about **7.9M NEAR** every 90 days to pay validators, at an emission rate that was halved from 5% to **2.5% a year** in late 2025. Against that, a fee-funded buyback bought roughly **0.84M NEAR** on the open market and the gas burn removed another **0.06M** — together about **0.9M NEAR**, far short of the mint. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.54% net** over the last 90 days and roughly the same forward, against a monitor reading of **+0.50%**. NEAR is **uncapped and 100% circulating** — the mint, not an unlock calendar, is the whole story.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads NEAR at **+0.54% net**: about **7.9M NEAR** of newly minted supply against roughly **0.9M NEAR** of buy-side offset, on a circulating base of **1,302.2M NEAR**. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+0.50%**, a gap of just **0.04 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and needs no reconciliation walk. The small residual is exactly what the buyback-and-hold mechanism predicts: the framework counts the buyback as buy pressure, but because the bought NEAR sits in treasury wallets the monitor still classifies as circulating, the two readings converge rather than diverge. Looking forward, the framework reads **+0.54%** for the next 90 days. NEAR is **structurally and mildly inflationary** — an uncapped chain whose halved mint is now within reach of its own buy-side offsets, but not yet cancelled by them.

## Sell pressure: where new NEAR comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the entire sell side on NEAR, and it is a clean, measurable mint. NEAR pays validators a per-epoch reward set by a protocol inflation rate, and that rate was **halved from 5% to 2.5% a year** on **Oct 30 2025**. This build does not assume the rate — it reads the on-chain **max_inflation_rate** parameter directly, which returns 2.5%, and then measures the actual result off the block header. Reading total supply at both ends of the 90-day window, NEAR created about **7.9M NEAR** — roughly 2.5% a year on a ~1.30B base, an identity that closes against the on-chain parameter to within a fraction of a percent. Because NEAR is **uncapped**, this mint has no end date; it simply scales with total supply.

The other three sell rows are all **zero**, and each for a concrete reason. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is zero because NEAR's original five-year distribution to team, backers and the foundation, running from the Apr 2020 genesis, physically expired in 2025. The proof is that NEAR's circulating supply now equals its total supply: there is no non-circulating bucket left to release on a schedule. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is zero as a value but carries the one discretionary event of the window: the protocol treasury account moved about **19.6M NEAR** out of its wallet in mid-May 2026, falling from roughly 20.3M to 0.7M. It books nothing because every NEAR is already inside the circulating count, so a treasury-to-elsewhere transfer adds no new supply to the market, and the destination could not be identified from primary sources this session — so it is monitored, not projected. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is zero and structurally so: no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches NEAR.

## Buy pressure: where new NEAR goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is the more interesting of NEAR's two live buy-side mechanisms. Since **Feb 23 2026**, fees earned by **NEAR Intents** — the network's cross-chain swap layer — are converted into open-market NEAR purchases. This build reads the three published buyback wallets on-chain and measures the actual accumulation over the window: about **0.84M NEAR**, a figure that triangulates cleanly against the roughly $479k of monthly protocol revenue divided by the ~$1.6 NEAR price. The critical structural fact is that this is a buy-and-**hold**, not a burn: the bought NEAR is held and staked inside treasury multisigs. The confirmation is arithmetic — if the 0.84M had been burned, total supply would have fallen by that amount, but the measured burn over the same window is only 0.06M. So the buyback removes coins from the tradable float without shrinking total supply.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is real but tiny. NEAR burns **70% of every gas fee**, with the remaining 30% rebated to contract owners. This build measures the burn directly as the decay in on-chain total supply, and the result is only about **0.06M NEAR** over 90 days — an order of magnitude smaller than one would model from third-party gas-revenue estimates, because NEAR's gas price sits at the protocol floor and network fees are genuinely cheap. Modelling the burn up from dollar fee figures would overstate it; measuring it off the header is the honest read. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is zero, since the only foundation-side open-market buying is the Intents buyback already counted in Buy #1. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is zero because staking on NEAR is not a lock: a delegator can unstake within a few days, so staked NEAR stays part of the float, and the veNEAR governance lock sits in per-user sub-accounts with no fixed release quantum to book.

## Foundation and overhang

NEAR's team-controlled overhang is unusually small for a venture-funded layer-1, and the reason is the same fact that zeroed Sell #2: NEAR is **100% circulating**. Its CG circulating supply equals its total supply, so there is no locked foundation reserve, no investor cliff and no community allocation waiting to be released — those buckets vested out by 2025. What remains to watch is on the buy side. The three **buyback wallets** — two Sputnik DAO fund-admin accounts and one dedicated buyback multisig — held about **2.67M NEAR** combined at this build, all accumulated since the Feb 2026 fee-conversion launch. Because those tokens are already inside the circulating count, they are a monitored balance rather than a new-supply overhang.

The other balance worth tracking is the protocol treasury account, which drew down about **19.6M NEAR** in mid-May 2026 and is re-accruing a slice of each epoch reward. Every one of these balances is read from the chain at each rebuild, so movements are caught within a build cycle. If any of them falls between refreshes and the tokens reach the open market rather than another treasury wallet, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh rather than being absorbed silently — but on the evidence of this window, the treasury movement stayed inside already-circulating supply and the buyback wallets only grew.

## How NEAR compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake chains

NEAR belongs to the **uncapped continuous-emission** class of layer-1s — the same structural family as Solana, Cardano and Polkadot, and the opposite of a hard-capped, halving-model chain like Bitcoin. On those halving chains the cap and the emission schedule are the same fact, and the float converges on a fixed number. NEAR has no such ceiling: it mints 2.5% a year forever, so its supply grows without bound and the only question is how fast. In that sense NEAR's halving of its own rate — from 5% to 2.5% in late 2025 — is the closest analogue it has to a Bitcoin halving, except it was a governance decision rather than a protocol-encoded schedule, and it can be changed again by the same route.

Where NEAR now diverges from its uncapped peers is the buy side. Solana and Cardano mint new tokens every epoch with no protocol buyback and only marginal burns, so their net issuance simply tracks their emission curve. NEAR has bolted a **fee-funded buyback** onto an uncapped mint, which is structurally closer to an exchange token's revenue-buyback than to a classic L1. The comparison that matters is with fee-burn chains: Ethereum can post negative net issuance when its base-fee burn exceeds validator rewards, because the burn scales with real demand. NEAR's burn does not yet scale that way — it is stuck near the gas floor — so today the buyback, not the burn, is the mechanism that could eventually flip NEAR toward neutral. The passed **HSP-027** vote, which routes 100% of contract gas to the burn instead of 70%, nudges that lever, but only slightly.

The structural ceiling on how well NEAR can score this metric is therefore set by two growing streams racing a fixed one. The mint is fixed at 2.5% of a rising supply; the buyback grows with Intents volume and the burn grows with on-chain activity. NEAR turns neutral only if those two buy-side streams together overtake a ~7.9M-per-quarter mint — a threshold the buyback alone would need to roughly quadruple to reach.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the **nearcore v2.14** release, expected around **Aug 2026**, which activates the passed HSP-027 vote and burns 100% of contract gas rather than 70%. It lifts the burn from about 0.06M to about 0.09M NEAR a quarter — real but small, and the first thing to confirm is simply whether it ships on schedule and bumps the protocol version. Watch the **NEAR Intents buyback run-rate**: it accumulated about 0.84M NEAR this window, and because it scales with swap volume, a step up in Intents activity is the single fastest route to a lower net reading. Watch the protocol treasury account, which drew down about 19.6M NEAR in May 2026 — a repeat outflow whose destination reached the open market would enter Sell #3. And watch for any new governance proposal to change the 2.5% emission rate again, since that rate is set by vote, not by code, and is the largest single number in this ledger.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads NEAR as **mildly inflationary** at **+0.54% net** over both the last and next 90 days, a clean match to the monitor's **+0.50%**. The mechanism is a halved **2.5%** validator mint of about **7.9M NEAR** a quarter, offset by a fee-funded buyback of about **0.84M** that holds rather than burns, plus a gas burn of only about **0.06M**. The key risk — and the key opportunity — is that NEAR is **uncapped**: the mint never stops, so the token only reaches supply neutrality if its buyback and burn keep growing until they overtake it. The ceiling is that, for now, they do not: the buyback is real and rising but still an order of magnitude too small to cancel the mint.
