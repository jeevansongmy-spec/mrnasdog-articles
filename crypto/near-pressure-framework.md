NEAR Protocol minted **7.97M NEAR** over the last 90 days to pay validators, at an emission rate that was halved from 5% to **2.5% a year** in late 2025. Against that mint, the NEAR Intents buyback took **0.85M NEAR** off the open market and the gas burn destroyed another **0.06M** — together **0.91M NEAR**, about an eighth of the issuance. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.54% net** over the last 90 days and the same again forward, against a monitor reading of **+1.01%**. NEAR is **uncapped and effectively 100% circulating** — the mint, not an unlock calendar, is the whole story.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads NEAR at **+0.54% net**: **7.97M NEAR** of newly minted supply against **0.91M NEAR** of buy-side offset, on a circulating base of **1,303.6M NEAR**. The supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+1.01%**, a gap of **0.47 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and needs no reconciliation walk. The residual is exactly what the buyback-and-hold mechanism predicts: the framework counts the NEAR Intents buyback as buy pressure, but because the bought NEAR sits in multisig and DAO wallets the monitor still classifies as circulating, the framework reads a little below the monitor rather than diverging from it. Forward, the framework reads **+0.54%** for the next 90 days. NEAR is **structurally and mildly inflationary** — an uncapped chain whose halved mint is now within sight of its own buy-side offsets, but not yet cancelled by them.

## Sell pressure: where new NEAR comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the entire sell side on NEAR, and it is a clean, measurable mint. NEAR pays validators a per-epoch reward set by a protocol inflation rate, and that rate was **halved from 5% to 2.5% a year** on **Oct 30 2025** by validator upgrade after a contested governance vote. The whole 90-day window sits under the lower rate, so there is no blended sub-window to correct for. Reading the chain directly at both ends of the window, total supply rose from **1,295.68M** to **1,303.60M NEAR**; adding back the coins the gas burn destroyed in the same period gives a gross mint of **7.97M NEAR**. That figure sits within a percent of what the 2.5% emission rate predicts on paper, the small shortfall being the uptime haircut on epoch rewards that validators never earn. One further change is worth naming: the protocol treasury's old slice of each epoch reward now reads zero on the live chain, so the whole 2.5% goes to validators. Because NEAR is **uncapped**, this mint has no end date; it simply scales with total supply, which is observed rising on-chain build over build.

The other three sell rows are all **zero**, each for a concrete reason. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is zero because NEAR's original five-year distribution to team, backers and the foundation, running from the Apr 2020 genesis, physically expired with its final milestone on **Oct 13 2025**. There is no non-circulating bucket left to release on a schedule and no cliff inside this window. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is zero as a value but is where NEAR's team-controlled overhang is enumerated: the three published revenue and buyback wallets, holding about **2.81M NEAR** combined, plus the legacy protocol treasury account at **0.83M NEAR**. Because every one of those coins already sits inside the circulating count, a move between wallets adds no new supply to the market, and no market release was observed from primary sources this session — so the row is monitored, not projected. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is zero and structurally so: no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches NEAR.

## Buy pressure: where new NEAR goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is the more interesting of NEAR's two live buy-side mechanisms. Since **Feb 23 2026**, the protocol revenue earned by **NEAR Intents** — the network's cross-chain intent and swap layer — is spent buying NEAR on the open market. This build sizes the row the only defensible way, by reading the destination wallets at both ends of the window rather than dividing a fee headline by a spot price: the three published accounts that receive the bought NEAR grew from **1.96M** to **2.81M NEAR**, a realised accumulation of **0.85M NEAR** in 90 days. An independent angle agrees — the protocol-revenue slice of NEAR Intents over the same 90 days was about **$1.74M**, which at the window's price band buys roughly the same quantity. The critical structural fact is that this is a buy-and-**hold**, not a burn: the bought NEAR is held inside multisig and DAO accounts. The confirmation is arithmetic — had those 0.85M coins been burned, total supply would have fallen by that amount, and it did not. So the buyback removes coins from the tradable float without shrinking total supply.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is real but tiny. NEAR destroys **70% of every gas fee**, with the remaining 30% rebated to contract owners. Summing the chain's daily fee take across all 90 window days and applying that split gives a burn of only about **0.06M NEAR** — an order of magnitude smaller than headline gas-revenue estimates suggest, because NEAR's gas price sits at the protocol floor and network fees are genuinely cheap. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is zero, since the only foundation-side open-market buying is the NEAR Intents buyback already counted in Buy #1. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is zero because staking on NEAR is not a lock: roughly **47%** of supply is staked, but a delegator unbonds within days, so staked NEAR stays part of the float, and the governance lock sits in per-user sub-accounts with no announced release quantum to book.

## Foundation and overhang

NEAR's team-controlled overhang is unusually small for a venture-funded layer-1, and the reason is the same fact that zeroed Sell #2: NEAR is **effectively 100% circulating**, with the circulating count equal to total supply. Its five-year distribution vested out in 2025, so there is no locked foundation reserve, no investor cliff and no community allocation waiting to be released. What remains to watch is mostly on the buy side. The three revenue and **buyback wallets** — two DAO fund-admin accounts and one dedicated buyback multisig — held about **2.81M NEAR** between them at this build, up from 1.96M three months earlier, all accumulated since the Feb 2026 fee-conversion launch. Because those tokens already sit inside the circulating count, they are a monitored balance rather than a new-supply overhang.

The second balance worth tracking is the legacy protocol treasury account, holding about **0.83M NEAR** with no published release schedule — a residue of the old arrangement under which the protocol treasury took a tenth of each epoch reward, a share the live chain now reports as zero. Every one of these balances is read from the chain at each rebuild, so movements are caught within a build cycle. If any of them falls between refreshes and the tokens reach the open market rather than another treasury wallet, that **outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh** rather than being absorbed silently. On the evidence of this window, the buyback wallets only grew, and nothing reached the market as fresh supply.

## How NEAR compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake chains

NEAR belongs to the **uncapped continuous-emission** class of layer-1s — the same structural family as Solana, Cardano and Polkadot, and the opposite of a hard-capped halving-model chain like Bitcoin. On a halving chain the cap and the emission schedule are one fact, and the float converges on a fixed number. NEAR has no such ceiling: it mints 2.5% a year indefinitely, so supply grows without bound and the only question is how fast. In that sense NEAR's halving of its own rate — 5% to 2.5% in late 2025 — is the closest analogue it has to a Bitcoin halving, except it was a governance decision rather than protocol-encoded scarcity, and the same route can change it again.

Where NEAR now diverges from its uncapped peers is the buy side. Solana and Cardano mint new tokens every epoch with no protocol buyback and only marginal burns, so their net issuance simply tracks their emission curve. NEAR has bolted a **fee-funded buyback** onto an uncapped mint, which is structurally closer to an exchange token's revenue buyback than to a classic layer-1. The comparison that matters is with fee-burn chains: Ethereum can post negative net issuance when its base-fee burn exceeds validator rewards, because that burn scales with real demand. NEAR's burn does not yet scale that way — it is pinned near the gas floor — so today the buyback, not the burn, is the mechanism that could eventually flip NEAR toward neutral.

The ceiling on how well NEAR can score this metric is therefore two growing streams racing one fixed stream. The mint is fixed at 2.5% of a rising supply; the buyback grows with NEAR Intents volume and the burn grows with on-chain activity. NEAR turns neutral only when those two together overtake a roughly **8M-a-quarter** mint — a level the buyback alone would need to grow about ninefold to reach. Framed as a volume test, daily NEAR Intents volume near **$177M** would be enough to flip the chain net-deflationary, against a trailing average closer to **$77M**.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the client release that activates the passed **HSP-027** governance vote of **Jul 8 2026**, which removes the 30% developer gas rebate so 100% of contract gas burns. It had not reached mainnet at this build, and it lifts the burn only from about 0.06M to about 0.08M NEAR a quarter — real but small, so the thing to confirm is simply whether it ships. Watch the **NEAR Intents buyback run-rate**: it bought 0.85M NEAR this window, and because it scales with swap volume, a step up in NEAR Intents activity is the fastest route to a lower net reading. Watch the **Protocol Sovereign Fund** floated on **Aug 3 2026**, which would seed a roughly 30M NEAR treasury whose yield pays validators — the only live idea that could cap issuance outright, though it is at the comment stage with no vote scheduled. Watch the buyback wallets and the legacy treasury account, whose on-chain balances would surface any move reaching the open market. And watch for any new proposal to change the 2.5% emission rate, since that rate is set by vote, not by code, and is the largest single number in this ledger.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads NEAR as **mildly inflationary** at **+0.54% net** over both the last and the next 90 days, against a monitor reading of **+1.01%** — a 0.47-point gap inside tolerance. The mechanism is a halved **2.5%** validator mint of **7.97M NEAR** a quarter, offset by a NEAR Intents buyback of **0.85M** that holds rather than burns, plus a gas burn of only **0.06M**. The key risk — and the key opportunity — is that NEAR is **uncapped**: the mint never stops, so the token reaches supply neutrality only if the buyback and the burn keep growing until they overtake it. The ceiling for now is that they do not: the buyback is real, measurable and rising, but still roughly an eighth of the mint it is trying to cancel.
