# SKY Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply was growing, trend cooling

*Originally published at [https://mrnasdog.com/research/sky/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sky/inflation)*

Sky has not created a single SKY in three months — the token contract returns exactly **23,462,665,147** at both ends of the quarter, to the decimal — and yet the Pressure Framework reads **+0.78%** of net supply reaching the market. The whole figure is one wallet: the Sky protocol treasury paid **246.5M SKY** of staking rewards out, while the Smart Burn Engine bought **63.6M SKY** back off the open market and put it into that same treasury rather than a burn address. Our inflation monitor reads **+0.76%** for the same window, a gap of **0.02 percentage points**, so no data-conflict flag is raised. A governance change executed on **Aug 17 2026** cut the reward stream to **96.9M SKY** and sped the buyback up to **93.9M**, which flattens the next 90 days to roughly **+0.01%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 28 2026** the SKY ledger reads **246.5M** of sell pressure against **63.6M** of buy pressure — a net of **182.9M SKY**, or **+0.78%** on a circulating base of **23.43B SKY**. The monitor reads **+0.76%**. The two agree to **0.02 percentage points**, which is inside tolerance and needs no data-conflict flag, and the reason they agree so closely is worth stating: they are measuring the same event from opposite sides. The monitor sees the counted float grow by **176.8M** coins; the framework sees the Sky protocol treasury drain by **182.9M** coins. On a 23.4 billion base those are the same number. Sky is best labelled a **closed-loop redistributor** — a token whose supply cannot change at all, whose apparent inflation is entirely a treasury balance moving into holders' hands, and whose buyback exists to refill that treasury rather than to shrink the supply.

## Sell pressure: where new SKY comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the first thing to settle. **Protocol inflation books 246.5M SKY**, but not one of those coins is new. The SKY contract on Ethereum was read at both ends of the window and returned the byte-identical **23,462,665,147.365967** both times. Sky has no block reward, no emission curve and no mint event in the window. What the row actually measures is the Sky protocol treasury paying staking rewards to lsSKY stakers, in **19** weekly transfers, at **18.6M SKY** a week early in the quarter, stepping to **22.2M** after a mid-July governance vote, then collapsing to **7.5M** in the final week. On top of the issued amount, stakers drew a further **56.0M SKY** of rewards earned earlier out of the reward pool. One caveat belongs on the record because Sky's own marketing does not carry it: the mint permission on the SKY token is still live and still assigned to the Sky governance proxy. The **23.46B** ceiling is a governance choice, not something the code forbids, so no row on this coin is ever treated as permanently settled.

**Vesting unlocks are 0**, and by construction rather than by luck. SKY inherited the distribution of MakerDAO's MKR, a token that launched with no team tranche, no investor tranche and no foundation cliff, and the vest contract set aside for SKY holds nothing. There is no unlock calendar for this coin because there is nothing to unlock. **Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are 0** as well: the Sky protocol treasury made exactly one kind of outbound payment all quarter, and it is already counted in the row above, so booking anything here would double-count it. **Long-term locked or bankruptcy is 0** — no estate, no trustee, no court-ordered distribution has ever touched MakerDAO or Sky.

A fifth row exists on this coin because the **MKR-to-SKY conversion** fits none of the four and is the mechanism most write-ups get wrong. It is booked at **0**, deliberately. **69.4M SKY** left the upgrade converter this quarter as MKR holders swapped at the fixed **24,000-to-1** rate — but the converter hands over SKY allocated years ago rather than minting any, MKR's own supply fell by **3,006** units in the same move, and the converter's holdings are already inside the counted circulating base. It is a redenomination of supply that already exists and is already counted. Booking it as dilution would invent pressure that no holder experiences. The arithmetic closes cleanly: **3,006** MKR at 24,000 is a **72.2M SKY** entitlement, and the **2.8M** shortfall against the **69.4M** actually released is the delayed-upgrade penalty, which stepped from **3%** to **4%** on **Jun 4 2026** and stays inside the converter.

## Buy pressure: where new SKY goes

**The programmatic buyback is 63.6M SKY**, and it is the only non-zero entry on this side. Sky routes protocol surplus into the Smart Burn Engine, which buys SKY against USDS on a fixed interval — **724** separate purchases inside this window. The destination is the load-bearing detail, and the name of the mechanism actively misleads about it: the engine's receiver address is the Sky protocol treasury proxy, the same wallet that pays the staking rewards. Coins bought are held, not destroyed, and they leave again as rewards. That is why the treasury could pay **246.5M SKY** of rewards while holding far less than that at any moment.

**Protocol fee burn is 0**, and this was verified on both surfaces rather than one. The SKY contract's own supply is unchanged across the window, and the dead address holds **4.82** coins of ancient dust that did not move either. Neither the supply call nor the unspendable address registers a burn, so the zero is real and the "burn engine" label describes an accumulation programme. **Foundation buy is 0**: the Sky protocol treasury took in exactly one counterparty all quarter, the buyback pair, so there is no separate discretionary bid to count. **New long-term lock is 0**, and that zero deserves a footnote — Sky's lockstake engine grew by **303.7M SKY** over the quarter and now holds **10.26B**, close to **44%** of everything in existence. But those are coins the market already counts as tradable, and the position can be withdrawn, so it is a demand signal rather than supply leaving the board.

## Foundation and overhang

Sky's overhang is unusually small and unusually concentrated. The Sky protocol treasury proxy holds **37.2M SKY**, down from **220.1M** at the start of the window — it is the entire non-circulating bucket, and it is now the smallest it has been. The MKR-to-SKY converter holds **2,140.8M SKY**, but that is earmarked one-for-one against the **87,764** MKR still outstanding and can only be released by destroying MKR, so it is a claim rather than a discretionary reserve. The staking-reward pool holds **112.5M SKY** already committed to stakers; an expired reward pool from a programme that ended in **Jan 2026** holds **19.6M** of unclaimed leftovers; and a Sky foundation wallet holds **1.1M**. All five are read from the chain on every rebuild.

The number that matters is the first one, because it is now binding. The Sky protocol treasury holds **37.2M SKY** and is committed to paying **96.9M** of staking rewards over the next 90 days. It can only do that because the Smart Burn Engine is expected to pay **93.9M** back in — **131.1M** of capacity against **96.9M** of commitment. The buyback has stopped being a mechanism that shrinks supply and become the funding line for the reward stream. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How SKY compares to other revenue-buyback governance tokens

Set against exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks, SKY looks similar on the surface and behaves differently underneath. An exchange token typically buys its own supply and sends it to an unspendable address, so every purchase is permanent and the supply curve bends down for good. Sky buys with the same discipline and at a higher frequency, then routes the coins into a treasury that spends them again. The difference is not cosmetic: a burn is a one-way door, and an accumulation wallet is a revolving one. A framework that only watched the token's supply figure would call both mechanisms identical and score Sky as though its buyback were deflationary. Reading the destination address is what separates them.

Against uncapped proof-of-stake chains, SKY is the cleaner asset by a distance. A staking chain mints new units to pay its validators, so the payout and the dilution are the same act and the cap does not exist. Sky pays its stakers out of a finite pot bought on the open market with real protocol surplus, which means the payout is bounded by revenue rather than by a mint function. That is a materially better structure — but only for as long as the surplus lasts, and the **Aug 17 2026** change is exactly a governance decision that the old payout rate was outrunning it.

Against hard-capped chains with fixed schedules, SKY trades predictability for control. A halving-model coin tells you its next quarter's issuance years in advance. Sky's next quarter is set by a vote that can land any fortnight and has already re-based the reward stream three times inside this one window — **240.9M** on **Jun 18 2026**, **286.7M** on **Jul 16 2026**, then **96.9M** on **Aug 13 2026**, each over 90 days. Governance responsiveness is a real feature here; schedule certainty is not.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the reward vest itself: the **96.9M SKY** stream set on **Aug 13 2026** runs out around **Nov 15 2026**, and whatever number replaces it is the single biggest input to this page. Second, the Sky protocol treasury balance, at **37.2M SKY** — if it keeps falling while the reward rate holds, the buyback is not keeping up and a further cut becomes forced rather than chosen. Third, the buyback interval, cut to **3,748 seconds** on **Aug 17 2026** with only **55%** of surplus now routed to it; either parameter can move at any executive vote and both move this ledger directly. Fourth, the delayed-upgrade penalty on MKR conversions, which stepped to **4%** on **Jun 4 2026** and has risen roughly quarterly — a further step would slow the remaining **87,764** MKR through the converter. Fifth, and the one branch that would break the whole reading: the mint permission is still live, so watch the token's own supply figure, not just the treasury.

## Summary

The Pressure Framework reads SKY at **+0.78%** net supply growth for the 90 days to **Aug 28 2026**, projected to flatten to about **+0.01%** over the next 90. The structural mechanism is a closed loop and is worth stating plainly: no SKY has been created or destroyed, the entire reading is **246.5M** of staking rewards leaving one Sky protocol wallet against **63.6M** of open-market buying flowing back into it, and the Smart Burn Engine burns nothing at all. The key risk is that the loop is now tight — the treasury holds **37.2M SKY** against a **96.9M** reward commitment, so the buyback is no longer optional supply support but the funding line itself. The ceiling is real at **23.46B** and has not moved a wei in three months, but it is enforced by a governance vote rather than by the contract, which is a different kind of promise.
