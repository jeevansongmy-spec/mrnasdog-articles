Sky, the former MakerDAO, runs its SKY governance token under a fixed ceiling of about **23.46B** — no block reward, no open-ended mint, and an on-chain total supply that does not move. Over the last 90 days the only new float was a SKY staking-reward stream of roughly **419M SKY**, and against it the Smart Burn Engine bought and permanently burned only about **235M SKY** — held back because governance throttled the buyback in March. The framework reads **+0.79% net** versus our supply monitor at **+0.80%** — a gap of just **0.01 percentage points**, well inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip. SKY is a capped token whose staking-reward payout currently runs ahead of a throttled buy-and-burn.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **May 6 2026** to **Aug 4 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **SKY at +0.79% net**. Sell pressure totals **419M SKY**, buy pressure **235M SKY**, against a circulating base of **23.39B SKY**. Our supply monitor reads the realised circulating change at **+0.80%**, a gap of just **0.01 percentage points**, inside the half-point tolerance, so the page ships no chip. The two readings agree almost exactly because they measure the same thing from opposite ends: the reward schedule minus the burn is **+184M SKY** over 90 days, and the monitor sees circulating rise by **+185M**. Because the on-chain total supply is fixed, that rise is the reserve bucket draining into the float. SKY is best characterised as a **capped governance token that is faintly inflationary while its burn is throttled below its reward payout**.

## Sell pressure: where new SKY comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **419M SKY**, and it is the only meaningful source of new float on the token. Sky has no proof-of-work subsidy and no uncapped mint; an on-chain read this session shows the SKY total supply pinned at the governance cap of **23.46B**, of which about **23.39B** is already circulating. What produces new float is the SKY staking-reward programme, which pays SKY out of a pre-funded governance buffer. A rewards-normalization executed in early **March 2026** set the distribution at about **838M SKY over 180 days**, a cut of roughly 162M versus the prior schedule, which works out to **419M SKY** across this window. That schedule runs at a steady rate through late August, so the forward row holds at 419M. It is a payout of already-minted coins rather than fresh issuance above the cap, but it still lands with stakers and counts as new float.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and the reason is a subtlety worth stating plainly. The MKR-to-SKY upgrade converts each MKR into **24,000 SKY**, and roughly **19%** of MKR is still unconverted — a late-upgrade penalty that began in September 2025 rises every quarter to force stragglers. But the SKY reserved for that unconverted MKR is already minted and already inside the circulating figure, held by the converter, which releases those pre-allocated coins rather than minting new ones. A conversion moves SKY from the converter to a holder without creating anything, so it re-labels existing supply rather than adding to it; booking it as sell pressure would double-count. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** — the reward buffer, the converter reserve, and the surplus buffer are protocol-controlled, but nothing moved out of them beyond the scheduled rewards, and the on-chain non-circulating bucket is only about **68.5M SKY**. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero: there is no Sky estate, trustee or court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new SKY goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the mechanism that defines SKY, and unlike most buybacks it genuinely removes supply. The **Smart Burn Engine** spends protocol surplus, denominated in the USDS stablecoin, buying SKY on the open market and then **permanently removing** what it buys — the tokens are destroyed, not deposited into a liquidity pool and not accumulated in a treasury, so there is no buy-and-hold overhang a supply monitor would keep counting as circulating. The catch this window is the pace: governance **throttled** the engine to about **$37,600 a day** for three months from early March 2026 to conserve surplus, then restored it, with August running near **$177K a day**. Across the window that adds up to only about **235M SKY** bought and burned — well below the reward stream — even though the program has removed more than **1.8B SKY** since it launched.

The other three buy rows are **zero**. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero because Sky has no separate base-fee burn — the only SKY leaving supply is the Smart Burn Engine already counted in Buy #1, which is the thing doing the burning. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero because the protocol's only market buying is that same engine; no separate ecosystem entity has disclosed an open-market SKY purchase into a counted wallet. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero: SKY staking has a short unstake path, so staked SKY stays effectively liquid rather than being removed from float, and no fixed-term lockup contract was deployed in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

Three protocol-controlled pools are tracked, though the on-chain non-circulating bucket totals only about **68.5M SKY**. The first is the **SKY staking-rewards buffer**, the source of Sell #1; it is replenished by monthly governance settlements and pays out on the published 838M-per-180-day schedule, so its release is scheduled rather than discretionary. The second is the **MKR-to-SKY converter reserve**, which holds the SKY still owed to the roughly 19% of MKR that has not upgraded; those coins are already inside the circulating figure, so their movement is a reclassification, not a release. The third is the **USDS surplus buffer** that funds the burn — a buy-side resource, not a sell-side overhang. None of them sold into the market outside the scheduled reward stream during the window. Each is re-read every rebuild, and if any balance falls between refreshes in a way that is not the scheduled reward, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SKY compares to other capped governance tokens

The comparison that matters is emission versus burn under a hard ceiling. Many DeFi governance tokens still mint through liquidity-mining or staking curves, so their sell side scales with usage and has no natural end. SKY is capped near **23.46B** and pays its staking rewards out of a pre-funded buffer rather than minting fresh supply above the cap, so its worst case is bounded — the reward stream is a reclassification of already-counted coins, not new issuance stacked on top. That is a structurally safer inflation profile than an uncapped emissions token, even in a window like this one where the net reads positive.

The second comparison is burn versus buy-and-hold. Exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks often keep the purchased supply in a treasury or a liquidity pool, where it remains re-deployable and still counts as circulating — which is why those buybacks and a supply monitor structurally disagree. Sky does the opposite: the **Smart Burn Engine** destroys what it buys, so the reduction is irreversible and shows up cleanly in every supply feed. That is why SKY's framework net and our monitor land within **0.01 points** of each other, where a buy-and-hold token would show a persistent gap. The trade-off is that the burn is funded by protocol revenue and discretionary in size — this window proved it, when governance throttled the spend and the net drifted positive because the reward payout kept running while the burn was cut.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the burn pace: the throttle has rolled off, so the forward window runs at the restored rate — about **285M SKY** at the August pace — which narrows the net to roughly **+0.57%** but does not close it while rewards run at 419M. Second, the reward schedule, which holds through **late August 2026**; a future monthly settlement could cut the 838M-per-180-day rate, lowering Sell #1 directly. Third, the MKR-to-SKY conversion, where about 19% of MKR is still unconverted under a rising penalty — the conversions themselves are supply-neutral, but a wave of upgraders selling freshly received SKY would show up as real market pressure the framework does not currently book. Fourth, any governance move on the Smart Burn Engine's spend rate, since that is the single lever that flips SKY from mildly inflationary to flat. Fifth, protocol revenue itself, because the whole burn is funded from surplus.

## Summary

Sky is a capped governance token, so its inflation is not an emission in the usual sense but a reward payout: the SKY staking programme released about 419M SKY over 90 days on a fixed 838M-per-180-day schedule. Against that, the Smart Burn Engine bought and permanently burned only about 235M SKY, held back by a three-month throttle that started in March 2026. That leaves the framework at +0.79% net, mildly inflationary, with our supply monitor at +0.80% — a 0.01-point gap that needs no explanation because both sides measure the same small drift into the float. The key lever is the burn, which is discretionary in size and revenue-funded; the key structural fact is that the whole system operates under a fixed ~23.46B ceiling that governance cannot lift without redeploying the token.
