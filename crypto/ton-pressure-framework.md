# TON Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

TON, the coin of The Open Network — renamed **Gram** on **Jun 15 2026** in a name-only change that moved no supply — is **uncapped**, and it pays for its speed in new coins. A flat block reward that was never cut when blocks got roughly six times faster minted **50.74M TON** over the last 90 days. A readable early-supporter lock let out another **16.14M TON**, and the Telegram treasury wallet released **40.20M TON** in six dated tranches, against a fee burn of only **0.15M TON**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+3.85%** net supply growth on a circulating base of **2.78B TON**, and our supply monitor reads **+4.06%** — a gap of **0.21 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so the two readings agree and no flag is raised.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Sep 1 2026**, the framework reads TON at **+3.85%** net supply growth — **107.08M TON** of new, unlocked and released supply against **0.15M TON** removed — and projects **+3.52%** for the next 90 days. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+4.06%**, a gap of **0.21 percentage points**, well inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag is raised. The two readings agreeing matters here, because they are built from different things: the monitor watches the tradable float, while the framework rebuilds the float from the chain's own state. TON's **total** supply grew **0.975%** over the window — from **5,190.41M** to **5,240.998M** TON, measured directly from block state at both ends — while the float grew faster, because coins kept stepping out of a lock and out of a treasury into circulation. TON is **structurally inflationary by block subsidy**: an uncapped chain that mints a flat amount per block, and then made blocks far more frequent without touching the amount.

## Sell pressure: where new TON comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the bulk of the story at **50.74M TON** over 90 days. The Open Network has no maximum supply and no proof-of-work subsidy left — the original giver contracts mined out in 2022 — so every new TON in existence today is a block creation fee minted to validators. This build read that fee straight out of real blocks rather than trusting a published rate: the minting field returned **1.7 TON** on every one of **120** sampled masterchain blocks and **1 TON** on every one of **36** sampled basechain blocks, evenly spaced across the window. The masterchain produced **18,959,258 blocks** and the basechain **18,505,848** block-slots. That is a masterchain block every **0.41 seconds**. Annualised, TON is minting roughly **206M TON** a year — about **3.9%** of total supply, against the **0.6%** still printed on most tokenomics pages, which were written before the 2026 speed upgrades and were never revised.

That gap between the quoted rate and the measured one is the whole point of counting rather than dividing. The block reward is **block-indexed**, not time-indexed, and no protocol constant absorbs a faster block interval — so when the chain sped up, issuance sped up with it, one-for-one. Two traps sit inside the count, and this build checked both. First, The Open Network is a sharded chain, and its basechain splits under load — but a split **divides** the per-block reward rather than duplicating it: inside this window the basechain ran three shards minting **0.25**, **0.25** and **0.5 TON**, summing to exactly **1 TON** per slot, with the child chains advancing in lockstep. Counting slots is therefore exact across splits, and a build that counted split blocks at the full reward would have overstated this row. Second, TON publishes no supply endpoint at all, so total supply had to be **measured**: it is the masterchain's own state balance plus the state balance of every live basechain shard, summed at the same height. Both window ends were single-shard, so the reading is complete, and it lands within **0.0004%** of the independently published total.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, contributes **16.14M TON** — far less than the calendar suggests. The TON Believers Fund is an escrow that early holders voluntarily locked for two years from October 2023, releasing monthly across 36 installments through October 2028. The published entitlement is about **37M TON** a month, implying roughly **111M TON** over this window. The contract's own balance says otherwise: it fell from **1,277.23M TON** to **1,261.09M TON** across the 90 days, so only **16.14M TON** actually left. Vested-but-unclaimed coins simply stay inside the lock, where they are not tradable. The framework counts the coins that left, not the entitlement — booking the schedule instead would have overstated this row roughly sevenfold. Claiming is also slowing: the last 30 days drained only **3.53M TON**.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **40.20M TON**, and it is the largest single change in this rebuild. The treasury wallet labelled for Telegram on-chain fell from **152.62M TON** to **112.42M TON** across the window in six dated tranches — **5M** on **Jun 15 2026**, **0.2M** on **Jun 19 2026**, **5M** on **Jun 22 2026**, **10M** on **Jul 3 2026**, **10M** on **Aug 8 2026** and **10M** on **Aug 29 2026** — settling into a monthly **10M** rhythm since July. Context matters: Telegram took over the foundation's principal operating responsibilities in **May 2026** and is now the network's largest validator. The coins land in payout wallets that are already inside the tradable float, so leaving the treasury is the moment they reach the market. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: TON has no bankruptcy estate, and the one enormous frozen block on this chain — **1,081.39M TON** across 171 dormant early-mining accounts — does not thaw until **Feb 21 2027**, outside both windows. The largest of those accounts moved **3 TON** in 90 days.

## Buy pressure: where new TON goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the only live buy mechanism, and it is small: **0.15M TON** over 90 days. Part of every transaction and storage fee is destroyed rather than paid out, recorded block by block on the masterchain. It matters that the burn and the block subsidy are genuinely **two** mechanisms and not one flow counted twice — a basechain block with no transactions still mints its **1 TON** while burning nothing, so one surface plainly moves without the other. But their arithmetic is joined: gross minting of **50,736,587 TON** minus the burn of **145,145 TON** equals **50,591,442 TON**, which is precisely the measured change in total supply. The net supply change is that identity, not a third source, and booking it as one would double-count TON's entire buy side. Set against the mint, the burn offsets **0.29%** of new supply; set against market value, it retires about **0.02%** a year. TON's fee economy is far too small to bend its supply curve.

The other three buy rows are **0**. Buy #1, programmatic buyback: The Open Network runs no buyback contract, and network revenue is not routed back into buying the coin. Buy #3, foundation buy: no foundation or treasury open-market buying is observed on-chain or disclosed. A listed treasury company holds roughly **222.07M TON**, but it is a third party rather than the project, and its holding grew from staking rewards rather than purchases. Buy #4, new long-term lock: staking grew **151.0M TON** over the window, which looks like a lock and is not one — stake is returned each election round and keeps counting as circulating, so it removes nothing from supply.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs are tracked. The governance-frozen early-miner reserve is the largest at **1,081.39M TON** across 171 dormant genesis accounts, locked by a validator vote until **Feb 21 2027**; the largest member was read on-chain this session and is genuinely static. The Believers Fund lock holds an undrawn **1,261.09M TON**, draining slowly into Sell #2 and running to October 2028. The Telegram treasury wallet holds **112.42M TON** after this window's releases, and it is the one that is actually firing. Deliberately excluded: the elector contract's **1,341.27M TON** of validator stake, which belongs to the validators who posted it; exchange custodial wallets, which belong to depositors; and the listed treasury company's **222.07M TON**, which is a third party rather than project-controlled supply. If any tracked overhang's balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How TON compares to other uncapped layer-1 chains

TON belongs to the uncapped continuous-emission class, alongside the large smart-contract layer-1s — but it sits at an unusual corner of it. Most uncapped chains index issuance to **time** or to a target staking ratio: an annual inflation percentage is declared, and the protocol adjusts per-block rewards so the yearly total holds regardless of how fast blocks arrive. TON indexes issuance to **blocks**. The reward is a flat fee per block, so a performance upgrade is also a monetary policy change, whether or not anyone intended it that way. That is how a chain that ran at roughly **0.6%** a year now runs near **3.9%** without a single governance vote on emissions.

Against the fee-burn layer-1s, the contrast is sharper still. Those chains can offset or exceed issuance because their burn scales with demand for blockspace; TON's burn retires **0.29%** of what it mints, so the burn is a rounding line rather than a counterweight. Against the hard-capped chains with halving schedules, TON has neither a ceiling nor a scheduled step-down — the only proposed brake is a cut of the block reward from **1.7** to **0.35 TON** on the masterchain and **1** to **0.2 TON** on the basechain, which remains unenacted on-chain. And against the exchange tokens that shrink supply with revenue-funded buybacks, TON has no buyback at all. What TON does have that none of them do is Telegram-scale distribution; the supply side is simply not where that advantage shows up.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the block-reward cut. It is the single change that would move this page: enacted, it would cut Sell #1 by roughly **four fifths**. It has been proposed, not passed, and the chain still mints **1.7** and **1 TON** today. Second, the Telegram treasury cadence — three more **10M** tranches would land inside the next 90 days on the observed monthly rhythm, and a fourth or a pause would move the forward reading either way. Third, block frequency itself: the collator activation of **Aug 17 2026** lifted the rate again, and the **Aug 21 2026** validator ballot raised the minimum shard-split threshold, which caps how much further sharding can multiply block slots. Fourth, the Believers Fund claim rate, whose next monthly release lands around **Oct 12 2026** and which has been decelerating. Fifth, and beyond this window but worth diarising, **Feb 21 2027**: the thaw of **1,081.39M TON** of frozen early-mining supply, an overhang worth more than a third of today's float.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads TON at **+3.85%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+3.52%** projected for the next 90, on a circulating base of **2.78B TON** and a total supply of **5.24B**. The mechanism is a flat block subsidy on an uncapped chain that keeps getting faster, so issuance rises with performance rather than with policy — **50.74M TON** minted in 90 days against a fee burn of just **0.15M**, with another **16.14M** stepping out of a voluntary lock and **40.20M** out of the Telegram treasury wallet. The key risk is that none of this is capped or scheduled down: the only brake is a proposed reward cut that has not been enacted, and the largest event ahead is the **Feb 21 2027** unfreezing of **1,081.39M TON** of dormant early-mining supply. The rename to **Gram** changed the label on all of it and none of the arithmetic.
