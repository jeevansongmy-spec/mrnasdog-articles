# ADA Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Cardano (ADA) adds supply to the market through two taps and removes almost none. The Cardano reserve paid **113.4M ADA** to stakers over the last 90 days, and **249.2M ADA** of Cardano treasury money left escrow and custody wallets for vendors and the market — more than twice the staking release. Against that, only **19.9M ADA** was donated back to the treasury, and Cardano has no buyback and no burn. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ADA at **+0.91% net** over 90 days against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.01%**, a gap of **0.10 percentage points**, and projects **+0.62%** for the next 90 days. ADA supply is capped at **45B**, so the growth is a release of existing coins, not an open-ended mint.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 13 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **ADA at +0.91% net**: **362.6M ADA** reached the market against **19.9M ADA** taken out, on a circulating supply of **37.52B ADA**. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+1.01%**. The gap is **0.10 percentage points**, inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so ADA ships with **no data-conflict flag**. The forward column reads **+0.62%**: the reserve release shrinks a little each epoch, and treasury payouts are projected at their four-quarter average rather than at this quarter's unusually heavy pace. The label for Cardano is **inflationary by treasury spending**: a capped proof-of-stake chain whose governance budget, not its staking reward, is now the larger source of new market supply.

## Sell pressure: where new ADA comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **113.4M ADA**. Cardano does not mint above its **45B ADA** cap. New ADA is drawn out of a protocol reserve: every five-day epoch the Cardano ledger takes a fixed share of what remains, sends a fifth of it to the Cardano treasury, and pays the rest to stake pools and delegators. Read from the ledger at both ends of the window, the reserve fell **180.49M ADA**, the treasury kept **67.09M ADA** of that, and **113.4M ADA** reached holders. Because the draw is a share of a shrinking reserve, the next 90 days project slightly lower, at about **110.2M ADA**. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: the Cardano genesis allocations were distributed in full years ago, and no team, investor or foundation vesting schedule remains.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **249.2M ADA**, and it is the largest line in the ADA ledger. The Cardano treasury is spent by on-chain governance vote, and **17** treasury withdrawals worth **217.2M ADA** were enacted in the window. A vote is not a sale, though. Most Cardano treasury money is paid into escrow contracts run by the budget administrator, Intersect, and released to vendors as milestones are met. The framework therefore books what actually left. The administrator's contracts and custody wallet took in **214.87M ADA** from the treasury during the window and still hold **225.53M ADA**, down from **250.70M ADA** at the start, which means **240.05M ADA** went out to vendors and the market. Smaller vendor escrows and one direct grant released another **9.14M ADA**. The largest single vote of the quarter, the **120M ADA** PRIME DeFi budget enacted on **Aug 17 2026**, has not moved from its escrow contract, so none of it is booked. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: no estate or long-dated lock holds ADA.

## Buy pressure: where new ADA goes

Almost nowhere. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: no Cardano contract, treasury rule or company programme spends revenue on ADA. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **zero**: Cardano transaction fees are not destroyed but pooled with the reserve draw and paid back out to stakers and the treasury, and ledger supply plus the reserve still sums to exactly **45B ADA** at both ends of the window. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**; the Cardano Foundation has been diversifying its reserves toward bitcoin and cash rather than buying ADA. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**. About **57%** of circulating ADA is delegated to stake pools, but Cardano delegation never moves the coins: they stay in the holder's wallet and can be spent at any moment, with no bonding period and no slashing, so staking removes nothing from the tradable float.

The one real offset is Buy #5, treasury donations, at **19.9M ADA**. Cardano lets any holder send ADA back into the on-chain treasury, which takes those coins out of circulation. Almost all of this window's total, **19.50M ADA**, arrived in the five days to **Jul 3 2026**, with a further **0.35M ADA** in late August. None of it came from the budget administrator's wallets. There is no schedule behind these donations, so the forward column counts **zero**.

## Foundation and overhang

Three ADA overhangs are tracked. The first is the Cardano treasury itself, **1.36B ADA** at the latest epoch boundary, re-read from the ledger on every refresh; every ADA that leaves it needs a governance vote. The second is the administrator's escrow and custody perimeter, now **225.53M ADA**, of which **120M ADA** is the untouched PRIME budget. That perimeter is already counted in circulating supply, so releases from it move coins to the market without changing the headline float. The third is the Cardano Foundation's own reserve, last reported at **561M ADA** at the end of 2025; it discloses holdings once a year and shows no dated sale inside this window, so it carries no value in the ledger. If the treasury, the escrow perimeter or the Foundation reserve falls between refreshes and the ADA reaches the market, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ADA compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1s mint new coins for validators on a fixed annual rate and hope a fee burn offsets it. Cardano is built differently. ADA has a hard **45B** cap, and its staking reward is a share of a finite reserve, so the issuance side shrinks by itself every epoch and eventually runs out. On issuance alone, ADA is gentler than a flat-rate proof-of-stake chain, and it has no founder unlocks left, unlike most younger Layer 1 tokens that still carry team and investor vesting.

The difference is the Cardano treasury. Cardano funds its ecosystem from an on-chain treasury that votes out budgets by governance, and in the current window that treasury pushed more ADA toward the market than staking did. It also lacks the two offsets other chains use: there is no fee burn, and staking locks nothing, whereas chains with bonded staking and unbonding periods immobilise a real share of supply. ADA sits in the inflationary group with other governance-funded chains, but with a cap that bounds how far it can go.

Second, vendor payouts from the **225.53M ADA** still in the administrator's perimeter, which set the forward treasury figure of **122.1M ADA**. Third, the pending OpenZeppelin treasury request for **11.8M ADA**, whose vote closes by **Oct 16 2026**; it would go into escrow first, not straight to the market. Fourth, the Cardano Constitutional Committee renewal seated on **Sep 6 2026** keeps treasury votes running, so new withdrawals remain possible. Fifth, any further donation back to the treasury would add to Buy #5.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Cardano (ADA) at **+0.91% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.62%** over the next 90. The structural mechanism is a capped proof-of-stake chain that releases **113.4M ADA** a quarter from a shrinking reserve while its on-chain treasury, paid out through escrow, released **249.2M ADA** to the market, with no burn, no buyback and no staking lock to absorb it. The key risk is treasury pace: **225.53M ADA** is already sitting in escrow and custody waiting to be paid, and every new governance vote adds to that queue. The ceiling is the **45B ADA** cap, which bounds total supply no matter how fast the treasury spends.
