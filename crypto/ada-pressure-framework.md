# ADA Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply was growing, trend cooling

Over the last 90 days about **344.3M ADA** reached the tradable float on Cardano — **113.8M** drawn out of the Cardano protocol reserve and more than twice that, **250.1M**, voted out of the on-chain Cardano treasury, against only **19.6M** handed back. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ADA at **+0.92%** net for the window and **+0.31%** forward; our independent supply monitor reads **+0.92%**, a gap of **0.005 percentage points**. Cardano is hard-capped at 45 billion ADA and mints nothing above it — but the cap is a ceiling, not a brake, and the Cardano treasury, not the reserve, is the bigger of Cardano's two taps.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 1 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads Cardano at **+0.92%** net new supply — **363.9M ADA** of sell pressure against **19.6M** of buy pressure, over a circulating base of **37,504.3M ADA**. Our independent supply monitor reads **+0.92%** across the same window, a gap of **0.005 percentage points** — far inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag is raised on the ADA page. The two readings arrive from opposite directions and still agree: the framework adds up the on-chain flows entering Cardano's float, while the monitor measures the float itself. Forward, ADA reads **+0.31%** as the reserve keeps decaying and the treasury spending queue empties out. The label for ADA is a **capped chain that is inflating from a committee, not from a printer**.

## Sell pressure: where new ADA comes from

Cardano does not mint ADA. Its supply mechanic is unusual and it is the whole story here: all 45 billion ADA already exist, and new supply is **drawn out of a finite reserve** rather than created. Every five-day epoch the protocol takes a fixed fraction — **0.3%** — of whatever is **left** in that reserve, adds the epoch's transaction fees, hands **20%** of the result to the on-chain Cardano treasury and pays the rest to stake pools and their delegators. Because the slice is taken from a shrinking pot, Cardano's issuance decays geometrically: we measured the reserve falling **0.16130%** per epoch, every epoch smaller than the one before. Over the window the reserve gave up **181.2M ADA**, of which **67.5M** was taken by the treasury before it could reach anyone — so Sell #1, protocol inflation, is the **113.8M ADA** that actually reached the float. Emission, staking rewards and treasury inflow are three different slices of one pot, and mixing them is the easiest way to get Cardano wrong.

Sell #3, the Cardano treasury, is the larger tap and it is discretionary. **18 enacted treasury-withdrawal governance actions** moved **250.1M ADA** out of the treasury and into the market over the window — more than double the reserve's contribution. A single **120.0M ADA** liquidity withdrawal enacted on **Aug 17 2026** is nearly half of that total; the rest is a dense run of ecosystem grants, infrastructure maintenance and governance funding that fired in ten of the window's eighteen epochs. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is a structural zero: the Cardano public sale and the three founding-organisation allocations finished releasing in 2019, and no cliff, schedule or locked tranche remains. Sell #4 is zero as well — no bankruptcy estate or court-supervised trustee distributes ADA on a schedule.

## Buy pressure: where new ADA goes

Almost nowhere. Buy #1, a programmatic buyback, does not exist on Cardano — a plan to convert part of the treasury into other assets and repurchase ADA with the proceeds has been discussed publicly, but nothing has been deployed and no purchase has happened. Buy #2, a protocol fee burn, is a **structural** zero and worth stating precisely: ADA is the Cardano ledger's own asset, so no destroy step exists for it, and Cardano runs no dead-address burn convention. We checked both surfaces the framework requires and they agree — total supply plus the untouched reserve read exactly **45,000,000,000 ADA** at each end of the window, to the smallest unit the ledger counts, and supply itself rose rather than fell. Cardano transaction fees are pooled each epoch and recycled, one fifth to the treasury and the rest to stakers. Fees landing back in the Cardano treasury are not a buy: that ADA was already inside the counted supply and merely moved between two counted buckets.

Buy #3, a foundation buy, is zero — the founding foundation's latest published accounts move the other way, with ADA down to roughly half of its balance sheet from about three quarters a year earlier. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the row most people expect Cardano to win, and it is the row Cardano loses most clearly. **56.99%** of ADA is delegated to stake pools, and **none of it is locked**. Cardano delegation is a pointer, not a lock: the coins never leave the owner's wallet, there is no bonding step, no unbonding period and no slashing, and a delegator can spend the stake at any moment. The only ADA the network genuinely immobilises is a set of refundable protocol deposits totalling **5.3M ADA**, about **0.014%** of supply — and that figure **fell** over the window. The one real offset is a coin-specific fifth row: **19.6M ADA** was handed back from the market into the on-chain treasury, almost all of it in one step on **Jun 28 2026**. It leaves the float, but a future spending vote can release it again.

## Foundation and overhang

Three Cardano overhangs are tracked. The largest is the **on-chain Cardano treasury** itself, holding **1,348.9M ADA** with no fixed release schedule — every ADA leaves it by governance vote, which is exactly why it produced the window's biggest sell row. It is read directly from the chain each rebuild. The second is the administrator account holding already-withdrawn programme funding: **135.1M ADA** sits in one readable script account today behind staged release gates, most of it the **120.0M** tranche released on **Aug 17 2026**. That ADA is already inside the circulating figure, so a further outflow moves price rather than the denominator — it is tracked for completeness. The third is the founding foundation's own corporate reserves, which are opaque at wallet level and disclosed only in annual accounts, so they carry no value under the framework's evidence rule.

One thing deliberately excluded: the **6,146.7M ADA** protocol reserve is not an overhang. No entity can accelerate it, it is released mechanically by consensus rules, and it is already booked continuously in Sell #1 — counting it twice would be a real error. A governance-agreed spending ceiling now caps how fast the Cardano treasury can drain: only **42.6M ADA** of headroom remains under it through **Jul 2027**. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ADA compares to other proof-of-stake layer-1 chains

Against uncapped continuous-emission layer-1 chains, Cardano looks disciplined: those chains mint fresh units against a target staking ratio with no ceiling at all, whereas ADA can never exceed 45 billion and its release rate falls every epoch by construction. Against hard-capped halving-model chains, Cardano looks softer. A halving chain steps its issuance down on a fixed block schedule that nobody votes on; Cardano's reserve decays smoothly, but its **larger** supply tap — the treasury — is decided by governance, which means Cardano's inflation has a discretionary component a halving chain simply does not have. Over this window the discretionary tap out-delivered the mechanical one by more than two to one.

The sharpest contrast is with chains that burn. Fee-burning layer-1 chains route a base fee into destruction, so usage claws supply back and heavy demand can flip them deflationary. Cardano has no such valve — fees are recycled, never destroyed — so on Cardano usage funds the treasury instead of shrinking the float. That matters more given how little Cardano earns: annualised Cardano transaction fees run about **2.8M ADA**, which against ADA's market capitalisation is a fee-to-market-cap ratio of roughly **0.0076%** — fees in the numerator, market value in the denominator, so a low reading means the chain earns very little for what it is valued at. Even if Cardano did burn fees, at that scale a burn would be cosmetic. The comparison that genuinely separates ADA from its peers, though, is custody: on chains where staking moves coins into a bonding contract with a multi-week exit queue, a high staking ratio really does immobilise float. On Cardano the same headline number immobilises nothing, because delegation never moves the coins.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the constitutional-committee renewal takes effect on **Sep 6 2026**, refilling seats that were due to lapse — which means Cardano treasury withdrawals can keep ratifying rather than freezing, and the forward Sell #3 reading depends on it. Second, the two live spending votes close on **Sep 16 2026**, together worth **4.3M ADA**; they are the entire visible queue, and the rest of the pipeline is empty. Third, watch whether a new headline tranche is proposed at all — the agreed spending ceiling leaves only **42.6M ADA** of room to **Jul 2027**, so another 120M-scale withdrawal would need a fresh ceiling vote first. Fourth, watch the two monetary parameters that set the reserve draw and the treasury's cut; both were unchanged at each end of this window, including across the in-window Cardano hard fork, and a change to either would re-base Sell #1 immediately. Fifth, watch for a Cardano burn mechanism actually shipping — one has been discussed, and it is the only thing that would move Buy #2 off a structural zero.

## Summary

Cardano is a hard-capped proof-of-stake layer-1 that added **+0.92%** to its float over 90 days and is projected at **+0.31%** for the next 90 — growing, but cooling. The structural mechanism is a finite reserve released at **0.3%** of the remainder every five days, which guarantees the 45 billion ADA ceiling holds and guarantees issuance shrinks over time. The key risk is that Cardano's bigger supply tap is not that mechanism at all: **250.1M ADA** of treasury withdrawals beat the reserve's **113.8M** by more than two to one, and that tap is opened by vote rather than by code. Nothing offsets it — Cardano has no buyback, no burn, and a staking ratio of **56.99%** that locks no ADA whatsoever because delegation never moves the coins out of the wallet. The ceiling is real and permanent; the brake underneath it is a committee.
