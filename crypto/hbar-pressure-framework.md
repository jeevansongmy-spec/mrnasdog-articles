# HBAR Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Hedera created all **50 billion HBAR** at launch and has no way to make more, so HBAR inflation is not emission — it is the Hedera Governing Council moving already-minted HBAR out of reserve. Over the 90 days to **Sep 4 2026** the tradable float grew **458.04M HBAR**, while nothing at all removed supply: Hedera runs no buyback and burns no fees. The Pressure Framework reads **+1.05% net** against a supply monitor at **+0.90%**, a gap of **0.15 percentage points**, and the remaining **6.17B HBAR** of Council reserve is a hard ceiling on how much inflation Hedera has left.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 4 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads HBAR at **+1.05% net** supply growth and projects the same **+1.05%** forward. Total sell pressure was **458.04M HBAR**; total buy pressure was **zero**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.90%**, leaving a gap of **0.15 percentage points** — inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships on the HBAR overview. The two track each other closely for a reason worth naming: on Hedera, the figure the wider market treats as circulating supply is a value the Hedera network itself publishes, so both readings rest on the same on-chain quantity rather than on two competing classifications. The cite-able label for HBAR is a **fixed-ceiling layer-1 releasing reserve onto the float in lumps** — inflationary today at a pace just above the one-percent line, with a hard limit on how much inflation remains.

## Sell pressure: where new HBAR comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** — and zero in the strongest sense available. Hedera minted the entire **50 billion HBAR** supply at genesis in 2018, and the Hedera ledger has no operation that creates HBAR. Total supply equals maximum supply, and it read identical to the last unit at four separate points across the past year; raising the cap would take unanimous consent from every member of the Hedera Governing Council. Staking is not an exception. Hedera pays staking rewards out of a pre-funded reward pool that already sits inside the circulating float, so a reward payment moves HBAR between holders rather than minting any. That pool fell from **189.58M** to **142.56M HBAR** across the window, which is a real drain and a genuine watch item, but not new supply — and at this pace it has roughly three quarters left before a top-up from reserve would be.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is also **zero**. No dated team or investor cliff falls inside this window. Hedera's old purchase-agreement rounds — the regulated SAFT and token purchase contracts — are spent down to a rounding error: **0.18M HBAR** in the first quarter of 2026, nothing in the second, and **0.26M** pencilled in for the third, against a programme that has released **12.7B** over its life. What remains undistributed sits with the Governing Council and moves when the Council decides it moves. That is a discretionary treasury mechanism, not a vesting cliff, and it belongs in a different row rather than being counted twice.

All of HBAR's real supply growth therefore lands in Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, at **458.04M HBAR** over the window. Reading the Hedera network's own supply record at both ends gives a float of **43,374M HBAR** on **Jun 6 2026** and **43,832M HBAR** on **Sep 4 2026**. The treasury side confirms it independently, from a different list of accounts: the Council's reserve accounts fell from **6.63B** to **6.17B HBAR**, agreeing with the network figure to **0.0005%**. The internal split is the interesting part. Every unit came out of the Council's **allocated** accounts, which drained from **2.76B** to **2.31B**; the **3.86B** unallocated pool did not move at all, and actually took **0.29M** back. The shape matters as much as the total: it is a staircase, not a slope. Four lots did all of it — about **100M** on **Jun 18 2026**, **317M** on **Jul 3 2026**, **1M** on **Jul 18 2026** and **40M** on **Aug 17 2026** — and nothing has left the reserve since. Anyone modelling HBAR unlocks as a smooth monthly drip is modelling the wrong mechanism.

Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: no bankruptcy estate or court-ordered distribution holds HBAR, so there is no trustee release to track.

## Buy pressure: where new HBAR goes

The Hedera buy ledger is empty, and every row was checked rather than assumed. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: neither the Hedera network nor the Governing Council operates a contract or programme that repurchases HBAR, and nothing new was announced inside the window. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and this one was verified from both directions because either one alone can be wrong. Hedera's total supply read the same **50 billion** at both ends of the window, so nothing was destroyed at the supply level; and the address that would collect destroyed coins holds **0 HBAR**, because nothing has ever been sent to it. Neither surface moved, which is the honest answer: Hedera has no burn at all. Fees are collected instead of destroyed. Node operators take their share, the remainder lands in a network treasury account, and a documented tenth of it tops up the staking and node reward pools. That treasury account took in **0.04M HBAR** across the entire window — coins moved, not removed, so they stay in the float and never enter a buy row.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**: no public evidence of a Council or Foundation purchase inside the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, and Hedera is the clearest example of why staking participation is not the same thing as supply being locked. Hedera uses proxy staking: a holder points their account at a node and the coins never leave the wallet. **11.38B HBAR** — about **26%** of the float — is staked, and the amount actually immobilised is **zero**. There is no minimum, the staked balance stays liquid at all times, there is no bonding, no waiting period to undo a delegation, and no penalty for doing so. A chain where stake genuinely leaves the wallet behind an unbonding delay removes supply from the market for the length of that delay; Hedera removes none. A US spot fund custodies a small slice of the float, but its shares redeem on demand, so it counts as demand rather than as a lock.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is the whole story on HBAR, and it is unusually easy to enumerate because the Hedera Governing Council publishes the account list itself. Two buckets hold everything. The **allocated** reserve — supply already assigned a purpose but not yet released — stood at **2.31B HBAR** on **Sep 4 2026**, down from **2.76B** ninety days earlier, and it supplied every unit of this window's release. The **unallocated** reserve — supply with no purpose assigned yet — stood at **3.86B HBAR** and did not move. Together that is **6.17B HBAR**, about **12%** of the ceiling, still held by the Council and readable on-chain at any moment.

Sitting on top of that is the Council's own published release calendar, and this is where capacity has to be separated from behaviour. The current report guides **3.82B HBAR** of releases for the July–September quarter, of which a single ecosystem line accounts for **3.5B**. Through **Sep 4 2026** the chain shows **342M** released in that quarter, leaving **3.48B** guided and undrawn with weeks to go. That is not a one-off. The same forward column guided **3.72B** for the October–December 2025 quarter and delivered **384M**; guided **4.41B** for January–March 2026 and delivered **527M**; guided **3.97B** for April–June 2026 and delivered **187M**. Four quarters running, the calendar has overshot what actually left by **88%** to **95%**. We therefore read the released figure, not the scheduled one, and carry the undrawn remainder as an overhang. Exchange-held and unidentified balances are excluded on purpose — they belong to depositors, not to the Council. If the balance in any of these Council accounts falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How HBAR compares to other fixed-supply layer-1 chains

Against a halving-model chain with a hard cap, HBAR looks inverted. On a halving chain the cap is distant, the issuance is continuous and small, and every coin that will ever exist enters the market through mining on a published curve nobody can alter. On Hedera the cap is already fully minted and sitting in identified accounts, so there is no issuance curve at all — the entire remaining inflation is a set of transfers a governing body chooses when to make. That makes HBAR's supply path more predictable in size and far less predictable in timing. The ceiling is real and close: **6.17B HBAR** is everything left, and once it is out, HBAR supply growth is over permanently. But between now and then, the pace is a committee decision rather than a protocol rule.

Against an uncapped continuous-emission layer-1, HBAR wins on the ceiling and loses on the offset. An uncapped chain typically pairs its issuance with a fee burn, so heavy usage claws supply back and a busy chain can turn deflationary. Hedera has no such valve. Fees are collected and redistributed rather than destroyed, so activity on Hedera never removes a single HBAR. That leaves usage carrying no supply-side weight at all — and the usage is thin in absolute terms. Measured transaction by transaction this week, the whole Hedera network charges about **7,100 HBAR** a day, roughly **$204,000** a year, against a market capitalisation near **$3.47B**. Naming the direction of that comparison matters, because it ships backwards constantly: the ratio is annual fee revenue divided by market capitalisation, so higher means more earned per dollar of valuation. Hedera's reads about **0.006%** — the market pays roughly **17,000 times** the chain's yearly fee take — which places HBAR in the bottom band of the field on that measure. None of the verdict above rests on that, though; the reading rests on the measured direction of the supply flow.

Against chains whose staking genuinely bonds coins, HBAR is the clean counter-example. A quarter of the HBAR float is staked, and none of it is committed. Proxy staking is a pointer, not a lock: no bonding, no unbonding delay, no penalty, and the coins never leave the holder's wallet. That is a genuine convenience for holders and a genuine absence on the buy side — where other chains can point to supply that physically cannot be sold this week, Hedera cannot.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, **Sep 30 2026**: the July–September release quarter closes with **3.48B HBAR** still guided and undrawn, and whether that ecosystem line finally lands or rolls forward a fifth time is the single biggest swing factor on this page. Second, the next Council treasury report, expected around **Oct 2026**, which will publish the October–December guidance and re-state whether the ecosystem commitment is still live. Third, the allocated reserve balance itself, now **2.31B HBAR** — it has funded every release so far, and it is the account to watch rather than the **3.86B** unallocated pool, which has not moved. Fourth, the staking reward pool at **142.56M HBAR**: if the Council tops it up from reserve rather than letting it run down, that top-up would be genuine new float and would appear in Sell #3. Fifth, any move to introduce a fee burn or a repurchase programme — Hedera has neither today, and the buy side of this page stays empty until one exists.

## Summary

Hedera is a fixed-ceiling enterprise layer-1 whose entire **50 billion HBAR** supply was minted at launch, so HBAR inflation is a treasury release rather than an emission. Over the 90 days to **Sep 4 2026** the Governing Council moved **458.04M HBAR** onto the float in four discrete lots, against a buy ledger of exactly **zero** — no buyback, no fee burn, and a staking system that locks nothing. The framework reads **+1.05% net** and projects **+1.05%** forward. The key risk is timing rather than size: **6.17B HBAR** of reserve remains, and a published calendar has guided far more than it delivered for four straight quarters, so the release pace is a decision that can change faster than any protocol rule. The ceiling, at least, cannot move.
