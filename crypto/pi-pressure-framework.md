# PI Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

PI is inflationary: circulating supply grew about **4.13%** in the 90 days to Oct 25 2026, and the next 90 days look much the same at **+4.11%**. No PI is minted on-chain, because all 100B were created at launch, but mined Pi keeps joining the supply as Pioneers migrate it to mainnet (**349.86M PI**), and project wallets sent **118.76M PI** to exchanges. Fees removed only **2.42M PI**; other trackers read **+3.24%** because they count migration alone.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Pi Network's circulating supply rose a net **+4.13%** between Jul 27 and Oct 25 2026, from **468.62M PI** of new supply against **2.42M PI** taken out, on a circulating base of **11.29B PI**. Other trackers show **+3.24%** for the same stretch, a gap of **0.89 points**. The gap has a plain cause: those trackers count only migrated mining rewards, so they do not see the PI that Core Team, Foundation and liquidity wallets moved onto exchanges. Because the cause is known and the amounts add up, the coin page carries no warning note. The projection for the next 90 days is **+4.11%**. In one line: PI is a pre-mined coin whose supply grows by migration and by steady project-wallet selling, with almost nothing taking coins back out.

## Sell pressure: where new PI comes from

Protocol inflation, the first row, is where mining shows up. Pi Network does not mint new PI on-chain; every coin has existed since launch, and 65B of them were set aside as a mining reserve that still holds **48.65B PI**. Pioneers earn Pi in the app, and when they pass the identity check and migrate, the network pays them out of that reserve. Between Jul 27 and Oct 25 2026 Pi Network paid **349.86M PI** to **657,309** Pioneer balances on mainnet. That is the coin's biggest source of new supply. Payouts ran steadily into late September, paused from Sep 22 to Oct 15 and restarted on Oct 16 2026; on Sep 17 2026 Pi Network had announced fixes to unblock migration for hundreds of thousands of Pioneers.

Vesting unlocks, the second row, books **0**. Pi Network counts a Pioneer's balance as circulating from the moment it is migrated, even while the Pioneer's chosen lockup keeps it from being spent. So when a mainnet lockup ends, the coin was already counted. About **6.05B PI** sits in those lockups today, and **477.55M PI** of it opens by Jan 23 2027. Those coins become spendable, which can matter for selling, but they do not add to the supply count. The team, Foundation and liquidity wallets have no on-chain vesting schedule at all.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, the third row, is **118.76M PI**. These are project-held coins that reached exchange deposit addresses in the window: **81.01M PI** from the liquidity reserve's hot wallet, **23.75M PI** from Core Team wallets and **14.00M PI** from the Foundation. Each of the three moved in monthly batches through August, September and October 2026. The liquidity reserve topped up its hot wallet with **50M PI** on Sep 3 and **100M PI** on Oct 21 2026, and **88.56M PI** is still waiting there.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy, the fourth row, is **0**. No court-held pile or bankruptcy estate of PI exists; the large reserves that could be sold are counted under the Foundation row instead.

## Buy pressure: where new PI goes

Programmatic buyback is **0**. Pi Network runs no buyback, and no project wallet buys PI back from the market.

The protocol fee burn is **2.42M PI**. Every Pi Network transaction pays a small fee into a pool that no wallet owns and nothing can spend, and that pool grew from 9.54M to **11.96M PI** in the 90 days. Pi Network has never run a token burn, so this fee pool is the only path by which PI leaves circulation, and it cancels only about half of one percent of the new supply.

Foundation buy is **0**. The Pi Foundation sells into the market rather than buying, and its venture arm invests in outside companies, which takes no PI off the market.

New long-term lock is **0**. Holders locked **126.93M** of their own PI in new mainnet lockups during the window, but locked Pi still counts as circulating, so the supply count does not fall.

## Foundation and overhang

Pi Network's project wallets hold far more PI than circulates. The mining reserve holds **48.65B PI**, and a further **4.67B PI** sits parked for migration payouts, with two payout wallets holding about 0.37B. The Core Team's 20B allocation still sits mostly in **8,870** untouched wallets with **17.74B PI**, plus **1.94B PI** in the wallets it pays out from. The Foundation reserve holds **9.50B PI** and five Foundation sub-wallets another 0.45B; the liquidity reserve holds **4.00B PI**, its sub-wallet 0.77B and its hot wallet 88.56M. None of these has a published release schedule, and none counts as circulating today. If any of these balances falls between checks and the coins reach the market, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next check.

## How PI compares to other pre-mined Layer 1 chains

PI belongs with the pre-mined Layer 1 coins such as XRP, Stellar's XLM and Hedera's HBAR, where every coin was created at launch and new supply reaches the market through releases from project wallets rather than through block rewards. On those chains the release rules are published: XRP's escrow opens on a fixed monthly calendar, and Hedera reports its treasury releases. Pi Network has no comparable public calendar for its Core Team, Foundation or liquidity reserves, so the only way to see what is released is to read the wallets themselves.

What makes PI different is mining. A proof-of-stake chain like Ethereum pays validators new coins every few minutes by a fixed protocol rule; Pi pays its miners in batches when they pass identity checks and migrate, so the pace follows the project's migration work rather than the protocol. That is why PI's supply can pause for weeks, as it did from Sep 22 to Oct 15 2026, and then jump. PI shares one trait with Stellar, whose code it builds on: fees fall into a pool that nothing spends, a small but permanent removal, and much smaller than the fee burns on busy chains.

Against fixed-cap coins with halvings, such as Bitcoin, PI looks very different. Its cap is 100B, but only **11.29B** circulates, so most of the supply that could ever come out is still ahead, held in project wallets.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The Pi Ecosystem Summit runs online from Nov 17 to Nov 19 2026; watch for any news on migration, lockups or the reserves, since the agenda so far covers only developer tools and a hackathon.

The migration pace after the restart on Oct 16 2026 matters most: Pi Network paid **41.25M PI** to Pioneers in the week to Oct 25, and the next payout-wallet top-up will show whether the faster pace holds.

Mainnet lockups open **173.67M PI** in November, **144.17M PI** in December and **124.63M PI** from Jan 1 to Jan 23 2027. That adds no new supply, but it does free up coins that holders could sell.

The liquidity hot wallet still holds **88.56M PI** after its Oct 21 2026 top-up, and the Foundation and Core Team have sent batches roughly every four weeks; the next ones would be due around mid-November and mid-December 2026.

## Summary

PI is inflationary: circulating supply grew **+4.13%** in the 90 days to Oct 25 2026 and is projected at **+4.11%** for the next 90 days. Pi Network mints nothing on-chain, but mined Pi reaches mainnet through migration (**349.86M PI**) and project wallets sold **118.76M PI** into exchanges, while fees removed just **2.42M PI**. The key risk is the overhang: about 88B PI sits in mining, Core Team, Foundation and liquidity wallets with no published release schedule. The hard ceiling is 100B PI, of which only about 11% circulates today.
