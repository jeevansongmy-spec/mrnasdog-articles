All **100,000,000,000 PI** were minted at genesis, and the Pi mainnet total read exactly **100,000,000,000.0000000** at both ends of the 90-day window. Supply grows purely because pioneers keep migrating already-mined Pi onto the mainnet — **588.9M PI** of it over the last 90 days, against **zero** buyback, **zero** burn and **zero** foundation buying. Our supply monitor reads **+5.75%** for the same window — a gap of **0.42 percentage points**, small enough that the two readings agree and no data-conflict flag is raised.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 11 2026**, the framework reads Pi Network at **+5.33%** net supply pressure: **588.9M PI** of mainnet migration on the sell side and **0** on the buy side, because Pi Network operates no buyback, no fee burn, no treasury bid and no net new lockup. Forward it projects **+3.73%**, using the realised migration rate of **4,577,412 PI per day** measured between **Jun 15 2026** and **Aug 11 2026** rather than the faster blended average of the trailing window. Our supply monitor reads **+5.75%**, so the gap is **0.42 percentage points** — small enough that the two readings agree and the page carries no data-conflict flag, and explained by the monitor deriving supply from market cap divided by price, which put its **Aug 10 2026** reading **331,000 PI** above the official figure published a day later. PI is a **migration-gated inflationary asset with no offsetting mechanism**: the ceiling is fixed forever, the float is not, and nothing in the protocol ever takes a coin back.

## The basis: circulating supply here is a migration counter

One measurement detail decides almost everything on this ledger, so it is worth stating before the rows. Pi Network's own published supply figure defines circulating supply **as** the migration counter: circulating equals cumulative migrated mining rewards, exactly and to the decimal, with the locked pool sitting inside it as a subset. It is not a sum of spendable balances.

That single definition has three consequences. A pioneer lockup expiring turns locked Pi into spendable Pi **inside** the already-counted total, so it cannot be a sell row. A new voluntary lockup moves spendable Pi back into the locked pot, again inside the counted total, so it cannot be a buy row. And Pi spent on network gas does not decrement the counter either, so the growing fee pool cannot be booked as a burn. Only one flow moves the basis: **new migration**. That is why this ledger is one non-zero row and seven zeros, and why booking anything else here would be mixing two different measurement bases into one number.

## Sell pressure: where new PI comes from

It does not come from the Pi blockchain. Sell #1, protocol inflation, carries the entire ledger at **588.9M PI**, and yet the Pi mainnet minted not one unit in the window — the chain-level total read **100,000,000,000.0000000** at the ledger closing on **May 13 2026** and again at the ledger closing on **Aug 11 2026**. The whole 100 billion cap was created in a single genesis event. What grows is the migration counter: the cumulative mining rewards that pioneers have moved out of the Pi app and onto the mainnet, which is exactly the figure Pi Network publishes as circulating supply. That counter went from **10,466,532,412 PI** on **May 14 2026** to **11,041,698,882 PI** on **Aug 11 2026**. Mobile mining plus identity-gated mainnet migration is Pi Network's issuance mechanism, and it is the only mechanism moving the number.

The rate is falling, sharply and consistently. Four dated readings of the same official supply figure give **9.8M PI per day** between **May 14 2026** and **Jun 15 2026**, **5.5M PI per day** to **Jul 14 2026**, and **3.6M PI per day** to **Aug 11 2026**. That decay is structural rather than incidental: Pi Network's own regulatory filing states that the systemwide base mining rate is dynamically adjusted against the network's supply limits, and the backlog of mined-but-unmigrated Pi that identity verification releases is finite. This is why the framework re-bases only the forward column, to **412.0M PI**, while leaving the last-90-day column at the measured **588.9M PI**.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each for a specific structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because Pi Network sold nothing to funds and has no investor cliff calendar; pioneer lockups do expire month by month, but locked Pi is a subset of the migrated total that already counts as circulating, so an expiry converts locked coins into spendable ones without adding a single new coin to the counted supply. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because no dated outflow from any team-controlled allocation was observed this window and the filing states that neither the issuer, the Pi Foundation nor any affiliate engages in direct selling efforts. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: there is no estate and no trustee winding Pi into this market.

## Buy pressure: where new PI goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows on the Pi Network ledger read **0**, and that is the single most important structural fact about PI inflation. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: Pi Network's regulatory filing answers the supply-adjustment questions with a flat negative — no supply adjustment protocol, no supply adjustment mechanism — and a community campaign asking the core team to fund buybacks and burns was declined. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** for the same reason: no treasury bid programme is disclosed anywhere, and none was observed.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is the interesting zero. Pi Network charges a small gas fee on every on-chain transfer, and those fees collect in the chain's protocol fee pool, which on-chain reads show has only ever grown — **580,819 PI** in **April 2025**, **5,231,159 PI** in **February 2026**, **7,751,368 PI** in **May 2026** and **9,859,564 PI** now, never once redistributed in sixteen months. It behaves like a one-way sink, but it is not a burn: those coins sit inside the pre-minted 100 billion and never left the migration counter that defines Pi Network's circulating supply, so booking them would mix two different measurement bases. The row stays at **0** and the pool is tracked instead. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** on the same logic — and the realised numbers agree, because the locked pool actually shrank from **6,221.1M PI** on **Jun 15 2026** to **6,183.0M PI** on **Aug 11 2026**.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled or pre-minted overhangs sit behind the Pi Network float, and all three dwarf the counted supply. The first is the pioneer lockup pool, **6,183.0M PI** as of **Aug 11 2026**, read straight off the chain — it lives inside the counted 11.04 billion, so draining it raises the tradable float without raising counted supply. The second is the non-mining side of the cap that has already been activated alongside migration: effective total supply of **16,987.2M PI** minus circulating **11,041.7M PI** leaves **5,945.5M PI** covering the core team allocation, the Pi Foundation treasury of up to **10 billion PI** and the **5 billion PI** liquidity pool, both created at genesis. The third is the remainder behind the ceiling: **83,012.8M PI** of the **100 billion** cap has never been mined or migrated at all. All three are watched on the chain, and the standing rule holds for all of them — if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How PI compares to other pre-minted, migration-gated chains

Mechanically, Pi Network is the opposite of a halving-model chain. Bitcoin and its hard-capped descendants issue new coins block by block on a schedule that shrinks by protocol rule, so the cap is approached asymptotically from below and every unit ever mined is immediately circulating. Pi Network inverted that: the cap was struck in full at genesis, so the chain has nothing left to issue, and the entire question of inflation collapses into a classification question — how fast does pre-minted supply cross from unmigrated into migrated. A halving chain's inflation is protocol-determined and therefore forecastable years out; Pi Network's is determined by how many pioneers clear verification and press migrate this month, which is why the observed rate can fall by two thirds in a single quarter.

Against continuous-emission proof-of-stake L1s, Pi Network looks worse on the buy side and better on the ceiling. A staking L1 typically pairs an uncapped or high-cap emission with a fee burn that claws part of it back, so its net figure is a genuine contest between two live mechanisms. Pi Network has no contest: the sell side runs alone. Every fee-burning chain we track can in principle flip deflationary in a high-activity month; Pi Network cannot, because no mechanism in the protocol destroys a coin or bids for one. What it has instead is a fixed **100 billion** ceiling that cannot be raised, and a migration flow that is visibly exhausting itself.

The closest structural analogue is not a chain at all but a locked-allocation token in the middle of a long unlock — an asset whose float grows by calendar rather than by issuance, and whose inflation ends when the schedule does. The difference is that Pi Network has no schedule. There is no dated cliff to point at, because migration is a user action, not a contract firing. That makes PI harder to forecast than a vesting token and harder to underwrite than an emission curve, and it is why this page re-derives the run rate from dated readings on every rebuild instead of trusting a published calendar.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the migration rate itself: if the **3.6M PI** per day observed through **Aug 11 2026** keeps decaying, the forward reading falls below **+3%** and PI leaves the heaviest inflation band for the first time. Second, the Protocol 26 mainnet upgrade whose node deadline was **Aug 11 2026**, and the Protocol 27 release flagged behind it — neither carries a supply mechanism today, but a protocol release is the only realistic vehicle for one being added. Third, any reversal on burns or buybacks: the core team has declined both, and a change of position would be the first buy-side row this ledger has ever carried. Fourth, the pioneer lockup pool, which fell **38.1M PI** between **Jun 15 2026** and **Aug 11 2026** — sustained draining lifts the tradable float even while counted supply is unchanged. Fifth, any disclosed deployment from the Pi Foundation treasury or the **5 billion PI** liquidity allocation, which would open Sell #3 for the first time.
