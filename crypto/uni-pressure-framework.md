# UNI Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Uniswap destroys UNI out of real swap fees, the Uniswap DAO pays UNI out of its treasury, and the two are almost exactly the same size — but this window is the first in which the burn came out ahead. Over the 90 days to Sep 7 2026 the Uniswap DAO released 5.00M UNI of growth budget while the burn mechanism destroyed 5.05M UNI, leaving the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reading UNI at −0.03% net supply and projecting −0.14% for the next 90 days. The finding that moves the number is a dated one: two Uniswap governance proposals executed on Jul 27 2026 extended protocol fees to a new chain and to v4 pools across seven chains, and the UNI burn rate stepped up 36% the moment they did. UNI has still never minted a token — supply reads exactly 1,000,000,000 — but the mint switch is live, not dead.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days ending Sep 7 2026, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads UNI at −0.03% net — 5.00M UNI of growth-budget release against 5.16M UNI of absorption, on a tradable float of 623.21M UNI — and projects −0.14% for the next 90 days. Our supply monitor reads the same window at +0.42%, so the gap is 0.44 percentage points, inside tolerance, and this build ships no data-conflict flag. The two numbers are close for a reason worth stating: the monitor infers UNI supply from market value divided by price, while the framework reads the Uniswap treasury balance and the burn-address balance on Ethereum directly, and this ledger reconciles against the tradable float to six decimal places — −161,138.52 UNI measured, −161,138.52 UNI derived. The label for Uniswap today: a fee-funded burn that has just overtaken a fixed treasury budget.

## Sell pressure: where new UNI comes from

Not from minting. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is zero: reading the UNI contract on Ethereum at both ends of this window returned exactly 1,000,000,000 UNI, the genesis number from Sep 2020, untouched. That flat reading is a real measurement rather than a hardcoded constant, because the UNI mint function writes the very storage the supply figure is read from — a mint anywhere in the window would have moved it. Flat is not the same as impossible, though. UNI carries a perpetual mint provision whose waiting period has already elapsed, whose ceiling is 2% a year, and whose caller is the Uniswap governance timelock. A live mint with a non-zero cap is a dormant option, not a closed door, so the Pressure Framework keeps this row watched rather than retired — roughly 20M UNI a year of capacity sits behind a single Uniswap governance vote.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero and this one really is finished. The original four-year distribution of UNI to the Uniswap team, investors and advisors completed in 2024, and UNI is fully unlocked with no cliff calendar left to run. The chain confirms it from the other direction, and this is the load-bearing check: every UNI that is not counted as circulating sits in exactly one place, the Uniswap governance treasury, so there is no vesting escrow anywhere quietly releasing tokens against a schedule.

The entire sell side is Sell #3, at 5.00M UNI. The UNIfication upgrade created a growth budget of 20M UNI a year, paid quarterly out of the Uniswap treasury to Uniswap Labs, starting Jan 1 2026. Three slices have now been paid, each exactly 5,000,000 UNI: Jan 5 2026, Apr 10 2026 and Jul 14 2026, about 95 days apart. One of those landed inside this window and it is the treasury’s only outgoing UNI payment across the whole 90 days. Sell #4, bankruptcy or long-term locked supply, is zero — Uniswap has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution anywhere in its supply.

## Buy pressure: where new UNI goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is 5.05M UNI and it carries the buy side. Under UNIfication a slice of every Uniswap swap fee accumulates in a vault that can only be emptied by buying UNI on the open market and destroying it. Over this window 5,048,001 UNI reached the burn address, verified transfer by transfer: 2,065 separate burns from 26 different senders, reconciling against the balance change with 0.0000002 UNI unaccounted for. Two details make this a reading rather than a headline. First, the Uniswap supply counter never moved — the UNI contract has no burn function at all, so a build watching total supply sees nothing and reports a wrong zero on the coin’s largest mechanism. Second, the burn is not paid for by the Uniswap treasury: the treasury’s only outgoing payment was the growth-budget slice, which went to a wallet that has sent nothing onward, and none of the 26 senders feeding the burn address is the treasury or that wallet.

The rate change is what carries the forward column. Two Uniswap governance proposals executed on Jul 27 2026 — one extending v2 and v3 protocol fees to Robinhood Chain, one switching on v4 protocol fees across seven chains — and the burn ran 48,204 UNI a day before that date against 65,512 UNI a day after it, a 36% step. The trailing column stays as measured; only the projection is re-based onto the post-vote pace, which is itself the conservative choice, since the most recent 30 days ran faster still. Buy #2, a protocol fee burn, is zero — not because no UNI burns, but because that flow is already counted once above; no UNI is destroyed per swap, and booking the purchase-and-destroy step twice would double the buy side. Buy #3, foundation buying, is zero. Buy #4 is 0.11M UNI: 113,138 UNI came back into the Uniswap governance treasury on Jul 30 2026, and treasury-held UNI is not counted as circulating, so that return takes those tokens off the tradable float.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled UNI overhangs are tracked. The largest is the Uniswap governance treasury itself at 267.2M UNI, roughly 43% of the tradable float, with no published release schedule beyond the quarterly growth budget. Next is the growth-budget wallet: it has received 15.0M UNI from the Uniswap treasury across the three quarterly slices, has ever released only 1.0M, and moved nothing at all inside this window — it currently sits on 14.0M UNI. Third, the original UNI distribution contract still holds 12.5M UNI of unclaimed genesis allocation, draining about 31,600 UNI across the window into coins that were already inside the counted float. Fourth is the dormant mint capacity, 2% of UNI supply a year, which is capacity rather than a holding but belongs on the same watch list. All four are read directly on Ethereum at every rebuild, and the rule is simple: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How UNI compares to other fee-burning DeFi tokens

The comparison that matters is fee-funded buy-and-burn against emission-funded incentives, and Uniswap sits at the far end of the first group. Uniswap’s pools collected about $141.8M in swap fees over 30 days against a market value near $4.39B — annualised, a fee base worth something like 39% of the token’s entire market value, among the highest readings in the set the framework scores. UNI is emphatically not a thin-fee token. Uniswap also still leads its category on volume: about 24% of everything traded across decentralized exchanges in the last 30 days went through Uniswap, roughly 2.2 times the next-largest venue.

What separates the fee base from the token is how much of it is allowed to reach UNI. Most of those fees still belong to Uniswap liquidity providers; the protocol’s own share runs about $11.1M over 30 days, and it is that slice — not the headline fee number — that funds the burn. Against exchange tokens that buy back on a quarterly schedule out of corporate profit, Uniswap’s burn is both more honest and more volatile: it is mechanical and permissionless and impossible to skip, but it rises and falls with swap volume rather than with a board decision. Against uncapped emission chains, UNI has the advantage of a supply that has genuinely never grown. Against a pure hard-cap asset, it has the disadvantage that the cap is a governance choice rather than code.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The next quarterly growth-budget slice of 5.00M UNI is due around Oct 17 2026 on the pattern observed so far, and it is the single largest scheduled UNI supply event in the window. Swap volume is the other side of the scale: at the post-vote pace of 65,512 UNI a day the burn beats the budget, and at the pre-vote pace of 48,204 it does not. Watch for the second half of the v4 fee activation and the wider v3 expansion Uniswap governance has signalled for the fourth quarter, either of which raises the burn without touching the budget. Watch whether the growth-budget wallet, holding 14.0M UNI untouched, begins releasing. And watch the mint provision, dormant since 2020 but still callable once a year at 2%.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Uniswap as close to flat and tilting slightly deflationary: 5.00M UNI released against 5.16M UNI absorbed over the 90 days to Sep 7 2026, a net of −0.03%, and a projected −0.14% for the next 90 days. The mechanism is genuinely good — a permissionless, fee-funded buy-and-burn that has removed 110.4M UNI in total, cannot be quietly switched off, and stepped up 36% the day Uniswap governance widened it — but it is running level with a 20M UNI a year treasury budget, so very little of that shrinkage reaches the holder. The key risk is not inflation but concentration: 267.2M UNI sits in the Uniswap treasury with no schedule attached, and the supply ceiling is a governance decision rather than protocol code, since the 2% annual mint has never been used but has never been renounced either.
