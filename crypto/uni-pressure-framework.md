# UNI Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Uniswap burns UNI out of real swap fees, and Uniswap also pays UNI out of its treasury — and for the first time the two are the same size. Over the 90 days to **Aug 25 2026** the Uniswap DAO released **5.00M UNI** of growth budget while the fee mechanism destroyed **4.64M UNI**, leaving the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reading UNI at **+0.06% net** supply — very slightly inflationary, not deflationary. Forward it turns over: the v4 fee switch that Uniswap governance executed on **Jul 27 2026** lifted the burn to a pace worth **5.29M UNI** per 90 days, so the next window reads **−0.05%**. UNI has never minted a single token — the on-chain supply still reads exactly **1,000,000,000** — but the mint switch is live, not dead.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days ending **Aug 25 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **UNI at +0.06% net** — **5.00M UNI** of growth-budget release against **4.64M UNI** of realised burn, on a tradable float of **623.35M UNI** — and projects **−0.05%** for the next 90 days on the post-upgrade burn rate. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **−1.75%**, so the gap is **1.81 percentage points** and this build ships a **monitor-gap flag**. The gap is identified in full and deliberately not closed: on **Jun 1 2026** a passed Uniswap governance vote returned **12,500,001 UNI** from eight delegation contracts into the governance treasury, DAO-owned tokens that were parked for voting power and were never tradable float. A supply tracker registers that as float leaving the market; the Pressure Framework does not, because nothing was bought and nothing was burned. The label for Uniswap today: **a usage-driven burn racing a fixed treasury budget, currently neck and neck**.

## Sell pressure: where new UNI comes from

Not from minting. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**: reading the UNI contract on Ethereum at both ends of this window returned exactly **1,000,000,000 UNI**, the genesis number from **Sep 2020**, untouched. That is not the same as impossible. UNI carries a perpetual mint provision whose waiting period has already elapsed, whose ceiling is set at **2% a year**, and whose caller is the Uniswap governance timelock. A live mint with a non-zero cap and an expired time lock is a dormant option, not a closed door, so the Pressure Framework keeps this row watched rather than retired; roughly **20M UNI** a year of capacity sits behind a single Uniswap governance vote.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **5.00M UNI** — and this is the row that changed. The original four-year linear vesting to team, investors and airdrop recipients ran from the **Sep 2020** launch to **Sep 2024** and is finished. What replaced it is the growth budget created by the UNIfication package Uniswap governance passed in **Dec 2025**: **20M UNI** a year, paid in **5M** quarterly tranches out of the governance treasury. The on-chain firings are **Jan 5 2026**, **Apr 10 2026** and **Jul 14 2026**, and the July one falls inside this window: exactly **5,000,000 UNI**, in a single transfer. The destination matters and was checked directly — it is an address carrying no contract code at all, an unrestricted operating wallet rather than a lock or escrow, so those tokens are spendable the moment they land and the supply classifier counts them as circulating from that block onward. The next tranche is due around **Oct 14 2026**, inside the forward window, so Sell #2 carries **5.00M** on both columns.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: no team-controlled Uniswap wallet sold into the market in this window, and the ability to sell is not the same as a habit of selling. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero as well — no estate, no trustee and no court-supervised distribution touches UNI.

## Buy pressure: where new UNI goes

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, carries the entire UNI buy ledger at **4.64M UNI**, and the mechanism is unusual enough to state precisely. UNIfication turned on the Uniswap protocol fee in **Dec 2025**; those fees accumulate in a vault contract, and the only way to withdraw them is to burn UNI through a second contract. Burning UNI is therefore a market purchase performed by whoever wants the accumulated fees. The destroyed tokens land at the conventional dead address, which held **104.76M UNI** on **May 27 2026** and **109.40M UNI** on **Aug 25 2026** — a realised burn of **4,640,000 UNI**. That is not just a balance difference: every transfer into the address was pulled and summed, and **1,800** separate burns from **23** distinct sender contracts add up to the same **4,640,000**, so this is many independent participants rather than one institutional movement. The address itself holds no code, which is what makes it a burn rather than a treasury park — the tokens are unreachable, not merely relocated. Cumulatively **109.40M UNI** has now been destroyed, of which the one-time **100M** treasury burn of **Dec 28 2025** sits outside this window.

The forward number is **5.29M UNI** rather than the trailing **4.64M**, because a documented mechanism change landed mid-window. Uniswap governance proposal 100, "Activate v4 Protocol Fees", passed with **46.6M** votes for against **1.27M** and was executed on **Jul 27 2026**, switching on protocol fees for v4 pools across seven chains. The burn address confirms the step: about **48,300 UNI** a day before that date, about **58,800 UNI** a day after it. The Pressure Framework projects from the post-change rate rather than the blended average, and applies that re-basing to the fee side only — the growth budget is fixed at **20M** a year by a separate proposal and is untouched by the v4 vote, so no sell row moves with it. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: there is no Uniswap-run buyback wallet, and both the fee vault and the release contract were read at each window end holding essentially nothing, which is what a pass-through looks like. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero, with no disclosed open-market accumulation by Uniswap Labs or the Uniswap Foundation. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero: UNI still has no native staking and no lock-to-earn escrow.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on UNI is large, concentrated and slow. The Uniswap governance treasury held **267.2M UNI** on **Aug 25 2026**, up from **259.6M** at the start of the window, and it is the source of every growth-budget tranche; only the **20M** a year budget is scheduled, and the rest has no release plan at all. Downstream of it, the growth wallet holds **14.0M UNI** of accumulated tranches it has not spent since **Feb 17 2026**. Two older Uniswap DAO deployment vehicles hold a further **14.8M** and **2.7M UNI**, both effectively static across the window, and the Uniswap Foundation's own last published report put its holding at **15.1M UNI**. Behind all of it sits the dormant **2%** mint provision — not a balance, but real capacity to add roughly **20M UNI** a year to a supply that has never grown by a single token. If any of these balances falls between readings, that outflow enters the UNI sell ledger at the next reading.

## How UNI compares to other fee-burning tokens

Mechanically UNI now belongs with the fee-burn tokens rather than the governance tokens it launched alongside. The closest structural analogue is a base-layer chain that burns part of every transaction fee: supply reduction scales with usage, nobody votes on it each quarter, and the burn is verifiable at one address. The difference used to be the issuance side — a fee-burning smart-contract chain still pays a validator subsidy, while UNI mints nothing. That advantage is now smaller than it looks. Uniswap does not mint, but it does distribute, and a **20M** a year treasury budget paid into a spendable wallet is economically the same shape as a **3%** issuance schedule on a **623M** float. The burn has to outrun it either way.

Against exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks, UNI is close in spirit and different in plumbing. An exchange token's issuer buys on the open market with its own revenue and burns on an announced schedule, which makes the flow predictable and the trust assumption large. Uniswap instead makes the burn a precondition for claiming fees, so no Uniswap entity has to be trusted to execute it and the burn runs continuously — but its size is set by trading volume, not by a committee, so a quiet quarter shrinks it automatically while the growth budget keeps paying out on the calendar. That asymmetry is the real risk in this ledger: the sell side is fixed and the buy side is cyclical.

The comparison UNI still loses is against tokens with a must-hold reason to exist. UNI is not gas, it is not required to trade on Uniswap, and it cannot be staked with the protocol for a yield. The fee switch made UNI a claim on Uniswap's cash flows in the indirect sense that those flows shrink the supply, but the token is still, in every direct sense, a governance token. That is a business-model observation rather than an inflation one, and it does not change the supply arithmetic on this page.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the next growth-budget tranche, due on the quarterly rhythm around **Oct 14 2026**: **5.00M UNI** leaving the Uniswap treasury into spendable hands, and the single largest booked item on the sell side. Second, the burn rate, which is now the only moving part on the buy side — the **Jul 27 2026** expansion lifted it about **22%**, and a "Part 2" proposal covering the remaining chains has been flagged but not yet submitted; if it executes, the forward reading turns clearly deflationary. Third, the **2%** mint provision, a live Uniswap governance option and the largest single change that could hit this page. Fourth, any binding vote on paying protocol fees to UNI stakers instead of burning them, which would convert Buy #2 into a yield and remove the deflation entirely. Fifth, Uniswap trading volume, which is the input that sets the burn and the reason a bear quarter would flip this reading positive.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Uniswap at **+0.06% net supply** over the 90 days to **Aug 25 2026** and projects **−0.05%** forward. The structural mechanism is a usage-funded burn running against a fixed treasury budget: swap fees are claimable only by destroying UNI, which sent **4.64M UNI** to the burn address in this window and **109.40M UNI** in total, while the DAO paid **5.00M UNI** of growth budget out of its treasury on **Jul 14 2026**. The key risk is that those two sides are not symmetrical — the budget is contractual and the burn is cyclical — so a slow quarter for Uniswap volume turns UNI inflationary without anyone voting for it. The ceiling is firm: **1 billion UNI** was the maximum on day one, **109.40M** of it is already unreachable, and the only route above that ceiling is the dormant **2% a year** mint provision that Uniswap governance has never used.
