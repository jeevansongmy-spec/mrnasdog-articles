The airdrop that used to release BTT finished in **February 2025**, and the quarterly buyback BitTorrent announced on **Jul 6 2026** has not published a size or executed a purchase. Our supply monitor reads **-0.03%**, a gap of **0.03 percentage points** that is well inside tolerance, so **no data-conflict flag** ships with this page.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **May 15 2026** to **Aug 13 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **BTT at 0.00% net**: sell pressure of **zero** against buy pressure of **zero**, on a circulating base of **987.04T BTT** out of a hard **990T** cap. Every one of the eight canonical rows is empty, and each is empty for a structural reason rather than for lack of looking. Our supply monitor reads **-0.03%** for the same period, a gap of **0.03 percentage points** that sits far inside the framework's half-point tolerance; that small negative is market-cap-and-price rounding on a token whose on-chain total supply is a fixed constant, not a real contraction. The label for BitTorrent is a **frozen supply — capped in both directions, with a buyback that has been announced but not yet sized**.

## Sell pressure: where new BTT comes from

It comes from nowhere, and the token contract is the proof. We read the deployed BTT contract's own function list on TRON this session: it can transfer, approve, adjust allowances and report balances — and nothing else. There is **no mint function** in the code, so Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** permanently; the full **990T BTT** was created once at deployment and the on-chain total supply has read exactly that ever since. BitTorrent Chain validator and delegator staking rewards are paid out of a pre-funded rewards contract holding existing BTT, so they redistribute coins rather than issue them. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: the monthly BTT airdrop to TRON holders, which ran from **March 2019** and grew each year, paid its final **1,402,500,000 BTT** tranche on **Feb 11 2025** and is finished. A residual partnership and ecosystem release is documented as running to **2028**, but no dated cliff with a published quantum falls inside this window, and the tradable float did not rise — the framework books what was released, not what was scheduled. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: about **2.96T BTT** sits outside the circulating count, roughly **0.30%** of the cap, and none of it moved. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: no estate, trustee schedule or court order touches BTT.

## Buy pressure: where new BTT goes

The same contract that forbids minting also forbids destruction, and that reshapes the buy side. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** and permanently so: the BTT contract exposes **no burn function**, which means a "burn" of BTT can only ever be a transfer to an address nobody holds the keys to. The coins are stranded, not destroyed, and the reported total supply stays at **990T** however many are retired. In its entire life the main dead address on TRON has accumulated about **7.9B BTT** — under **0.001%** of supply — and it has received none since **Dec 5 2025**. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** today but is the live story: on **Jul 6 2026** BitTorrent announced that **100%** of revenue from its decentralised services — BitTorrent Speed and the BTTInferGrid AI-inference network launched **Jun 17 2026** — will buy BTT on the open market each quarter, with the repurchased tokens sent to a burn address. Buying runs through Q3 2026 and the first published figures are due **mid-Oct 2026**. No size, no rate, no revenue number and no burn address has been disclosed, and nothing is visible on-chain, so the framework carries the row at zero and surfaces the date instead of guessing the amount. That restraint matters here more than usual: at BTT's price, every **$1M** of open-market buying would retire roughly **0.39%** of the circulating supply, so a buyback that looks small in dollars could be large in percentage terms. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — no disclosed accumulation outside the announced programme. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** — no new escrow took BTT off the market in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

BTT's team-controlled overhang is small and, for now, still. The one item we track is the **2.96T BTT** that sits between the **990T** total supply and the **987.04T** classified as circulating — about **0.30%** of the cap, attributable to the residual partnership and ecosystem allocation that runs to **2028**, with no public wallet address and no published release calendar. We refresh it on a fortnightly walk of the supply classification, and over this window it did not shrink; the circulating figure ended the period marginally lower rather than higher, which is the opposite of what a release would look like. Separately, the BitTorrent Chain staking contract holds roughly **26.19T BTT** posted by validators and their delegators — a genuine long-term lock, but one made of user deposits rather than team supply, so it is context rather than overhang. The largest single wallets on TRON are exchange custodial and belong to depositors, so they are excluded too. The trigger sentence stands: if the reserve balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and the same applies to the buyback once an address is published.

## How BTT compares to other fixed-supply utility tokens

The natural peer group for BTT is fixed-supply utility and exchange tokens that fund buybacks out of revenue, and the comparison exposes a structural difference most write-ups miss. **BNB** runs an auto-burn that genuinely reduces its total supply, because the BNB contract can burn and the destroyed coins leave the supply figure for good. BTT cannot do that. Its buyback-and-burn will move coins out of reach, which has the same effect on the tradable float, but the headline **990T** number will never fall — so anyone comparing BTT's "supply" to BNB's over time is comparing two different accounting treatments, not two different outcomes.

Against uncapped, continuously-emitting chains, BTT looks far better on paper. **Dogecoin** mints a fixed reward every block forever and dilutes holders a few percent a year; BTT cannot dilute at all, because there is no issuance mechanism to switch on and no governance path to add one to an immutable contract. Against fair-launch meme tokens such as **SHIB**, BTT is close in structure but not identical: SHIB is also mint-renounced, yet it can actually burn, so its supply drifts slowly down while BTT's stays pinned. The one place BTT carries risk that SHIB does not is the residual partnership allocation running to **2028** — small at **0.30%**, but a real scheduled overhang rather than none at all.

The comparison that will decide the next reading is with exchange tokens that publish a quarterly buyback figure. Those tokens earn a deflationary score because the destroyed amount is measurable and recurring. BitTorrent has now committed to the same shape — revenue in, open-market buying, quarterly disclosure — but has published nothing yet. Until **mid-Oct 2026**, BTT belongs in the flat-supply class, not the deflationary one, and the framework will not move it until a number exists.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the **mid-Oct 2026** buyback report — the amount purchased, the share of supply it represents, the burn address and the transaction hash. That single disclosure is the only thing that can move BTT off a zero buy ledger, and because of the price it could be a large percentage even if the dollar figure is modest. Second, whether BTTInferGrid revenue actually materialises, since the programme is funded entirely by service income rather than by treasury reserves — no revenue means no buyback. Third, whether the **2.96T** non-circulating reserve starts releasing under the partnership schedule that runs to **2028**; a visible step up in circulating supply would open Sell #3. Fourth, confirmation each rebuild that the token contract is unchanged and still exposes no mint and no burn function — it is immutable and not a proxy, but it is the assumption the entire read rests on.
