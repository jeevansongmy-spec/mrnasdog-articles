## The verdict, in one paragraph

Between Aug 17 and Nov 15 2026, PYTH added **0** new coins to its circulating supply and removed **0**, so the net change is **0.00%** on about **7.87B PYTH** in circulation. Other supply trackers show **+0.0024%** for the same 90 days, a gap of **0.0024 points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. The next 90 days, to Feb 13 2027, also read **0.00%**. The label for PYTH today: a quiet supply between unlock steps, with buybacks that pile up in the DAO's treasury rather than leave the market for good.

## Sell pressure: where new PYTH comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**, and it can never start: the power to create new PYTH was removed on-chain, so the **10B PYTH** made at launch is all there will ever be. Pyth pays no staking inflation either. Rewards for data publishers come out of a pot of PYTH that was set aside at launch and is already counted as circulating.

Vesting unlocks are **zero** for this window. Pyth opened **1.5B PYTH** at launch on Nov 20 2023 and locked the other 8.5B, to be released in four equal steps of **2.13B** at 6, 18, 30 and 42 months. Three steps are done, in May 2024, May 2025 and May 2026. The fourth and last, about 21% of all PYTH, falls around May 20 2027, well past both 90-day windows.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are **zero**. The DAO did move coins: on Nov 4 2026 it sent **4.45M PYTH** to its community council for the next six months of work and **225,000 PYTH** in pay to the members of its retired price-feed council. Those coins sat in the DAO treasury, which is already counted as circulating, so paying them out adds nothing new. Long-term locked or bankrupt holdings are **zero**: no court case or failed company is holding PYTH waiting to release it.

## Buy pressure: where new PYTH goes

The buyback books **zero**, even though it is busy. Since December 2025 the Pyth DAO has spent part of its income on PYTH every month, and in September 2026 the DAO voted to send all of its eligible income from Pyth Pro subscriptions, listings, indices and fees into buybacks without a monthly vote. Between Aug 17 and Nov 15 2026 that bought **18.36M PYTH**: about 670,000 for August, 2.58M for September and **15.11M** for October, the biggest month so far. A council wallet holds another **5.36M PYTH** bought in November and not yet handed over. The bought coins go to the DAO treasury, where anyone can follow them on-chain, and they cannot be sold without a new vote. But the treasury is still counted as circulating, so the coins never leave the count.

The fee burn is **zero**, because Pyth has none. Holders have burned about **41.27K PYTH** by hand since launch, far too little to matter. Foundation buying is **zero**: nobody else buys PYTH for the network. New long-term locks are **zero** too. PYTH staked for votes still counts as circulating, and the publisher rewards that stay locked for a year come from a pot that was already counted.

## Foundation and overhang

The biggest overhang is the last unlock step: **2.13B PYTH** still locked until around May 20 2027, most of it meant for ecosystem growth and publisher rewards rather than for sale. Inside the circulating count, the Pyth DAO treasury holds about **51.52M PYTH**, mostly coins it bought back, and the council wallet that runs the buybacks holds **5.36M PYTH** on its way to the treasury. The community council received its 4.45M PYTH on Nov 4 2026 and pays it out over six months, including 75,000 PYTH a month for community moderators.

We read the treasury and council wallets at each rebuild. If the treasury's balance falls between rebuilds for a reason other than a council payout, or if the DAO ever votes to spend or sell its bought-back PYTH, that outflow is reported in the Foundation row at the next rebuild.

## How PYTH compares to other oracle networks

PYTH and Chainlink's LINK share the same basic shape: a fixed supply that can never grow, no staking inflation, and no fee burn. The difference is how locked coins reach the market. LINK's circulating count rises whenever its team moves coins out of its own wallets, at times it chooses. PYTH's locked coins open only on a published calendar, in a few big steps, so for most of the year its supply does not move at all, and then about a fifth of the total opens on one day.

Both networks now buy back their own coin with real income, and both keep what they buy instead of burning it. That matters for how to read a buyback. A burn shrinks the supply forever. Coins held in a treasury still exist and still count as circulating, so they only stop being a weight on the market for as long as the holders choose not to spend them. For PYTH that choice needs a public vote. Many newer oracle tokens still release coins every month, which makes PYTH's quiet stretch between steps unusual.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Early December 2026: Pyth's main developer company sends the DAO its share of November income, and the November buyback report should follow around Dec 1 2026. October's report showed 15.11M PYTH bought, so we watch whether the pace holds.

Late 2026: the election of the sixth Pythian Council, with a proposal from Nov 9 2026 to raise pay to 45,000 PYTH per member, up to 360,000 PYTH for a six-month term. Any such pay comes from coins already counted.

Any vote to spend, lend or burn the bought-back PYTH. Ideas to burn part of the reserve have been raised on the forum, but none has been voted on. A burn would be the first time PYTH buybacks truly lower the supply.

Around May 20 2027, outside this window: the last unlock step of 2.13B PYTH. It enters the 90-day view from about Feb 19 2027, and from then on the page will show it as new supply.
