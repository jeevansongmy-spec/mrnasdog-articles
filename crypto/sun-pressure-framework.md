# SUN Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

*Originally published at [https://mrnasdog.com/research/sun/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/sun/inflation)*

Sun Token cannot be created. The SUN.io token contract on TRON has no mint function, no burn function and no owner, and it reported the same **19,900,730,000** total supply at both ends of the last 90 days. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework still reads SUN at **+0.31% net** over the quarter and **+0.33%** forward, because expiring vote-locks returned **67.7M SUN** of principal to the tradable float against a revenue-funded buyback-and-burn of **9.0M SUN**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.02%**, a gap of **0.29 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships. SUN is a fixed-supply token whose loosening happens entirely inside the count.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SUN at **+0.31% net**: **67.7M SUN** of sell pressure against **9.0M SUN** of buy pressure, on a circulating base of **19,222,181,989 SUN**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.02%** for the same window — a gap of **0.29 percentage points**, comfortably inside the half-point tolerance, so this build ships no data-conflict flag. The two readings agree for a reason worth stating plainly, because it is the whole story of SUN. The counted supply of SUN is arithmetic: total supply minus whatever sits at TRON's dead address. Nothing else is excluded — not the vote-escrow vault, not the eleven billion SUN sitting in the project's untouched reserve cluster. So the only event that can move the counted number is the burn, and the burn is small. Everything else the framework measures — locks expiring, locks forming, reserves moving — happens invisibly inside a total that never changes. SUN is **fixed in supply and loose in float**: the coin count is frozen, and the share of it that can actually be sold is rising.

## Sell pressure: where new SUN comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and on SUN that zero is unusually strong. This build read the deployed TRC-20 contract at **TSSMHYeV2uE9qYH95DqyoCuNCzEL1NvU3S** — the current post-migration SUN token, not the retired SUNOLD contract — and pulled its full interface. It exposes eleven functions: name, symbol, decimals, totalSupply, balanceOf, transfer, transferFrom, approve, allowance, increaseAllowance and decreaseAllowance. There is no mint function. There is no owner slot that could add one. The entire **19,900,730,000 SUN** was created in the constructor in May 2021 and the supply call returned that same figure at both ends of the window. SUN's inflation is not low by policy; it is absent by code.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** as well, and a dated event inside this window is why it will stay that way. SUN.io ran a five-year governance mining programme that paid SUN out of a pre-funded reserve, and its published rules put the end of the final phase at **Jun 6 2026** — five days into this quarter. The wallet TRON labels as SUN.io's locked reserve, **TEPMzyy5Qo9vP5g24ACo1o7wquyQxRC1xY**, made no transfer of any kind in the last 90 days. The mining tap is closed and the reserve behind it did not move.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** by measurement and it is the row that hides the largest single fact about SUN. On **Mar 13 2025** the locked reserve sent **9,599,430,527 SUN** to a distributor wallet, which on **Apr 11 2025** fanned it into five siblings. Those six wallets today hold about **11.22B SUN** between them — **56.4%** of every SUN in existence — and every one of them recorded zero transfers across this window. A separate wallet holding **2.52B SUN** did move, sending **4.9M** on **Jun 26 2026** and **1.2M** on **Aug 19 2026**, but its own funding history is dominated by wallets labelled as exchange hot and cold storage, so those coins belong to depositors and the framework leaves them out of the team-controlled overhang. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: SUN.io is a going concern with no estate and no trustee distributing tokens on a court schedule.

That leaves Sell #5, and on SUN it is the entire sell side. SUN's vote-escrow vault at **TXbA1feyCqWAfAQgXvN1ChTg82HpBT8QPb** takes SUN under a lock of at least six months and issues veSUN against it; while the lock runs, the SUN inside is not tradable. Across this window the vault paid out **67,908,596 SUN** of expired principal and took in **195,293 SUN** of new locks, a net **67.7M SUN** returned to the market — **347** coins leaving for every one arriving. The flow is lumpy and back-loaded: **11.3M** in June, **4.8M** in July and **51.9M** in August, with three individual expiries on **Aug 15 2026**, **Aug 27 2026** and **Aug 29 2026** accounting for **40.9M** of the August total. The collapse in relocking is not a mystery either: veSUN's main economic use was a mining boost worth up to 2.5x, and the mining programme it boosted ended on Jun 6 2026. There is still **490.1M SUN** locked in the vault behind this quarter's releases.

## Buy pressure: where new SUN goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the only positive row and it is real, on-chain and verifiable to the coin. SunSwap retains **0.05%** of each swap fee, converts it to SUN and, together with revenue from the platform's launchpad, sends the result to TRON's dead address at **T9yD14Nj9j7xAB4dbGeiX9h8unkKLxmGkn**. Exactly one such execution landed inside this window: **9,025,027 SUN** on **Jul 25 2026**, from the collecting vault **TP1VirfLotFs6QCTKUgSfP4eNVdU3UcAvJ**. Three independent angles agree on it — the transfer log itself, the project's published cumulative burn of **669,522,161 SUN** as of **Apr 25 2026** against a chain-derived opening balance of **669,522,980**, and the collecting vault's own ledger, which took in **4,987,253 SUN** across the quarter and holds **1,014,944 SUN** queued for the next round. That queue is why the forward figure is **5.0M** rather than **9.0M**: the July burn drained a backlog that had been accumulating since April, and the honest projection is what the vault actually collects.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and the reason matters more than the number. A burn on this token must be verified on both surfaces, because many projects burn by transferring to an unspendable address while their supply figure never moves. On SUN the dead-address balance rose from **669,522,980** to **678,548,008** on that single transfer, while the supply call stayed identical — and it stayed identical because it can never fall, there being no burn function in the contract at all. The test for whether these are two mechanisms or one flow is whether either surface could move alone. Here only one of them can move at all, so this is a single mechanism with a single readable surface, booked once. Counting the dead-address rise and calling the flat supply a second confirmation would have doubled SUN's entire buy side. There is also no second revenue stream: the fee income that would fund a separate fee burn is the same income already funding the buyback above.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** — every SUN the platform buys is destroyed rather than kept, so no accumulation wallet exists to bid for it. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** on the page for a bookkeeping reason: the **195,293 SUN** of new vote-locks formed in the window is already netted inside the Sell #5 figure, and counting it again here would flatter the ledger twice.

## Foundation and overhang

SUN's overhang is enormous, static, and the single most important thing to watch. The first and largest is the reserve cluster: distributor **TScVwVTjqoqPEJ6atnvGCtErWnCyNbzmUL** holding **3.11B SUN**, plus the five wallets it funded on Apr 11 2025 — **1.79B**, **1.68B**, **1.66B**, **1.50B** and **1.48B** — for about **11.22B SUN** in total, with no published release schedule and zero transfers in the window. Behind it sits the funding wallet TRON labels as SUN.io's locked reserve, also motionless. Third is the buyback destination, which is not an overhang at all: bought SUN goes to the dead address and can never come back, and the only transient balance is the **1,014,944 SUN** sitting in the collecting vault waiting to be destroyed. Fourth is the vote-escrow vault itself, holding **490.1M SUN** of still-locked principal that will return to the float as individual locks mature. Each of these is re-read from the chain on every rebuild, and the rule is the same for all of them: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SUN compares to other exchange-revenue tokens

SUN belongs to the family of decentralised-exchange tokens funded by trading fees — the class that includes Curve's CRV, Raydium's RAY and the fee-buyback tokens of the larger perpetual venues. Against CRV the contrast is instructive, because the two share a mechanism and differ on the one axis that matters. Both run vote-escrow: lock the token, receive voting weight, direct emissions. Both therefore have a sell channel that no unlock calendar reports, since expiring vote-locks return principal to the market on no published schedule. But CRV still mints — a gauge emission written into its contract in 2020 — and deliberately runs no buyback at all, so its buy side is exactly zero. SUN is the mirror image: it cannot mint a single coin, and it does run a revenue buyback. That is the better structure on paper, and this quarter it still was not enough, because the lock expiries were roughly eight times the burn.

Against the exchange tokens that burn on a quarterly schedule — the large centralised-venue coins whose auto-burns retire a fixed share of supply each quarter — SUN's burn is smaller in relative terms and less predictable in timing. Those programmes publish a target and hit it. SUN's burn is whatever **0.05%** of swap volume plus launchpad revenue happens to buy, which over this window was **0.047%** of circulating supply. The comparison that flatters SUN is against uncapped continuous-emission chains: SUN has no issuance curve, no validator subsidy and no tail emission, so the ceiling of **19,900,730,000** is permanent in a way a governance vote cannot undo. What SUN gives up in exchange is transparency of float — a supply figure that never moves tells a reader nothing about how much of it is actually loose.

The structural lesson is one the framework was built to surface. A token can be capped, unmintable and burning, and still be getting looser, because supply and float are different quantities. On SUN the gap between them is **11.22B SUN** of motionless reserve plus **490.1M SUN** of live locks — none of it excluded from the circulating figure the market reads, all of it capable of reaching the market without a single new coin being created.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the next buyback-and-burn, due around **Oct 25 2026** on the quarterly rhythm the last two executions established. Its size is bounded by what the collecting vault has gathered; at the rate observed since the July burn it will be closer to **5.0M SUN** than to **9.0M**, and a materially larger figure would mean trading volume has recovered. Second, the vote-escrow vault balance, currently **490.1M SUN** — with the mining programme finished as of **Jun 6 2026**, the question is whether the August cluster of large expiries was a one-off or the beginning of a sustained drain. Third, the reserve cluster: any transfer out of those six wallets, which have not moved since **Apr 11 2025**, would be the single largest supply event in SUN's history and would move this page immediately. Fourth, any SUN DAO vote that creates a new use for locking, since the only thing that reversed the outflow historically was a reason to relock. Fifth, whether the platform announces a replacement for governance mining; nothing has been published as of **Aug 30 2026**.

## Summary

Sun Token is a fixed **19,900,730,000** TRC-20 on TRON whose contract contains neither a mint function nor a burn function, so the coin count cannot rise and cannot fall. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework still reads it at **+0.31% net** over the last 90 days and **+0.33%** forward, because **67.7M SUN** of expiring vote-locks returned to the tradable float against a **9.0M SUN** revenue buyback-and-burn — and because the counted supply excludes only the dead address, none of that movement is visible in the supply figure the market watches. The key risk is not issuance but concentration: **11.22B SUN**, more than half of everything that exists, sits in six wallets with no published release schedule, and a further **490.1M SUN** is locked on timers that keep expiring. The ceiling is real and permanent; the floor under the float is not.
