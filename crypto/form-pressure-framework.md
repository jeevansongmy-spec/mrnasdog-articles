# FORM Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

FORM, the coin of Four and its Four.meme meme-coin launchpad on BNB Chain, shrank slightly over the last 90 days: the only new FORM came from holders swapping the older BinaryX (BNX) coin one for one, which adds nothing overall, while a first-ever buyback on **Sep 21 2026** bought **408,602 FORM** and burned it. Against a small release of **20,547 BNX** from a project multisig, net supply fell **0.10%**. For the next 90 days we project **+0.01%** — flat — because no second buyback is scheduled, and the hard ceiling stays at **580M FORM**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Sep 29 2026**, FORM added **20,547** coins to the market and removed **408,602**, a net change of **−0.10%** of the **381.87M** circulating supply. The monitor, which watches the classified circulating figure, reads **+0.02%** for the same window — a gap of **0.12 percentage points**, well inside our 0.5-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown. For the next 90 days the multisig payouts continue at about the same size and the buyback counts zero, which gives **+0.01%**. In one label: FORM is a **no-emission token with a one-off burn** — its supply only changes when the team chooses to act.

## Sell pressure: where new FORM comes from

**Protocol inflation is 0.** FORM pays no staking or mining reward. The FORM contract mints new coins in only two ways: through a minter the owner has approved, and through a swap that takes older BNX, sends it to a dead address and hands back the same number of FORM. The list of approved minters was empty at both ends of the window. The swap did run — **203,782 FORM** were created in 90 days — but exactly **203,782 BNX** were destroyed in the same calls, to the decimal. The first-generation BinaryX coin works the same way one level up: it converts into 100 new BNX each, and the **17,944 BNX** created that way match the old coins destroyed. So the older coins changed form and no new coin entered the market. The contract caps FORM at **580M**; because the owner could still approve a minter up to that cap, we do not call the supply permanently fixed.

**Vesting unlocks are 0.** Neither FORM nor BNX has a published vesting calendar, and no unlock tracker lists a coming FORM release. The project's reserves sit in wallets with no dated schedule, so they are tracked as overhang instead.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks: 20,547 BNX.** One project multisig holding **75.9M BNX** paid **6,849 BNX** on Jul 25, Aug 13 and Sep 23 2026. Each payment passed through a second multisig into a BinaryX game reward pool that pays players. The same 6,849 BNX payment also went out on May 20 and Jun 22 2026, so this is a roughly monthly habit, and we carry about three payments — **20,547 BNX** — into the next 90 days. The amount is tiny, about **0.005%** of supply.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy is 0.** There is no estate, trustee schedule or time-lock releasing FORM. The largest FORM wallets belong to an exchange holding coins for its users; their moves this window — one exchange wallet down 28.1M FORM, another up 18.1M, and withdrawals to users — shuffled coins that were already on the market.

## Buy pressure: where new FORM goes

**Programmatic buyback is 0.** FORM has no standing buyback. Four.meme does run a buyback every day from Sep 9 to Dec 9 2026, but it spends one product's revenue on the top community meme coin of the day, not on FORM.

**Protocol fee burn is 0.** Launch and trading fees on Four.meme are charged in other coins, and none of them destroys FORM. The token has no public burn function, and its total supply did not fall; a burn shows up only as coins sent to a dead address.

**Foundation buy: 408,602 FORM, burned.** On **Sep 21 2026** a new wallet bought FORM thirteen times in about half an hour from the two main FORM trading pools, one paired with USDT and one with BNB, then sent all **408,602 FORM** to a dead address in a single transfer. The team described it as its first buyback, paid for from platform profit. The price jumped about 40% that day before giving much of it back. Because nothing says when or whether it will happen again, the next 90 days count **0** for this row. Apart from this burn, the dead address took in less than a hundredth of a coin from ordinary holders.

**New long-term lock is 0.** No staking lock or time-lock removed FORM from circulation. A BinaryX game vault holds about **0.57M BNX**, but players deposit and withdraw every day, so it holds coins that are still theirs.

## Foundation and overhang

The real overhang is the BNX that was never swapped. About **280.2M BNX** is still outstanding next to **296.8M FORM**, and almost all of it sits in eight places: five multisigs holding **75.9M**, **61.6M**, **25.2M**, **16.4M** and **1.4M BNX**; two single wallets holding **63.1M** and **24.7M BNX**; and a contract holding **9.6M BNX**. Only the 75.9M multisig moved this window, with the small monthly payouts described above. None of these holders has published a schedule, and any of them could swap to FORM and sell at any time, so we read their balances at every rebuild. The circulating figure we divide by has not changed since the start of 2026, so it will not show a release on its own — our wallet reads are what catch it. If any of these balances falls between rebuilds, that outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next rebuild.

## How FORM compares to other launchpad and exchange tokens

Most tokens tied to a trading venue earn their supply story through a steady buyer. Some launchpad tokens send a fixed share of each day's revenue into buybacks, so coins leave the market every day by rule. BNB, the chain FORM lives on, burns coins every quarter by a published rule. FORM has neither: its fees are not tied to the token, and its one buyback was a single decision. That makes FORM's supply quieter but also less predictable — it will not grow on its own, but nothing guarantees it will shrink either.

On the sell side FORM also differs from new launchpad and game tokens that still carry team and investor vesting. FORM has no vesting calendar left and no reward emission; its risk is concentrated in a few large, unswapped BNX reserves rather than a steady unlock stream. A coin with monthly unlocks shows its pressure every month. FORM's pressure, if it comes, would come as one large move from a wallet that has been still for a long time.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The next monthly multisig payout, expected around late October 2026, should again be about **6,849 BNX**; a bigger payment would raise the sell side. Any announcement of a second FORM buyback, or of a share of platform revenue going to FORM, would add a buyer the ledger does not count today. The meme-coin buyback programme ends on **Dec 9 2026**; watch whether its successor includes FORM. Watch the two large single BNX wallets (**63.1M** and **24.7M BNX**) for any swap or transfer. And watch the FORM contract for a new approved minter, which would be the first new-coin path since the swaps began.

## Summary

FORM's supply was roughly steady over the 90 days to Sep 29 2026, down **0.10%** after the team's first buyback burned **408,602 FORM** against a **20,547 BNX** multisig release. FORM has no reward emission and no vesting; new FORM appears only when older BNX is swapped one for one, which adds nothing overall. The key risk is the roughly **280M BNX** never swapped, most of it in a few project wallets with no schedule. The contract ceiling is **580M FORM**, and with no second buyback planned we project **+0.01%** for the next 90 days.
