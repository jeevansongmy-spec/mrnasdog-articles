# SYRUP Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply roughly steady, emission fading

Maple Finance mints new **SYRUP** on one schedule — a **5% a year** emission of about **3.08M SYRUP** a month — and a revenue-funded buyback takes most of it straight back off the market. About **9.2M SYRUP** was minted over the trailing 90 days against roughly **6.4M** bought back, and the next 90 days taper to about **6.15M** minted against **2.85M** bought as the emission ends in **Sep 2026** and the buyback rule tightens. The Pressure Framework reads **+0.24%** net trailing and **+0.28%** forward — roughly flat, on a circulating base near **1,167.19M SYRUP**. Our supply monitor reads **+7.16%**, but that is an **Aug 4 2026** market-cap-basis reclassification, not real issuance, so a data-conflict flag ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window opening **Aug 5 2026**, the framework reads **SYRUP at +0.28% net** forward, almost exactly where it sat trailing at **+0.24%** — a supply that is barely moving. Maple has one mint, a **5% a year** emission that funds staking rewards and the treasury, and it deliberately buys SYRUP back out of protocol revenue, so the two sides nearly cancel. The reason both readings are mild is that the mint and the buyback are shrinking together: the emission schedule finishes around **Sep 2026**, and the buyback rule steps down from **25%** of revenue to a tiered **10–30%** at the same time. Our supply monitor disagrees loudly, reading **+7.16%**, but the deep walk settles it: the monitor's supply series jumped from about **1,164M** to **1,245M** in a single day on **Aug 4 2026**, which is the market-cap basis switching from circulating supply to on-chain total supply, not a mint — circulating itself held near **1,167M**. So the gap of about **6.9 percentage points** ships with a data-conflict flag and the framework keeps its primary read. SYRUP is best characterised as **a mild, self-offsetting inflation that flattens further as its only mint expires**.

## Sell pressure: where new SYRUP comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the entire sell ledger for SYRUP, and it is a known quantity rather than a discretionary one. Maple mints new SYRUP on a **5% a year** schedule approved for three years, about **3.08M SYRUP** a month, to fund staking rewards and recapitalise the treasury. The trailing 90 days caught that rate in full for about **9.2M SYRUP**. The important detail is that the schedule is finite: it finishes around **Sep 2026**, so the forward window catches only two more months, roughly **6.15M SYRUP**, before issuance drops to zero. This is the single most important fact in the ledger — SYRUP's structural inflation has an end date inside the next 90 days.

Every other sell row is zero. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**. SYRUP was created as the successor to the old MPL token, and holders converted at **1 MPL to 100 SYRUP** during a window that closed **Apr 30 2025**. That migration is finished, the supply is reported fully unlocked, and no vesting cliff falls inside this window. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** as a flow: no team wallet sent SYRUP to the market off-schedule, though the overhang behind it is real and tracked below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** permanently, because there is no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holding SYRUP.

## Buy pressure: where new SYRUP goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is what makes SYRUP unusual, and it is genuinely active. Maple spends protocol revenue buying SYRUP back into its Syrup Strategic Fund. Over the trailing 90 days that ran under the older rule of **25%** of revenue and lifted about **6.4M SYRUP** off the market, offsetting most of the **9.2M** that was minted. From **Aug 2026** a new rules-based program takes over under proposal **MIP-021**: **10%** of monthly revenue while the protocol earns under **$1.5M** a month, **20%** between **$1.5M and $2M**, and **30%** above **$2M**. At current revenue near **$1.47M** a month that is the bottom tier, about **2.85M SYRUP** over the next 90 days. The one caveat for this framework is destination: the bought SYRUP is **held** in the strategic fund, not burned, so it leaves the open market but is not destroyed.

The other three buy rows are zero. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**, because Maple has no burn address in its fee path; the buyback holds rather than destroys. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero** as a separate line, because the only entity buying SYRUP is the strategic fund through the revenue program already counted in Buy #1. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: SYRUP staking rewards were retired in **Nov 2025** when Maple moved from streaming yield to buybacks, so there is no live lockup soaking up fresh supply.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on Maple is concentrated in two places. The first is the **Maple Treasury**, which holds roughly **30%** of supply and is the pool the emission schedule draws from. The second is the **Syrup Strategic Fund**, which holds about **75.78M SYRUP** plus around **$4.42M** in liquid assets and is where every bought-back token accumulates. That second point matters for reading the monitor: because the fund is classified as circulating, the buyback removes SYRUP from the open market without shrinking the reported float, so the buy pressure is real for a holder but invisible to a pure supply count. Both balances are read every rebuild through Maple's transparency reporting. If either the treasury or the strategic fund's balance falls between refreshes and the SYRUP reaches the market off-schedule, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh rather than being absorbed silently into the emission row.

## How SYRUP compares to other revenue-buyback tokens

SYRUP sits in the small class of protocol tokens that route real cash flow back into their own supply, and the cleanest comparison is to an exchange token running a quarterly buy-and-burn. Both models earn genuine revenue and spend a defined share of it on their own token. The difference is the destination: a buy-and-burn exchange token **destroys** what it buys, printing a permanently shrinking supply, while Maple **holds** what it buys in a strategic fund. For an open-market holder the immediate effect is similar — supply leaves the float either way — but the burn version is irreversible and the hold version is not, since a fund can in principle redeploy its tokens later. That is why SYRUP's buy side offsets its mint without pushing the net negative.

Against an uncapped, continuously-emitting layer-1, SYRUP looks far tamer. A typical uncapped chain mints indefinitely and its inflation has no end date; SYRUP's only mint is a fixed three-year emission that expires around **Sep 2026**, after which the sell ledger goes quiet and the revenue buyback is the only structural force left. In that sense SYRUP is converging on the profile of a fixed-supply governance token with an active buyer — a rare combination, because most fixed-supply tokens have no revenue mechanism buying them back at all.

The nuance that separates SYRUP from a true deflationary token is the hold-versus-burn distinction and the modest scale. At current revenue the tiered buyback is running at its lowest band, so it buys enough to offset most of a shrinking mint but not enough to turn the net negative. The honest reading is that SYRUP is a mild, self-cancelling inflation today, with a credible path to flat-or-slightly-deflationary once the emission ends — provided protocol revenue holds up and the buyback keeps firing.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the emission schedule wind down toward its finish around **Sep 2026** — the moment the **5% a year** mint stops, the sell ledger loses its only line and the buyback stands alone. Watch the first buybacks under the new **MIP-021** rule, which begin in **Aug 2026** and are logged on Maple's transparency dashboard; the tier that fires depends on monthly revenue, so a month above **$1.5M** or **$2M** would lift the buy side to **20%** or **30%** of revenue. Watch protocol revenue itself, currently near **$1.47M** a month against a Q2 run of **$4.4M**, because the entire buy side scales with it. And watch the strategic fund's balance, about **75.78M SYRUP**, for any redeployment that would put held tokens back on the market.

About **9.2M SYRUP** was minted against **6.4M** bought back over the trailing 90 days, tapering to roughly **6.15M** against **2.85M** forward, leaving the framework at **+0.24%** net trailing and **+0.28%** forward — roughly flat on a circulating base near **1,167.19M SYRUP**.
