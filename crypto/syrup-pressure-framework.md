# SYRUP Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Maple Finance minted **zero** new SYRUP over the last 90 days: on-chain total supply held at **1,244,677,492.51** from **May 18 2026** to **Aug 16 2026**, because the inherited 5%-per-year emission fires in lumps and the last one landed on **Apr 22 2026** — inside the Maple treasury, not on the market. SYRUP's real supply tap is vesting: **2.67M SYRUP** claimed out of a grant contract plus **0.40M** of direct contributor payments, against **3.35M** repurchased by the Syrup Strategic Fund. The Pressure Framework reads **−0.02% net** for the window and **+0.04%** for the next one, against a supply monitor reading of **+0.69%** — a **0.72 percentage point** gap that ships with a flag.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 16 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **SYRUP at −0.02% net**: sell pressure of **3.07M SYRUP** against buy pressure of **3.35M SYRUP**, on a circulating base of **1.17B**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.69%** over the same window, a gap of **0.72 percentage points**, which is over tolerance and therefore ships with a **monitor-gap flag**. The deep walk found two dated, mechanical causes rather than a measurement error. First, the **3.35M SYRUP** Maple repurchased in **Jun 2026** and **Jul 2026** is held in the Syrup Strategic Fund rather than burned, and the wider market still counts held tokens as circulating — worth about **0.29 percentage points** on its own. Second, the upstream non-circulating bucket shrank roughly **5M SYRUP** more than the chain shows leaving Maple's team wallets. Maple Finance is best characterised as **a credit business whose token is effectively flat, where the headline emission number describes tokens that never reach the float**.

## Sell pressure: where new SYRUP comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**, and that is the single most important finding on this page. The SYRUP emission everyone quotes — 5% per annum for three years, carried over from the old MPL treasury recapitalisation under MIP-010 — is real, but it is not paid out in monthly slices. Reading the SYRUP contract at both ends of the window returns exactly the same number, **1,244,677,492.51**, and the step before it was a single lump of **28,550,344.51 SYRUP** minted on **Apr 22 2026**. Dividing the annual schedule by twelve produces roughly **3.08M SYRUP** a month of imaginary issuance; the chain says the correct figure for this window is none. Just as important, when the emission does fire it mints into the Maple treasury and then into the Maple DAO safe, both of which sit outside the tradable float — so the mint does not become sell pressure on the day it happens.

Vesting unlocks are where SYRUP actually reaches the market, and this row carries **2.67M SYRUP**. Maple runs team and contributor allocations through an on-chain grant contract that the DAO tops up every few weeks; recipients claim from it on their own timing. Over the window the contract received **5.27M SYRUP** of top-ups and paid out **2.67M** in claims, leaving it holding **15.26M SYRUP** — meaning the vesting backlog is filling faster than it drains, and the undrawn remainder is a live overhang rather than a closed schedule. Because the contract is readable on-chain, the framework books the realised claim rather than any calendar entitlement, which is why several aggregators that describe SYRUP as "fully unlocked" miss this stream entirely.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks carry **0.40M SYRUP**: contributor and grant payments made straight out of Maple's operating wallet, outside the grant contract, on an unbroken monthly rhythm of about **135K SYRUP**. Long-term locked or bankruptcy is **zero** because Maple Finance has no bankruptcy estate and no trustee distribution schedule — the protocol survived the 2022 credit cycle as an operating lender rather than a claim, so there is no estate residual to release.

## Buy pressure: where new SYRUP goes

The programmatic buyback is the only live buy row, and it carries **3.35M SYRUP** across two published repurchases: **2,500,000 SYRUP** for **$375,000** in **Jun 2026** and **852,840.70 SYRUP** for **$136,768** in **Jul 2026**. These are measured from Maple's own transparency reporting, not projected from an announced percentage — a distinction that matters here, because the two produce very different numbers. Under MIP-019 the Syrup Strategic Fund receives 25% of protocol revenue, and 25% of Maple's **$4.4M** second-quarter revenue would imply a far larger repurchase than the one that actually settled. The framework books what landed.

The forward column is re-based rather than averaged, because a mechanism changed inside the window. MIP-021, which opened for voting on **Jul 13 2026** and passed with **99.97%** in favour, replaced discretionary buybacks with a tiered rule: 10% of monthly revenue below **$1.5M**, 20% between **$1.5M** and **$2M**, and 30% above **$2M**, running for six months from **Aug 2026**. The June lot was a round-number discretionary purchase that the new rule will not repeat; the July lot, at roughly 9% of average monthly revenue, already sits at the tier-one rate. So the forward buyback is the July lot times three — **2.56M SYRUP** — and that single re-basing is what flips the reading from mildly deflationary to mildly inflationary.

The remaining buy rows are zero for structural reasons. Protocol fee burn is **zero**: SYRUP has no burn address and no fee-burn mechanism, Maple's lending fees settle in stablecoins, and every repurchased token is transferred to the treasury and held. The token count therefore never falls, it only changes custody — a real distinction from a burn, and the reason the framework's reading and the wider market's reading diverge. Foundation buy is **zero** only because the Syrup Strategic Fund's purchases are already counted in the buyback row; booking them twice would double the buy side. New long-term lock is **zero** because MIP-019 retired stSYRUP staking in **Nov 2025** and nothing replaced it — the old staking vault has been unwinding ever since, falling from **232.0M SYRUP** to **176.0M** across this window, and because vault SYRUP counted as tradable throughout, that unwind is not a sell either.

## Foundation and overhang

Six team-controlled addresses are tracked on-chain at every rebuild. The Maple DAO safe held **28.09M SYRUP** on **Aug 16 2026**, up from **5.04M** at the start of the window because it absorbed the April emission tranche. A second reserve safe held **48.62M SYRUP** and has not moved a unit in 180 days. The operating wallet held **0.77M**, its downstream distributor **0.11M**, the grant contract **15.26M**, and the treasury minter has been drained to effectively nothing. On top of those balances sits up to **23.20M SYRUP** still unminted against the published terminal supply of **1,267,875,000** before the emission programme closes in **Sep 2026**.

Two of those balances double as the denominator check. Total supply of **1,244,677,492.51** minus the DAO safe, the reserve safe and the operating wallet — **77,491,602.96** together — equals **1,167,185,889.55**, which matches the published circulating figure to eight significant digits. That identity is why the circulating base used here is auditable rather than assumed. Summed across all six wallets, team-controlled SYRUP fell from **95.92M** to **92.85M** with total supply flat, so the tradable float grew by exactly **3.07M SYRUP** — the same number the sell rows produce from a different direction. If any of these balances falls between refreshes in a way the vesting and payment streams do not explain, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SYRUP compares to other revenue-buyback tokens

SYRUP's structural class is the revenue-linked buyback token — projects that route a defined share of real protocol income into repurchasing their own supply. The sharpest mechanical divide inside that class is burn versus hold. Exchange tokens that run quarterly repurchase-and-burn programmes destroy what they buy, so total supply falls permanently and every measurement system, however it classifies wallets, eventually agrees on the number. Maple Finance holds instead: repurchased SYRUP moves into the Syrup Strategic Fund alongside stablecoins and other liquid assets, strengthening a balance sheet the DAO controls. That is a defensible treasury choice, but it is not the same asset-side event, and it is the direct cause of the 0.72 percentage point gap on this page — held tokens can come back, burned tokens cannot.

Against uncapped continuous-emission layer ones, SYRUP looks unusually quiet. A typical proof-of-stake chain mints to validators every block, so its issuance is smooth, predictable and permanently on. Maple Finance mints in occasional lumps against a fixed three-year programme that expires in **Sep 2026**, and those lumps land in the treasury rather than the float. The practical consequence is that SYRUP's inflation is not a rate at all — it is a distribution decision, made by a DAO, about tokens that already exist. That makes the vesting contract and the operating wallet the things worth watching, not the emission schedule.

Against other governance tokens with large team allocations, SYRUP's honest weak point is the same grant contract that makes its ledger legible. Many peers front-load cliffs, which produce big dated unlock events that trackers publicise. Maple Finance instead runs a rolling grant stream topped up every few weeks, which never produces a headline cliff but never stops either — and because the contract is currently absorbing more than it releases, the eventual claim on the float is growing. A tracker that reports SYRUP as fully unlocked is reading the original distribution table rather than the contract.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the first buyback executed under the MIP-021 rule is due to be published for **Aug 2026**; if it lands materially above the **852,840.70 SYRUP** July lot, Maple has crossed into the 20% revenue tier and the forward reading turns deflationary. Second, the emission programme ends in **Sep 2026** with up to **23.20M SYRUP** unminted — whether that final tranche fires at all, and whether it stops at the DAO safe as the April one did, is the single largest supply question of the quarter. Third, the grant contract's balance, **15.26M SYRUP** today, is the forward claim on the float; a change in its top-up rhythm moves Sell #2 before any claim appears. Fourth, MIP-023, posted in **Jun 2026** and proposing a 90% programmatic buyback alongside a freeze on core-team proposals, would rewrite the buy side entirely if it ever reaches a vote. Fifth, MIP-021's six-month term expires around **Jan 31 2027**, so a renewal proposal should appear inside the next window.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Maple Finance (SYRUP) at **−0.02% net supply change** over the 90 days to **Aug 16 2026**, against a supply monitor reading of **+0.69%**, and projects **+0.04%** for the next 90 days. The structural mechanism is not the 5%-per-year emission the token is usually described by — that minted nothing in this window and lands in the treasury when it fires — but a vesting grant contract that released **2.67M SYRUP**, set against **3.35M SYRUP** of revenue-funded repurchases. The key risk is that those repurchases are **held rather than burned**, so the supply they remove is removed by custody rather than by destruction and can return by a DAO decision, while the grant contract quietly accumulates a **15.26M SYRUP** forward claim on the float. There is no hard cap; the only true ceiling is the published terminal supply of **1,267,875,000 SYRUP** at the emission programme's end in **Sep 2026**, leaving at most **23.20M** left to mint.
