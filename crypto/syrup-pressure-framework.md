# SYRUP Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

Looking for the buy-or-sell answer? It is on the SYRUP coin page, with demand and price drivers: [mrnasdog.com/research/syrup](https://mrnasdog.com/research/syrup)

SYRUP, the token of the Maple Finance lending platform, is mildly inflationary: Maple's own wallets released **12.98M SYRUP** into the market over the last 90 days, nothing left it, and supply grew **+1.11%**, with about **+1.14%** projected for the next 90 days. The three-year SYRUP issuance ended on **Oct 1 2026**, so the new coins now come from the DAO, the Syrup Strategic Fund and an operations wallet, which still hold about **90.92M SYRUP** between them. The monthly Maple buyback bought **2.90M SYRUP**, but those coins stay in a wallet that counts as circulating. The monitor reads **+0.28%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 21 to Oct 19 2026, SYRUP supply in the market grew **+1.11%**, and we project **+1.14%** for the next 90 days. The inflation monitor reads **+0.28%** over its own 90 days, a gap of **0.83 percentage points**, so the SYRUP page carries a warning chip. The reason is dated and specific: the circulating figure the monitor follows has stood at **1,167.27M SYRUP** since Aug 22 2026, and the **9.69M SYRUP** that Maple wallets released on Aug 31, Sep 29, Oct 8 and Oct 13 2026 never showed up in it. On-chain, the releases are plain to see. In one line: SYRUP is a finished-issuance token whose float still grows from treasury spending.

## Sell pressure: where new SYRUP comes from

Protocol inflation adds **0 SYRUP** to the market. Maple Finance paid SYRUP issuance for three years under a recapitalization plan of about 5% a year, and the issuance contract stopped paying on **Oct 1 2026**: its current rate is zero and no new period is scheduled. The last **23.20M SYRUP** earned before that date was minted on Oct 9 2026, which lifted total supply to **1,267.88M SYRUP**. Those coins went to the Maple treasury and then to the DAO safe, a wallet outside the circulating count, so the mint itself did not reach the market.

Vesting unlocks add **0 SYRUP**. SYRUP was created in 2024 by converting the old MPL token at 1 MPL to 100 SYRUP, and no investor or team vesting remains; unlock trackers list SYRUP as fully unlocked. A rewards vesting contract holds **16.58M SYRUP**, but it already counts as circulating, so its payouts move coins between holders.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are the whole sell side: **12.98M SYRUP** in 90 days. Three Maple wallets sit outside the circulating count. The operations wallet paid out **8.98M SYRUP**, in chunks of 3.00M on Aug 3, 3.00M on Aug 31 and 2.30M on Sep 29 2026, plus smaller payments, and the DAO safe sent **4.00M SYRUP** to a contributor-payments wallet on Oct 13 2026. Over the last 12 months the same wallets released **53.93M SYRUP**, and they released some in every quarter, so we project about **13.30M SYRUP** for the next 90 days.

Long-term locks and bankruptcy add **0 SYRUP**. No bankruptcy estate or trustee holds SYRUP. The MPL that holders never converted was turned into SYRUP for the Syrup Strategic Fund in 2025, and that safe is part of the treasury overhang below.

## Buy pressure: where new SYRUP goes

The programmatic buyback takes **0 SYRUP** out of the float, even though it is real. Under MIP-021, voted in July 2026, Maple spends 10% of monthly revenue on SYRUP when revenue is under $1.5M, 20% between $1.5M and $2.0M, and 30% above that, buying after each month ends. Three buys landed in this window: **852,841 SYRUP** on Aug 5, **676,294 SYRUP** on Sep 8 and **1,371,166 SYRUP** on Oct 8 2026, **2.90M SYRUP** in all. The coins go to a Maple wallet that counts as circulating, and part of what sat there was later passed to a trading desk, so the buys change who holds SYRUP but not how much is in the market.

The protocol fee burn is **0 SYRUP**. Borrowers pay Maple in stablecoins, and no SYRUP is destroyed: total supply only rose in this window and the burn addresses did not grow. The foundation buy is also **0 SYRUP**, since no Maple wallet bought SYRUP apart from the revenue buyback.

New long-term locks take **0 SYRUP**. Staking rewards ended in November 2025 under MIP-019, and stakers keep leaving: staked SYRUP fell from **188.36M** to **151.73M** over the 90 days. Staked SYRUP was already counted as circulating, so the exits add nothing, but it means no lock is pulling coins out of the market either.

## Foundation and overhang

The SYRUP overhang is the treasury that sits outside the circulating count: the DAO safe with **42.29M SYRUP**, which received the final 23.20M issuance on Oct 13 2026, and the Syrup Strategic Fund safe with **48.62M SYRUP**, which has not moved since January 2026. The operations wallet that turns DAO grants into payments is empty after this window. Together that is **90.92M SYRUP**, about 7.8% of the circulating supply. Inside the circulating count we also watch the buyback wallet (**1.60M SYRUP**), the contributor-payments wallet (4.36M) and the rewards vesting contract (16.58M); their outflows are moves between holders. We read every one of these balances on-chain at each rebuild. If the DAO safe or the Strategic Fund safe falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the foundation row at the next refresh.

## How SYRUP compares to other lending-protocol tokens

Among lending tokens, SYRUP now sits in a rare spot: its scheduled issuance is finished. Many DeFi lending tokens still pay new or reserved tokens to users or stakers every day, and others still have investor and team vesting running for years. Maple Finance has neither. What remains is a treasury model: the DAO and the Strategic Fund hold a fixed stock of SYRUP and spend it on contributors, liquidity and growth, and that spending is the only way new SYRUP reaches the market.

The buyback is where SYRUP differs from lending tokens that burn or lock what they buy. Maple buys SYRUP with a share of revenue, but keeps it in a wallet counted as circulating and reuses it, so the buyback supports the price without shrinking supply. A buyback that burns coins, or that parks them in a locked safety fund, removes them from the float; the SYRUP buyback does not, at least for now.

So the SYRUP supply story is simpler than most: no cap, no issuance, no unlock calendar, and one variable, the pace at which Maple spends a treasury of about 90.92M SYRUP. At the 12-month pace that is roughly 13M SYRUP a quarter, a little over 1% of the float.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

By **Nov 15 2026**, Maple executes the buyback for October revenue; the tier it lands in (10%, 20% or 30%) shows whether revenue is still above $1.5M a month.

By **Dec 15 2026** and **Jan 15 2027**, the last two buybacks under MIP-021, which runs for six months; a new vote is needed to keep the rules going, and a vote to burn or lock bought coins would turn the buyback into a real buy-side row.

Any transfer out of the DAO safe (**42.29M SYRUP**) or the Strategic Fund safe (48.62M) adds to the sell side; a release far above the usual 3M to 5M chunks would push the next reading up.

The SYRUP issuance contract can only restart through a governance vote; the vote portal shows no such proposal after MIP-021. Maple's next community AMA is on **Nov 11 2026**.
