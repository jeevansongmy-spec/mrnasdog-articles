# SYRUP Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Maple Finance minted no new SYRUP at all over the last 90 days — total supply read **1,244,677,492.51** at both ends of the window, so the entire supply story is custody, not issuance. What reached the market was **~3.50M SYRUP** crossing out of Maple team wallets, against a revenue-funded buyback of **~3.35M SYRUP** that is transferred to the Syrup Strategic Fund and **held, not burned**. Net supply growth is **+0.01%**, essentially a standstill, against **+0.77%** on the inflation monitor — a gap of **0.75** percentage points that the buyback-and-hold structure explains directly. The forward quarter reads **+0.08%**, because the new revenue-linked buyback buys slightly less than the discretionary lot it replaced.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework reads SYRUP at **+0.01%** net supply growth over the trailing 90 days and **+0.08%** for the next 90. The inflation monitor reads **+0.77%** over the same window, leaving a gap of **0.75** percentage points, which is over tolerance, so a data-conflict chip ships on the SYRUP overview. The gap is mechanical rather than a disagreement about facts, and it decomposes cleanly: **3.35M SYRUP** that Maple repurchased in Jun and Jul 2026 sits in the Strategic Fund and is still counted as tradable by the wider market reading, which is worth **0.29** percentage points, and the upstream supply series rose about **5.4M** more than the chain shows leaving Maple wallets, worth another **0.46**. On mechanism, SYRUP is a **zero-issuance credit-protocol token whose float is governed by treasury releases and offset by a buyback that accumulates rather than destroys**.

## Sell pressure: where new SYRUP comes from

Nothing was minted. The SYRUP contract on Ethereum returned exactly **1,244,677,492.51** at the open of the window and exactly the same figure at the close, confirmed on two independent archive nodes, so Maple's protocol inflation row is **0** by measurement rather than assumption. That matters because SYRUP's published schedule invites a wrong answer: dividing the headline annual emission by twelve would have booked roughly **14.6M** of issuance that never happened. Maple's emission fires in lumps, and the last one was a single mint of **28,550,344.51 SYRUP** on **Apr 22 2026**, over a month before this window opens — and it went into Maple's own treasury rather than to the market.

The real sell pressure is vesting. Maple's grant contract, which pays team and contributor allocations, took in **5,272,222.69 SYRUP** in top-ups across the window and paid out **3,007,292.08** to beneficiaries, leaving **14,823,541.62** still undrawn. That single row is the largest source of new SYRUP float. It is also the row most likely to be missed: the vesting aggregators describe SYRUP as fully unlocked with no unlock events scheduled through **Feb 2027**, and the Maple grant contract flatly contradicts them. Alongside it, direct team payments that bypass the grant contract came to **488,996.33 SYRUP**, paid in batches on **Jun 9**, **Jul 10**, **Jul 27**, **Jul 28**, **Jul 29** and **Aug 21 2026** — a steady pattern, not a one-off. The fourth sell row is **0**: Maple has no bankruptcy estate, and the MPL-to-SYRUP migration minted its full conversion tranche up front in **Aug 2024**, so there is no un-migrated overhang waiting to be created.

## Buy pressure: where new SYRUP goes

Maple runs a revenue-funded buyback, and it published both in-window purchases with amounts and prices: **2,500,000 SYRUP** for **$375,000** in Jun 2026, then **852,840.70 SYRUP** for **$136,768** in Jul 2026 — **3.35M SYRUP** in total. The destination is the decisive fact and it is easy to get wrong: repurchased SYRUP is transferred to the Syrup Strategic Fund and held there. It is not burned. Maple's unspendable dead address held **0** SYRUP at both ends of the window and total supply never moved, so the protocol fee burn row is **0** on both surfaces — the transfer route and the supply route. A buyback that accumulates removes SYRUP from the tradable float only for as long as the treasury chooses to keep holding it, which is a weaker claim than destruction and is why this page separates the two.

The third buy row is **0** because it would double-count: the Strategic Fund takes **25%** of Maple's protocol revenue and that same pot funds the repurchases already booked. The fourth is **0** for a structural reason — Maple retired SYRUP staking by governance vote in **Oct 2025**, and the staking vault has been unwinding ever since, falling from **220,338,938.78 SYRUP** to **169,841,386.17** across this window alone. Vault SYRUP counts as tradable either way, so the unwind adds nothing to the sell ledger, but holders now have no protocol-level way to lock SYRUP off the market at all.

## Foundation and overhang

Maple's team-controlled overhang is large and fully enumerable on chain. The DAO treasury safe holds **23.09M SYRUP**, a dormant reserve safe holds **48.62M** and has not moved a single token in the window, the operating wallet holds **5.69M**, the grant contract holds **14.82M** undrawn, and a pass-through wallet holds **0.08M**. On top of those balances, Maple's published issuance programme leaves up to **23.2M SYRUP** still unminted against a stated end-point of **1,267,875,000** by **Sep 2026**. The buyback destination belongs on the same list — the Syrup Strategic Fund discloses roughly **79.6M SYRUP**, and none of it has been destroyed. There is one live signal worth naming: Maple's treasury safe moved **5,000,000 SYRUP** into the operating wallet on **Aug 25 2026**, which is staged but not yet paid out. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the SYRUP sell ledger's third row at the next refresh.

## How SYRUP compares to other revenue-buyback tokens

The cleanest comparison class for SYRUP is the exchange and perpetual-DEX tokens that route trading revenue into open-market repurchases. The mechanism looks identical from the outside and behaves very differently underneath, and the difference is exactly the one this page turns on: destination. A token that sends repurchased units to an unspendable address posts a permanent reduction in float that no later governance vote can reverse, and its supply reading can go genuinely negative in a strong revenue quarter. Maple sends SYRUP to a treasury it controls, so the same dollar of revenue produces a reversible withdrawal from the float rather than a destruction of it — and any wider supply reading that classifies the treasury as tradable will keep counting those tokens, which is the whole of the **0.29** percentage-point structural component of the SYRUP monitor gap.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains the contrast runs the other way, and it flatters Maple. Those chains mint every block, so their sell ledger has a floor they cannot get under; SYRUP's protocol inflation row was **0** this quarter and can stay there for months at a time, because the emission is lumpy and lands in treasury custody rather than on the market. Against vesting-driven tokens, though, SYRUP looks ordinary: with issuance switched off, the SYRUP float is governed almost entirely by how fast the Maple grant contract and the treasury release what already exists, which is a discretionary tap rather than a schedule anyone can read off a calendar. That is the structural risk here — not inflation, but a treasury with **77.4M SYRUP** outside the tradable float and no published release timetable.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The forward window closes around **Nov 26 2026**. Watch Maple's issuance programme end-point of **Sep 2026**: up to **23.2M SYRUP** remains unminted, and while every tranche so far has landed in the treasury rather than the market, a tranche that minted directly into a tradable address would change the SYRUP sell ledger's first row immediately. Watch the **5,000,000 SYRUP** staged in Maple's operating wallet since **Aug 25 2026** — it is the clearest loaded charge in the ledger. Watch the monthly buyback prints: MIP-021 passed with **99.97%** support on **Jul 13 2026** and pays **10%** of monthly revenue below **$1.5M**, **20%** between **$1.5M** and **$2M**, and **30%** above **$2M**, on a six-month term — so a revenue quarter that pushes Maple into the higher tier would roughly double the SYRUP buy side. And watch the dormant reserve safe holding **48.62M SYRUP**: it has not moved, and the first time it does is the single largest event available to this ledger.

## Summary

SYRUP is a zero-issuance quarter that reads almost exactly flat: Maple minted **0** new SYRUP, released **~3.50M** from team wallets — **~3.01M** in vested grant claims and **~0.49M** in direct payments — and bought back **~3.35M** with protocol revenue, for a net of **+0.01%** that becomes **+0.08%** in the forward quarter under the new revenue-linked buyback. The structural mechanism is a treasury-governed float rather than an emission curve, and the load-bearing detail is that Maple's buyback **holds rather than burns** — repurchased SYRUP joins roughly **79.6M** in the Syrup Strategic Fund and can be released again. The key risk is that same discretion: **77.4M SYRUP** sits outside the tradable float across a treasury safe, a dormant reserve safe and an operating wallet, with no published release timetable and up to **23.2M** more still to mint by **Sep 2026**. SYRUP has no hard cap, so the ceiling here is a governance decision, not a protocol rule.
