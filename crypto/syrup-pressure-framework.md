# SYRUP Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

## The verdict, in one paragraph

From Aug 9 to Nov 7 2026 SYRUP's circulating supply rose by a net **+0.84%** on **1,167.27M** circulating coins, and the next 90 days come to about **+0.82%**. Other trackers show **-6.22%**, a difference of **7.06 percentage points**, so the coin page carries a warning note. Their figure fell by 77.49M SYRUP on Aug 12 2026, the day Maple's treasury wallets were taken out of the circulating count, and it has not moved since Aug 22 2026, even though those wallets paid 9.69M SYRUP into the market after that day. No SYRUP was burned in the window. In one line: SYRUP is a finished-minting lending token whose supply now grows only as fast as Maple's own treasury pays coins out.

## Sell pressure: where new SYRUP comes from

Protocol inflation adds nothing to the count now. Maple's plan of about 5% new SYRUP a year ran out on Oct 1 2026, and the last batch, **23.20M SYRUP**, was minted on Oct 9 2026 straight into Maple's treasury, which is not counted as circulating. Those coins only reach the market when the treasury pays them out, and on-chain no new minting is scheduled; restarting it would take a Maple governance vote.

Vesting unlocks are zero as a source of new coins. No investor or team cliff is due, and Maple's vesting contract, which held **10.79M SYRUP** on Nov 7 2026 and paid **10.89M SYRUP** to 23 wallets during the window, already counts as circulating. Its payouts move coins that were counted before, so the real new supply is what the treasury sends into it.

That treasury spending is the whole sell side. Maple's DAO treasury and its operations wallet paid **9.77M SYRUP** into the market between Aug 9 and Nov 7 2026: about 3.09M in August, 2.32M on Sep 29, 0.36M on Oct 8 and 4.00M on Oct 13. Most of it went into the vesting contract, which pays people every month. Over the last year the treasury released SYRUP in every single month, about 3.19M a month on average, so we expect about **9.58M SYRUP** in the next 90 days.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is zero. No court estate holds SYRUP, and staked SYRUP can be withdrawn at any time. Since staking rewards ended, the staking contract shrank from 188.24M to 146.71M SYRUP in 90 days, but those coins were already counted as circulating, so the drop adds nothing.

## Buy pressure: where new SYRUP goes

The buyback is real but takes nothing out of the count. Since Jul 13 2026 Maple spends a set share of each month's revenue on SYRUP: 10% when revenue is under $1.5M, 20% up to $2.0M and 30% above. It bought **676,290 SYRUP** on Sep 8 2026 for August and **1,371,166 SYRUP** on Oct 8 2026 for September, when revenue first reached the 20% level. The coins go to Maple's strategic fund wallet, which counts as circulating, held **1.60M SYRUP** on Nov 7 2026 and passed 2.10M SYRUP on to another holder on Sep 29 2026, so the purchases change who holds the coins, not how many circulate. October's purchase had not happened by Nov 7 2026.

The protocol fee burn is zero because Maple does not burn SYRUP; no coins went to a burn address in the window. A foundation buy is zero too, because the treasury paid coins out in every month of the last year and the monthly buyback is its only buying. A new long-term lock is zero: staking rewards ended after a governance vote, staked SYRUP can leave at any time, and no new lock was set up in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

Two Maple wallets outside the circulating count hold everything that can still add to it. The DAO treasury Safe holds **42.29M SYRUP**; it took in the final 23.20M mint on Oct 13 2026 and is the wallet that funds the monthly payouts. A reserve Safe holds **48.62M SYRUP** and has not moved since Jan 27 2026. Together that is **90.92M SYRUP**, about 7.8% of today's circulating supply, and Maple has published no schedule for it beyond the steady monthly pace.

Inside the count, Maple also runs the vesting contract with 10.79M SYRUP and the strategic fund wallet with 1.60M SYRUP; neither adds new supply when it pays out. If the treasury or reserve balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the sell side at the next refresh.

## How SYRUP compares to other DeFi lending tokens

SYRUP now sits closer to a fixed-supply token than most lending tokens. Its minting has ended, so its supply grows only when Maple's treasury chooses to pay coins out, and that pace has been steady at about 3M a month. AAVE also has no new minting; its supply moves when the Aave DAO's reserve pays stakers and grant streams, and its buyback has been switched off since April 2026. MORPHO is at a different stage: founder and partner unlocks still run on a schedule, and nothing buys MORPHO back.

The difference that matters for SYRUP is where its buyback sends coins. A buyback that burns coins, or parks them in a wallet outside the count, shrinks supply; Maple's keeps them in a wallet that still counts and has passed coins on before, so for now it works as market demand rather than a supply cut. And because the buyback is tied to revenue, a stronger month for Maple's lending business buys more SYRUP, while the treasury's payouts do not depend on revenue at all.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the October buyback: under the current rule it should land in early November 2026, as the last two came on the 8th of the month, sized by October revenue, and it will show whether revenue stayed in the 20% level.

Second, the monthly treasury payouts at the end of November 2026, December 2026 and January 2027; a month far above the usual 3M would lift the next reading.

Third, the buyback rule itself, which was set for six months from Jul 13 2026 and so needs renewing around mid-January 2027, and any proposal to burn bought coins or hold them outside the count.

Fourth, the 48.62M SYRUP reserve Safe, quiet since Jan 27 2026; any outflow from it would be new supply. And fifth, any governance vote to schedule fresh minting, which would restart the protocol inflation row.
