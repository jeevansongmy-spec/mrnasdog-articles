## Sell pressure: where new SYRUP comes from

Maple Finance runs a three-year issuance plan, voted in 2023 and carried over when MPL became SYRUP at 100 to 1, that builds up new coins for the Maple treasury — about **52.3M SYRUP a year** in 2026. Those coins only exist once Maple claims them from the issuance contract. No claim happened in this window; the last one minted **28.55M SYRUP** on Apr 22 2026. About **22.9M SYRUP** are waiting today, and the plan stops adding more on Oct 1 2026, capping the pending claim at about **23.2M SYRUP**. Past claims came every two to six months with no public date, so the forward figure stays at zero until a claim is announced or seen on chain.

## Buy pressure: where new SYRUP goes

The programmatic buyback books **0 SYRUP**, even though it is real. Since the MIP-021 vote closed on Jul 17 2026, Maple spends a fixed share of each month's revenue on SYRUP — 10% while monthly revenue is under $1.5M, 20% up to $2M and 30% above that — and buys by the 15th of the next month. It bought **852,841 SYRUP** for $136,768 on Aug 5 2026 and **676,294 SYRUP** for $147,098 on Sep 8 2026, **1.53M SYRUP** in the window. The bought coins are kept in a Maple wallet, not burned, and that wallet is counted as circulating, so the buyback takes coins off the order books without taking them out of the count. At today's price the next three monthly buys come to about **1.28M SYRUP**.

The protocol fee burn is **0 SYRUP**: nothing is burned, total supply did not fall, and the burn addresses did not grow. Foundation buying is **0 SYRUP** beyond the buyback itself, and new long-term locks are **0 SYRUP** — staking rewards ended in November 2025 and holders have been leaving the staking pool since.

## Foundation and overhang

Only one wallet sits outside the SYRUP circulating count: a reserve holding **77.36M SYRUP** that has not moved since December 2025. If it starts to send coins out, those coins would be new sell pressure. Everything else Maple controls is already counted: the DAO safe with **23.09M SYRUP**, the strategic fund safe with **48.62M**, an operations wallet with **2.68M**, the buyback wallet with **2.33M** and the reward vesting contract with **15.77M**. On top of that sits the unminted issuance, about **22.9M SYRUP** today and **23.2M** once the plan stops on Oct 1 2026; a claim mints it straight into the treasury, which is counted as circulating. We re-read every one of these balances on chain at each rebuild; if the reserve's balance falls, or a claim mints new SYRUP, between refreshes, that outflow enters the sell side at the next refresh.

## How SYRUP compares to other DeFi lending tokens

Among DeFi lending tokens, SYRUP sits between two models. Some lending tokens fund a standing buyback from protocol revenue and hold the bought coins in a treasury, which is what Maple now does under MIP-021 — though Maple's buyback is small, at about **$140K–$150K a month** at the 10% tier, against a market value of about **$259.7M**. Tokens like MORPHO rely on unlocks and team payouts with no buyback at all. SYRUP has neither a vesting cliff nor a burn; its supply risk is a single discretionary mint from a plan that is ending.

The key mechanism difference is custody versus destruction. A burn removes coins from total supply for good; SYRUP's buyback keeps coins inside a Maple wallet that can sell them again, so on a supply ledger it counts as a move, not a removal. And unlike uncapped lending chains that mint every block, SYRUP's issuance stops on Oct 1 2026 unless governance votes a new schedule — after the pending claim, the token would have no scheduled source of new supply.
