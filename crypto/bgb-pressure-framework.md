# BGB Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Bitget Token burned **3.01M BGB** inside this window — a real, on-chain, permanent destruction on **Jul 14 2026** — and the Pressure Framework still reads BGB at **0.00%** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** over the next 90. The reason is where the burned BGB came from: traced back by sender, every one of those coins sat in the Morph Foundation treasury, which is the entire non-circulating bucket, so the burn lowered the ceiling without taking a single tradable coin off the market. Sell pressure is **0**, buy pressure is **0**, and BGB cannot be minted at all — the token contract has no mint function, no owner and no upgrade path.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **700.0M BGB**, the framework books **0** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **0.00%** — and projects **0.00%** for the next 90 on the same structure. The inflation monitor reads **-0.09%** for the same window, a gap of **0.09 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so this page ships with no monitor-gap warning. That residual is not a supply event: the monitor infers supply from market cap divided by price, while the classified circulating figure for BGB did not move by a single unit across the window. The label for BGB is **a fixed-supply exchange token whose burn is reserve-funded**: the headline total falls every quarter, and the tradable float does not move with it.

## Sell pressure: where new BGB comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is unusually easy to prove for BGB. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**. Bitget Token is a plain ERC-20 on Ethereum, and its deployed code answers exactly **eleven** outside calls — name, symbol, decimals, totalSupply, balanceOf, allowance, approve, transfer, transferFrom and the two allowance adjusters. There is no mint function, no owner, no role system, no pause and no upgrade path, and the executable body contains no delegate-call, no create and no self-destruct. Calling a mint on it reverts because the function does not exist. The count of BGB in existence read **2,000M** at both ends of the window, and that figure lives in writable contract storage rather than being compiled into the code — so it could have moved and did not. Flatness here is a measurement, not an artefact.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0**, because there is no vesting cohort left to unlock. Every BGB that has ever existed sits in one of three places, and at both ends of the window they add to the original 2,000M exactly: **700.0M** circulating, **1,089.1M** at the dead address, and **210.9M** inside a single Morph Foundation treasury contract. The identity closes to under a millionth of a coin against the classified circulating figure, which means there is no fourth bucket and no unenumerated locked allocation anywhere. An outside unlock tracker still prints a pre-2025 cliff table for BGB running to 2029; those coins were burned or handed to the Morph Foundation in September 2025, and the on-chain arithmetic leaves no room for them.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** — and this is the row where the schedule and the chain disagree. The Morph Foundation treasury is documented as releasing up to **2% a month** of its original **220.0M BGB**, which over 90 days would be an entitlement of roughly **13.2M**. The realised outflow to market was zero. The treasury's balance fell from **213.9M** to **210.9M** across the window, and a full sweep of its transfers found one outgoing transaction and no incoming ones, closing against the balance change with a residual of zero. That single outgoing transaction was the burn. Read further back, the treasury held exactly **220.0M BGB** on Oct 1 2025 and again on Jan 1 2026: it has never in its life paid a coin out to the market. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well — BGB has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new BGB goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. Older write-ups describe a repurchase funded by a share of Bitget's exchange and wallet profits, and that framing still circulates, but nothing was bought in this window. The test is not the announcement, it is the destination: the treasury contract that pays for the burn received **zero** incoming transfers across all 90 days, so no BGB was purchased from holders and parked anywhere. The burn is paid out of coins the Morph Foundation was handed in 2025, not out of coins bought back from the market.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is the row worth explaining, because the burn genuinely fired and is still booked at **0**. The Morph Foundation removed **3,010,400 BGB** on **Jul 14 2026** and published it on **Jul 15 2026** as the Q2 2026 quarterly burn, sized off Morph ecosystem fees, the average BGB price and a governance multiplier. Both burn surfaces were read at both window ends, on both chains BGB lives on. On Ethereum the count of BGB in existence never moved — this token burns by transfer, not by a supply-reducing call, so a supply-only reading would have shipped a wrong zero — while the dead address rose from **1,086.1M** to **1,089.1M**, agreed by four independent providers, in exactly one step. On Morph, where BGB is the gas and governance token, the dead addresses did not move at all. So the burn settles on Ethereum, and it is real. What makes the row zero is the sender: the burning transfer originated in the Morph Foundation treasury, which is definitionally outside the tradable count, so the destruction removed **0** circulating BGB. The circulating figure is identical at both window ends.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** for the same reason Buy #1 is: the Morph Foundation acquired nothing, because its treasury took in nothing. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** — the **210.9M BGB** still held back was locked in September 2025 and is already excluded from the tradable count, so it cannot be credited again, and no staking or escrow programme took BGB off the market inside these 90 days.

## Foundation and overhang

BGB has two overhangs, and only one of them is the one everybody looks at. The first is the Morph Foundation treasury contract, holding **210.9M BGB** at the end of the window against **213.9M** at the start. It is the entire non-circulating supply of the token — total supply minus circulating supply equals its balance to the wei at both ends — and it is the pot that funds every quarterly burn. There is no buyback accumulation wallet, because there is no buyback; no investor or team vest, because Bitget transferred all **440.0M** of the BGB it controlled to the Morph Foundation in September 2025 and half of that was burned immediately; and no bankruptcy residual. What matters about this overhang is that its written release ceiling and its behaviour do not match, and the framework follows the behaviour: a 2% monthly ceiling on **220.0M** would allow about **4.4M BGB** a month into ecosystem programmes, and the realised release since the treasury was funded is zero, with every outflow it has ever made going to the burn address.

The second overhang is larger and sits inside the tradable count already: a single identified company wallet holding **227.6M BGB**, unchanged to eight decimal places at both ends of the window — about **32.5%** of the entire float. Because it is classified as circulating, no unlock, vote or schedule stands between those coins and an order book, which makes it a bigger structural risk to a BGB holder than the reserve everyone watches. It did not move in these 90 days. Both balances are read on chain at every rebuild, and the trigger sentence applies to each: if either the Morph Foundation treasury or the company wallet falls between refreshes by more than the quarterly burn accounts for, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How BGB compares to other exchange tokens with quarterly burns

BGB belongs to the exchange-token class whose defining mechanism is a quarterly buyback-and-burn — the shape BNB made standard and that most large venues have copied. Structurally, though, BGB now sits at the far end of that class. A classic exchange-token burn is demand-linked: the venue takes profit, buys the token on the open market, and destroys what it bought. That does two things at once — it bids the float and it shrinks the total. BGB's burn does only the second. The Morph Foundation destroys coins it already holds, sized off Morph network fees rather than off exchange profit, so the mechanism never touches the order book. On the Pressure Framework's axis, which measures coins entering and leaving the tradable float, a buy-then-burn programme reads negative and a reserve-funded burn reads exactly zero, even when the reserve-funded one destroys more tokens.

Against a capped, non-minting chain the comparison runs the other way. BGB is stricter than almost any of them on issuance: there is no emission schedule to decay, no halving clock, no staking reward — the contract simply has no mint path, which is a harder commitment than a cap enforced by governance. And BGB is stricter than a recently-launched token working through a four-year vest, because it has no vest left at all. What it shares with neither is the missing offset: a token with a live buyback can read negative, and BGB cannot, until the burn starts consuming coins that are actually in circulation.

The size gap is worth stating plainly. The Q2 2026 burn was **3.01M BGB** against a circulating **700.0M** — about **0.43%** of the float, if it had come from the float. At the current run rate the treasury has roughly seventeen years of quarterly burns left in it before it is exhausted. That is the distance between the mechanism existing and the mechanism mattering to a holder of circulating BGB.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q3 2026 quarterly burn, covering Jul 1 2026 to Sep 30 2026 and expected to publish around **Oct 15 2026** on the two previous quarters' timing; the number to check is not its size but its funding source, because a burn sourced from bought-back or fee-collected BGB rather than from the treasury would move Buy #2 off zero for the first time. Second, the Morph Foundation treasury balance at **210.9M BGB**: it is read on chain at every rebuild, and any fall beyond the quarterly burn is an ecosystem deployment that books straight into Sell #3. Third, Morph governance, which sets the multiplier that sizes each burn and could in principle approve a treasury release. Fourth, Morph network fees, since the burn size is derived from them — the Q2 2026 calculation started from an ecosystem fee value of about **$4,071**, which is what makes the multiplier, not usage, the dominant term today. Fifth, the token contract itself: it has no mint path now, and because it also has no upgrade path, that cannot change without migrating to a new contract — an event that would be visible and loud.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads BGB at **0.00%** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** projected forward: mixed flows, supply roughly steady. The structural mechanism is neither inflation nor deflation but reclassification — Bitget Token cannot be minted, has no vesting left, and burned **3.01M BGB** on **Jul 14 2026** out of a treasury that was never part of the tradable float, so the headline total fell to **910.9M** while circulating supply stayed at **700.0M** to the last decimal. The key risk is not the burn at all: it is the **227.6M BGB** held in one identified company wallet that is already counted as circulating and needs no unlock to reach a market, and behind it the **210.9M** treasury whose written 2% monthly ceiling would allow roughly **13.2M BGB** a quarter into the float if it were ever used. Neither moved in these 90 days.
