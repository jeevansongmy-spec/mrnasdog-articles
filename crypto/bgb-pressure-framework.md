## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **Apr 28 2026** to **Jul 27 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **BGB at 0.00% net**: sell pressure of **zero BGB** against buy pressure of **zero BGB** on a tradable base of **699.99M BGB**. Our supply monitor reads **−0.23%** for the same period — a gap of only **0.23 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships with this page. The two readings agree because they are measuring the same thing and finding almost nothing: the market's circulating count for BGB has sat at **699,992,03x** since **Sep 4 2025**, and the small negative in the monitor is price-derived noise around that constant. Bitget Token is best labelled a **hard-fixed exchange token with a burn that eats its own reserve** — permanently deflationary in total supply, perfectly flat in tradable supply.

## Sell pressure: where new BGB comes from

Nowhere. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and it is zero in the strongest sense available to this framework. The BGB contract on Ethereum is a plain token contract whose published source is a constructor and nothing else: it created **2,000,000,000 BGB** once, at deployment, and exposes no mint function, no owner and no minter role. Bitget itself could not issue another BGB if it wanted to. That is a permanent zero rather than a quiet quarter, and it is the single most important fact about BGB's supply.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the row where BGB is most commonly misread, and it is also **zero**. When Bitget handed **440M BGB** to the Morph Foundation on **Sep 4 2025**, half was burned on the spot and the other **220M** went into a wallet under a stated release of **2% a month** — about **4.4M BGB** monthly, or **13.2M** across a 90-day window on paper. That wallet is readable on a public block explorer, and its complete transfer history since the day it was funded consists of one inbound of **220,000,000 BGB** and four outbound burns. Not one BGB has gone toward the market. On paper roughly **47M BGB** should have been released by now; the realised figure is **zero**. The framework books what moved, not what was scheduled, so this row carries nothing.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** on observed behaviour, with two pools enumerated below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** because Bitget is a going concern and no estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution touches BGB. The entire sell ledger for Bitget Token is empty, and for a token with no mint that is the expected shape.

## Buy pressure: where new BGB goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is the headline mechanism and it reads **zero on the float**, which needs explaining because the burn itself is entirely real. The Morph Foundation now sets the quarterly BGB burn from network usage: ecosystem fee value divided by the average BGB price, multiplied by a governance factor and a booster. For Q2 2026 that produced **3,010,400 BGB**, destroyed on-chain on **Jul 14 2026**, taking total supply from **913.93M** to **910.92M**. The transaction is verifiable and the arithmetic closes: the burn address now holds **1,089,079,125 BGB** out of the original **2,000,000,000** mint, so more than half of every BGB ever created has been destroyed.

The catch is the funding source. Every 2026 burn was paid out of the same locked Morph Foundation wallet, and the market's tradable count is defined as total supply minus exactly that wallet — the two figures reconcile to the token. So when the burn fires, total supply falls and the excluded bucket falls by the identical amount, and the tradable float does not change at all. It did not step down on **Jan 23 2026**, on **Apr 9 2026**, or on **Jul 14 2026**. Across the full 90 days the tradable supply of BGB moved by **6 BGB**, from a dust burn sent by an unrelated address. Treating a **3.01M** burn as **3.01M** of absorbed float would have invented a **−0.43%** reading that no market participant experienced.

The remaining buy rows are all **zero**. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero** because Bitget no longer buys BGB on the open market to fund the burn; the tokens come from reserve, so no bid reaches an order book and no accumulation wallet builds a position. This matters: the older Bitget model, in which roughly **20%** of quarterly profit bought BGB and burned it at around **30M** a quarter, is retired, and quoting it today overstates the buy side by an order of magnitude. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**, with no disclosed open-market purchase by Bitget or the Morph Foundation. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: the **220M** lock was created on **Sep 4 2025**, before this window, and nothing new has been escrowed since.

## Foundation and overhang

BGB carries two identified team-controlled pools. The first is the Morph Foundation ecosystem wallet, holding **210,928,845 BGB** at the last read — the residue of the **220M** tranche after the burns. It sits outside the tradable count, it has never sent a token toward the market, and it is being drawn down at roughly **3M** a quarter by the burn itself, which means the overhang is shrinking rather than waiting. The second is Bitget's own corporate address, holding **227,596,749 BGB**, and this one sits **inside** the tradable count already. Its single movement in this window was **20,000,100 BGB** on **May 28 2026**, sent to another address in the same operator's wallet cluster which now holds nothing — an internal routing hop rather than a distribution to buyers. Both pools are re-read on every rebuild. If either wallet's balance falls toward the open market between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and on the corporate address in particular, a genuine distribution would be the largest single supply event this ledger could record.

## How BGB compares to other exchange tokens

The natural peer group is exchange tokens with quarterly burn programmes, and on the headline metric BGB is at the extreme end of it: more than half of all BGB ever created has already been destroyed, against roughly a third for the largest exchange token in the sector. But the mechanism underneath is different in a way that changes what the number means. The classic exchange-token burn is revenue-funded — the venue takes a slice of profit, buys the token on the market, and destroys what it bought — so the burn is simultaneously a bid and a supply cut, and both halves land on the float. Bitget's current programme is reserve-funded: the tokens are already in hand, so there is a supply cut and no bid.

That makes BGB behave less like a revenue-share instrument and more like a fixed-supply token quietly cancelling its own unissued allocation. Compared with an uncapped chain that mints new supply every block to pay validators, BGB has no issuance at all and the comparison is not close. Compared with a fee-burn chain, where the burn scales with usage and comes straight out of circulating balances, BGB's burn also scales with usage — the Morph Foundation ties it to network fee value — but it is drawn from reserve, so it lands on the future rather than the present. The honest reading is that BGB is a strong long-run scarcity story with a weak short-run flow story: the tradable float has been flat since **Sep 2025**, and it will keep being flat until either the reserve is exhausted or the Foundation starts releasing the ecosystem tranche it has so far left untouched.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q3 2026 quarterly burn, expected around **mid-Oct 2026** on the observed rhythm of **Jan 23 2026**, **Apr 9 2026** and **Jul 14 2026**; the number to check is not the size but the funding wallet, because a burn paid from anywhere other than the locked reserve would turn Buy #2 non-zero immediately. Second, the governance factor inside the Morph Foundation's burn calculation, held at its **100%** baseline for Q2 2026 — the Foundation can adjust it, and a change would move the quarterly quantum without any announcement of a new programme. Third, the Morph Foundation ecosystem wallet: the stated **2%** monthly release has produced nothing for ten months, and the first genuine outflow to an exchange would put roughly **4.4M BGB** a month into Sell #2. Fourth, Bitget's **227.60M** corporate holding, which needs no unlock at all to reach the market. Fifth, the long-run target of reducing BGB to **100M** tokens, which at the current **3M**-a-quarter pace would take decades and, on present funding, would exhaust the **210.93M** reserve long before it got there — at which point the burn would start biting the float for the first time.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Bitget Token at **0.00%** net supply change over the last 90 days and projects the same for the next 90. BGB cannot mint — its contract has no mint function and its **2,000,000,000** supply was created once — and the quarterly burn that has already destroyed **1.09 billion BGB** is funded from a locked ecosystem reserve rather than from open-market purchases, so it cuts total supply without touching the tradable float. The key risk is not dilution from issuance, which is impossible, but distribution from the two identified team wallets holding **210.93M** and **227.60M BGB**, the second of which needs no unlock to sell. The ceiling is permanent at **2,000,000,000** and falling: BGB's scarcity is real, but for now it accrues to the future rather than to the market.
