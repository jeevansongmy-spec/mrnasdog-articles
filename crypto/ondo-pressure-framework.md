# ONDO Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Originally published at [https://mrnasdog.com/research/ondo/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/ondo/inflation)

Ondo Finance has never minted a single ONDO — the on-chain total has sat at **10 billion ONDO** since launch day — and yet about **241.8M ONDO** reached the market in the last 90 days, released out of Ondo's own custody wallet on dates that appear on no published vesting calendar. That wallet has now done this **six times in a year**, for **695M ONDO** in total, so the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ONDO at **+4.97% net** supply growth and carries the same rate forward. The buy side is **empty**: no buyback, no burn, no fee share, no staking.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days ending **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ONDO at +4.97% net** — **241.8M ONDO** of realised sell pressure against **zero** buy pressure, on a tradable float of **4,869.33M ONDO** — and projects the same **+4.97%** for the next 90 days. The supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.26%**, essentially unchanged, so the gap is **4.70 percentage points** and this build ships a **monitor-gap flag**. The gap is explained rather than closed: the tracked circulating figure and the Ondo custody wallet together come to about **348.5M** more than the fixed **10 billion** total, meaning part of the escrowed balance is already counted as float, so a release out of that wallet cannot register as new supply in the tracked number. The chain shows it plainly — the custody wallet fell from **5,629,207,574 ONDO** to **5,479,207,574 ONDO** on **Jun 22 2026**, and the payout wallet downstream of it drained from **101.2M** to **9.4M** over the same window. The label for Ondo Finance today: **a hard-capped token diluting from an off-calendar escrow, with no absorption engine at all**.

## Sell pressure: where new ONDO comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** for ONDO, and it is genuinely zero. ONDO is an ERC-20 token on Ethereum with a **10,000,000,000** supply, no block reward, no staking emission and no emission curve of any kind. The on-chain total read this session returned exactly **10,000,000,000 ONDO** — the same figure it has carried since the token was created — so not a single new ONDO has ever been made. Worth naming precisely, because it is often overstated: the Ondo token contract does carry a mint function, gated behind a permission and with no hard ceiling written into the code. It has simply never been used. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero as well: Ondo Finance has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-supervised distribution of ONDO.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, carries the entire ONDO sell ledger at **241.8M ONDO** — and the striking part is that the published vesting calendar says the figure should be zero. Ondo's documented schedule releases tokens once a year on the January anniversary of the January 2024 launch, running to **Jan 18 2029**, and its next dated cliff is **Jan 18 2027**. Nothing at all was scheduled inside this window. The Pressure Framework's rule for a vesting allocation held in a readable on-chain escrow is that the realised outflow beats the calendar entitlement, because what matters is supply reaching the market, not supply vesting on paper. Reading the whole Ondo-controlled cluster at both window ends — the custody wallet plus the payout wallet it feeds — returned **5,730.79M ONDO** at the start and **5,489.01M ONDO** at the end. The difference, **241.78M ONDO**, is supply that genuinely left Ondo's control: one **150M** release out of custody on **Jun 22 2026**, plus eleven separate payouts of **14M** to **26M** each between **May 20 2026** and **Aug 13 2026**, every one of them to a different recipient wallet.

The forward reading rests on the wallet's own record rather than on any calendar, because the calendar has been wrong about this token twice already. Walking the Ondo custody wallet transfer log back two full years shows sixteen releases and no schedule behind any of them; six landed in the last twelve months alone — **70M** on **Sep 15 2025**, **100M** on **Oct 24 2025**, **100M** on **Jan 13 2026**, **125M** on **Apr 22 2026**, **150M** on **May 11 2026** and **150M** on **Jun 22 2026**. That is **695M ONDO** in a year, at gaps ranging from **19** to **99 days**, with the size of each release trending up rather than down. Downstream the payouts are close to continuous, the most recent landing on **Aug 13 2026**, four days before this reading. So Sell #2 carries **241.8M** forward, the trailing realised rate, which sits between the twelve-month run rate of **178.7M** per 90 days and a date-by-date projection of the observed tranche cadence at roughly **280M**. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, books at **zero** purely to avoid counting the same tokens twice.

## Buy pressure: where new ONDO goes

Every buy row on ONDO is **zero**, and that empty buy side is the weakness of the token. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero: Ondo Finance earns real management fees on OUSG and USDY, its tokenized US Treasury products, and on its tokenized equities, and not one cent of that revenue is routed into buying ONDO. A fee switch that would change this has been discussed in Ondo commentary for over a year and has never been put to a binding vote. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero: the token has no burn function, total supply has never moved off **10 billion**, and the conventional dead address holds under **8 ONDO** in total since launch. Several crypto-media pages described a governance vote to burn **100M ONDO** around **Jul 25 2026**; the Ondo DAO vote portal shows no 2026 proposal of any kind — the last executed proposal passed on **Jan 12 2024** — and no burn has landed on-chain, so the Pressure Framework watches the claim rather than counting it.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero: across this window the Ondo custody wallet only sent, never received. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero as well, and this row is the clearest statement of ONDO's token design: ONDO has no native staking, no lock-to-earn escrow and no gas function. Holding ONDO grants a governance vote and nothing else, so there is no mechanism by which a holder can take ONDO off the market in exchange for a protocol reward. Ondo abandoned its planned Ondo Chain layer-1 on **Jul 27 2026** and replaced it with Ondo Network, an off-chain execution layer built on secure enclaves — a change that removes even the theoretical route by which ONDO might have become a staked or gas asset.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is the dominant fact about ONDO. Ten billion tokens exist, **4,869.33M** are counted as tradable, and the Ondo custody wallet alone held **5,479.21M ONDO** on **Aug 17 2026**. That single wallet holds **54.79%** of everything that will ever exist — more ONDO than the entire tradable float — and it is read fresh on-chain at every rebuild. Note the arithmetic: **5,479.21M** in the wallet against the **5,130.67M** the published calendar still calls locked means the custody wallet is **348.5M** ahead of its own schedule, the accumulated effect of releasing operationally instead of on cliff dates. The payout wallet downstream of it holds a further **9.43M ONDO** and is the pipe through which every release reaches the market; a retired payout wallet holds a dormant **0.37M**. Behind all of them sit the published buckets — Ecosystem Growth, Protocol Development, Private Sales and the Community Access Sale — running to **Jan 18 2029**. One more thing sits on top: control of the entity that signs those releases is contested. Ondo Finance founder Nathan Allman died in **May 2026**, a court appointed his estate's representative on **Jun 26 2026**, the reconstituted board moved to remove the sitting chief executive on **Jul 24 2026**, and litigation over that removal was reported on **Aug 6 2026**. If any of these balances falls between readings, that outflow enters the ONDO sell ledger at the next reading.

## How ONDO compares to other real-world-asset tokens

Mechanically, ONDO belongs to the class of capped governance tokens attached to a revenue-generating off-chain business — the same shape as a tokenized-asset issuer whose product is a yield-bearing instrument rather than the token itself. Against a hard-capped proof-of-work coin, ONDO looks superficially better: it has never minted anything, where a proof-of-work chain issues a block subsidy every ten minutes. The difference is that a proof-of-work coin's issuance is fully disclosed, dated and shrinking on a fixed halving schedule, while ONDO's dilution comes from a discretionary custody wallet whose release dates are published nowhere and are only observable after the transaction lands. Predictable issuance from a mint is easier to price than unpredictable release from an escrow.

The comparison that matters more is against exchange tokens and other fee-capturing tokens, because that is the design ONDO has chosen not to adopt. An exchange token with a quarterly buyback converts business revenue into permanent supply reduction, so growth in the underlying business mechanically shrinks the float. Ondo Finance runs a real business with real management fees on tokenized Treasuries and tokenized equities, and routes none of it back to ONDO, so business growth and token supply are entirely disconnected. Compared to other real-world-asset tokens, Ondo's product traction is at the front of the sector; its token mechanics are not. Until a fee switch is actually voted and executed, ONDO is a governance claim on a business rather than a claim on its cash flows.

The third analogue is any token still working through a multi-year unlock, and here ONDO's position is unusually heavy. Most tokens two and a half years past launch have released the majority of their supply. ONDO has released **48.69%**, with the schedule running to **Jan 18 2029** and the largest single dated event — roughly **1.7B ONDO**, about a third of today's float — still ahead on **Jan 18 2027**. A token whose remaining locked supply exceeds its entire circulating float carries a dilution profile that no amount of product success resolves on its own.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The first watch item is the Ondo custody wallet itself: the payout wallet it feeds is now down to **9.43M ONDO**, so the next tranche out of custody has to land soon for the payouts to continue, and that transfer is the whole forward reading. The second is the corporate-control dispute; whoever ends up controlling the signing keys controls the release timing, and the litigation reported on **Aug 6 2026** has no scheduled resolution. The third is the fee switch: any binding Ondo DAO vote to route management-fee revenue into an ONDO buyback or a staking reward would create the first non-zero buy row this token has ever had. The fourth is the reported **100M** burn — if it actually executes on-chain, total supply drops for the first time and it books as a one-off buy-side event. The fifth is the **Jan 18 2027** cliff, which sits outside this window but is the single largest dated supply event in the schedule.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Ondo Finance at **+4.97% net supply growth** over the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026**, and projects the same rate forward. The mechanism is unusual and easy to misread: ONDO has never minted a token and is capped in practice at **10 billion**, so every unit of dilution comes from a discretionary release out of a custody wallet still holding **5,479.21M ONDO** — a wallet that has released **695M ONDO** across six transfers in the last year on no published calendar. The key risk is that these releases are invisible to calendar-based supply trackers, which is exactly why the framework and the monitor disagree by **4.70 percentage points**. The ceiling is real — **10 billion** and no more, unless governance uses a mint function it has never touched — but with an empty buy side, no burn, no buyback and no staking, that ceiling only tells you where the dilution stops, not when the wallet moves next.
