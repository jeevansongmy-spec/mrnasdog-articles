# JST Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

JUST (JST), the governance token of the JustLend DAO lending protocol and the USDJ stablecoin on TRON, has created **zero** new tokens since its April 2020 genesis, and its vesting schedule finished on **Mar 30 2023** — so every sell row in the MrNasdog Pressure Framework ledger reads **0**. Against that, on **Jul 17 2026** JustLend DAO destroyed **355.1M JST** in a single day: **248.4M** bought on the open market with lending revenue and sent to the TRON burn address, plus **106.7M** funded from years of accumulated USDJ stability fees and removed by cutting the token's total supply outright. That puts the framework's net supply reading at **−4.34%** over the trailing 90 days and **−2.50%** forward, against a supply monitor reading of **−4.02%**. JST is one of the few tokens where the ceiling is fixed and the floor is actively falling.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads JUST at **−4.34%** net supply, meaning supply shrank by that share of the float, and projects **−2.50%** forward because the quarterly buyback-and-burn fires again inside the forward window while the one-off stablecoin fee sweep does not repeat. Our supply monitor reads **−4.02%** over the same window, a gap of **0.32 percentage points** — inside the framework's tolerance, so JST ships with no data-conflict chip. The residual is a measurement artefact rather than a disagreement about mechanism: the monitor infers supply from market value divided by price, and its 90-day-ago figure of **8,531.74M** sits about **12M** below the true pre-burn supply of **8,543.77M**, which is ordinary rounding noise on a ten-cent token. JST is best characterised as **deflationary by structural buyback, on a supply that cannot be refilled** — the JUST contract has issued nothing in six years, so the buyback is not fighting an emission, it is simply subtracting.

## Sell pressure: where new JST comes from

Nowhere — and that is the unusual part. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**. The JST contract on TRON does carry an issuance function and its owner address has never been renounced, so the capability technically exists, but the chain's own event log holds exactly one issuance event in the token's entire life: **Apr 3 2020**, for the full **9,900,000,000 JST**. Nothing has been created since. JST is a governance and fee token rather than a network reward, so using JustLend — supplying collateral, borrowing, minting USDJ — never pays anyone in newly made JST.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** as well. The three-year distribution that began at the April 2020 launch completed on **Mar 30 2023**, covering the community and ecosystem allocation, the airdrop, the strategic-partner tranche and the seed round. There is no cliff, no monthly release and no unlock calendar left anywhere in the JUST tokenomics, which is why JST behaves so differently from a same-vintage DeFi token still working through investor vesting.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** by measurement rather than by assumption. The wallet that executes the burns holds **300M JST**, a real balance worth watching, and its complete transfer history — sixteen movements across three years — resolves cleanly: every outflow went either to the TRON burn address as part of a quarterly round, or into a JustLend governance proposal contract as a deposit of **200,000,001 JST** that returns afterwards, exactly one token above the **200M** minimum JustLend requires to open a proposal. The most recent of those, on **Aug 16 2026**, is a governance deposit and not a sale. The two wallets that fund the buyback both hold **0 JST**, so nothing is quietly stockpiled behind them. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: neither JUST nor JustLend has entered any insolvency or trustee process, so no estate holds JST that a court could distribute.

## Buy pressure: where new JST goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, carries most of the ledger at **248.4M JST**. JustLend DAO commits its protocol revenue to purchasing JST on the open market and sending it to the TRON burn address, from which it cannot return. The fourth round settled on **Jul 17 2026** at **08:49 UTC**, moving **248,357,799 JST** — about **$24.2M** — from the DAO's execution wallet into the burn address, and the burn address balance confirms the transfer rather than merely echoing the announcement. Four rounds are now on the chain: **Oct 19 2025**, **Jan 14 2026**, **Apr 15 2026** and **Jul 17 2026**, spaced 87, 91 and 93 days apart. Together with the fee burn below, they have removed **1,711,249,863 JST** — **17.29%** of everything ever issued.

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, adds **106.7M JST** and deserves its own row because the money came from somewhere else entirely. On the same day, **Jul 17 2026** at **08:43 UTC**, JUST destroyed **106,663,731.97 JST** funded from the stability fees its USDJ stablecoin had collected over the preceding years. This burn never touched the burn address: it cut the token's supply figure directly, which is why the JST contract now reports a total of **9,793,336,268** rather than the original **9.9B**. It is the only destruction of that kind in the token's history, and JUST's own quarterly report frames it as a one-time sweep of a historical backlog — so the framework books it in the trailing window and carries **0** forward, treating a restart as an upside watch item rather than a projection.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**, and for a good reason: there is no accumulation to record, because every JST the DAO buys is destroyed rather than kept. That distinction matters more than it sounds. A buyback that parks tokens in a treasury creates an overhang a future vote can release back onto the market; a buyback that burns removes the option entirely. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well — depositing JST for JustLend governance is reversible at the holder's discretion and the deposited tokens still count inside the circulating supply, so the framework does not treat them as supply removed from the market.

## Foundation and overhang

The most important overhang finding for JST is a negative one. The decisive test for any burn programme is whether it is funded from tokens that sit outside the circulating count — if it is, the burn is theatre, because it destroys supply the market was never exposed to. For JUST that test comes back clean in two independent ways. The published total supply and the published circulating supply are the same number, **8,188,743,036 JST**, meaning there is no non-circulating bucket at all. And reading the chain directly, the contract's total of **9,793,336,268** minus the burn address balance of **1,604,592,401** gives **8,188,743,867** — within **830 JST** of the published circulating figure on a base of over eight billion. Circulating JST is defined as everything except the burn address, so both destruction paths removed tokens that were genuinely in the float.

That leaves exactly one team-controlled balance to enumerate: the DAO's burn-execution wallet, holding **300M JST**, or roughly **3.7%** of the float. It is re-read on every rebuild alongside the two funding wallets, which currently hold nothing, and alongside the burn address itself. Its observed behaviour is entirely inward — buy, hold briefly, destroy — with governance deposits as the only other use. If that wallet's balance falls between refreshes and the tokens land anywhere other than the burn address, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and the framework's reading of JUST changes materially, because that single wallet is larger than a full quarter's burn.

## How JST compares to other DeFi governance tokens

Most DeFi governance tokens of JST's vintage are still diluting. The standard design pairs a large ecosystem or liquidity-mining allocation with a multi-year emission, so even a protocol earning real fees hands out new tokens faster than it retires them, and the framework reads them inflationary year after year. JUST sits on the other side of that line for a purely mechanical reason: its distribution is finished. With emission at zero and vesting expired, JustLend's revenue does not have to outrun an issuance schedule before it can matter — every dollar of protocol income that reaches the buyback subtracts from a supply nothing is adding to.

The closer comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly profit-funded burns, and JST holds up well against that class on the two tests that separate a real burn from a decorative one. The first is destination: tokens must be destroyed, not accumulated, and JUST sends them to the TRON burn address or cuts supply outright rather than parking them in a treasury that a later vote could unwind. The second is whether the burn actually fires. Several exchange tokens publish an impressive quarterly schedule whose on-chain destination has not moved in months, because the funding stopped while the announcements did not. JUST's four rounds are each visible as a dated transfer, and the burn address balance rose by the announced amount on the announced day.

Where JST is weaker than a hard-capped proof-of-work coin is predictability of the downward force rather than of the upward one. A halving-model coin issues new supply on a schedule written into the protocol that nobody can pause; JUST issues nothing, but the size of its burn depends on how much revenue JustLend earns and what JST costs when the DAO buys. The four realised rounds show that clearly — **559.9M**, **525.0M**, **271.3M** and **248.4M** tokens, a steadily falling count, even though the dollars spent stayed near **$21M** to **$24M** each time. The programme is stable in money and variable in tokens, which is why the framework projects it forward in dollars and converts at the current price rather than repeating the last token figure.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch for the fifth buyback-and-burn round around **Oct 2026**: four rounds spaced roughly 90 days apart put the next one inside the forward window, and it is already carried in the **−2.50%** forward reading at **204.4M JST**. Watch the size JustLend actually commits — its provisional third-quarter budget is about **$21.55M**, which at today's price would be closer to **198.7M JST**, so a shortfall against revenue expectations shows up as a smaller burn rather than a missed date. Watch whether the USDJ stability-fee burn repeats: it was framed as a one-time sweep of historical fees and is carried at **0** forward, so any second such destruction is pure upside to the reading. Watch the burn-execution wallet holding **300M JST**, since an outflow to anywhere but the burn address would be the first sell pressure JST has shown in years. And watch JustLend's governance surface — the buyback commits a defined share of revenue through the end of **2026**, so the proposal that extends, resizes or ends the programme after that is the single most consequential vote for JST holders.

## Summary

JUST (JST) is a fixed-issuance TRON governance token whose supply only moves downward, and the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads it at **−4.34%** net supply for the trailing 90 days and **−2.50%** forward. The structural mechanism is a quarterly buyback funded by JustLend DAO's lending revenue, which destroyed **248.4M JST** on **Jul 17 2026**, joined that day by a one-time **106.7M** burn of accumulated USDJ stability fees — together **17.29%** of every JST ever issued has now been removed. The key risk is that the downward force is discretionary and revenue-linked rather than protocol-enforced: the token count burned has fallen for three consecutive rounds even as the dollars spent held steady, and the programme's current commitment runs only through the end of **2026**. The ceiling is the genuinely useful constraint here — **9.9B JST** was created once in April 2020, vesting closed in March 2023, and nothing in the design can refill what the burn takes out.
