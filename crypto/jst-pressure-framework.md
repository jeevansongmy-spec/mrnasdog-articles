# JST Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

JST, the governance token of JUST and JustLend DAO on TRON, removed **4.34%** of its circulating supply over the last 90 days and is projected to remove another **2.70%** over the next 90. Every sell row in the Pressure Framework ledger reads **zero** — JST has one mint event in six years, dated **Apr 3 2020**, and its vesting schedule expired in **March 2023** — while JustLend DAO destroyed **355.0M JST** on **Jul 17 2026** through two separate burn routes funded by the lending protocol's own revenue. The constraint is not a hard cap: the JST contract still exposes a mint function under a live owner address, so JST's deflation is a governance behaviour with a six-year record, not a rule written into the protocol.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads JST's 90-day net supply change at **−4.34%** of circulating JST, against an independent monitor reading of **−4.18%** — a gap of **0.16** percentage points, comfortably inside the framework's tolerance, so no data-conflict flag is raised on the JST overview. The two readings agree because the burn is genuinely float-negative: JST publishes no non-circulating bucket at all, so both destruction routes removed coins that were already counted as tradable supply. The characterisation for JST is a **zero-issuance token with a revenue-funded burn** — an asset whose entire supply story is subtraction, where the only open question is how long the lending business that funds the subtraction keeps earning.

## Sell pressure: where new JST comes from

It does not come from anywhere. Sell row one, protocol inflation, is **zero**: the JST contract's own creation log holds exactly one entry across its entire life, **9,900,000,000 JST** issued on **Apr 3 2020**, and nothing has been created since. JST is not a staking asset with a reward stream, not a proof-of-work coin with a block subsidy, and not a chain with an emission curve — the JUST protocol pays its lenders in interest, not in new governance tokens. The one honest caveat, and the reason this row is marked as watched rather than closed for good, is that the contract still exposes a mint function whose owner address is live rather than renounced. Six years of an empty creation log is strong evidence of intent; it is not the same thing as a cap enforced by code, and the widely quoted **9.9B** maximum supply is a historical issuance total, not a ceiling the contract would refuse to exceed.

Sell row two, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the schedule has physically run out. The JUST launch distribution released its allocation across roughly 36 tranches from **Apr 30 2020** to **Mar 30 2023**, covering the community and airdrop share, the strategic-partner and seed-round share, and the ecosystem remainder. That calendar ended more than three years before this window opened. There is no cliff left to fall due, no locked tranche behind a future date, and no escrow contract holding vested-but-unclaimed JST — so the framework's released-beats-scheduled rule has nothing to arbitrate here.

Sell row three, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** on value but not on scope. One address is identified as team-controlled: the wallet JustLend DAO uses to execute each burn round, holding **500.0M JST** at the latest read. Its complete movement history over the window resolves into two patterns and nothing else — coins going to the burn address, and a fixed **200.0M JST** block leaving and returning through governance proposal contracts, which is the deposit a JustLend proposal requires to be initiated. Not one transfer to an exchange or a market venue has been observed, so the framework books the balance as a monitored overhang at zero rather than as projected supply. Sell row four, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the simplest possible reason: JUST has never been in administration, so there is no estate and no trustee distribution queue attached to JST.

## Buy pressure: where new JST goes

Buy row one, the programmatic buyback, is the engine. JustLend DAO takes the lending protocol's quarterly revenue — interest income, liquidation penalties and stablecoin fees — buys JST on the open market with it, and sends the coins to an address on TRON that nobody holds the keys to. On **Jul 17 2026** that address received **248.4M JST** in a single transfer, funded by roughly **$20.6M** of Q2 2026 earnings and accumulated prior reserves. It is the fourth such round: **559.9M** on **Oct 19 2025**, **525.0M** on **Jan 14 2026**, **271.3M** on **Apr 15 2026**, then this one — spaced 87, 91 and 93 days apart, which is what makes the programme projectable rather than promotional.

Buy row two, the protocol fee burn, is where JST teaches a lesson most supply trackers get wrong. On the same day, a further **106.7M JST** was destroyed by a different route entirely: instead of moving coins somewhere unreachable, the token count itself was cut. Reading only the total supply figure would have shown a fall of **106.7M** and missed the **248.4M** buyback completely; reading only the burn address would have caught the buyback and missed this. The framework reads both surfaces, which is how it arrives at **355.0M JST** — the exact figure JustLend DAO announced for the round. This second burn was funded by years of accumulated USDJ stability fees swept into the burn pool for the first time. It is the only event of its kind in the JST contract's six-year history, so the framework treats it as a one-off and projects **zero** from it forward, carrying it as upside rather than baseline.

Buy rows three and four are both **zero**. There is no separate foundation bid beside the DAO programme — all open-market purchasing runs through the buyback above, and counting a second buyer would double-count the same money. And nothing was sealed into a new lock-up this window: the DAO's own holding moved only through governance deposits that return in full, and no staking cap or fresh escrow contract was announced. Forward, the framework carries **221.3M JST** of buyback into the next 90 days, derived from JustLend DAO's published provisional Q3 2026 budget of about **$21.55M** at the current JST price, with one round falling inside the window.

## Foundation and overhang

JST's overhang picture is unusually clean, and worth stating precisely because it is the reason the burn actually shrinks the tradable float. The circulating supply and the total supply published for JST are the same number, **8,188.7M JST** — meaning the non-circulating bucket is exactly zero. There is no locked ecosystem reserve, no foundation treasury sitting outside the count, and therefore no pool a burn could be quietly funded from without touching real float. The chain agrees: total issued supply of **9,793.3M** minus the burn address balance of **1,604.6M** lands within a few hundred JST of the published circulating figure. Both of JustLend DAO's destruction routes removed coins that were counted as tradable, which is the opposite of the pattern seen on exchange tokens that burn from an unlisted reserve.

The single identified team-controlled overhang is the DAO burn-execution wallet, holding **500.0M JST** — about **6.1%** of the float — with no published release calendar and no observed sale in its history. The two addresses that fund each round both read **0 JST**; they are pass-throughs, not reserves. The other large balances on the token are unlabelled externally-owned addresses with no disclosed controller, which the framework deliberately does not treat as team overhang. The standing trigger is this: if the DAO execution wallet's balance falls between refreshes and the outflow does not land at the burn address, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and JST's reading changes accordingly.

## How JST compares to other revenue-burn tokens

JST belongs to the class of tokens whose supply falls because a business buys them back, not because a protocol caps them. That puts it beside the large exchange tokens — BNB with its quarterly auto-burn, OKB, KCS, GT — and apart from both hard-capped chains like Bitcoin and fee-burn chains like Ethereum. The mechanism difference matters more than the size difference. A hard cap is enforced by code and cannot be lobbied away; a fee burn scales with network usage and needs no discretionary decision at all. JST's burn requires a DAO to keep earning and keep voting, which makes it the weaker guarantee by construction — and yet, measured over the window, it produces a far larger reduction than either.

Against the exchange tokens specifically, JST scores better on one axis that is easy to miss. Several exchange-token burns are float-neutral: the coins destroyed come from a reserve that was never counted as circulating, so the burn is real on paper and invisible in the tradable float. JST cannot do that, because it has no non-circulating bucket to burn from — every JST destroyed since **Oct 2025** was bought on the open market or written off the total count directly. Against other TRON-ecosystem assets, JST is also the unusual one: TRX itself runs a fee burn tied to network activity with ongoing issuance behind it, whereas JST has no issuance at all and a burn tied to lending revenue instead of transaction count.

The honest structural weakness is the mint function. Bitcoin's cap and Ethereum's burn are properties of the protocol; JST's zero issuance is a property of an owner address choosing not to act, six years running. Nothing in the contract would stop a new issuance, and the framework tags the row accordingly rather than calling it permanent. Anyone treating JST as capped is trusting a track record, not a rule.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the fifth quarterly buyback-and-burn round, expected around **Oct 16 2026** on the 87-to-93-day spacing the four executed rounds have held — the framework projects **221.3M JST** from JustLend DAO's stated Q3 2026 budget, and the actual round will confirm or reset that run rate. Second, the quantum trend itself: the four rounds have fallen from **559.9M** to **248.4M** in token terms, so a fifth round materially below **200M** would signal lending revenue contracting rather than JST's price rising. Third, whether the USDJ stability fee sweep repeats; JUST framed the **106.7M** destruction as a one-time clearing of historical fees, and a second one would be new information, not a continuation. Fourth, any movement out of the DAO execution wallet's **500.0M JST** that does not end at the burn address. Fifth, and least likely but most consequential, any call to the contract's mint function — the creation log has one entry today, and a second would rewrite the whole ledger.

## Summary

JST is the cleanest deflation reading the MrNasdog Pressure Framework currently produces on a token of its size: every sell row is **zero**, the only issuance event on record is dated **Apr 3 2020**, vesting closed in **March 2023**, and JustLend DAO destroyed **355.0M JST** on **Jul 17 2026** through an open-market buyback and a direct supply write-down, for a net of **−4.34%** over 90 days and a projected **−2.70%** over the next. The structural mechanism is a lending business converting its own revenue into permanent supply reduction, and because JST has no non-circulating reserve, that reduction lands on real tradable float rather than on paper. The key risk is that the mechanism is discretionary at both ends: the burn depends on JustLend DAO continuing to earn and continuing to vote, and the contract's mint function remains live under an owner address rather than renounced. There is no protocol cap here — the **9.9B** figure is what was issued once, not a ceiling the code enforces.
