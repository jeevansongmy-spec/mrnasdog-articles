# JST Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

JUST (JST) mints nothing and unlocks nothing. Its 9.9B supply is capped, its emission ended years ago, and team tokens vested back in 2022. The only live force is a quarterly buyback-and-burn, which destroyed **355M JST** in this window — the largest round yet at about **$34.59M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **−4.34%** net supply over 90 days, and our supply monitor agrees at **−3.99%**, a gap of just **0.35 percentage points** that ships no data-conflict chip. JST is deflationary by structural buyback, not by promise. Cumulative burns now top **1.71B JST**, more than **17%** of everything ever issued, and the next round should remove roughly **204M JST** more.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 6 2026**, the framework reads **JST at −4.34% net** — every sell row is zero, and the only entry on the ledger is a **355M JST** buyback-and-burn against a circulating base of about **8.19B JST**. Our supply monitor reads **−3.99%** for the same window, a gap of only **0.35 percentage points**, well inside the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip appears on the JST overview. The two agree because they measure the same event from two angles: the on-chain burn transaction removed 355M JST, and the market's circulating-supply figure fell by about 340M over the window. JST is best characterised as **a fixed-cap DeFi token that a revenue-funded buyback keeps net-deflationary** — the cleanest structural shape the framework tracks, with a mint that has never fired and a burn that fires every quarter.

## Sell pressure: where new JST comes from

There is no sell pressure to walk — all four rows are **zero**, which is the defining feature of JST. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**: JST has a fixed **9.9B** maximum, no mint function that issues new tokens, and a three-year distribution that finished long ago. The token was issued with its full supply in 2020 and the on-chain figure has only ever moved downward, through burns.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**: the release schedule ran for three years from the May 2020 launch and is long complete, with team tokens fully vested by April 2022, so no cliff remains to add tradable JST. A handful of aggregator pages still list a small locked slice, but the on-chain schedule is spent and the market counts the entire non-burned supply as circulating — the only gap left to the 9.9B cap is the **1.71B JST** already burned. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** this window — no coordinated team distribution occurred, though it carries a real overhang discussed below. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: no estate or court-ordered distribution touches JST.

## Buy pressure: where new JST goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is the entire ledger. Each quarter JustLend DAO spends protocol revenue — lending fees, liquidation penalties and USDJ stablecoin stability fees — to buy JST on the open market and send it to a TRON black-hole address, permanently removing it from supply. The most recent round destroyed **355M JST** (about **$34.59M**), the largest round by value to date, and it is the single reason JST supply shrank over the window. Cumulative burns since the program launched in October 2025 now exceed **1.71B JST**, more than **17%** of the maximum supply.

The other three buy rows are **zero** by structure, not by absence of activity. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** because JST fees are not burned directly — they fund the market buyback, so the destruction is counted once under Buy #1 rather than double-booked. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** because the DAO's purchases go straight to the burn address rather than into a treasury to hold. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**: no new escrow or lockup was announced. The result is a ledger with a single, large deflationary entry and nothing on the other side.

## Foundation and overhang

One overhang sits behind JST: the JUST ecosystem and community allocation, which aggregator tokenomics pages describe as a multi-billion reserve earmarked for rewards and development. It is watched, but two facts keep it off the sell side. First, the market's circulating-supply classification already counts the full non-burned supply — there is no separately recognised non-circulating bucket to release. Second, the DAO's demonstrated posture is the opposite of distribution: it is spending revenue to buy JST back and burn it, quarter after quarter. No release from any identified team wallet was observed in the window. If that reserve's balance were ever drawn down and sold into the market between refreshes, the outflow would enter Sell #3 at the next refresh — but nothing about the current program points that way.

## How JST compares to other exchange-and-DeFi buyback tokens

JST belongs to the class of tokens whose scarcity comes from a revenue-funded buyback-and-burn rather than from a fixed issuance schedule — the same mechanism that drives an exchange token's quarterly auto-burn or a lending protocol routing fees to repurchases. The structural signature is identical: a business generates cash flow, and some of that cash is used to retire tokens. What makes JST unusually clean is that it has **no offsetting issuance at all** — no staking emission, no unlock calendar, no team vesting — so every burned token is a direct, unclouded reduction in supply. On chains where a buyback runs against ongoing emission, the net can still be inflationary; on JST the burn has nothing to fight.

Against a hard-capped, mint-based asset the contrast is sharper. A halving-model coin approaches its cap from below, issuing new supply on a shrinking schedule; JST has already distributed its full **9.9B** and now moves in the other direction, burning back toward a smaller float. It is capped like a mint-based coin but deflationary like a burn token — a combination that depends entirely on JustLend continuing to earn fees. That dependence is the key difference from a protocol-encoded burn: JST's burn is discretionary DAO spending tied to business revenue, so it scales up in a strong quarter and could shrink in a weak one.

Compared to the many DeFi governance tokens that inflate to pay liquidity incentives, JST is a structural outlier. Most lending-and-yield tokens dilute holders to fund growth; JST does the reverse, converting protocol income into permanent supply reduction. That is why a fixed-cap token with a years-old distribution can still post a **−4.34%** quarter. The honest weakness of the model is concentration: the entire reading rests on one recurring discretionary event, so if revenue collapses or the DAO retires the programme, the sell rows stay at zero but the buy row goes to zero too — and a token with no mint and no burn is simply flat, not deflationary.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the Q3 2026 buyback-and-burn, guided at about **$21.55M** (roughly **204M JST** at current prices) and expected around **mid-October 2026** on the quarterly schedule — it is the single event that sets the next window's net, and on the guidance the framework projects about **−2.49%**. Watch JustLend's protocol revenue, because the burn is funded by fees and liquidation penalties: a busier lending market means a larger burn, a quieter one means a smaller one. Watch for any change to the burn policy itself — a pause, a size cap, or a shift from burning to holding would move JST from deflationary toward flat. And watch the ecosystem reserve for any sign of distribution, which is the only realistic path to a sell-side row.

## Summary

JST is a fixed-cap TRON DeFi token that is deflationary by structure: it mints nothing, unlocks nothing, and its only supply force is JustLend DAO's quarterly buyback-and-burn, which destroyed **355M JST** in the window for net new supply of **−4.34%**; our monitor reads **−3.99%**, a **0.35-point** gap inside tolerance. The key strength is the clean mechanism — every burned token reduces supply with no issuance to offset it — and the key risk is its dependence on protocol revenue, since a revenue-funded burn shrinks when fees shrink. With the next round guided at roughly **204M JST** around mid-October for a projected **−2.49%**, JST is projected to keep shrinking, and its direction rests less on its 9.9B ceiling than on whether JustLend keeps earning enough to burn.
