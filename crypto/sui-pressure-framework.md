Sui adds about **64.06M SUI** to circulation over the next 90 days — roughly **39.94M** from scheduled unlocks and **24.12M** from the staking subsidy — while the Sui Foundation's stablecoin-funded buyback takes back only about **0.72M**, and even that is handed back out rather than burned. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.55% net** forward, up from **+1.32%** over the last 90 days purely because three monthly releases land in the next window instead of two. SUI is capped at **10B** total supply, but only about **41%** of it circulates — the unlock calendar, not a protocol mint, is the whole story.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SUI at **+1.32% net**: about **54.25M SUI** of new float against roughly **0.56M SUI** of buy-side offset, on a circulating base of **4,074.5M SUI**. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+1.06%**, a gap of about **0.26 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and needs no reconciliation walk. The two agree for a structural reason: the Sui Foundation publishes its own month-by-month circulating-supply schedule, the broad market republishes that number verbatim, and the framework books the same series instead of inferring one. Looking forward, the framework reads **+1.55%** for the next 90 days. SUI is **structurally inflationary on the active float** — a hard-capped token whose supply can never shrink, releasing a shrinking but still relentless monthly tranche of previously locked coins.

## Sell pressure: where new SUI comes from

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the dominant row, at a net **27.45M SUI** over the last 90 days and about **39.94M SUI** over the next. Sui does not release locked supply in lumpy annual cliffs; it steps circulating supply up once a month on a published calendar that runs to 2030, drawing from the Community Reserve, the Mysten Labs treasury, the early-contributor allocation and the Series A and Series B investor tranches. The cliff dates decide this row, and they were checked one by one against the window: **Jul 1 2026** (+23.14M) and **Aug 1 2026** (+22.20M) land inside it and each contributes its full quantum, while **Jun 1 2026** (+23.78M) misses the window opening by a single day and contributes nothing. That one cliff is the entire reason this row reads lower than the last build — per release it is unchanged. Three releases land in the forward window instead of two: **Sep 1 2026** (+22.01M), **Oct 1 2026** (+21.73M) and **Nov 1 2026** (+20.77M), with **Dec 1 2026** falling two days past the close.

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the Sui staking subsidy, and on Sui it is a finite escrowed pool rather than an open-ended mint. Reading the Sui mainnet system state directly on-chain, the network distributes **282,430 SUI** per epoch, and one Sui epoch is exactly one day. Crucially that pool is readable at both ends of the window: it held **267.99M SUI** ninety epochs ago and **241.19M SUI** today, so the realised outflow was **26.80M SUI** — matching the summed per-epoch distributions to the last unit, because the protocol forces the draw every epoch and no backlog can build up. The rate steps down **10%** every 90 epochs: it stepped on **Jul 16 2026**, splitting the last window into 44 epochs at the old amount and 46 at the new one, and it steps again around **Oct 14 2026**, inside the forward window. There is no catch-up and no reset — each cut is permanent, and the pool has only about **241.19M SUI** left in it, a little over two more years at the current rate.

One trap is worth naming, because it would double the sell side if missed. The Sui Foundation's published monthly step is not pure unlock: it already carries 30 epochs of staking subsidy inside it. The arithmetic proves it to the cent — the drop from the July step to the August step, **941,432 SUI**, is exactly thirty times the per-epoch subsidy cut that landed on Jul 16. So Sell #2 is booked as the monthly steps net of the subsidy share, and Sell #1 carries the subsidy on its own. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** on this build, but the overhang behind it is enormous: about **5,925.5M SUI** remains outside circulation. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** by construction: Sui has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee distribution schedule and no court-ordered release affecting SUI.

## Buy pressure: where new SUI goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is real but small. The Sui Foundation repurchases SUI on the open market, funded by yield earned on its stablecoin reserves. Its own published purchase ledger runs from **Jun 22 2026** to **Aug 30 2026** and records **558,700 SUI** bought for roughly **$403,000**, averaging about **8.0K SUI** a session, which projects to roughly **0.72M SUI** forward. The catch is the destination: bought-back SUI is **not burned** and **not held**. It is reinvested into the Sui ecosystem as grants, developer programmes, liquidity incentives and validator support, so the coins flow straight back to the float and are tracked as a matching Sell #3 flow-back. The buyback is genuine open-market demand, but it is a redistribution loop, not a supply sink.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero** because Sui destroys nothing at all, and this build tested that two ways rather than one. Total supply read exactly **10B SUI** at both ends of the window, and Sui has no dead-address sink in its design; the nearest thing, the protocol storage fund, grew from **2,586,799 SUI** to **2,760,723 SUI** while total supply did not move, which makes it a transfer into a fund rather than a burn. The recycling is provable too: summed across all **90** epochs, total staking rewards minus the staking subsidy equals total gas fees to within four SUI. That fee take is worth sizing precisely, because it is routinely quoted the wrong way round. Sui collected **233,628 SUI** in gas fees over the 90-day window — an annualised **947,468 SUI per year**, or about **0.023%** of circulating supply per year. Fees on Sui are far smaller than issuance, not larger: the whole window's fee take is **0.87%** of what the staking subsidy paid out over the same 90 days. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is zero beyond the buyback already counted. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is zero, and Sui makes the reason unusually clear: on-chain total stake is about **7,078.4M SUI** across **128** active validators, which exceeds circulating supply outright, because locked and unvested SUI can be staked before it vests. A Sui delegator can withdraw at the next epoch, one day later, so staked SUI is not removed float — and total stake actually fell across the window.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on Sui is the largest single fact about SUI supply. Roughly **5,217.2M SUI** — about **52%** of total supply — sits in a tranche with no release calendar at all, because the published schedule simply ends in **May 2030**; the Sui Foundation controls when that tranche moves. Another **708.3M SUI** is queued on the published monthly schedule between now and then. The staking subsidy pool holds a further **241.19M SUI**, readable on-chain and draining at a protocol-fixed rate no one can accelerate, and the protocol storage fund holds **2.76M SUI**. The buyback destination is the one opaque item: the Sui Foundation has not published a buyback wallet address, and the tokens are dispersed to ecosystem partners rather than parked, so it is monitored through the Foundation's own disclosure on a bi-weekly walk. Exchange custodial wallets and unidentified large holders are deliberately excluded — they are not team-controlled. The trigger is simple: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, and the framework books it as real sell pressure rather than potential.

## How SUI compares to other capped Layer 1 chains

SUI belongs to a specific and often misread class: the **hard-capped, fully-premined Layer 1**. Total supply is fixed at **10B SUI** and every coin was minted at genesis, so unlike an uncapped continuous-emission chain, SUI can never mint a coin that did not already exist. That sounds deflationary and is not. The measure that matters to a holder is the tradable float, and on Sui the float is only about **41%** of the cap, with the rest arriving on a calendar. A halving-model chain with a hard cap issues new coins slowly but has essentially no locked overhang; Sui has no issuance in the strict sense but carries a **5,925.5M SUI** overhang that behaves exactly like issuance from the market's point of view.

Against an uncapped Layer 1 with a fee burn, the contrast is sharper still. Those chains mint continuously but destroy a share of every transaction fee, so heavy usage can push net supply negative. Sui has the opposite shape: no mint and no burn. Because there is no burn anywhere in the Sui design, network activity cannot reduce SUI supply no matter how high it goes — and at **0.023%** of supply a year, the Sui fee base is nowhere near large enough to matter even if the design changed tomorrow. A Sui that processes ten times today's transactions still runs the same unlock calendar. The one genuinely favourable structural comparison is against a peer whose unlock schedule is still accelerating: Sui's monthly step is unambiguously in decline, from **52M SUI** in May 2026 to about **20.77M SUI** by November, and the staking subsidy shrinks **10%** every 90 epochs on a rule no vote can reverse. SUI's inflation is high today and mathematically committed to falling.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four dated items would move this reading. First, the monthly circulation steps on **Sep 1 2026**, **Oct 1 2026** and **Nov 1 2026**, adding **22.01M**, **21.73M** and **20.77M SUI** respectively — if any step lands materially above its published figure, the schedule itself has changed. Second, the staking subsidy step-down around **Oct 14 2026**, which cuts the per-epoch distribution by **10%** and is already priced into the forward column. Third, the Sui Foundation buyback ledger, running near **8.0K SUI** a session and scaling with stablecoin reserves, so a large jump in reserve size would lift the only buy row on the page. Fourth, any Sui Foundation disclosure touching the post-2030 tranche — a decision to release any part of that **5,217.2M SUI** early is the single event that would change SUI's inflation profile rather than merely nudge it.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Sui (SUI) as **structurally inflationary on the active float**: **+1.32%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+1.55%** projected for the next, against a supply monitor reading of **+1.06%** — a **0.26 percentage point** gap that needs no reconciliation. The mechanism is a published monthly unlock calendar plus a finite, escrowed staking subsidy, with a small stablecoin-funded buyback that recycles rather than removes. The key risk is the overhang: **5,925.5M SUI** — most of it in a post-2030 tranche with no calendar — sits outside the float, and Sui has no burn of any kind, so supply can only ever go up. The ceiling is the one genuine comfort: total supply is capped at **10B SUI**, every coin already exists, and both the monthly step and the staking subsidy are contractually shrinking.
