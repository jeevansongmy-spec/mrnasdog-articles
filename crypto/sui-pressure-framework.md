Sui adds about **64.5M SUI** to circulation over the next 90 days — roughly **39.7M** from scheduled unlocks and **24.8M** from the staking subsidy — while a new stablecoin-funded buyback takes back only about **1.9M**, and even that is redeployed rather than burned. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.54% net** forward, easing from **+1.64%** over the last 90 days as both the monthly release step and the subsidy shrink. SUI is capped at **10B** total supply, but only **40.7%** is circulating — the unlock calendar, not the protocol mint, is the story.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SUI at **+1.64% net**: about **69.1M SUI** of new float against roughly **2.1M SUI** of buy-side offset, on a circulating base of **4,074.5M SUI**. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+1.61%**, a gap of about **0.03 percentage points** — well inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and needs no reconciliation walk. The two agree because Sui publishes its own month-by-month circulating-supply schedule and the framework books that schedule directly rather than inferring it. Looking forward, the framework reads **+1.54%** for the next 90 days. SUI is **structurally inflationary on the active float** — a hard-capped token whose supply cannot shrink, releasing a shrinking but still steady monthly tranche of previously locked coins.

## Sell pressure: where new SUI comes from

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is the release-side stream, at a net **43.7M SUI** over the last 90 days across three monthly steps and about **39.7M SUI** over the next. Sui does not release locked supply in lumpy annual cliffs; it steps circulating supply up once a month on a published calendar that runs to 2030, drawing from the Community Reserve, the Mysten Labs treasury, the early-contributor allocation and the Series A and Series B investor tranches. The single most important fact remains that the step got smaller: the monthly increase ran at roughly **52M SUI** through May 2026 and fell to roughly **22M SUI** from June onward, easing further to about **21M SUI** by November. One caution for readers comparing sources: public unlock trackers quote a larger headline cliff — around **24M SUI** a month gross, with a **Sep 3 2026** event of about 24.1M — because they count the gross vesting-contract release. Sui's own circulation schedule reports the net monthly increase, and the framework follows the network's own number, net of the staking subsidy booked below.

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the staking subsidy, and on Sui it is a finite pool rather than an open-ended mint. Reading the Sui mainnet system state directly on-chain, the network distributes about **282,430 SUI** per epoch, and one Sui epoch is exactly one day, which works out to about **25.4M SUI** over the last 90 days and about **24.8M SUI** over the next. The distribution steps down about **10%** every 90 epochs by protocol rule, the next step-down lands in **mid-October 2026** inside the forward window, and the subsidy pool has only about **247.7M SUI** left in it — roughly two and a half more years at the current rate. This is the part of SUI inflation that is genuinely mechanical, genuinely predictable, and genuinely disappearing.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** on this build, but the overhang behind it is not small. About **5.93B SUI** remains outside circulation. The framework books nothing here because every one of those buckets releases through the same published monthly calendar already counted in Sell #2, and no unscheduled foundation outflow was observed in the window. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** and structurally so: there is no bankruptcy estate, no trustee distribution schedule and no court-ordered release affecting SUI.

## Buy pressure: where new SUI goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **no longer zero**. Sui now repurchases about **23.8K SUI** a day on the open market, funded by yield on the native USDsui stablecoin reserve plus gas fees — roughly **2.1M SUI** over the last 90 days, about **$602K** in the trailing month and easing at around **15%** month-on-month, so the framework books about **1.9M SUI** forward. The catch is the destination: bought-back SUI is **not burned**. It is redeployed to ecosystem participants — DeFi, validators and partners — with total supply unchanged, so the tokens flow back to the float and are tracked as a matching Sell #3 flow-back. The buyback is real open-market demand, but it is a redistribution loop, not a supply sink.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero** because Sui explicitly does not burn. Computation gas is paid straight through to validators, and storage fees flow into a permanent storage fund whose principal is preserved and staked to pay for future storage; the on-chain fund even posted a small net inflow in the latest epoch, confirming nothing is destroyed. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is zero beyond the stablecoin-funded buyback already counted: the Sui Foundation runs no separate open-market accumulation programme, and a Nasdaq-listed treasury, SUI Group Holdings, holding more than **105M SUI**, runs buybacks on its own shares rather than on the token, so the framework does not book it. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is zero: a Sui delegator can withdraw at the next daily epoch, so staked SUI stays part of the float and no new fixed-size lockup was announced.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on Sui is large but unusually well documented. Roughly **5.93B SUI** — about **59%** of the fixed 10B total supply — sits outside circulation across identifiable buckets: a post-2030 reserve at about **52%** of total supply, the Community Reserve at about **10.7%**, the stake-subsidy pool at about **2.5%** of supply and draining, the Series A and Series B investor allocations, the early-contributor allocation, the Community Access Program and the Mysten Labs treasury at about **1.6%**. The subsidy pool is the one overhang readable directly on-chain: it holds about **247.7M SUI** and drains at a rule-fixed rate rather than a discretionary one. Alongside it sits the buyback flow-back — about **2.1M SUI** a quarter bought and redeployed rather than retired.

How closely each bucket can be watched differs. The subsidy pool, the storage fund and the buyback are read from the Sui mainnet system state and the Foundation's on-chain buyback ledger at every rebuild, so any change is caught within a build cycle. The remaining allocations are tracked against the Sui Foundation's published circulation schedule, which is re-fetched on each rebuild and compared step by step against the previous read — that is how the June 2026 step-down from roughly 52M to roughly 22M a month was caught. If any of these balances falls between refreshes faster than the published schedule allows, the excess outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh rather than being absorbed silently into the vesting row.

## How SUI compares to other uncapped-float layer-1 chains

SUI belongs to a specific structural class: the hard-capped, venture-funded layer-1 whose scarcity is real at the level of total supply but almost meaningless at the level of tradable float. Compared with a halving-model chain like Bitcoin, where the cap and the emission schedule are the same fact and the float converges on the cap, Sui's 10B cap tells you almost nothing about near-term supply — what matters is that only 40.7% of it is circulating and the rest arrives on a Foundation-published calendar. In that respect SUI reads much more like Aptos or Celestia than like Bitcoin or Litecoin: the dominant supply variable is a vesting calendar, not a block subsidy.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains such as Solana or Cardano, the comparison inverts. Those networks mint genuinely new tokens every epoch and anchor their inflation to a declining protocol curve that is predictable years out. Sui does have such a curve — the staking subsidy stepping down about 10% every 90 epochs — and it is now roughly **24.8M SUI** of the **64.5M** forward total, with scheduled unlocks providing the larger **39.7M**. As the vesting calendar drains, the mechanical subsidy's finite **247.7M SUI** balance becomes the clearest long-run signal on Sui supply.

The sharpest contrast is with fee-burn and buyback chains. Ethereum can post negative net issuance when base-fee burn exceeds validator rewards, and BNB retires supply on a quarterly schedule — both permanently remove tokens. Sui's new stablecoin-funded buyback looks superficially similar but is categorically different: it repurchases SUI and then redeploys it, so it recycles float rather than shrinking it. Because computation gas goes to validators, storage fees are preserved, and buybacks are redistributed rather than destroyed, SUI net supply cannot mathematically fall — the best case is a net figure approaching zero once the vesting calendar exhausts and the subsidy pool empties, not a deflationary print. That is the structural ceiling on how well SUI can ever score on this metric.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The **Sep 1 2026** scheduled step adds about **22.0M SUI** to circulating supply, the **Oct 1 2026** step adds about **21.7M SUI**, and the **Nov 1 2026** step adds about **20.8M SUI** — three near-identical monthly steps with no cliff among them. Watch whether realised circulating supply tracks those figures or overshoots them, because a divergence would mean the Foundation has amended its schedule. Watch the staking-subsidy step-down around **mid-Oct 2026**, when the distribution counter reaches its next 90-epoch boundary and the per-epoch amount falls another 10%. Watch the USDsui buyback: its run rate is already cooling about 15% month-on-month, and whether Sui ever routes bought-back SUI to a burn address instead of redeploying it would be the first genuine supply sink on the network. And watch for any Sui Improvement Proposal that would introduce a fee burn or alter the storage-fund rules.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SUI as **structurally inflationary on the active float** at **+1.54% net** over the next 90 days, easing from **+1.64%** over the last 90 — a supply picture that keeps cooling as both Sui's monthly release step and its staking subsidy shrink. The mechanism is release-side: about **39.7M SUI** of scheduled unlocks plus about **24.8M SUI** of finite staking subsidy, against a buy ledger that offsets only about **1.9M SUI** through a buyback that redeploys rather than burns. The key risk is that **5.93B SUI** — nearly 60% of the fixed 10B cap — is still locked and arrives on a schedule the Sui Foundation itself can amend. The ceiling is equally clear: with no burn and a buyback that recycles rather than retires, SUI supply can flatten but can never shrink.
