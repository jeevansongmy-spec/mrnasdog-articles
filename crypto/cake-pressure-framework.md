# CAKE Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply shrinking, projected to keep shrinking

PancakeSwap removed **6.70M CAKE** from the market over the last 90 days and put back only **2.11M** — **1.96M** of farm, product and ecosystem emission plus **0.15M** draining out of two retired lock contracts. That is **-1.43%** net, against **-1.53%** from our supply monitor, a gap of **0.10 percentage points** and no warning chip. CAKE is **deflationary by revenue**: the burn is funded by trading fees, not by a schedule, and PancakeSwap was fair-launched with no premine, so the sell side has no vesting row at all. The **400M** ceiling, cut from 450M in **January 2026**, is an accounting promise rather than a line of code.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 23 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **CAKE at -1.43% net**, with **-1.29%** projected over the next 90 days. Our supply monitor reads **-1.53%** for the same trailing window — a gap of **0.10 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip is attached to the CAKE overview page. Getting there took an unusual amount of care, because CAKE's raw numbers are a trap: the token's supply figure only ever rises, it climbed **768M** inside this window alone, and the burn address took in **773M** over the same period. Neither figure is the ledger. PancakeSwap's farm contract still mints at its original **44 CAKE a block** and then destroys **99.72%** of it in the same step, so almost all of that traffic is an accounting wash that never touches a holder. Strip it out and the real flows are small, clean and firmly one-way. CAKE is **deflationary by structural buyback**.

## Sell pressure: where new CAKE comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **1.96M CAKE**, and it is the only place new PancakeSwap supply comes from. The rate is set on-chain and was read at both ends of the window without moving: the farm contract pays **0.1133 CAKE** a block to farms, syrup pools, position-manager vaults, ecosystem grants and the V3 reward stream, which over the **17,263,131** blocks BNB Chain actually produced in this window works out to **21,732 CAKE** a day. PancakeSwap's own monthly burn reports use the same method and give **21,748** a day — agreement to **0.1%**, on figures derived completely independently. The block count matters more than it sounds: BNB Chain ran at **0.45 seconds** a block over this window, so assuming a nominal block time instead of counting the blocks would have understated the emission by roughly **40%**.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**, and it is zero permanently rather than quietly. PancakeSwap was fair-launched in **September 2020** with no presale, no venture round and no team premine; there is no vesting contract anywhere in the design, so no cliff can land in this window or any future one. Very few tokens in this catalogue can say that. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is also **zero**: no estate, trustee or court-administered pool holds CAKE.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — books **zero** because there is no team-held CAKE allocation to release, but the row still carries the scope, and it is not empty. Sell #5, a CAKE-specific row, carries **0.15M CAKE** of genuine selling that no supply read would ever surface. PancakeSwap retired its vote-escrow lock on **Apr 23 2025** and closed redemption on **Oct 23 2025**, but the contract is not empty — it held **5.93M CAKE** at the start of the window and **5.84M** at the end, while the retired staking pool went from **13.52M** to **13.45M**. Together they gave back **154,666 CAKE** to the market. Those balances sit outside the counted float, which means every coin a straggler withdraws is a coin the float gains without anything being minted.

## Buy pressure: where new CAKE goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is the whole story at **6.70M CAKE**, more than three times what the farms handed out. PancakeSwap routes between **15%** and **23%** of spot trading fees, **20%** of perpetual trading profits, every launchpad fee and **20%** of lottery entries into CAKE and destroys it. That figure is measured, not quoted, and the distinction matters here more than on any other page in this catalogue — because PancakeSwap publishes **two** burn figures for the same period and they disagree with each other by **10%**. The monthly burn reports give **2,402,150 CAKE** for June 2026 and **1,944,441** for July, which annualises to about **6.41M** a quarter. The weekly posts inside the same window read **496,000**, **600,000** and **556,000**, which works out to about **7.07M**. The chain settles it at **6.70M**, or roughly **521,000** a week — between the two, **4.3%** above the monthly series and **5.5%** below the weekly one.

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — reads **zero**, which looks wrong until you follow the coins. PancakeSwap genuinely does buy CAKE on the open market with trading revenue; it simply never keeps any of it. Every repurchased coin goes straight to the burn address, so there is no accumulation wallet, no destination overhang and no balance that could later reverse into selling. Counting the same tokens in both Buy #1 and Buy #2 would double the buy side, so the buyback is booked once, in the burn row. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero**: no separate foundation or corporate treasury buys CAKE on a published timetable. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** too, because term locking was retired in **April 2025** and nothing replaced it; CAKE simply cannot be locked for a fixed period any more, and the two retired contracts are draining rather than filling.

## Foundation and overhang

CAKE's overhang is unusually small, because there is no allocation behind it. Three contract balances are tracked instead of team wallets. The retired vote-escrow contract holds **5.84M CAKE** and the retired staking pool holds **13.45M**; both are read on-chain every rebuild, both sit outside the counted float, and both are shrinking — their outflow is already booked in Sell #5. The third is the bridge lock, holding **10.08M CAKE** that backs CAKE on nine other chains including Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, zkSync, Linea, opBNB, Aptos and Solana. That balance is a mirror, not an overhang: those coins are already counted here, and the mirrored legs added up to **6.11M** at the start of the window and **6.13M** at the end, leaving a constant offset rather than a drifting one — which is exactly how you tell a mirror from a leg you have failed to find.

The one thing that could still be called an overhang is the ceiling itself. PancakeSwap's **400M** cap, cut from **450M** by a vote that passed on **Jan 19 2026** with unanimous support, leaves **66.5M CAKE** of unused room. It is a commitment, not a constraint: the token's mint function is live and uncapped, owned by the farm contract, and the raw supply figure has already passed **5 billion**. Nothing in the code stops PancakeSwap from minting past 400M — only its own governance does. If any of these three balances falls between refreshes, or if the ceiling is ever raised, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CAKE compares to other exchange tokens

CAKE belongs to the **revenue-burn exchange token** class — tokens whose supply is retired using the fee income of the venue that issues them. Its closest structural analogues are centralised-exchange tokens running quarterly buybacks, and the comparison flatters CAKE in one respect and not in another. A quarterly buyback is lumpy and discretionary: the exchange decides the size, announces it, executes it and can quietly skip a quarter. PancakeSwap's burn is continuous and rule-bound — a fixed share of every trade, every perpetual position and every launchpad sale — so it scales with usage automatically and does not depend on anyone choosing to act. Against a token with no burn at all, that difference is the entire page.

The place CAKE is weaker is the supply ceiling. A hard-capped proof-of-work coin has its issuance written into consensus and no vote can raise it; CAKE's **400M** cap is a governance promise sitting on top of an uncapped mint function, and the same governance that cut it from 450M could move it the other way. The framework treats it accordingly and awards no permanence to any row on cap grounds. Against fee-burning smart-contract platforms, CAKE's mechanism is more direct but narrower: a base-fee burn destroys the chain's own gas token on every block from every application, whereas PancakeSwap's burn depends on one venue's trading volume. When BNB Chain volume falls, CAKE's burn falls with it — and the **Jun 22 2026** treasury vote showed the other risk in the same design, because governance can redirect the revenue that funds the burn without touching the burn mechanism itself.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the effect of the treasury-optimisation vote that passed on **Jun 22 2026** with **99.57%** support, which redirected side-product fee streams to the treasury and left only AMM v2, v3 and Infinity feeding the burn. The chain already shows it: net destruction ran at **63,851 CAKE** a day before the vote and **47,836** after, which is why the forward column on the overview page reads **-1.29%** rather than **-1.43%**. Second, the weekly burn posts and the monthly report — the streak stands at **35** consecutive months of shrinking supply and a cumulative **57.2M CAKE** removed since **September 2023**, and the first month that breaks it will be visible immediately. Third, any governance proposal touching the burn share, the emission rate or the 400M ceiling, since all three are votes rather than code. Fourth, the two retired lock contracts: **19.29M CAKE** is still sitting in them, and a faster drain would push Sell #5 up. Fifth, BNB Chain trading volume, which is the single input the burn size depends on.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CAKE as **genuinely and durably deflationary**: **2.11M CAKE** reaching the market per 90 days against **6.70M** destroyed, a net of **-1.43%** realised and **-1.29%** projected, in line with our monitor's **-1.53%**. The structural mechanism is a revenue-funded buy-and-burn running against a small, fixed per-block emission, on a fair-launched token with no vesting, no premine and no investor cliff anywhere in its history. The key risk is that every part of that machine is governance-controlled rather than code-enforced — the burn share, the emission rate and the **400M** ceiling can all be changed by a vote, and one such vote in **June 2026** already slowed the burn by roughly a quarter. The ceiling is real enough in practice, with **66.5M CAKE** of room left under it, but it is a promise the community keeps rather than a rule the contract enforces.
