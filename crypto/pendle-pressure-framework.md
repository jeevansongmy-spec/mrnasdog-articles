Pendle Finance mints nothing, and PENDLE supply still grows. Over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** the PENDLE contract on Ethereum held total supply at **281,527,448.4585** to the wei, exactly as it did a year earlier — yet **1.79M PENDLE** reached the tradable market, **1.07M** of it escaping the retiring vePENDLE vote-lock and **0.72M** released from Pendle's own governance and team multisigs. Buy pressure is **zero**, because the fee-funded buyback bought **1.23M PENDLE** and handed every unit to the sPENDLE staking contract, which is counted inside the circulating float. On a base of **172.2M PENDLE** the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+1.04%** against our supply monitor's **+0.79%** — a gap of **0.25 percentage points**, so no monitor-gap flag ships. PENDLE is a token with no live issuance whose float still inflates from escrow.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 19 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **PENDLE at +1.04% net**: sell pressure of **1.79M PENDLE** against buy pressure of **zero**, on a circulating base of **172.2M PENDLE**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.79%** for the same window, a gap of **0.25 percentage points**, inside the half-point tolerance, so no flag is raised on this build. The two sides are not counting the same thing, and the agreement should not be read as corroboration: the monitor divides market value by price to infer a classified circulating figure, while the framework reads four wallet balances and sweeps their transfer logs. They land in the same place because that classified figure is, in practice, Pendle's total supply minus the vePENDLE contract and three project multisigs — a four-address identity that reproduced the published number to **0.01%** in January 2026 and to **0.42%** today. Pendle Finance is best labelled non-inflationary on paper and mildly inflationary on the float.

## Sell pressure: where new PENDLE comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0.60M PENDLE**, and the mechanism behind it is the most misread thing about this token. PENDLE's documented emission — a weekly incentive that decayed **1.1%** a week until April 2026 and then switched to a terminal **2%** a year — did not issue a single new coin in this window. The Ethereum contract reports the same total supply at both window ends, and at every sample point back a full year. The liquidity incentives that Pendle actually paid came instead out of the governance multisig, which released **200,000 PENDLE** on **Jun 12 2026** and **400,000** on **Jul 20 2026** and took in nothing. That purpose was proved downstream rather than assumed: an operations wallet moved **200,000** into Pendle's gauge controller — the only inbound that contract received in the entire window — and a second wallet fed Pendle's multi-token Merkle distributor in fifty- and hundred-thousand slices. This is emission, so it belongs in Sell #1; it is simply emission paid from reserve rather than from a mint.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**. Pendle's team, investor and advisor allocations completed vesting in **Sept 2024**, and the ecosystem vesting contract reads **0.00 PENDLE** at both ends of the window — spent, not paused. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0.12M PENDLE**: Pendle's team multisig sent **120,000 PENDLE** on **Jun 25 2026** through a single intermediate wallet and straight into an exchange hot wallet. That is a sale, not incentive spending, so the framework keeps it out of Sell #1 rather than blending the two. Sell #4, long-term locked, is **1.07M PENDLE** and it is the largest force on this page. The vePENDLE vote-escrow contract fell from **64,898,624.65** to **63,827,677.13**, and the log was swept rather than the endpoints alone: **262** outbound withdrawals totalling **1,070,947.52 PENDLE** against **zero** inbound transfers of any size. Pendle paused new vePENDLE locks on **Jan 29 2026**, so that contract can now only drain.

## Buy pressure: where new PENDLE goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **zero** — and this is the single call that decides the page, so it is worth stating precisely. The buyback itself is real, funded and completely visible. Pendle routes **80%** of Pendle V2 yield and swap fees into open-market PENDLE purchases, harvested fortnightly and executed in hourly time-weighted slices; over this window the buyback contract accumulated **1,225,112.52 PENDLE** and delivered every unit onward in seven batches, on **May 22**, **Jun 5**, **Jun 19**, **Jul 3**, **Jul 17**, **Jul 31** and **Aug 14 2026**, leaving the contract empty. What decides the row is the destination. Every batch went to the sPENDLE staking contract, and sPENDLE is counted as circulating: when sPENDLE absorbed roughly **33M PENDLE** between **Jan 20 2026** and **Apr 19 2026**, the published circulating figure moved from **169.7M** to **166.9M** — a fall of **2.8M**, not **33M**. Tokens bought out of the float and delivered back into the float change pockets; they do not shrink supply.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, checked in every shape a burn can take. Total supply never falls; the null address held **0.00 PENDLE** and the dead address held **0.10** at both window ends with neither moving; and the token's public burn function is gated by an on-chain switch that reads **false** today, so it reverts. Pendle Finance points its fee revenue at buybacks, not at destruction. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — none of the four wallets that gate the float received anything at all: the governance multisig took in nothing, the team multisig took in nothing, the Ecosystem Fund recorded no transfer of any size, and vePENDLE recorded no inbound. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, and it is the trap PENDLE sets. The sPENDLE contract grew **2,022,169.85 PENDLE** over the window, which reads like two million taken off the market. It is not: **1,225,112.52** of that growth is the Buy #1 buyback delivery, leaving roughly **0.80M** of genuine user staking, and sPENDLE sits inside the counted float regardless. It is not a long-term lock either — holders exit in **14** days, or instantly for a **5%** fee. The one lock that genuinely sits outside the float, vePENDLE, moved the other way.

## Foundation and overhang

Pendle's overhang is unusually legible, because the project publishes which wallets sit outside the float. The governance multisig at **0x8119EC16F0573B7dAc7C0CB94EB504FB32456ee1** holds **20,851,622 PENDLE** and is the reserve that funds liquidity incentives — it is also the address that controls the token's emission settings. The Ecosystem Fund at **0x399Be606db281a054E359Eb709df9F21E922eC9a** holds **16,120,440 PENDLE** and recorded not one transfer in ninety days, so it carries a value of zero this build; a wallet that has not moved in a year is not a source of supply. The team multisig at **0x918cF6b16D1426B5aA0Edf0492ced1Aa89f9659A** holds **7,838,190 PENDLE** after its **120,000** release. And the largest pool of all is not team-controlled: **63,827,677 PENDLE**, about **22.7%** of everything that exists, still sits inside the retiring vePENDLE contract, every unit of it owned by a user and every unit of it due to come free by **Jan 29 2028** at the latest.

There is a fifth overhang that no wallet holds, and it is the finding of this build. PENDLE's mint path was never removed — the token contract still carries a claim function that mints one weekly emission for every week that has elapsed, callable by a single designated multisig. That function has not been called since the week ending **Mar 25 2025**. Replaying the contract's own arithmetic across the **74** weeks since gives **8,953,713 PENDLE** of accrued, unclaimed emission — **3.18%** of total supply and **5.2%** of the tradable float — which can be created in one transaction with no vote, no notice and no timelock, and which grows by about **106,938** every week it stays unclaimed. The framework values it at zero because it has not fired in over a year, which is the correct treatment for a power that has never once been used. The rule for all five is the same: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, or if that claim function fires, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh at whatever size the chain shows.

## How PENDLE compares to other fee-funded DeFi governance tokens

PENDLE belongs to the class of DeFi governance tokens that route protocol revenue back into their own token, alongside the exchange tokens and lending tokens running quarterly buybacks. Within that class the decisive question is never the size of the buyback — it is where the bought tokens land. A protocol that buys its token and burns it removes float permanently and drives its net negative by force. A protocol that buys its token and parks it in a treasury outside the circulating count also removes float, though reversibly. Pendle Finance does neither: it buys aggressively and hands the tokens straight to stakers, in a wrapper that the market counts as circulating and that unwinds in a fortnight. The buyback is therefore a demand story and a yield story, and not a supply story at all, which is why a programme that bought **1.23M PENDLE** in ninety days leaves this page reading positive.

Against a vote-escrow peer, PENDLE is now the mirror image of what it used to be. A classic vote-escrow token grows its escrow year after year, quietly shrinking the tradable float as holders lock for longer terms; PENDLE ran that model until **Jan 29 2026** and then switched it off, replacing multi-year vePENDLE locks with liquid sPENDLE. The escrow can now only empty, so the mechanism that used to absorb float has become the mechanism that releases it — and at **1.07M** a quarter against **63.8M** still inside, that release is barely started. Against a hard-capped token, the contrast is subtler still. A hard cap is a promise the code cannot break. PENDLE's flat supply is not a cap at all: it is a mint that nobody has bothered to call, running at a terminal **2%** a year in the background, with the accrued total waiting. Those are very different guarantees wearing the same flat supply chart.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the emission claim. If Pendle's liquidity incentives multisig calls the claim function, **8.95M PENDLE** is minted at once and total supply rises for the first time since **Mar 25 2025** — the single largest supply event available to this token, and the only one that would break the flat-supply story outright. Second, the vePENDLE drain rate: **63.8M PENDLE** must all leave by **Jan 29 2028**, and the current **1.07M** a quarter is far below the pace that timetable implies, so any acceleration lands directly in Sell #4. Third, the governance multisig's release pattern — **200,000** in June and **400,000** in July is lumpy batch pre-funding, and a larger batch would move Sell #1 immediately. Fourth, the fortnightly buyback deliveries, which have run **132,000** to **240,000 PENDLE** per batch; they do not change this reading while they land in sPENDLE, but any governance decision to burn the bought tokens instead, or to hold them outside the float, would flip Buy #1 from zero to full value overnight. Fifth, the Ecosystem Fund at **16,120,440 PENDLE**, which has been perfectly still and whose first move would be its own headline.

## Summary

PENDLE has issued nothing for over a year — total supply is **281,527,448.4585** at both ends of the window and identical to the wei twelve months back — yet the Pressure Framework reads **+1.04%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026**, projected at **+1.04%** next quarter, because supply reaching the market has nothing to do with supply being created. **1.79M PENDLE** crossed into the tradable float from escrow: **1.07M** out of the retiring vePENDLE lock, **0.60M** of incentives paid from the governance reserve and **0.12M** sent by the team multisig to an exchange. Pendle Finance's fee-funded buyback bought **1.23M PENDLE** over the same period and offset none of it, because the sPENDLE contract it delivers to is counted as circulating supply. The key risk is the **8.95M PENDLE** of accrued but unclaimed emission sitting inside the token contract, mintable in one transaction; the ceiling on the other side is that Pendle has no burn in service and no cap in code, so this ledger can slow but has no mechanism to turn negative.
