Pendle does not mint. The PENDLE total supply on Ethereum has stood at **281,527,448** without moving for more than a year, so every "emission" the protocol pays is a release of reserve that already existed. Over the 90 days to **Aug 10 2026** the reserve and the retiring vePENDLE lock together put **2.37M PENDLE** onto the market, while the fee-funded buyback and net new sPENDLE staking took **3.49M** off it. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads net supply at **−0.65%** against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.09%**, a gap of **1.74 percentage points** that ships a data-conflict chip. PENDLE is a fixed-supply DeFi governance token whose float is being pulled into a liquid staking contract faster than the incentive reserve refills it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window to **Aug 10 2026**, the framework reads **PENDLE at −0.65% net** on both the trailing and the forward window, because all four live mechanisms are continuous rather than one-off. The supply monitor reads **+1.09%** for the same trailing window, so the gap is **1.74 percentage points** — well past the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, and the PENDLE overview therefore carries a monitor-gap chip. The disagreement is mechanical, not a mistake on either side: the monitor tracks how the market classifies supply, and it counts staked sPENDLE as circulating, so the **3.48M PENDLE** the staking contract absorbed in this window earns no credit there. Strip that out and the two readings describe the same chain: roughly **2.37M PENDLE** of reserve and expired-lock tokens genuinely reached the market, which is almost exactly the **1.86M** of circulating growth the monitor measured. PENDLE is best characterised as **a non-minting DeFi governance token whose tradable float is set by lock flows, not by issuance**.

## Sell pressure: where new PENDLE comes from

The first thing to say about PENDLE inflation is that there is no minting. Reading the PENDLE token contract on Ethereum at both ends of the window, and at points stretching back to February 2025, returns the same total supply every time: **281,527,448 PENDLE**. A full sweep of the chain's mint events over the 90 days found none. That means Pendle's published emission schedule — the 216,076-per-week base set in September 2024, decaying 1.1% each week until April 2026, then flipping to a terminal **2% per year** — describes how fast pre-minted reserve is handed out, not how fast new coins appear. It is a distribution rate, and the framework books it as sell pressure only because the reserve sits outside the market until it is paid.

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1.32M PENDLE** for the window. That is the amount the protocol's own reserve wallets actually sent out to fund liquidity incentives: 1.2M from the governance wallet in three transfers, and a further 120,000 from a second genesis reserve wallet. It lines up with the documented terminal rate — 2% a year on the 281.5M total works out to **1.39M** across 90 days, within 5% of what the chain shows — and with the protocol's published all-streams cap of 90,000 PENDLE a week. Where the schedule and the chain disagree, the framework ships what actually moved.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and permanently so. Team, investor and advisor allocations completed vesting in September 2024, and PENDLE now reads as fully unlocked with no cliff of any size left on the calendar. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero** — not because there is no reserve, but because the only reserve outflow in the window is the 1.32M already booked in Sell #1, and counting it twice would invent pressure that does not exist. Sell #4, long-term locked, is **1.05M PENDLE**: the old two-year vePENDLE lock stopped accepting new deposits in late January 2026 and is now draining, and 1.05M PENDLE was actually withdrawn from it during the window. The framework uses that realised figure rather than the expiry calendar, because expired vePENDLE only moves when a holder claims it — and most of it has not been claimed.

## Buy pressure: where new PENDLE goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **1.03M PENDLE**. Pendle routes **80%** of its V2 yield and swap fees into a buyback contract, which buys PENDLE on the open market in small hourly slices and then forwards the accumulated batch to stakers roughly every two weeks. The chain shows the whole loop: 448 hourly purchases in the window, six delivery batches between **May 22 2026** and **Jul 31 2026**, and a contract balance of exactly zero, because nothing is held back. Crucially the buyback does not burn — the PENDLE is paid to sPENDLE stakers, so it lands inside the staking contract rather than back on the market.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**. Pendle has no burn address and no base-fee burn, because the entire fee share that could fund one is already committed to the buyback. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** as well: no treasury purchasing separate from the fee-funded buyback was disclosed or visible on-chain, and the reserve wallets only sent PENDLE out this window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the largest line in the whole ledger at **2.46M PENDLE**. The sPENDLE staking contract, live since **Jan 20 2026**, took in 3.48M PENDLE net over the window; subtracting the 1.03M of buyback tokens already counted in Buy #1 leaves 2.46M of genuinely new staking. sPENDLE now holds **37.26M PENDLE**, about 13.2% of total supply, behind a 14-day exit — or an instant exit at a 5% penalty.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled or protocol-controlled pools are tracked on PENDLE, and the framework watches all of them. The Pendle governance wallet holds **20.85M PENDLE** and is the source of the incentive funding — it released 1.2M in the window. A second reserve wallet holds **16.12M PENDLE** and did not move a single token across the entire 90 days. A third holds **7.84M PENDLE** and released 120,000. The original ecosystem-fund contract holds nothing at all. Separately, the retiring vePENDLE lock still holds **63.9M PENDLE**, 22.7% of the whole supply — the single largest overhang on the token, and the one worth watching most closely, because every token in it is eventually claimable by its owner.

The buyback needs no overhang line, because it accumulates nothing: bought PENDLE passes straight through to stakers, and the buyback contract's balance is zero. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and if the vePENDLE balance starts falling faster than the roughly 1.05M per 90 days seen here, Sell #4 rises with it.

## How PENDLE compares to other fee-buyback DeFi tokens

PENDLE now belongs to the same structural family as the large exchange and DeFi tokens that spend fee revenue on buying their own token — but it sits at an unusual corner of it. Most of that family burns what it buys: the bought tokens go to a dead address and total supply falls, which shows up in every supply feed automatically. Pendle instead pays the bought PENDLE to stakers. Supply-wise that is weaker than a burn, because the tokens still exist and can be unstaked; float-wise it is close to equivalent for as long as stakers stay staked, and it has the advantage of paying a real yield rather than a theoretical one.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains, PENDLE looks unusually tight. There is no block reward, no validator issuance, no mint function to turn on — the 281.5M supply is what exists, and the only question is which bucket it sits in. Against hard-capped halving-model coins, PENDLE is less predictable, because its float depends on human behaviour: a wave of unstaking or a rush of vePENDLE claims can add supply to the market faster than any emission schedule ever would. And against vote-escrow peers still running multi-year locks, Pendle has deliberately traded lock depth for liquidity. The 14-day exit brings more capital in — the staking contract grew by 2.46M net in a single quarter — but it also means the 37.26M sitting there is far less sticky than a two-year lock would be. That trade-off is the central supply risk on PENDLE, and it is a governance choice, not an accident.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The next buyback batch lands around **Aug 14 2026**, and one roughly every two weeks after; batch size tracks protocol fee revenue directly, so a fall in Pendle's trading and yield fees shows up in Buy #1 within a fortnight. The vePENDLE balance is the second watch line: it stood at **63.9M PENDLE** on **Aug 10 2026**, and any acceleration in claims lifts Sell #4 above the current 1.05M per quarter. Third, the sPENDLE balance — **37.26M PENDLE** today — is the swing factor in the whole ledger; because exit takes only 14 days, a reversal there would flip the framework's reading from negative to positive faster than any other mechanism on the token. Fourth, the incentive rate itself: the terminal 2% a year has been live since April 2026, and any governance change to it moves Sell #1 directly. Fifth, the dormant **16.12M** reserve wallet, untouched for the entire window, remains the one large release that would arrive with no schedule and no warning.

## Summary

Pendle (PENDLE) is a non-minting DeFi governance token: total supply is fixed on-chain at **281,527,448** and has not changed in over a year, so its inflation is entirely a question of which pool the tokens sit in. In the 90 days to **Aug 10 2026** the incentive reserve released **1.32M PENDLE** and the retiring vePENDLE lock returned **1.05M**, while a fee-funded buyback bought **1.03M** and net new sPENDLE staking absorbed **2.46M** — a net of **−0.65%** of circulating supply on a base of about **172.08M**, against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.09%** that does not treat staked PENDLE as removed. The key risk is the softness of that removal: sPENDLE exits in 14 days, and the old lock still holds 22.7% of all PENDLE. The ceiling, on the other hand, is genuinely hard — with no mint function, PENDLE can never exceed 281.5M tokens.
