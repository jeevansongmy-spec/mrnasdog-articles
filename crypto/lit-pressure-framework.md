The LIT supply of Lighter is flat today: only **100,000 LIT** reached the market from a locked wallet in the last 90 days, against **0** taken out, a net change of **+0.04%** of the **250M** circulating LIT, and **0.00%** for the next 90 days. The fee buyback and the Jul 10 2026 burn of **15.64M LIT** both look big, but neither moved the circulating count. The real supply event is the team and investor unlock of **500M LIT**, which starts around **Dec 30 2026**, two days after our next window ends.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

LIT reads **+0.04%** over the last 90 days (Jul 1 to Sep 29 2026) and **0.00%** for the next 90 days. Our supply monitor reads **−0.06%** over the same 90 days, a gap of **0.10** percentage points, well inside our half-point tolerance, so no warning flag is shown. The two readings agree because both say the same thing: the number of LIT in the market has barely changed since launch. Lighter is a perpetual-futures exchange built as a zero-knowledge rollup on Ethereum, LIT has a total of **1B**, and three quarters of it is still held back. In one line: a flat supply today with a large, dated unlock waiting just past the window.

## Sell pressure: where new LIT comes from

Protocol inflation is **0**. LIT has no emission schedule: all 1B LIT were created in October 2025, and the total supply of the LIT contract on Ethereum read exactly 1B at both ends of the window. Lighter does pay stakers a 6% yearly reward, and **1,445,297 LIT** went out between Jul 13 and Sep 28 2026, about 17,800 LIT a day lately. But those rewards come from a wallet holding bought-back LIT, coins that were already part of the circulating count, so the staking reward adds nothing new to the market.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. The team holds **260M LIT** and investors hold **240M LIT**, all still locked. Their one-year lock runs from the token launch on Dec 30 2025, and the first monthly release, about **13.5M to 13.9M LIT**, lands around Dec 30 2026. Our next 90-day window closes on Dec 28 2026, so the release counts as 0 here, by two days.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks come to **100,000 LIT**. On Jul 3 2026 the Lighter ecosystem reserve sent 100,000 LIT to the wallet that paid stakers at the time. It is the only time in the window that coins crossed from a locked wallet into the market. The reserve has not paid anything out since Jul 10, so the forward figure is 0. Long-term locks and bankruptcy releases are **0**: there is no estate or court schedule releasing LIT.

## Buy pressure: where new LIT goes

The programmatic buyback books **0**, even though it is real. Lighter spends its trading fees on LIT every day, and since Jul 1 2026 it bought **2.39M LIT** for about **$7.2M**, an average of about $3.01 per LIT: roughly 922,000 LIT in July, 924,000 in August and 545,000 so far in September. All of it sits in the buyback account, which held **2.40M LIT** on Sep 29 2026. Those LIT were bought from holders who are already part of the circulating count, and the account itself is still inside that count, so the buyback only moves LIT from one pocket of the market to another until the bought LIT is destroyed.

The protocol fee burn also books **0**. On Jul 10 2026, **15,638,703 LIT** were sent to a burn address on Ethereum, and they are gone for good. But the burned LIT came from the ecosystem reserve, which was never part of the circulating count. On the same day, the same number of bought-back LIT left the exchange for a new project wallet, the one that now pays stakers. Lighter had said it might burn reserve LIT instead of the exact bought LIT, since the result is the same for total supply. For the market, it means the burn removed nothing that was trading. Foundation buying is **0**, and new long-term locks are **0**: about 113.85M LIT is staked, but stakers can leave after three days and staked LIT stays in the circulating count.

## Foundation and overhang

Four piles of LIT sit with the project and its backers. The largest is the team and investor allocation, **500M LIT**, which did not move at all this window and starts to release about 13.5M to 13.9M LIT a month from around Dec 30 2026, for three years. Next is the ecosystem reserve, **234.26M LIT**, down from almost 250M at the start of the window after the 100,000 LIT draw and the 15.64M LIT burn; it has no published release schedule and is meant for staking rewards, future points seasons and partners. Then there is the wallet holding the bought-back LIT from the first half of the year, **12.07M LIT**, which pays stakers about 18,000 LIT a day and has other payouts too, and the buyback account itself, **2.40M LIT**. If the reserve or the team and investor wallets fall between our checks, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh; if bought-back LIT reaches a burn address, it enters the buy side.

## How LIT compares to other exchange tokens

LIT is closest to the tokens of other perpetual-futures exchanges that use their fees to buy their own token back. The difference is where the bought coins end up. Some exchanges send bought tokens straight to a burn address, so every purchase takes coins out of the market at once. Lighter keeps the bought LIT in a project account and wallet, and its one burn so far used reserve coins instead. So in LIT's case the fee buyback changes who holds the coins, not how many are trading.

LIT also differs from staking chains that create new coins to pay stakers. Those chains grow their supply every day. LIT has no such emission: its staking reward is paid from coins the project already holds. Where LIT looks most like other young exchange tokens is the unlock: half of the supply belongs to the team and investors, on a one-year lock followed by three years of monthly releases. That is the usual shape for a token launched in late 2025, and it means the LIT supply story in 2027 will look very different from the flat reading today.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the burn of the third-quarter buyback. About 2.40M LIT sits in the buyback account, and Lighter says bought LIT is to be burned; after the Jun 30 quarter end the burn came about ten days later, so a similar step may come in October 2026. If Lighter burns the bought LIT itself, it counts as buy pressure; if it burns reserve LIT again, it counts as 0.

Second, the team and investor release around Dec 30 2026, about 13.5M to 13.9M LIT, just outside this window but the biggest supply event on the LIT calendar. Third, any new draw from the 234.26M LIT reserve for points seasons, partners or staking. Fourth, the staking wallet: at about 18,000 LIT a day, its 12.07M LIT lasts well into 2028, but a change to the 6% reward would change that pace.
