Over the last 90 days the four Kite distribution vaults released **161.0M KITE** into the tradable float — every unit of it a vesting release, against buy pressure of exactly **0**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads KITE at **+6.73%** net for the window and **+10.35%** forward; our independent supply monitor reads **+4.87%**, a gap of **1.86 percentage points**. Kite is hard-capped at 10 billion KITE and the token contract contains no mint function at all — so the vesting calendar, not the chain, is the entire inflation story, and the twelve-month team and investor cliff opens on **Nov 3 2026**, inside the next window.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 30 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads Kite at **+6.73%** net new supply — sell pressure of **161.0M KITE** against buy pressure of **0**, divided by a circulating supply of **2,391.2M KITE**. Our independent supply monitor reads **+4.87%** for the same window, a gap of **1.86 percentage points**, which is over tolerance and raises a data-conflict flag on the KITE overview. The deep walk found the reason and it is dated: a security incident on **Aug 6 2026** paused the original KITE contract, a one-for-one swap ran to **Aug 20 2026**, and the migrated Ethereum contract was only unpaused on **Aug 28 2026**. KITE was non-transferable for 22 of the window's 90 days, so part of what the Kite vaults released could not finish rotating into the measured float before the window closed. The framework books the outflow it can see on-chain; the monitor books the float. KITE in August 2026 is a **hard-capped chain with no mint and a heavy unlock calendar** — the cleanest possible supply contract wrapped around the least forgiving vesting schedule.

## Sell pressure: where new KITE comes from

Kite's protocol inflation is **0**, and it is zero in the strongest sense available on a blockchain. The full 10 billion KITE was created once, at token generation on **Nov 3 2025**, and the verified interface of the live KITE token contract has no mint entry point — not an owner-only one, not a paused one, not one behind a role. The contract is not a proxy either, so no upgrade can add one later, and ownership cannot even be renounced because that function is a disabled stub. Kite is a proof-of-stake layer-1, but validator, delegator and module rewards are paid out of the pre-minted ecosystem allocation inside the cap rather than by issuance, so staking on Kite moves KITE between holders without creating any.

That leaves vesting unlocks carrying the entire sell ledger at **161.0M KITE** for the window, and that figure is measured rather than billed. Kite's allocations sit in ordinary multisig vaults whose balances can be read at both ends of the window: the four distribution vaults held **3,195.99M KITE** on **Jun 1 2026** and hold **3,035.03M** today, a release of exactly **161.0M**. The published Kite release calendar billed about **327.8M** over the same three months, so the vaults paid **49%** of their entitlement. That gap is structural rather than temporary: the vaults are multisigs, not timelocks, so the vesting calendar is an entitlement the Kite team may draw, not a mechanism that forces tokens out. Since token generation the pool has paid about **165.0M** against roughly **642.6M** billed, which leaves an undrawn backlog near **477.6M KITE**.

The step change is **Nov 3 2026**. Kite's team allocation of **2.00B KITE** and investor allocation of **1.20B KITE** both released nothing at the token generation event and both sit behind a twelve-month cliff that opens on the anniversary, then runs as 37 monthly tranches to **Nov 3 2029**. That is a cliff which starts a stream rather than paying a lump, so the combined first tranche is about **86.5M KITE** and lands 65 days inside the forward window. The on-chain read agrees that nothing has moved yet: the KITE investor multisig holds exactly **1,200,000,000 KITE** at both ends of the window with no outgoing transfer at all. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks therefore come in at **0** — every identified Kite multisig reads identical at both window ends — and long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0** too, because KITE is a 2025-vintage token with no estate and no trustee attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new KITE goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **0**, and on Kite that is a structural statement rather than a quiet quarter. Programmatic buyback is **0**: the Kite design papers describe a commission taken from AI-service payments that can be swapped for KITE on the open market, but no buyback contract, destination wallet, dashboard or executed amount has ever been published. A single unverifiable description is not a measurement, so the row stays opaque at zero rather than being sized from an intention.

Protocol fee burn is **0**, and it was checked on both surfaces a burn can appear on. The unspendable dead address holds nothing, and total supply on the live KITE contract has not moved — it cannot move, because the interface contains no burn function of any kind. Gas consumed on Kite mainnet itself runs to a few coins a day against a supply measured in billions, which rounds to zero at the precision this ledger reports. Foundation buy is **0** with no disclosed open-market purchase; the Kite Foundation spent the window running a contract migration, not accumulating. New long-term lock is **0** as well: Kite specifies stake sizes for module owners, validators and delegators, and twenty wallets do hold exactly the **30,000,000 KITE** module-owner amount, but those balances are unchanged across the window rather than a lock created inside it.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on Kite is very large and, unusually, very legible. The KITE investor allocation sits in one multisig holding exactly **1,200,000,000 KITE**, untouched. Five further multisigs hold between **227.3M** and **800.0M KITE** each, and twenty module-operator wallets hold exactly **30,000,000** apiece, **600.0M** in total. On top of those sits the undrawn calendar backlog of roughly **477.6M KITE** the Kite vaults were billed for and never paid. Against a circulating supply of **2,391.2M**, about **7,608.8M KITE** remains outside the float. Every one of these wallets is read on-chain at each refresh, and the exchange custodial wallets that also appear in the KITE holder set are deliberately excluded because they belong to depositors, not to the project. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How KITE compares to other capped layer-1 chains

A hard cap means two very different things depending on when the coins are made. On a halving-model chain the cap is enforced by a block subsidy that shrinks on a published schedule, so the cap and the emission brake are the same mechanism and holders can see decades of issuance in advance. Kite is the other shape: the whole 10 billion KITE exists already, so the cap is a completed fact rather than a promise, and there is no emission curve to decay. That removes an entire class of risk — no governance vote can raise KITE issuance because there is no issuance to raise, and no upgrade can add a mint because the KITE contract is not upgradeable. It also removes the brake. On a halving chain time reduces new supply; on Kite time releases it.

Against uncapped continuous-emission layer-1s, Kite trades a small permanent tax for a large temporary one. A typical proof-of-stake chain issues low single-digit percentages a year forever; Kite issues nothing forever but is running at a **+6.73%** quarterly release rate today because only about a quarter of its supply is in the float. And against the exchange tokens that anchor the deflationary end of this framework, the contrast is total: those coins run quarterly burns funded by real revenue, so their buy ledger is the largest line on the page. Kite's buy ledger does not exist. There is no burn function to call and no buyback that has ever executed, which means nothing on the KITE page can offset the unlock calendar — only the Kite team choosing not to draw it, which is exactly what the 49% draw rate shows them doing so far.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The dated items are unusually clean. The Kite release calendar bills about **109.3M KITE** a month on **Sep 1 2026**, **Oct 1 2026** and **Nov 1 2026**, and the number that matters is what share of it the vaults actually pay — the draw rate has been about half, and a move toward full draw would push this page materially higher. The twelve-month team and investor cliff opens on **Nov 3 2026** with a first tranche of about **86.5M KITE**; if that first release instead arrives as a twelve-month catch-up near **800M**, the vesting model on this page is wrong and the next rebuild will say so loudly. Watch whether the migrated KITE contract stays unpaused, since the owner retains pause and blacklist powers and used the pause in August 2026. Watch for the first published buyback execution, because the commission-swap mechanism is the only thing in Kite's design that could ever put a number in the buy ledger. And watch the untouched Kite multisigs — the 1,200,000,000 investor vault above all — for a first outgoing transfer.

## Summary

Kite is a hard-capped AI-payments layer-1 whose 10 billion KITE were minted once and whose token contract has no mint function and no burn function, so supply cannot rise above the cap and cannot fall below it either. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+6.73%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 30 2026** — a measured **161.0M KITE** of vault release against a buy ledger of zero — and **+10.35%** forward as the **Nov 3 2026** team and investor cliff adds its first tranche on top of the drip. The key risk is that roughly **7,608.8M KITE** still sits outside a float of only **2,391.2M**, held in ordinary multisigs that can pay their undrawn backlog whenever they choose. The ceiling is the cap itself: 10 billion KITE, already created, with nothing on the buy side of the ledger to take any of it back.
