# KITE Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

KITE supply is growing and is projected to grow much faster. The Pressure Framework reads KITE at **+6.19%** over the last 90 days and **+42.43%** over the next 90, against **+2.07%** on the monitor. Kite mints no new KITE, so every point comes from coins that already exist leaving locked wallets: **147.97M KITE** paid out by Foundation ecosystem wallets so far, and a one-year team and investor lock that ends on **Nov 3 2026** and frees **800M KITE** at once. The ceiling is a fixed **10,000M KITE**, and nothing is burned or bought back.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **2,391.2M KITE**, the framework books **147.97M KITE** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days, a net of **+6.19%**, and **1,014.64M KITE** of sell pressure over the next 90 days, a net of **+42.43%**. The inflation monitor reads **+2.07%** for the trailing window, a gap of **4.12 percentage points**, which is over the 0.5-point line and so ships with a monitor-gap warning on the overview. The gap has a clear cause. The monitor follows a circulating count that the Kite team files by hand; it stepped up once, on **Jul 1 2026**, and has not moved since, while the Foundation wallets kept paying out into an exchange. The label for KITE is **a capped token whose biggest unlock is still ahead**.

## Sell pressure: where new KITE comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**. KITE has a hard ceiling of **10,000M** coins, all created at launch in Oct 2025. The token lives on Ethereum, BNB Chain, Avalanche and HyperEVM, joined by a bridge that removes coins on one chain and adds them on another, and the four chains added together read exactly **10,000M KITE** at the end of the window. Staking rewards on Kite are paid out of the existing ecosystem pool, not from new coins. One event needs explaining: on **Aug 6 2026** the Kite Foundation caught unusual transfers on Ethereum, paused the token and the bridge, and moved every holder to a new contract one for one. The old contract is frozen. That swap added no KITE. Because the contract owner can still change the bridge settings, this row is watched rather than closed for good.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is the whole story. In the trailing window it is **147.97M KITE**. Four Foundation ecosystem wallets paid out that amount between **Jun 25** and **Aug 5 2026**, in small batches of under 4M each, and every batch ended at one exchange wallet. The balances of those four wallets were read at both ends of the window and their transfer records add up to the same fall. The published calendar allowed about **327.8M KITE** of ecosystem and module coins over the same 90 days, so the Foundation paid out less than half of what it could. The module wallets paid out nothing at all. Payouts stopped at the Aug 6 pause and had not restarted by **Sep 23 2026**.

The next 90 days are different. The team holds **2,000M KITE** and investors **1,200M KITE**, both under a one-year lock followed by a four-year unlock. The lock ends on **Nov 3 2026**, one year after launch, and releases a quarter of both at once: **500M** team and **300M** investor KITE, **800M KITE** in total. The rest then releases month by month over 36 months, about **66.67M KITE** a month, starting **Dec 3 2026**. Both wallets are readable: the investor wallet holds exactly 1,200M KITE and the team wallet group exactly 2,000M, and neither has moved a coin since Oct 2025. Add the ecosystem payouts at their trailing pace and the next-90-day figure is **1,014.64M KITE**.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**: no locked wallet released anything outside the payouts above. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**, because KITE has no bankruptcy estate, trustee or court-ordered payout.

## Buy pressure: where new KITE goes

Nowhere. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. Kite describes a future where a cut of AI-service fees is swapped into KITE on the open market, but no buyback contract, wallet or purchase has been published or seen on chain. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**: KITE has no burn. Both places a burn could show were checked at both ends of the window. The dead address held nothing on Ethereum and only dust on BNB Chain, and total supply did not fall below the cap.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**. The Kite Foundation bought no KITE, and no wallet it controls grew across the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**. The Aug 2026 contract swap copied balances one for one and locked nothing away, and no staking contract holds a material amount of KITE.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on KITE is very large, and almost all of it sits in wallets that can be read on chain. The four ecosystem wallets and one airdrop wallet hold **3,262.4M KITE** together, down from **3,410.3M** at the start of the window. A group of 28 wallets holds **1,997.8M KITE** and has not moved since Oct 2025. A group of 61 team wallets holds **2,000.0M KITE**, the investor wallet holds **1,200.0M KITE**, and one unlabelled wallet holds **168.2M KITE**. Together these wallets hold far more than the circulating float.

What makes this overhang unusual is how little of it follows a fixed rule. These are ordinary multi-signature wallets, not lock contracts, so the calendar says what may be released while the Foundation decides what actually is. So far it has released less than the calendar allows. Every wallet is read at each rebuild. If any of their balances falls between rebuilds by more than the payouts already counted, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How KITE compares to other capped launch-stage tokens

KITE belongs to a common class: a young token with a fixed supply, most of it locked at launch, released on a calendar to the team, investors and an ecosystem fund. That is very different from a proof-of-work coin like Bitcoin, which mints new coins with every block on a fixed halving schedule, and from staking-reward chains, which add a few percent of new supply each year to pay validators. KITE mints nothing. On the pure issuance test it is stricter than both.

And yet KITE reads **+42.43%** for the next 90 days, far above any mature capped chain. A hard cap limits total supply, not tradable supply. With only about a quarter of the cap circulating, each unlock is large next to the float. The contrast with a continuous vest is useful too. A token that unlocks in a smooth straight line spreads the same amount over every day; KITE's team and investor schedule puts a full year of accrued release on a single date, **Nov 3 2026**, and then settles into monthly steps.

The last comparison is with exchange tokens that buy back and burn part of their revenue each quarter, which can push their net reading below zero. Kite has the idea of a fee swap in its design papers, but nothing has run. Until it does, and at a size that matters against 800M-coin unlocks, the buy side of the KITE ledger stays at zero.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, **Nov 3 2026**: the end of the one-year team and investor lock, when **800M KITE** can leave the investor wallet and the team wallet group; whether it all moves that day, or sits in the wallets like the module coins have, is the single biggest question for this page. Second, **Dec 3 2026**, the first monthly team and investor release of about **66.67M KITE**. Third, the ecosystem calendar steps on **Oct 1**, **Nov 1** and **Dec 1 2026**, and whether Foundation payouts to the exchange restart after the pause. Fourth, the bridge and contract owner settings after the Aug 2026 incident, since a bridge change is the only way new KITE could appear. Fifth, any launch of the fee swap into KITE, which would be the first real buy pressure.
