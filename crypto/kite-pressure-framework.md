Kite grew its tradable KITE float by **+6.73%** over the last 90 days and the Pressure Framework projects **+10.35%** for the next 90. The Kite protocol mints nothing at all — KITE is hard-capped at **10B** and every unit was created at genesis — so all of that growth is vesting: four project vaults on Ethereum released **161.0M KITE** into the **2.39B** float last quarter, against a buy ledger of exactly **0**. Our monitor reads **+4.78%** for the same window, a **1.95 percentage point** gap, so a monitor-gap flag ships. The step change is **Nov 3 2026**, when the twelve-month cliff on the Kite team and investor allocations opens 13 days inside the next window.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026** the Pressure Framework reads KITE at **+6.73%** net supply growth and projects **+10.35%** for the next 90 days. Our monitor reads **+4.78%** for the historical window, a gap of **1.95 percentage points**, which is over tolerance and triggers the flag. The disagreement is a timing artefact rather than a factual one. The classified float figure for KITE moves in catch-up steps: it sat pinned at **1.80B** from **Apr 21 2026** to **May 6 2026** and then jumped **472.6M** in a single day on **May 7 2026**, even though the Kite unlock calendar had been paying monthly since **Feb 1 2026**. The framework reads the vaults instead, and the vaults are unambiguous. KITE is best labelled a **hard-capped, zero-issuance token whose entire supply story is the unlock calendar** — a coin where nothing is created and everything is released.

## Sell pressure: where new KITE comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and it is zero by construction. Kite created all **10B KITE** at genesis under a hard cap, and the Kite chain has no block reward, no staking issuance and no fee-driven minting. The whitepaper's roughly **4%** staking yield is paid out of the fixed ecosystem allocation rather than newly created, so it surfaces as a vesting release and is already counted one row down. Gas on the Kite network is charged in stablecoins, which closes the last route by which network activity could create KITE. The same address exists on three chains, but that is a burn-and-mint bridge, not additive supply: the three balances sum to the **10B** cap, and Ethereum carries **9.76B** of it including every locked allocation.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is the whole ledger, and it reads **161.0M KITE** for the last 90 days and **247.5M KITE** for the next. We measure what actually left rather than what the Kite calendar bills, because the allocations sit in readable multisig vaults on Ethereum. Four live distribution vaults held **3,196.0M KITE** on **May 20 2026** and **3,035.0M KITE** on **Aug 18 2026**, so **161.0M** reached the market. The published schedule for the same window was **335.2M** — the ecosystem stream at **65.2M** a month plus the module stream at **46.5M** a month — so under half of the entitlement was actually drawn. That distinction matters more on Kite than on almost any token we track: booking the calendar would have doubled the row. We also swept every one of the **148,496** KITE transfers in the window to be sure no vault had round-tripped a large flow back to a flat balance, and none had. The forward column carries the same realised drip and adds the cliff.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: no release outside the scheduled vaults was observed anywhere in the sweep. Sell #4 is **zero** as well — there is no Kite estate, no trustee and no court-supervised seller, so the long-dated insider locks belong to the vesting row and the overhang below.

## Buy pressure: where new KITE goes

Every buy row on Kite reads **zero**, and that is the second defining fact of this page. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. The Kite tokenomics page describes the protocol taking a small commission on AI-service payments and swapping that stablecoin revenue for KITE on the open market, which would be a genuine buyback if it were running. It is not: there is no buyback contract, no published dashboard, no disclosed destination wallet, and the 90-day transfer sweep shows no address accumulating KITE from the market other than exchange wallets. An announced design is not a realised flow, so the row stays at zero and the destination is recorded as unstated.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and it is zero by design. Kite charges gas in stablecoins rather than in KITE, which means network usage never destroys the token; the burn address received nothing across the window. The small amount routed to the zero address on Ethereum is the cross-chain bridge burning before re-minting the identical coins on another chain, not destruction. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** — the Kite Foundation is a distributor of supply, not a purchaser. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: no lock contract absorbed KITE on Ethereum in the window, validator and module staking runs on the Kite chain where almost nothing has been bridged, and no lock-up programme with a stated size was announced.

## Foundation and overhang

About **7.61B KITE**, roughly **76%** of everything that exists, sits outside the tradable float. The four live distribution vaults hold **3,035.0M** between them and pay out slowly and irregularly. Four further vaults have never moved a single token: one holding **1,200.0M**, one holding **800.0M** and two holding **500.0M** each, together covering the Kite team allocation of **2.0B** and the investor allocation of **1.2B** that begin unlocking on **Nov 3 2026**. A third overhang is quieter and specific to Kite: roughly **573.7M KITE** has already vested on paper since the **Nov 3 2025** launch but was never drawn from the vaults, a backlog that carries no schedule at all and can be released whenever the vault signers choose. A fourth is interpretive — one reading of the cliff terms would free about **800M** at once on **Nov 3 2026** rather than a monthly tranche, and because the project has never stated it we watch it instead of booking it. Each of these is watched, not projected: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How KITE compares to other hard-capped Layer-1 chains

A hard cap means something very different on Kite than it does on a proof-of-work chain. Bitcoin's **21M** cap sits at the end of an emission schedule: coins are still being created, just at a known and halving rate, and the float grows because the protocol mints. Kite's **10B** cap sits at the beginning: every KITE already exists, the protocol mints nothing, and the float grows only because locks open. That inverts where the risk lives. On a halving-model chain the dilution rate is protocol-encoded and cannot be changed by anyone; on Kite the dilution rate is a multisig decision, which is why the realised **161.0M** and the scheduled **335.2M** can differ by a factor of two in a single quarter.

Against uncapped continuous-emission Layer-1s, Kite looks better on paper and worse in practice. Those chains typically run **3%** to **7%** annual issuance against a mostly-distributed float, and their fee burns give back a meaningful fraction. Kite runs **0%** issuance but **+10.35%** quarterly unlock growth against a float that is only **23.9%** of supply, and it has no burn and no buyback to give anything back. The comparison that fits best is the cohort of young AI-sector tokens that launched in late 2025 with a small float and a large locked majority: for those the emission line is never the story and the calendar always is. Kite follows that pattern exactly, with one distinguishing feature in its favour — its vaults are readable on Ethereum, so the gap between what is billed and what is paid is measurable rather than assumed.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, **Nov 3 2026** — the Kite twelve-month cliff, when the team and investor allocations begin unlocking together. On the structured schedule that is a first monthly tranche of about **86.5M KITE** starting a stream to **Nov 2029**; on the alternative reading it is a single release near **800M**. Which of the two lands is the largest open question on this token, and it resolves 13 days inside the forward window. Second, the monthly ecosystem and module unlocks on **Sep 1 2026**, **Oct 1 2026** and **Nov 1 2026**, each billing about **111.7M KITE** — the number to watch is not the billing but how much of it the vaults actually pay. Third, the undrawn backlog of about **573.7M KITE**: if the vaults start clearing it, the realised rate jumps toward the calendar without any schedule changing. Fourth, any first evidence that the module commission buyback has executed — a disclosed wallet or a confirmed purchase would move Buy #1 off zero for the first time in KITE's history. Fifth, Kite network activity itself, which is the only thing that could eventually make that buyback large enough to matter.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Kite as clearly inflationary and accelerating: **+6.73%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+10.35%** projected for the next, entirely from vesting, against a buy ledger of exactly **0**. The structural mechanism is unusual — KITE is hard-capped at **10B** and the protocol mints nothing, so dilution is not issuance but the opening of multisig vaults, which released **161.0M KITE** of a billed **335.2M** last quarter. The key risk is the **Nov 3 2026** team and investor cliff landing inside the next window on a float of only **2.39B**, with roughly **7.61B KITE** still locked behind it. The cap is real and it is the ceiling: no KITE will ever be created beyond **10B**, but the distance between today's float and that ceiling is the entire dilution ahead.
