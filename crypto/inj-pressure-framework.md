> Originally published at **[mrnasdog.com/research/inj/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/inj/inflation)** by MrNasdog.

Injective markets a burn, and the burn is real — but it is much smaller than the mint. Staking rewards created about **1.10M INJ** over the last 90 days at the protocol's **4.4%** maximum rate, while the monthly Community BuyBack removed only about **0.14M INJ**, roughly an eighth as much. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.96% net** — mildly inflationary, not deflationary. Our supply monitor reads **+0.20%**, a gap of **0.76 percentage points**, because the market-data supply feed it uses is frozen near **100M INJ** while the chain has actually minted to about **121.9M**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 6 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **INJ at +0.96% net** for both the trailing and forward windows — the staking mint of **1.10M INJ** outweighs the **0.14M INJ** Community BuyBack burn by about eight to one, against a circulating base of **100M INJ**. Our supply monitor reads only **+0.20%** for the trailing window, a gap of **0.76 percentage points** that lands outside the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so a **monitor gap** chip ships on the INJ overview. The gap is not a disagreement about mechanism — it is a frozen data feed: the monitor derives supply from market cap over price, and that figure has been pinned near **100M INJ** across every daily snapshot in the window, even though the chain minted staking rewards at its 4.4% ceiling the whole time. Reading Injective's own nodes, total supply is about **121.9M INJ** and still rising. INJ is best characterised as **a mildly inflationary uncapped proof-of-stake chain whose real burn is too small to offset its staking mint** — the opposite of the "deflationary by design" label the buyback branding invites.

## Sell pressure: where new INJ comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is about **1.10M INJ**, and it is the entire supply-creation story. Injective's mint module targets a bonded ratio of **60%**: when less INJ than that is staked, the inflation rate climbs toward its maximum; when more is staked, it falls toward its **2.2%** floor. With roughly **47%** of INJ bonded — below the target — the rate has been pinned at the **4.4%** ceiling and cannot go higher. The detail most trackers miss is the block rate. The mint pays a fixed slice of new INJ every block against an assumed pace of about **63.07M** blocks a year, but the chain actually produces around **52.4M** blocks a year — a slower cadence near **0.6 seconds** a block — so realised issuance lands near **3.65%** a year rather than the nominal 4.4%. That works out to about **1.10M INJ** created over the 90-day window, and the observed on-chain supply change over the period confirms the smaller figure. A read that assumes the faster parameter pace over-books the mint by about 20%.

The remaining sell rows are all **zero**, and for structural reasons. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because Injective's unlock calendar is exhausted: INJ launched in **2020**, and its genesis allocations — ecosystem, team, private and seed sale, community and advisors — vested out on a cliff schedule that finished on **Jan 21 2024**. The token is 100% unlocked, so no cliff or linear release remains. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because nothing was released — the tracked overhangs, the on-chain community pool of about **26.5K INJ** and the Injective Foundation and ecosystem treasury, showed no outflow event this window. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero: there is no Injective estate, no trustee and no court-ordered INJ distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new INJ goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is about **0.14M INJ**, and it is the mechanism Injective is best known for. A slice of protocol and dApp revenue funds a monthly Community BuyBack — successor to the older weekly burn auction — that purchases INJ on the open market on a 28-day cycle and permanently burns it. The rounds inside this window were about **$315,000** on **Jun 3 2026**, the largest to date, and about **$246,000** on **Jul 1 2026**; converted at prevailing prices and cross-checked against reported per-round burns, they removed roughly **0.14M INJ** between them. The tokens are destroyed rather than parked in a wallet, so there is no accumulation overhang to track. The decisive fact is scale: this real burn is about **an eighth** of the 1.10M INJ the mint added, so it softens the inflation but nowhere near cancels it.

The other three buy rows are **zero**. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero because Injective has no separate EIP-1559-style base-fee destruction — its exchange and trading-fee revenue is precisely what funds the Community BuyBack above, so counting a fee burn here would double-count the same INJ. Buy #3, foundation buy, is zero because no Injective Foundation open-market purchase distinct from the revenue-funded buyback was disclosed this window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero because staking is not a lock: bonded INJ can be withdrawn after a **21-day** unbonding period and stays inside the circulating figure the entire time.

## Foundation and overhang

Two team-controlled overhangs sit behind INJ. The first is the on-chain **community pool**, which the chain publishes directly: it holds about **26.5K INJ** and can be drawn down only by a passing governance proposal. No such INJ spend passed this window, so the pool released no INJ to the market. The second is the **Injective Foundation and ecosystem treasury**, which stewards grants and ecosystem funding; its INJ is fully vested but held in wallets whose exact token count is not published, so it is tracked as an opaque position. It is also the reason the framework keeps the market's **100M** circulating figure rather than the full **121.9M** on-chain supply as its denominator — the presence of a foundation reserve is what disqualifies the frozen-feed escape hatch. Both overhangs are re-read on every rebuild — the community pool by on-chain read, the Foundation through its disclosures. If either balance falls sharply between refreshes through a single large distribution, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How INJ compares to other proof-of-stake L1s

The first comparison is cap versus control loop. Halving-model chains with a hard ceiling have a sell side that shrinks on a published schedule; INJ has neither a fixed cap nor a schedule. Its issuance is a control loop tied to how much INJ is bonded, and because bonding sits near **47%**, well under the 60% target, the loop has parked at its 4.4% maximum. Like the uncapped Cosmos-family chains it descends from, INJ's inflation does not decay with time — it decays only if staking participation rises.

The second comparison is burn versus no burn, and this is where INJ stands apart from a plain staking chain. Unlike a network that returns every fee to stakers — as a chain like Cosmos Hub does, giving it a buy side of exactly zero and a net reading equal to its full mint — Injective routes protocol revenue into a buy-and-burn, so activity genuinely removes supply. That makes INJ the more disciplined design of the two. The catch is magnitude: the burn is real but it is roughly an eighth of the mint, so it does not flip the sign. That places INJ between the pure-mint proof-of-stake chains, which have nothing on the buy side, and the exchange tokens whose quarterly buybacks are large enough to print net-negative supply. INJ has the mechanism of the second group at the scale of the first — the mechanism is deflationary in direction, but not in magnitude right now.

The third comparison is what the market data shows. Many tokens carry heavy non-circulating buckets whose release calendar is the main risk; INJ's risk is subtler. Its published circulating figure has frozen near its old 100M cap while the chain quietly minted past **121.9M**, so an observer trusting the headline supply would read INJ as flat when it is in fact growing. The framework reads the chain, not the frozen feed, which is the whole reason the two numbers disagree by more than half a point.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the monthly Community BuyBack rounds, the next of which is expected around **Sep 1 2026**: their size scales with protocol revenue, and a run of larger rounds is the only near-term path to flipping INJ net-deflationary. Second, the bonded-stake ratio near **47%** — it holds inflation pinned at the 4.4% cap, and only a sustained move past the **60%** target would let the mint rate start falling toward the 2.2% floor. Third, the block rate near **0.6 seconds** a block, because realised issuance scales with how many blocks the chain produces, so a faster or slower chain moves the true inflation number even with the rate parameter unchanged. Fourth, whether the widely-quoted circulating figure un-freezes from **100M**; the day the market data catches up to the real **121.9M**, the monitor gap on this page collapses on its own. Fifth, any governance change to the dynamic inflation band or a new supply mechanism, and the community pool near **26.5K INJ** for any large INJ-denominated distribution — the first such spend proposal is what would put a non-zero number in Sell #3.

## Summary

Injective (INJ) is mildly inflationary, not deflationary, with a real but undersized burn. Its staking mint is pinned at the **4.4%** ceiling because bonding sits below the **60%** target, and because blocks arrive slower than the mint parameter assumes, realised issuance runs near **3.65%** a year — about **1.10M INJ** over 90 days. The monthly Community BuyBack burns roughly **0.14M INJ**, about an eighth of the mint, so the buy side trims but does not cancel new supply. The framework reads **+0.96% net**; our monitor reads **+0.20%** because its supply feed is frozen near the old 100M genesis figure while the chain has minted to about **121.9M**, a gap of **0.76 percentage points** that ships a data-conflict chip. The paths to lower inflation are a rise in bonding past target or a larger revenue-funded burn — neither of which has happened yet.
