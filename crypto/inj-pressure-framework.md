# INJ Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

INJ supply is growing, not shrinking. Over the last 90 days Injective paid **1.06M INJ** of new coins to stakers, while the monthly Community BuyBack burned **96.1K INJ**. Net, supply rose **+0.97%** on the 100M-coin market count, and the next 90 days project about **+1.01%**. The burn is real, but it is about a tenth of the new issuance.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The Pressure Framework reads INJ at **+0.97%** net over the last 90 days (Jul 1 to Sep 29 2026) and **+1.01%** for the next 90 days. The monitor reads **−0.09%**, a gap of **1.06 percentage points**, so the page carries a ⚠ monitor-gap chip. The walk found why: the circulating count the monitor follows has sat near 100M INJ every day since Nov 11 2025, while the Injective chain kept minting new INJ every block and burning some each month. That fixed count cannot move, so its reading is day-to-day noise. Our number stays. In one line: INJ is a staking coin whose monthly burn slows its inflation but does not reverse it.

## Sell pressure: where new INJ comes from

Protocol inflation is the whole sell side. Injective runs a Cosmos-style mint module: every block creates new INJ and pays it to validators and stakers, with 5% going to the community pool. Governance set the yearly rate between **2.2%** and **4.4%** in the INJ Supply Squeeze proposal, passed on Jan 19 2026. Only about **47%** of INJ is staked, below the 60% goal, so the rate sits at the **4.4%** ceiling. Today that is about **0.086 INJ** per block.

Two details change the real number. First, the mint is paid per block and assumes blocks every 0.5 seconds, but Injective blocks came every **0.61 seconds** over the window, so the real yearly rate is closer to **3.6%**. Second, the rate is paid on all INJ on the chain, including INJ bridged in from Ethereum. About **12.06M INJ** crossed that bridge in late July 2026, which raised the base the mint is paid on. Across 12.72M blocks, staking issuance came to **1.06M INJ**. On today's larger base, the next 90 days project about **1.10M INJ**.

Vesting unlocks are zero: the original 100M INJ finished vesting on Jan 20 2024, and no team or investor tranche is left. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero: no Foundation wallet is publicly labelled, and community pool grants pay out coins the mint already created. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is zero: there is no estate or trustee releasing INJ.

## Buy pressure: where new INJ goes

The Community BuyBack is the only buyer. Once every 28 days, holders commit INJ to a buyback round; when it closes, the committed INJ is burned by the auction module and the committers share the fees Injective apps collected. Three rounds closed in the window and burned **96.1K INJ**: 43.5K on Jul 8, 27.4K on Aug 5 and 25.2K on Sep 2 2026. Round sizes follow the dollar value of the fees, so they shrank as fees fell.

The next 90 days hold four rounds. The Sep 30 2026 round already holds **29.1K INJ**. The Oct 28, Nov 25 and Dec 23 rounds are estimated at about 20.7K INJ each at today's price, for a forward burn of **91.2K INJ**. There is no separate fee burn: network fees go to validators. There is no Foundation buy, and staking is not a long-term lock, because staked INJ still counts as circulating.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is small and mostly structural. The community pool held **12,622 INJ** at this check and paid 24,885 INJ in liquidity rewards on Sep 15 2026; it refills from the mint, so its payouts add nothing new. The buyback contract held about 43.4K INJ of holders' commitments, which are burned or returned at round end. The Injective Foundation received the 36.3% ecosystem allocation at launch, but that allocation vested long ago and no Foundation wallet is labelled, so it cannot be tracked as a unit. A listed company holds INJ as a treasury asset, bought on the open market. We check these balances every rebuild; if any of them falls between checks, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How INJ compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1s

INJ sits between two groups. Like other Cosmos-based chains, it pays stakers with a floating inflation rate tied to how much is staked, and it has no supply cap. Unlike most of them, it pairs that issuance with a recurring burn funded by app fees. Governance has cut the ceiling from 10% in April 2024 to 4.4% today, which makes INJ one of the lower-issuance chains in that family.

Compared with Ethereum, the shape is similar: new coins go to validators and part of the fees disappear. The difference is the size of the burn. At current activity the INJ buyback removes about 9% of what the mint creates, so INJ, like Ethereum today, is mildly inflationary. Compared with exchange tokens that run large buybacks from company profits, the INJ burn depends on on-chain fees, which fell over the past year.

The project describes INJ as deflationary. On the chain, that holds only if the monthly burns grow to match roughly 1.1M INJ of new coins each quarter, about ten times today's pace.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The Sep 30 2026 buyback round, which will burn about 29.1K INJ. The Oct 28, Nov 25 and Dec 23 2026 rounds, whose size depends on Injective fee income and the INJ price. The staking ratio: if it climbed past 60%, the inflation rate would start falling toward the 2.2% floor. Block speed after the Sep 24 2026 upgrade, which has run a little faster and would lift issuance slightly if it holds. Any new governance vote on the mint or the buyback.

## Summary

INJ supply grew **+0.97%** over the last 90 days and projects **+1.01%** over the next 90. Staking issuance of about 1.06M INJ a quarter outweighs a monthly Community BuyBack burn of about 96.1K INJ. The key risk is that the burn keeps shrinking with fees, while the mint grows with the base. There is no supply cap; the 4.4% ceiling and the slow blocks keep real issuance near 3.6% a year.
