# STX Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

*Originally published at [mrnasdog.com/research/stx/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/stx/inflation). MrNasDog Pressure Framework · Inflation Analysis.*

<!-- main-page -->
This is the long supply read. For the signal, the price drivers and the questions people ask about Stacks, see [mrnasdog.com/research/stx](https://mrnasdog.com/research/stx).

STX supply is growing and is set to keep growing: Stacks mints new coins for every Bitcoin block and burns none. Between Jul 29 and Oct 27 2026 miners received **13.06M STX** and the ecosystem Endowment received **10.69M STX**, while buybacks and burns removed **0**, so supply rose **+1.26%** in 90 days and is on track for about **+1.28%** in the next 90. Stacks has no maximum supply, and other trackers show **+1.48%** for the same stretch.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

STX added **23.75M** new coins in 90 days against about **1.88B** in circulation, a net rise of **+1.26%**. Other trackers read **+1.48%** for the same 90 days, a difference of **0.21 points**, which is small enough that the coin page carries no warning note. At today's pace the next 90 days add about **24.04M STX**, or **+1.28%**, slightly more than the last 90 because both mints only reached their current rates on Jul 30 2026. The plain label for STX is a two-mint chain with no burn: steady, built-in inflation of about 5% a year, with nothing on the other side of the ledger.

## Sell pressure: where new STX comes from

The first source is the miner reward. Stacks miners spend bitcoin to win the right to produce blocks, and each Bitcoin block pays them brand-new STX. A rule change in April 2026 had cut that pay from 1,000 to 500 STX a block, but the PoX-5 upgrade on Jul 30 2026 put it back to **1,000 STX** and removed the planned later cuts. Over the 90 days miners received **13.06M STX**, and at the current pace of about 147,000 STX a day the next 90 days pay them about **13.20M STX**. When a Bitcoin block passes with no Stacks miner, its reward rolls into the next one, so the pay follows Bitcoin's clock, not Stacks'.

The second source is the Endowment mint, approved by holders in 2025. At the start of every miner tenure, Stacks mints a fixed amount for the ecosystem Endowment into a dedicated contract. That amount stepped up from 475 to **1,140 STX** per tenure on Jul 30 2026, and it added **10.69M STX** in the 90 days. At about 120,000 STX a day, the next 90 days add about **10.83M STX**. The schedule steps again, to 1,705 STX per tenure, around mid-2027, and the whole program ends about five years after it began.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. Every STX already counts as circulating, including the 100M STX the Endowment receives in monthly slices over 24 months, so each slice is a coin that was already counted. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are also **0** for the same reason: the Endowment's wallets are part of the circulating count, and moving coins out of them creates nothing new. There is no bankruptcy estate or court-held pile of STX, so the long-term locked row is **0** as well.

## Buy pressure: where new STX goes

No programmatic buyback exists, so that row is **0**. The Endowment's founding plan says a burn program would only be put to a community vote if its liquid assets pass $1B, and nothing close to that has been announced. The fee burn is **0** because Stacks burns no fees: users pay fees to miners, and the known burn address gained less than one STX in 90 days.

Foundation buying is **0**; neither the Stacks Foundation nor the Endowment announced any STX purchase in the window. New long-term locks are **0** too. Staking is popular and growing: about **493M STX** is staked to earn bitcoin, up from 392M right after the July upgrade. But staked STX still counts as circulating and returns to holders at the end of each cycle, so it removes nothing from the supply count.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest pool the ecosystem controls is the Endowment's mint contract, which held about **66.24M STX** on Oct 27 2026. The Endowment did not claim from it during the 90 days, so the balance simply grew with each tenure. Its receiving wallet held about **6.86M STX**, down from 45.94M on Jul 29 2026, after it sent **39.08M STX** to a second Endowment wallet on Sep 24 2026. That second wallet held about **12.24M STX** at the check and has been paying out a few million STX at a time in late October. Part of those payouts reached a wallet that now holds about **70.77M STX**, which together makes about **156.11M STX** held by the Endowment and the wallets it feeds.

All of these coins already count as circulating, so their moves add nothing to the 90-day number. They matter for a different reason: they are the coins most likely to reach the market. The Endowment has said it sells its locked portion through private deals with lockups of 12 to 36 months, not on the open market. We read these balances at every rebuild; if one falls sharply, the drop shows up in the Foundation row note at the next check.

## How STX compares to other Bitcoin layer-2 chains

Stacks borrows its rhythm from Bitcoin but not its supply rule. Bitcoin itself has a hard cap and halves its reward every four years. Stacks has no cap: it planned halvings, cut the reward once in April 2026, then reversed that cut in July 2026 to keep miners and stakers paid while Bitcoin staking starts. That makes STX an uncapped, continuous-emission coin, closer to a proof-of-stake chain than to Bitcoin.

Many other Bitcoin-linked networks have no mining reward at all and pay operators from fees or a fixed treasury, so their new supply comes from scheduled unlocks rather than steady mints. STX is the opposite: almost no vesting is left, and nearly all new supply is minted in small, predictable amounts every block. There is also no fee burn, which sets Stacks apart from chains that destroy part of each fee. The rewards stakers earn are paid in bitcoin, not in new STX, so the staking yield itself adds no STX supply.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Bitcoin Staking Bond 3 opens around Nov 10 2026, the first of the planned monthly rounds; it changes no supply rule, but it shows how much STX holders lock up next to bitcoin. Epoch 4.1 is planned for early December 2026, with its activation height to be announced 30 days ahead; it makes Clarity 7 the default for new contracts and has no announced change to either mint. Any PoX-6 proposal matters most, because the 1,000 STX miner reward is only provisional until that review sets a new level. The Endowment's wallets are the fourth line: a large move out of its contract or its main wallets, or its next quarterly report, would show how fast the new coins reach buyers.

## Summary

STX supply grew **+1.26%** in the 90 days to Oct 27 2026 and is projected to grow about **+1.28%** in the next 90. Two built-in mints drive it: 1,000 STX to miners for every Bitcoin block and 1,140 STX to the ecosystem Endowment at every tenure start, with no buyback and no fee burn to offset them. The key risk is the Endowment's growing stock of unclaimed and held coins, which already count as circulating but can still be sold. Stacks has no maximum supply, and the miner reward stays at 1,000 STX until a future upgrade decides otherwise.
