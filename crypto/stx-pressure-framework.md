# STX Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

STX supply is growing and the pace just went up. In the 90 days to Sep 29 2026 the Stacks chain created **19.13M new STX** — **10.74M** to miners and **8.39M** to the Stacks Endowment growth fund — and nothing was burned or bought back, so supply rose **+1.02%**. The Jul 30 2026 hard fork doubled the miner reward to 1,000 STX per Bitcoin block with no planned cuts, so the next 90 days point to about **22.93M STX**, or **+1.23%**. Our monitor reads **+0.99%** — the same story.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days STX supply grew **+1.02%** net (19.13M STX created, 0 removed, on a circulating base of **1.87B STX**). The independent monitor reads **+0.99%** over its own 90-day window, a gap of just **0.03** percentage points — well inside the half-point line, so no warning chip is shown. Looking forward, the ledger projects **+1.23%** for the next 90 days, because the whole forward window runs at the new, higher reward. That is roughly **5% a year** of new STX with nothing on the other side of the ledger. In one line: Stacks is **a steadily inflating Bitcoin layer with no burn**.

## Sell pressure: where new STX comes from

**Protocol inflation — 10.74M STX.** Stacks miners bid BTC to win the right to produce blocks, and each Bitcoin block pays a coinbase reward in newly created STX. A 2024 plan (SIP-029) had cut that reward from 1,000 to 500 STX in April 2026, and the reward sat at 500 for the first 29 days of this window. On Jul 30 2026 the Epoch 4.0 hard fork — the Bitcoin Staking upgrade, SIP-045, passed almost unanimously by stacked STX — restored 1,000 STX per Bitcoin block and removed every scheduled cut until a later upgrade reviews it. Measured from total supply at both ends of the window, with the growth-fund mint taken out, miners received **10,739,858 STX**. Since the fork they have been paid about 992 STX per Bitcoin block, so the next 90 days hold about **12.83M STX**.

**Growth-fund mint — 8.39M STX.** Since Aug 2025 a second mint runs next to the coinbase: at the start of every miner tenure the chain creates STX for the Stacks Endowment, under the SIP-031 five-year plan. The amount was 475 STX per tenure until Jul 30 2026 and 1,140 STX since. The fund's contract took in **8,394,295 STX** this window. About 69% of Bitcoin blocks start a new tenure, so the next 90 days hold about **10.11M STX**. The plan steps up again to 1,705 STX per tenure around mid-2027, then eases down until it ends in 2030.

**Vesting unlocks — 0.** The founder and early-investor vesting finished years ago; the chain reports 100% of STX unlocked. The growth fund still releases its second 100M STX in 24 monthly steps of about 4.17M STX, but those coins were created in Aug 2025 and already count as circulating, so a release adds nothing new. **Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — 0.** The fund moved large amounts this window, all of it between wallets already inside the float (see below). **Long-term locked or bankruptcy — 0.** There is no estate or trustee, and the release of old stacking locks at the fork added nothing because stacked STX was always counted as circulating.

## Buy pressure: where new STX goes

**Programmatic buyback — 0.** Nothing buys STX back. The BTC that miners bid goes to stakers as their reward; it never turns into STX purchases. **Protocol fee burn — 0.** Stacks transaction fees are paid to miners, not destroyed. Total supply only rose across the window, and the known burn addresses gained about 0.1 STX. The Bitcoin Staking upgrade did end a burn, but it was a burn of miners' BTC, not of STX. **Foundation buy — 0.** The Stacks Endowment sells and spends STX to fund grants, liquidity and staff; its founding plan only promises to put a burn to a vote if its liquid assets pass $1B, and nothing suggests that is close.

**New long-term lock — 0.** Bitcoin Staking asks every BTC bond to be paired with STX locked for about six months, and about **448M STX** is stacked in the current cycle. That lock is real and it may grow as bigger holders arrive. But locked STX still counts as circulating supply, so a larger lock does not take coins out of the float in this ledger.

## Foundation and overhang

The one tracked team-held overhang is the **Stacks Endowment**, the growth fund created by SIP-031. Its mint contract holds about **62.48M STX** — mostly the unreleased part of the second 100M plus mints not yet claimed. Its claim wallet holds about **6.86M STX**, its operating wallet about **41.78M STX**, and a wallet it funded in July holds about **61.02M STX** — roughly **172M STX** in all, a little over 9% of supply. This window the fund claimed **58.5M STX** from the contract and sent about **42M STX** onward. Because circulating supply already equals total supply, every one of these wallets sits inside the float, so the moves add nothing to the ledger. We read each balance from the chain at every rebuild; if any of them ever sat outside the float and its balance fell between refreshes, the outflow would enter Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How STX compares to other Bitcoin layers and emission-funded chains

**STX vs BTC.** Stacks settles on Bitcoin and its miners pay in BTC, but the two coins run opposite supply rules. Bitcoin has a hard cap and halves its reward every four years. Stacks planned to copy that rhythm, then voted twice to keep the reward high — first delaying the cut in SIP-029, then reversing it in SIP-045. STX now has no cap and no scheduled cut, and creates about 5% a year against Bitcoin's well under 1%.

**STX vs burn-offset chains.** Smart-contract chains that burn part of every fee can offset their issuance when the network is busy. Stacks has no such burn: fees go to miners, so heavier use raises what miners earn but never shrinks supply. Every new STX stays in the market until someone decides to lock it.

**STX vs treasury-mint chains.** Some networks fund a foundation from their own emission. Stacks does it openly and on a fixed ladder: the growth-fund mint is now about 44% of all new STX. That makes the fund's selling the main thing to watch — not because it adds to supply again, but because it decides how fast the new coins reach buyers.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Growth-fund releases around Oct 29 2026 and Nov 29 2026:** two more monthly steps of about 4.17M STX leave the contract. They do not change the count, but they give the fund more STX to deploy. **Bitcoin Staking uptake:** the Genesis Bond opened on Sep 10 2026 with 250 BTC, and a large US custodian said on Sep 24 2026 that it is building support; more BTC bonds mean more STX locked. **Stack Sats rewards, Sep 10 2026 to Dec 10 2026:** a 3 BTC DeFi incentive paid in BTC, not in new STX. **Any new emission proposal:** the 1,000 STX reward is described as temporary and is due for review with the next staking upgrade; a new plan would move this ledger at once.

## Summary

STX supply grew **+1.02%** in the 90 days to Sep 29 2026 and is on track for about **+1.23%** in the next 90 days, because the Jul 30 2026 hard fork restored a 1,000 STX miner reward per Bitcoin block while the Stacks Endowment mint rose to 1,140 STX per tenure. Nothing on the buy side offsets it: no fee burn, no buyback, and staked STX still counts as circulating. The main risk is steady dilution of about 5% a year plus a growth fund holding roughly 172M STX that it can sell. There is no hard cap; the only brake is a future vote to cut the reward again.
