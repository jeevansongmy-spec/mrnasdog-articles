# VET Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads VeChain at **0.00%** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** over the next 90 — every one of the four sell rows and every one of the four buy rows measured zero. VET is the supply token of a two-token chain: VeChainThor mints no VET at all, pays every reward in the separate VTHO gas token, and burns **100%** of its base fee in VTHO rather than in VET. The ceiling is a genesis **86,712,634,466 VET** that no function can raise, and the counted supply of **85,985,041,177 VET** plus the **727,593,289 VET** destroyed in 2019 accounts for that ceiling exactly — so nothing is held back, and nothing is being removed.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **85,985M VET**, the framework books **0** of sell pressure and **0** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **0.00%** — and projects **0.00%** for the next 90. The inflation monitor reads **+0.00%** for the same window, a gap of **0.0001 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so this build ships with no monitor-gap warning on the overview page. That residual is not a disagreement about VeChain at all: the monitor infers supply from market capitalisation divided by price, and on a genuinely fixed supply that inference wobbles by a few tens of thousands of units in either direction while the chain does not move. The label for VET is a **fixed-supply chain that spends a second token**: the thing that inflates, deflates and burns on VeChainThor is VTHO, and it is not the asset on this page.

## Sell pressure: where new VET comes from

Nowhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**. VET is the native coin of VeChainThor, not a token contract, so there is no mint function to call and no minting permission to grant; the entire supply was created in the 2018 genesis block, and VeChain's own documentation states that no new tokens will ever be created. The Hayabusa upgrade that moved VeChainThor from Proof of Authority to Delegated Proof of Stake in December 2025 did not change that — it rewrote how VTHO is issued, routing all of it to validators and delegators who stake VET, and it mints no VET. A flat supply reading has to be earned rather than assumed, so the framework proved the field is live first: across this window the protocol wrote VTHO into one VeChainThor account continuously while that account's VET balance stayed byte-identical, and elsewhere on the chain VET balances moved by more than a billion units. The VET supply can move; it did not. Because a future hard fork could still add issuance, the row is watched rather than closed permanently.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0**, and this is the finding that carries the sell side. On most chains a zero here means no cliff fell inside the window. On VeChainThor it means there is no locked bucket at all. The counted supply of **85,985,041,177 VET** plus the **727,593,289 VET** stranded in 2019 — when Authority Masternodes froze 469 addresses belonging to a thief and a network vote converted them into permanent burn addresses — sums to the genesis ceiling of **86,712,634,466 VET** exactly, residual zero, on two independent readings taken this session. That identity leaves no room for a treasury allocation, a team vest, an escrow or a foundation reserve sitting outside the circulating count, and the 2018 distribution left no schedule behind. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** for the same reason, and Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well: VeChain has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new VET goes

Also nowhere, and Buy #2 is the row worth explaining, because VeChain genuinely runs one of the most aggressive burns in the sector. The Galactica upgrade introduced an Ethereum-style dynamic fee market in July 2025 and destroys **100%** of every transaction's base fee, up from the 70% burned before. That base fee is denominated in VTHO. Every unit the burn destroys is a unit of the gas token, and not one VET is removed by it — which is why the row books **0** on a chain whose headline mechanism is a total fee burn. Both VET burn surfaces were read at both ends of the window anyway. The count of VET in existence was identical on both dates. The unreachable dead address rose from **922.81** to **2,013.81 VET**, a movement of **1,091 VET** — misdirected user sends, worth under ten dollars, **0.0000013%** of supply — while the zero address did not move by a single unit. Those two surfaces are independent here, and their disagreement is itself the finding: the dead-address balance can rise without the counted supply changing, because that count is a fixed classification rather than a live tally.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. The only VET repurchase programme VeChain ever announced was a $25 million plan whose first phase ran from July 2019; it is retired, the wallet it used read **0 VET** at both ends of this window, and no replacement mandate appears in the 2026 roadmap. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** — no market purchase is disclosed anywhere, and the Foundation reports a dollar treasury value rather than a token balance, so the opacity is carried on the sell side instead of credited as demand. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** on a measurement that moved a great deal: the two VeChain staking contracts together went from **14,473.8M** to **15,493.8M VET** across the window, an inflow of **1,020.0M VET** that now holds about **18%** of all VET. It does not count, for two independent reasons. The chain's own supply definition already treats staked VET as tradable float, so it never left the measured base. And it is not a long-term lock: a StarGate position matures in 2 to 60 days, a paid Boost skips that wait outright, an undelegated position unstakes with no cooldown, a delegated one exits at the end of a 7 to 30 day validator cycle, and the position NFT itself can be sold while it is still delegated.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on VET is a single item, and its size is genuinely unknown. No VeChainThor address is publicly labelled as the VeChain Foundation's, and the Foundation reports a treasury value rather than a wallet — the most recent, covering Q2 2025, put the whole treasury at about **$167M** across stablecoins, BTC, ETH and VET without breaking out the VET slice, and nothing newer was published inside this window. A labelled sweep of the largest 250 VET accounts this session, about **80%** of supply, returned exchange custody, the two staking contracts and unlabelled holders — no wallet identifiable as the project's. That sweep could still miss an unlabelled Foundation wallet, and it cannot see the **17,464M VET** held below rank 250, so the figure is reported as unknown rather than estimated. The one readable team wallet, the retired 2019 buyback address, held **0 VET** at both ends. Everything else large here is excluded by definition: exchange custody belongs to depositors, the staking contracts hold user-owned stake, and the 2019 burn addresses are gone rather than held. If the Foundation's position falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How VET compares to other fixed-supply, dual-token chains

VET sits in an unusual class: a hard on-chain ceiling, zero issuance, and a burn that is real, large and pointed at a different asset. Compare it to a halving-model chain. Bitcoin still mints on every block, just at a decaying rate, so its inflation reading is a small positive number on a known clock. VeChainThor mints nothing at all — its VET issuance is a flat zero, not a small figure — and unlike a proof-of-work chain there is no subsidy schedule left to run down. On the pure issuance axis VET is stricter than Bitcoin and far stricter than any uncapped continuous-emission L1, where a staking-linked emission of several percent a year is normal and the ceiling is a policy rather than a number in the genesis block.

The sharper comparison is to exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and gas burns. Those chains offset issuance with a demand-linked removal that scales with usage, and their inflation readings can go genuinely negative. VeChain has the same shape of mechanism, running harder than most — **100%** of the base fee destroyed on every transaction — and it still produces a zero on this page, because the burn consumes VTHO. This is the structural fact a reader has to hold: on a two-token chain the deflationary machine and the investable asset can be two different things, and VeChain has deliberately built it that way. VTHO is the token whose supply is being squeezed by Hayabusa's roughly 50% issuance cut and the full base-fee burn; VET is the token that generates it. The benefit to a VET holder is indirect — scarcer gas makes the right to generate gas more valuable — but it is not a reduction in VET supply, and the Pressure Framework measures supply.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Interstellar upgrade, VIP-255, which passed an all-stakeholder vote held **Aug 10 2026** to **Aug 17 2026** with **99.97%** in favour and activates at block **25,902,540** on **Sep 16 2026**. It bundles eleven Ethereum EIPs and carries no VET or VTHO tokenomics clause, no migration and no staking change — which is exactly why the forward column reads zero, and the thing to check is that the shipped upgrade matches the proposal. Second, the staking balance, which added **1,020.0M VET** across this window and is read from both staking contracts at every rebuild: a governance change attaching an exit delay of 90 days or more, or any forfeiture, would convert it into a genuine Buy #4. Third, the dead-address balance at **2,013.81 VET**, the only VET burn surface that has ever moved. Fourth, the VeChain Foundation's next financial report — the last covered Q2 2025, and the first one that breaks out a VET balance would close the largest unknown on this page. Fifth, any proposal that would introduce VET issuance, the only way the sell side stops being zero by construction.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads VET at **0.00%** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** projected forward: mixed flows, supply roughly steady. The structural mechanism is a two-token design — VeChainThor mints no VET, pays every reward in VTHO, and destroys **100%** of its base fee in VTHO rather than in VET — sitting on a genesis ceiling of **86,712,634,466 VET** whose counted supply and 2019 burn account for it exactly, leaving no locked bucket to release. The key risk is not supply at all but attribution: readers routinely credit VeChain's aggressive burn to VET, and it removes none, so a page that scores VET on scarcity alone will over-rate it. The ceiling is the genuine comfort — no vote is needed to keep VET from being minted, only to start it, and neither the chain nor the Interstellar upgrade has moved that.
