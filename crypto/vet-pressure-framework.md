# VET Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

VeChain runs two tokens, and every supply mechanism people associate with VeChain belongs to the other one. VET is capped at **86,712,634,466** coins, all minted in the **2017** genesis block, with no mint function anywhere in VeChainThor; the staking rewards introduced by Hayabusa and the **100%** base-fee burn introduced by Galactica are both denominated in VTHO, the separate gas token. So the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads VET at **0 VET** of sell pressure, **0 VET** of buy pressure and **0.00%** net for the last 90 days and the next 90, against our supply monitor at **-0.08%** — a gap of **0.08 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no data flag ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 18 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **VET at 0.00% net**, with the forward view also at **0.00%**. Our supply monitor reads **-0.08%**, so the gap is **0.08 percentage points** — well inside tolerance, and no monitor-gap flag is warranted. That residual is sampling noise around a constant figure rather than a real flow: the monitor infers supply from market cap divided by price, and VeChain's counted float has sat at **85,985,041,177 VET** throughout. The reconciliation is measured, not assumed. Every contract that could hold or release VET was read directly on the VeChainThor node at both ends of the window, block **24,869,244** against block **25,646,844**, and the picture is the same at both: no mint, no burn, no escrow draining. VeChain is a **fixed-supply chain whose entire inflation lives on a second token**.

## Sell pressure: where new VET comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and it is zero in the strongest sense the framework recognises. VeChain's own token documentation states that the total supply of VET is fixed at **86,712,634,466** and that no new tokens will ever be created. VeChainThor has no VET mint path at all. What the Hayabusa hard fork changed on **Dec 2 2025** — moving consensus to a permissionless delegated proof-of-stake model and tying VTHO generation to the amount of VET actively staked rather than merely held — changed the VTHO curve, not the VET supply. Validators and delegators on VeChain are paid in VTHO. Not one of those rewards is a new VET.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the whole supply was created at once in 2017 and the original allocation schedules expired years ago; no cliff and no linear release falls inside the next 90 days on any published schedule. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** for the window, and this one was tested rather than assumed. Every large VET holder was read at both window ends, and the static team-sized balances — one pair of wallets holding **2,137.5M** and **2,137.3M VET**, another at **802.4M**, another at **771.3M** — moved exactly nothing. The wallets that did move are exchange hot and cold wallets and unidentified holders, which is float changing hands rather than supply arriving. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: VeChain has no bankruptcy estate, and the staking contracts moved the wrong way for a sell row, taking in a net **1.17B VET** over the window instead of releasing any.

## Buy pressure: where new VET goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**. The only VET repurchase the VeChain Foundation ever ran was a one-off programme started in **Jul 2019**, finished long ago, and nothing has replaced it — so there is no buyback contract and no accumulation wallet to track. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is the row that traps people, and it is also **zero**. VeChain does burn aggressively: the Galactica upgrade brought a dynamic fee market modelled on Ethereum's EIP-1559, and **100%** of every base fee is destroyed. But the base fee on VeChainThor is paid in VTHO, so the burn shrinks VTHO and leaves VET untouched. Reading the chain confirms it from the other direction: the two unspendable addresses on VeChainThor absorbed about **102.4K VET** over the whole window from mistaken transfers, roughly a ten-thousandth of a percent of supply, and that is user error rather than a burn mechanism.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** — the VeChain Foundation publishes no mandate to buy VET on the open market. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the interesting zero. StarGate staking really did pull VET off the float: the protocol staking contract went from **13.38B** to **14.62B VET** over the window while the older StarGate pool fell from **905.8M** to **835.2M**, a net **1.17B VET** into custody. It still does not count, and the current StarGate rules are why. A staking NFT is held through a maturity period set by its node tier — **2 days** for Dawn, **5** for Lightning, **15** for Flash, **30** for Strength, **45** for Thunder, **60** for Mjolnir — and the Boost feature matures an NFT instantly for a fee. After maturity the owner unstakes at will, with no cooldown and nothing forfeited; a delegated NFT waits only for the current validator cycle, at most **30 days**. That is custody, not a lock, and the framework books custody at zero.

## Foundation and overhang

Two team-controlled overhangs are tracked for VeChain. The first is the unscheduled residual: the fixed cap is **86,712,634,466 VET** while the counted float is **85,985,041,177**, leaving **727.6M VET** — about **0.85%** of the cap — outside the circulating classification with no published release plan. The second is the VeChain Foundation treasury itself, which is genuinely opaque on the VET leg: the Foundation has published quarterly treasury and financial reports since 2017, but the most recent public one is the Q2 **2025** financial report, and no wallet on VeChainThor is publicly labelled as belonging to the Foundation. It is therefore monitored through official disclosure rather than by address. Against that opacity, the direct on-chain read is the useful control, and it came back clean: across every large holder, no static team-sized balance moved a single VET between the two window ends. The trigger is the same for both overhangs — if either balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How VET compares to other dual-token Layer 1 chains

VeChain belongs to a small structural family: chains that split the value token from the gas token so that network usage never dilutes the asset people hold. The comparison that matters is with single-token fee-burn L1s. On Ethereum, EIP-1559 burns the base fee in ETH itself, so heavy usage pushes ETH net supply toward negative and the burn is a genuine buy-side row. VeChain runs the same fee design through VIP-251 but burns VTHO, so the identical mechanism produces a buy row of exactly **zero** for VET. A reader who sees "100% fee burn" and infers deflation has read the mechanism correctly and the token wrong.

Against uncapped proof-of-stake L1s the contrast is cleaner still. A chain paying validators a percentage-of-stake yield in its own token has an emission rate that responds to participation and can be retuned by governance, with no ceiling; VeChain pays its validators in VTHO, so participation growth changes the gas token's issuance and leaves the **86.71B** VET cap untouched. Against hard-capped proof-of-work chains, VET is stricter: a halving-model chain like Bitcoin still mints new coins every block until its cap is reached, while VET reached its cap at genesis and has issued nothing since. And against exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks, VET sits at the opposite pole — those assets can push net supply negative by spending revenue, whereas VeChain neither creates VET nor removes it. That is why VET reads flat rather than deflationary: a fixed supply is not a shrinking supply, and the framework will not score it as one.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the VeChain Foundation treasury disclosure. The last public report is the Q2 **2025** financial report, and a fresh one naming wallets or reporting a VET drawdown would be the single most likely source of a non-zero Sell #3. Second, the **727.6M VET** outside the counted float: any announcement giving it a release plan, or a revision of the circulating classification, moves both the numerator and the denominator of this reading. Third, StarGate staking mechanics. The protocol staking contract has climbed to **14.62B VET**, and if a governance change ever attached a real exit delay or a forfeiture to unstaking, that balance would convert from custody into a genuine Buy #4 overnight. Fourth, anything that puts a burn or a buyback on the VET ledger itself rather than on VTHO — nothing in the 2026 roadmap, in the Thor **v2.4.4** release of **Jul 15 2026**, or in the AgentSuite launch of **Jul 6 2026** does so today. Fifth, validator-cycle and staking-cap parameters, which change how VTHO is produced and could change staking behaviour even though they cannot change the VET supply.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads VeChain's VET at **0.00%** net supply change for the last 90 days and the next 90, against a supply monitor at **-0.08%** — a **0.08 percentage point** gap that is sampling noise, not disagreement. The structural reason is the two-token design: VET is capped at **86,712,634,466** coins minted in 2017 with no mint function, while VeChainThor's staking rewards and its **100%** base-fee burn both operate on VTHO, the separate gas token. The key risk is not inflation but opacity — the VeChain Foundation's VET holdings are not published by address and its last public treasury report covers mid-2025, and **727.6M VET** sits outside the counted float with no release plan. The ceiling is absolute: VET cannot exceed **86.71B**, and because nothing burns it either, a fixed supply is the best this reading can be.
