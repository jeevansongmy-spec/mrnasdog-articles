## The verdict, in one paragraph

VeChain's VET supply held at **0.00%** between Aug 26 and Nov 24 2026, against **+0.03%** on other trackers, a difference of **0.03 points**, small enough that the coin page carries no warning note. All **85.99B VET** in the circulating count can already move, nothing vests, nothing is burned and nothing is bought back. The only VET outside that count is a pile of about **727.58M VET** frozen since January 2020, and it did not move either. VeChain is a fixed-supply coin whose network pays out in a different token: a still chain, by design.

## Sell pressure: where new VET comes from

Protocol inflation was **0 VET**. VeChain made all of its VET at launch, and the network's code has no way to create more: the only moves it allows are coins passing from one account to another. Since the Hayabusa upgrade of Dec 2 2025, validators and the people who stake with them earn VTHO, not VET, so even a fast-growing network leaves the VET count where it is.

Vesting unlocks were **0 VET**. VET has no vesting schedule, and no team, investor or partner batch is waiting for a date to come free.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks were **0 VET**. The VeChain Foundation holds VET alongside stablecoins, BTC and ETH, worth about **$167M** in all at its last public report on Jun 30 2025, but those coins already sit inside the circulating count, so a Foundation sale moves existing VET and adds none.

Long-term locked coins were **0 VET** released. About **727.58M VET** taken in a December 2019 theft from a Foundation wallet sits in 469 accounts the network has refused to process since January 2020. The pile was the same on Aug 26 and on Nov 24 2026, and freeing it would take a change to the network's rules that nobody has put forward.

## Buy pressure: where new VET goes

Programmatic buyback was **0 VET**. No contract buys VET today. A draft rule for a VET buyback and burn was filed on Feb 12 2026, but it has not changed since Mar 11 2026 and has never gone to a vote.

Protocol fee burn was **0 VET**. Every VeChain fee is paid in VTHO, and the base part of it is burned in VTHO, so heavy use shrinks the VTHO supply and leaves VET untouched.

Foundation buying was **0 VET**. The Foundation's last buyback ran in 2019, and the wallet it used was the one hit by the theft behind the frozen pile. No new buying plan has been announced.

New long-term locks were **0 VET**. Staking grew from about **14.62B VET** to about **15.01B VET** between Aug 26 and Nov 24 2026, because staking is now the only way to earn VTHO. Staked VET still counts as circulating and can be withdrawn, so a bigger stake takes nothing out of the count.

## Foundation and overhang

Two holders matter for VET. The first is the VeChain Foundation, whose treasury of about **$167M** at Jun 30 2025 mixes VET with other coins and has no public wallet list or schedule. Its VET is already circulating, so the Foundation's reports and news are watched for sales rather than counted as new supply.

The second is the frozen theft pile of about **727.58M VET** in 469 accounts, the only VET outside the circulating count, and its balance can be read on-chain at any time. If either holder's balance falls between checks, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How VET compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Most proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains pay their validators in the same coin people hold. Ethereum creates new ETH for validators and burns part of each fee in ETH, so its supply moves every day in both directions. Solana pays its stakers in new SOL on a falling yearly rate. On those chains, a bigger stake or a quieter network changes the main coin's count.

VeChain splits the two jobs. VET is the fixed coin people stake and hold; VTHO is the coin the network creates for stakers and burns for fees. NEO and Theta use a similar two-token split, with a fixed main coin and a second token for rewards or gas. The trade-off is clear: VET holders face no dilution, but heavy use of the network burns VTHO, not VET, so VET does not gain scarcity from activity either.

VET also differs from fixed-cap chains like Bitcoin, which still release new coins to miners until the cap is reached. VET reached its full supply on day one, so its count only changes if the network's rules change.
