# APT Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Aptos put **38.84M APT** onto the market in the last 90 days, and the chain itself minted only **4.91M** of it. The other **33.93M APT** came from the 2022 genesis allocation calendar, which releases **11.31M APT** on the 12th of every month — roughly seven times the mint. Against that, Aptos has no buyback at all and a single offsetting row: **100%** of gas is burned, worth **0.51M APT**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads APT at **+4.47% net** over the trailing window and **+3.67%** forward, against a supply-monitor reading of **+4.66%**. APT is hard-capped at **2.1B** forever, and the cap constrains the smaller of the two taps.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Aptos at **+4.47% net**: **38.84M APT** reaching the float against **0.51M APT** destroyed, on a circulating base of **858.23M APT**. The supply monitor reads the same window at **+4.66%**, a gap of **0.19 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so this build ships **no monitor-gap chip** and needs no reconciliation walk. The residual is not a disputed flow at all, it is the base each side divides by: running the framework's own **38.33M APT** of net float growth over the older base the monitor uses gives **+4.67%**, which is **0.01** percentage points from the monitor. Forward, the framework reads **+3.67%**. Aptos is **inflationary by calendar, not by consensus** — a capped, low-emission chain whose float grows mostly from coins created in **Oct 2022** and only now being handed out.

## Sell pressure: where new APT comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **4.91M APT**, and it is the smaller of the two taps despite being the only one that creates coins that did not exist before. Aptos pays stakers freshly minted APT once per epoch, and the reward rate is now sitting on a hard floor: the live on-chain rate is **5.936073e-6** per epoch, which annualises to exactly **2.60%**, and the protocol's minimum rate is set to the identical value, so the decay curve has nowhere left to fall. This build read that rate at **ten** separate historical ledger versions across the window and it was byte-identical at every one — proof, rather than assertion, that no mid-window rate change happened. There is a trap here worth naming: Aptos still exposes a deprecated legacy staking field that reads **7.00%** APR, and a build that trusted it would have overstated the Aptos mint by **2.7** times. The measurement is a staked base averaging **764.30M APT** across **1,083** epochs — a count taken from the chain, not divided out of the nominal epoch length, because governance reconfigurations force extra epochs and each one pays a full round of staking rewards.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **33.93M APT** and it is the whole story of Aptos supply. The Oct 2022 genesis distributed exactly **1,000,000,000 APT** across four buckets: community **510.22M**, core contributors **190.00M**, foundation **165.00M** and investors **134.78M**. Each bucket releases a fixed tranche on the 12th of every month, and the four tranches together come to **11.31M APT** — **Jun 12**, **Jul 12** and **Aug 12 2026** all fell inside this window. These are not newly minted APT; the coins have existed since genesis and the unlock simply moves them from locked to tradable, which is exactly why Aptos total supply barely moves while the float climbs steadily. The schedule is also being honoured rather than quietly deferred: the non-circulating pool fell **33.62M APT** over the same window, **99.1%** of the calendar entitlement, so nothing material is sitting undrawn behind a lock.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**, and on Aptos that is unusually well proven. Adding up what each of the four genesis buckets has already released, plus the realised staking mint of **207.98M APT**, reproduces the circulating supply to within **158 APT** on an **858M** base. That subtraction leaves no room for an unscheduled pool: every APT outside the float is inside a published calendar. The same arithmetic dates the end of the insider vest. Core contributors have **7.92M APT** left and investors **5.62M**, which are precisely two monthly tranches each — so the four-year Aptos vest that began at mainnet launch pays its final instalment on **Oct 12 2026**, not on the later date several public unlock trackers still show. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: no bankruptcy estate, trustee or court-ordered distribution has ever touched APT.

## Buy pressure: where new APT goes

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **0.51M APT** and it is the only counter-flow Aptos has. Every unit of gas paid on Aptos is destroyed: the protocol holds the burn capability and there is no collected-fee pool for validators, so **100%** of the gas fee is removed rather than redistributed, and the Apr 2026 overhaul raised gas about **tenfold** to make that burn bigger. The burn is measured, not assumed. Aptos destroys APT by calling a supply-reducing burn rather than by shovelling coins into a dead address, so total supply is the only surface that moves and there is exactly one flow to book. Differencing what the chain minted against how much supply actually rose gives **0.51M APT**, and converting **90** days of gas revenue at the price on each day it was paid gives **0.56M APT** — two independent angles within **11%** of one another. It is worth noting how far that sits from the marketing: one Aptos venue is projected to burn **32M APT** a year at scale, while the entire chain is currently burning at about **2.06M** a year.

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**. The Aptos Foundation has said it will **explore** buying APT on the open market out of licensing and ecosystem-investment revenue, but exploring is not a mechanism: there is no contract, no published accumulation address, and no supply-affecting proposal among the on-chain Aptos governance items raised during the window, all of which were framework upgrades or feature flags. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero** for the same reason, with no disclosed or observed open-market purchase. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is also **zero**, and this one deserves the explanation. The Aptos Foundation permanently staked **210M APT** and now funds its operations from staking rewards instead of treasury sales, which is a genuine and large removal. It simply happened in **Apr 2026**, before this window opened, so it belongs to a quarter this page does not measure.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on Aptos is large, fully enumerated and entirely scheduled. The community bucket holds **237.55M APT** and releases **3.21M** a month into the 2030s. The Aptos Foundation bucket holds **98.67M APT** and releases **1.33M** a month on the same ten-year line. Core contributors hold **7.92M APT** and investors **5.62M**, two tranches each, both ending **Oct 12 2026**. Those four sum to **349.75M APT** against a measured non-circulating total of the same figure, **158 APT** apart — so the unscheduled-unlock category, usually the largest and most dangerous line on a coin like this, is genuinely empty. Sitting outside that count is the **210M APT** the Aptos Foundation locked permanently, which was already circulating before it was staked and has no release schedule of any kind. There is no buyback accumulation wallet because there is no buyback, no DAO treasury separate from the Foundation, and no bankruptcy residual. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How APT compares to other capped proof-of-stake Layer 1s

Aptos now looks, on paper, like the most disciplined uncapped-turned-capped Layer 1 in its class. It has a protocol-enforced ceiling of **2.1B APT**, a staking emission floored at **2.60%**, and a fee burn that takes **100%** of gas rather than the partial base-fee burn that fee-burning smart-contract chains typically run. Compared with an uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1, where issuance is the dominant and permanent supply force, Aptos issuance is small and bounded by the cap: at the current staked base the mint contributes only about **0.57%** of circulating supply a quarter, and it can never carry total supply past the cap.

The comparison that actually matters is with the other **2021–2022** venture-backed Layer 1s, because Aptos shares their real supply mechanism rather than their advertised one. On those chains the float is set by a four-year allocation calendar, not by consensus, and the framework reads them the same way: the calendar is the tap, the mint is a rounding error next to it. Aptos is a clean example — **33.93M APT** of calendar against **4.91M** of mint, a ratio of about seven to one. A halving-model chain with a hard cap has the opposite shape, where issuance is the only tap and it is known decades ahead; an exchange token with a quarterly buyback has a buy side large enough to make net supply negative. Aptos has neither: it has one very large scheduled sell tap, one small mint, and a burn worth about **1.3%** of the sell side. What separates Aptos from its cohort is that its calendar is close to exhausted on the insider half, and the chain has already done the structural work — cap, emission floor, full gas burn, permanent foundation lock — that the rest have not.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Sep 12 2026** — a full **11.31M APT** monthly tranche, the second-to-last of its size. **Oct 12 2026** — the final four-year tranche for core contributors and investors; this is the single most consequential date on the Aptos supply calendar, because it retires **6.77M APT** of recurring monthly release. **Nov 12 2026** — the first slimmed month, at **4.54M APT**, which is what the calendar looks like for the rest of the decade. **Oct 11 2026** — the annual review point for the staking reward rate; the rate is already at its floor with no headroom to cut, so a change here would have to come from a new Aptos governance proposal rather than the existing curve. And the standing watch line: whether the Aptos Foundation converts its stated intent to explore a programmatic buyback into an actual contract with a readable address, which is the one thing that could put a real number in Buy #1.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Aptos at **+4.47%** net supply growth over the trailing 90 days and **+3.67%** forward, against a monitor reading of **+4.66%** — a gap small enough to need no reconciliation. The mechanism is unusual for how little it has to do with the chain: Aptos mints only **4.91M APT** a quarter at a reward rate already floored at **2.60%**, and burns **100%** of gas, but the 2022 allocation calendar hands out **11.31M APT** every month regardless of any of that. The key risk is simply duration — the community and foundation buckets, together **336.22M APT**, keep releasing into the 2030s long after the insider vest ends. The ceiling is real and permanent at **2.1B APT**, but it bounds the mint, not the calendar, and it is the calendar that sets the price of APT supply.
