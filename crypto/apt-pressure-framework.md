> Originally published at **[mrnasdog.com/research/apt/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/apt/inflation)** by MrNasdog.

Aptos now has a protocol hard cap of **2.1 billion APT**, a staking-reward rate cut to **2.60%** a year, and a burn that destroys **100%** of gas — and APT supply is still growing at about **+4.47%** per 90 days. The reason is the unlock calendar, not the mint: the Aptos genesis allocation releases **11.31M APT** on the 12th of every month, which is roughly seven times the **4.87M** the chain mints for stakers over a full quarter, while the gas-fee burn removes only **0.46M**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Aptos at **+3.15%** forward, with the whole slope hinging on **Oct 12 2026**, when the four-year investor and core-contributor vest expires.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework books **38.80M APT** of sell pressure against **0.46M APT** of buy pressure over the last 90 days, a net of about **+4.47%** of the **857.36M** circulating float, and **+3.15%** forward. Our supply monitor reads the same trailing window at **+4.53%**, a gap of **0.05 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap warning ships on this build. The two readings agree because the Aptos ledger reconciles cleanly: circulating supply rose **37.11M APT** across the window, of which **33.93M** is three monthly unlocks and the remaining **3.18M** is the share of the mint that lands on market-held stake rather than inside the Aptos Foundation's locked position. Aptos is best labelled **structurally inflationary on the calendar, not on the mint**: a chain that has already fixed its issuance and is now waiting out a distribution schedule.

## Sell pressure: where new APT comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **4.87M APT** over 90 days, and reading it correctly matters more on Aptos than on most chains. The chain still carries a deprecated staking-rate field that reports **7.00%** a year; the live rate lives in a separate on-chain rewards configuration and reads **2.60%** simple annual, or **0.6432%** compounded across the **1,080** two-hour epochs in a 90-day window. Applied to the **756.77M APT** average stake across the window, that gives **4.87M** newly minted APT. Two things pin it. First, the rate is byte-identical at both ends of the window, so the Feb–Apr 2026 tokenomics overhaul that cut it from **5.19%** did not land mid-window and nothing needs re-basing. Second, the rate has reached its own floor — the configured minimum equals the current rate — so Aptos issuance cannot decay further without another governance vote, and it cannot rise either.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **33.93M APT**, and it is the whole story. The Aptos genesis allocation of **1,000M APT** releases **11.31M** on the 12th of every month, split roughly **3.21M** to community, **3.96M** to core contributors, **1.33M** to the Aptos Foundation and **2.81M** to investors. Three firings fell inside the trailing window — **Jun 12**, **Jul 12** and **Aug 12 2026**. The framework checks released against scheduled before booking a vesting row, and on Aptos the two agree: the measured rise in circulating supply matches three full tranches plus the market-held share of the mint, with no unclaimed backlog sitting inside a lock contract. Forward, the framework counts by date rather than by average, so **Sep 12** and **Oct 12 2026** are in at **22.62M** combined and **Nov 12 2026** falls one day outside the window.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **0**, and only to avoid double-counting. Every locked APT that moved in this window moved on the published monthly calendar already carried in Sell #2, and no identified Aptos Foundation or Aptos Labs wallet made a discretionary release on top of it. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **0** for the simplest reason available: Aptos has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution of APT anywhere in its history.

## Buy pressure: where new APT goes

Buy #2 — the protocol fee burn — is the only non-zero row on this side of the Aptos ledger, at **0.46M APT** over 90 days. Since the overhaul, **100%** of gas paid on Aptos is destroyed rather than paid out, and gas was raised roughly tenfold in the same package. The framework measures the burn on chain rather than quoting a rate: Aptos minted **4.87M APT** for stakers over the window but total supply rose only **4.41M**, from **1,202.71M** to **1,207.11M**, so **0.46M** was burned away. An independent angle agrees — Aptos chain fees over the same 90 days total about **$398,000**, which at the window's average APT price converts to roughly **580,000 APT**, within the framework's triangulation band of the supply-derived figure. It is a real deflationary mechanism. It is also about a tenth of what the mint alone adds, and roughly one seventy-fifth of the unlock calendar.

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **0**. The Aptos Foundation has said it will explore a buyback funded from licensing revenue and ecosystem investments, but no proposal has been put to a vote, nothing has executed, and there is no accumulation address to read. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **0** for the same reason: no open-market accumulation by the Aptos Foundation or Aptos Labs is disclosed. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **0** inside this window even though Aptos did lock a very large position: the Foundation's permanent lock of **210M APT** executed in **Apr 2026**, before the window opened, and it locked APT that had never traded. Booking a permanent lock of never-circulating coins as buy pressure would credit Aptos with absorbing supply that was never on the market; the framework tracks it as an overhang instead.

## Foundation and overhang

Aptos carries **349.75M APT** that is minted but not yet circulating — the difference between the on-chain total of **1,207.11M** and the **857.36M** classified as float. Inside that sits the largest single identified position, the Aptos Foundation's permanent lock of **210M APT**, committed in **Apr 2026** with no release schedule of any kind; it is refreshed on a web walk rather than a schedule read, because there is no schedule to read. The remainder is the undrawn tail of the genesis allocation — community, Aptos Foundation, core-contributor and investor buckets — which moves on the monthly calendar and is therefore already priced into Sell #2 rather than counted again in Sell #3. Aptos runs no separate DAO treasury distinct from the Foundation, no buyback accumulation wallet, and no bankruptcy residual. If any of these balances falls between refreshes outside the published calendar, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How APT compares to other capped Layer 1 chains

Aptos now belongs to the small group of Layer 1 chains that have voluntarily converted from uncapped issuance to a hard cap. Compared with a halving-model chain, the difference is where the scarcity comes from. A halving chain's supply schedule is fixed at genesis and its float has been fully distributed for years, so its inflation reading is simply its block subsidy. Aptos fixed its ceiling only in **Mar 2026**, with **1,207.11M** of the **2.1 billion** cap already minted and **349.75M** of that still undistributed — so the cap constrains the far future while the near-term reading is dominated by a distribution schedule that a halving chain no longer has.

Against uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1s, Aptos now looks structurally tighter on the mint. A chain issuing at five to eight percent a year with no ceiling produces permanent dilution regardless of usage; Aptos issues at **2.60%** on staked supply, that rate is floored rather than merely current, and every unit of gas is destroyed. Where Aptos is weaker is the burn side. Chains with a fee-burn mechanism that actually bites — where transaction demand routinely destroys more than the chain issues — turn net deflationary in busy quarters. Aptos burned **0.46M APT** against **4.87M** minted this window, so its burn offsets under a tenth of issuance. Fees would need to rise by more than an order of magnitude before the mechanism changes the sign.

The closest structural analogue is a chain in the last stretch of a four-year investor vest. Those chains read badly on inflation for as long as the calendar runs and then re-rate mechanically when it stops, without anything about the protocol changing. Aptos is roughly two months from that point: after **Oct 12 2026** the monthly release drops from **11.31M** to about **4.54M**, a step down of roughly **60%**, and the framework's reading falls with it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the **Sep 12 2026** unlock of **11.31M APT** — the second-to-last full tranche, and the routine confirmation that released still equals scheduled on Aptos. Second, and far more important, the **Oct 12 2026** unlock, the final full tranche: it retires the **6.77M** monthly quantum belonging to investors and core contributors and is the single dated event that most changes this reading. Third, the **Nov 12 2026** release, which falls just outside this window and should be the first month at the reduced **4.54M** rate — if it is not, the schedule has changed and the ledger needs re-deriving. Fourth, any Aptos governance proposal touching the staking-reward rate: the rate sits at its configured floor, so a further cut requires a vote rather than the emission curve. Fifth, whether the Aptos Foundation converts its stated intention to explore a programmatic buyback into an executed on-chain programme with a readable address — that is the only realistic way Buy #1 stops being zero.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Aptos at about **+4.47%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+3.15%** forward, against a supply monitor at **+4.53%** — a **0.05 percentage point** gap that needs no warning. The mechanism is a distribution schedule, not an issuance problem: Aptos mints **4.87M APT** a quarter at a rate now floored at **2.60%**, burns **100%** of gas for **0.46M** back, and releases **11.31M** a month from a genesis allocation that still holds **349.75M** undistributed. The key risk is that the buy side is a single small row — there is no buyback, no treasury buying, and a burn that offsets under a tenth of the mint — so nothing cushions the calendar while it runs. The ceiling is real: **2.1 billion APT**, voted in on **Mar 1 2026**, with the sharpest improvement in this reading due on **Oct 12 2026**.
