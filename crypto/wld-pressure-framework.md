# WLD Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Over the 90 days to **Aug 28 2026** the Pressure Framework reads World at **+9.01% net** — sell pressure of **370.4M WLD** against buy pressure of **44.4M WLD** on a circulating base of **3,617M WLD** — against our supply monitor's **+7.80%**, a gap of **1.22 percentage points** that ships a flag. The unusual part is that World creates nothing. All **10,000M WLD** were minted in one transaction at genesis in **2023**, the contract read exactly that at both ends of the window, and no WLD can be destroyed because World Chain charges its gas in ETH. Every point of WLD inflation is pre-minted supply being handed out — an unlock schedule for early investors and the team, and the World Foundation selling community tokens to fund the business.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the window **May 30 2026** to **Aug 28 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **WLD at +9.01% net**. Our supply monitor reads **+7.80%** for the same window, a gap of **1.22 percentage points**, outside the half-point tolerance, so a monitor-gap flag ships on the WLD overview card. The deep walk found the reason rather than waving at it: on **Jul 24 2026** the World Foundation settled a strategic token sale of **194.4M WLD** out of its release safe in a single day, at roughly **27 cents** a token — about a **52.5M dollar** raise — and the counted float series shows no step at all on that date. The framework reads the chain; the classified float has not caught up. WLD is best labelled **a hard-capped token with no mint and no burn that is nevertheless one of the most inflationary assets we cover, because the entire supply story is distribution rather than issuance**.

## Sell pressure: where new WLD comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0**, and on World this zero is absolute rather than merely quiet. The WLD contract on Ethereum returned a total supply of exactly **10,000,000,000 WLD** at both ends of the 90-day window, and at every sample taken back to 2024. World has no block reward, no staking emission and no fee mint. World Chain, the network World runs for its own identity and payments traffic, charges gas in ETH rather than WLD, so activity on World Chain neither creates nor consumes the token. The one door to more WLD opens on **Jul 24 2038**, fifteen years after launch, and is capped at **1.5%** a year even then. Nothing on this page is new supply.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **150.0M WLD**, and it draws from one bucket only: the roughly **2,380M WLD** allocated to Tools for Humanity investors and the founding team. That allocation has been released on a fixed drip since **Jul 24 2024**, and the drip was cut inside this window — from **1.9M WLD** a day to **1.3M WLD** a day on **Jul 24 2026**. Fifty-five days at the old rate plus thirty-five at the new one gives **150.0M WLD**. Two things are worth knowing about this row. World's own definition counts investor and team tokens as circulating the moment they are delivered to the holder, whether or not the holder moves them, so the schedule is the measure the market uses. And the eight custody contracts holding the still-locked remainder — **929M WLD** between them — only paid out **39.1M WLD** across the window, so far more was released on paper than actually walked out the door.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **220.4M WLD**, and this row is the heavy one. It draws from a completely different bucket: the **75%** community allocation, which sits in a ladder of vesting contracts whose beneficiary is a single World Foundation release safe. That is what keeps the two rows from double-counting — every community WLD passes through that safe, and no investor or team WLD ever does. On **Aug 11 2026** the 2023–2026 community tranche matured and was swept in full, moving **674.6M WLD** into the safe. The Foundation then pushed **220.4M WLD** back out. Most of it, **194.4M WLD**, was the **Jul 24 2026** strategic token sale, paid in dollar-round lots with a one-unit test transfer to each buyer two days beforehand. The remaining **26.1M WLD** went out as user grants and ecosystem spending on World Chain. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** — World has never been through an insolvency and there is no trustee or estate distribution behind WLD.

## Buy pressure: where new WLD goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. The World Foundation runs no repurchase programme and has never announced one. It is doing the opposite — selling WLD from the community allocation to fund World ID expansion, which is exactly what Sell #3 measures. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0** for the same reason: the Foundation already holds **1,398M WLD** and has no reason to bid for more. Its treasury did grow sharply over the window, by **442M WLD**, but that growth is the vesting sweep arriving from its own lock contract rather than demand, so it is watched as overhang instead of counted here.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**, and both burn surfaces were read at both ends of the window rather than one. The unspendable dead address holds nothing at all on Ethereum, **690 WLD** on World Chain and a fraction of a single token on Optimism, and none of those balances moved. Total supply itself sat at exactly **10,000,000,000 WLD** on both dates. Neither surface moved because there is no burn mechanism to move them: WLD has no protocol burn path, and World Chain gas is paid in ETH. The **10,000M** cap is a ceiling and a floor at the same time, which is a genuine structural weakness — a token that cannot shrink has no way to answer a heavy unlock calendar.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **44.4M WLD**, and it is the one real offset in this ledger. Of the tokens sold on **Jul 24 2026**, **44,444,442 WLD** never reached liquid hands. They settled straight into four newly deployed lock contracts, each carrying a hard cliff at **Jul 24 2027**, and every one of them still holds its full balance today. That is supply taken back off the market for a year, so it is booked here against the gross sale it came from rather than quietly netted inside it. Nothing comparable is scheduled for the next 90 days, so the forward buy column is **0**.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang behind WLD is enormous, and almost all of it is readable on chain. The largest piece is the community vesting ladder: **1,750M WLD** releasing to the Foundation between **2026** and **2029**, **875M WLD** waiting for **Jul 24 2029**, and a final **875M WLD** waiting for **Jul 24 2032** and running to **2038** — **3,500M WLD** in total, refreshed by reading the vesting contracts directly. Sitting on top of that, the World Foundation release safe holds **1,398M WLD** and grew by **442M** across this window, its settlement safe holds **69.7M WLD**, and the grant and ecosystem wallets on World Chain hold **113.1M WLD** and are draining.

Two smaller overhangs complete the picture. The investor and team custody cluster — eight contracts first funded at the 2024 cliff — holds **929M WLD**, and paid out **39.1M** over the window. The four new cliff contracts from the Jul 24 sale hold **44.4M WLD** until **Jul 24 2027**. Added up, roughly **6,050M WLD** — well over half of every WLD that will ever exist — sits outside the market on a calendar that runs to 2038. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How WLD compares to other fixed-supply tokens

WLD belongs to a class that is easy to misread: tokens with a hard cap that were minted in full on day one. Bitcoin also has a hard cap, but Bitcoin issues its supply gradually through mining, so its cap and its emission are the same schedule and its inflation falls by half every four years without anyone deciding anything. World is the mirror image. Its emission finished in 2023 and its distribution is only a third done, so the number that matters is not how fast coins are created but how fast a foundation and a vesting calendar hand them out. On that measure WLD is far more inflationary than any proof-of-work chain we cover, despite creating nothing.

The comparison that actually bites is with exchange tokens and fee-burning networks. An exchange token typically pairs its distribution with a quarterly buyback and burn, so the float can shrink even while allocations unlock. Ethereum burns a share of every transaction fee, which means heavy usage directly removes supply. World has neither lever. World Chain charges gas in ETH, so no amount of adoption removes a single WLD, and there is no buyback contract to fund from revenue. That leaves the cap doing all the work, and a cap only bounds the eventual total — it does nothing about the pace of release. Among pre-minted distribution tokens, the closest structural analogues are large ecosystem-fund L1s whose foundations sell treasury tokens to fund operations; WLD sits at the heavier end of that group because its foundation controls three quarters of the supply outright.

The one genuinely favourable comparison is direction of travel. The **Jul 24 2026** step cut the aggregate release rate by **43%**, from about **5.1M WLD** a day to about **2.9M**, and that cut is encoded in the vesting contracts rather than voted on, so it cannot be reversed by a governance decision. Very few heavy unlockers have a scheduled, contract-enforced step down. WLD does, and it has two more coming in 2029 and 2032.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the World Foundation release safe. It holds **1,398M WLD** and it deploys in lumps rather than a stream, so a single day can move the reading by more than a month of unlocks did — the **Jul 24 2026** sale is the template. Second, whether another strategic sale lands; the Foundation raised money this way in March 2026 and again in July 2026, so the pattern is roughly quarterly. Third, the community vesting contract, which releases **1.6M WLD** a day to that safe and has an unclaimed balance already accruing. Fourth, the investor and team custody cluster, whose **929M WLD** is released on paper faster than it moves on chain — a step change in withdrawal behaviour would be visible immediately. Fifth, the four cliff contracts holding **44.4M WLD**, which unseal on **Jul 24 2027** and are quiet until then.

## Summary

World (WLD) is a hard-capped token that cannot mint and cannot burn, and it is still growing its tradable supply at **+9.01%** per 90 days, projected at **+7.01%** for the next 90. The mechanism is distribution, not issuance: **150.0M WLD** released to early investors and the team on a contract-enforced schedule, and **220.4M WLD** deployed by the World Foundation out of the community allocation, offset only by the **44.4M WLD** that went straight back into year-long lock contracts. The key risk is that the Foundation still holds **1,398M WLD** in an unscheduled treasury and sells it in lumps, which is why our reading runs **1.22 percentage points** above the classified float. The ceiling is real — **10,000M WLD**, never more — but with roughly **6,050M** still outside the market on a calendar running to **2038**, that ceiling is a long way from being a constraint.
