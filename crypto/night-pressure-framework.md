## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window running August 1 2026 to October 30 2026, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **NIGHT at 0.00% net** — a genuinely flat supply. Every ledger row is zero: there is no protocol mint, no in-window vesting cliff, no buyback and no burn. Our supply monitor reads the realized last-90-day change at **−0.01%**, a difference of about **0.01 percentage points** — well inside tolerance, so **no monitor-gap chip** ships. The window sits entirely after Midnight's federated mainnet went live on March 31 2026, which confirms the block-reward reserve is not yet emitting to the market. NIGHT is best characterised as a **fair-launched, hard-capped token with a large dormant overhang** — steady today, with the direction of travel set by when that overhang begins to move.

## Sell pressure: where new NIGHT comes from

The honest answer is that no new NIGHT comes from anywhere right now. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**. All 24B NIGHT were minted up front and the Midnight protocol has no mint function, so new coins can never be created above the cap. Block producers are paid from a pre-minted reserve on a disinflationary curve, but on the federated mainnet that reserve is not yet reaching the market, so no fresh supply lands on the float.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is also **zero** in net terms, and this is the subtle part of NIGHT. The community airdrop — the Glacier Drop and the Scavenger Mine, roughly **4.5B** NIGHT claimed — thaws in four equal quarterly tranches through December 2026, and one tranche falls inside this window. But those coins are already counted in circulating supply and have been tradable since the token generation event, so each thaw frees **already-circulating** NIGHT into a sellable form rather than adding new supply. It is a liquidity event, not an issuance event — and there is no team, seed or investor vesting at all, because NIGHT was a fair launch. Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is zero as a flow, though it carries the whole overhang story below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is zero, because no bankruptcy estate or court distribution applies to NIGHT.

## Buy pressure: where new NIGHT goes

There is no active buy pressure either, which is why the net reads flat rather than negative. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**: no protocol or treasury mechanism buys NIGHT back off the open market. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero** and always will be: NIGHT is never burned. Transaction fees on Midnight consume DUST, a separate non-transferable resource that holding NIGHT generates over time — the NIGHT supply itself has no burn path. Buy #3 — foundation buy — and Buy #4 — new long-term lock — are both zero, with no discretionary market buying and no new escrow of circulating NIGHT announced in the window. Staking NIGHT earns rewards and faster DUST generation, but it is not a supply lock. With every sell row and every buy row at zero, the framework's net is exactly flat.

## Foundation and overhang

This is where NIGHT's real supply question lives. Roughly **7.4B** NIGHT — the gap between the 24B cap and the ~16.6B circulating — sits outside the float as a loom. It is not a team or investor cliff: Midnight had no token sale, no VC allocation and no team-held pre-mine, so the whole 24B was earmarked for public distribution. What remains off-market is a **pre-minted block-reward reserve** that pays block producers on a declining curve, the **unclaimed** Glacier Drop, Scavenger Mine and Lost-and-Found allocations, and an on-chain **treasury** funded by unused block rewards. None of these has a dated release inside the next 90 days, so each is booked at zero and monitored. The trigger is mechanical: if the reserve begins paying public block rewards, or the treasury or the Lost-and-Found pool deploys tokens between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and NIGHT stops reading flat.

## How NIGHT compares to other fully-minted, hard-capped chains

NIGHT belongs to the class of **fair-launched, hard-capped tokens released through a community airdrop** — closer to a distributed governance token than to a continuous-emission proof-of-stake chain. Unlike an uncapped chain that mints new coins every block, NIGHT can never grow past 24B; unlike an exchange token with a fee-burn or a revenue buyback, it has no mechanism to shrink supply either, so it cannot go deflationary. Its supply can only travel in one direction over time — up, toward the cap — as the dormant reserve and treasury gradually enter the float.

The sharper contrast is with a token whose whole float is already liquid, like a fully-distributed fixed-supply coin. NIGHT looks flat today for the same reason those coins do — nothing is being minted, bought or burned — but it carries a much larger latent overhang: about **31%** of the cap is still off-market. So while the current reading matches a static-supply token, the forward risk is closer to a chain mid-way through its reward emission, just funded from a pre-minted reserve rather than fresh issuance. Because that reserve's reward curve is disinflationary, when emission does begin it starts higher and tapers, rather than arriving as one large unlock.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the mainnet decentralization milestone: the moment Midnight moves past its federated Kūkolu phase and the block-reward reserve starts paying public rewards is the single event that flips Sell #1 from zero to a live emission. Watch the quarterly Glacier Drop thaw running through December 2026 — it does not change the circulating count, but it governs how much claimed NIGHT becomes freely sellable each quarter, and the next tranche lands inside this window. Watch the on-chain treasury for any first deployment. And watch the unclaimed Lost-and-Found pool, whose allocations can drift into circulation as late claims are processed across the multi-year recovery window.

## Summary

NIGHT is a fair-launched, hard-capped community token with no mint, no buyback and no burn, so the Pressure Framework reads it at 0.00% net over the next 90 days — flat, and matching our monitor's −0.01% to within noise. The real question is not today's flow but the ~7.4B non-circulating overhang: a pre-minted block-reward reserve, an on-chain treasury, and the unclaimed airdrop allocations. As long as those stay locked, NIGHT's supply is steady; the first sustained reserve emission or treasury deployment is what would turn this flat read into mild inflation.
