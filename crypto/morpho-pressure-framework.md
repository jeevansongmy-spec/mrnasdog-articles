# MORPHO Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Morpho's MORPHO token has a coin count that never moves: all **1,000,000,000 MORPHO** were created once and the supply figure read identically at both ends of the 90 days to **Aug 22 2026**. What moves is ownership. Over that window **25.55M MORPHO** crossed from Morpho-controlled wallets into the market — **21.23M** spent by the Morpho Association treasury, **3.21M** of lending rewards actually claimed, and **1.12M** of locked allocations converting into the transferable token. Against that, Morpho offers nothing: no buyback, no burn, no lock, and a lending fee switch that has never been activated. On a circulating base of **657.07M MORPHO** the Pressure Framework reads **+3.89%** net, easing to **+2.65%** next quarter as the Association's one-off grant deployment decays. MORPHO is **a capped token whose active float keeps growing, with no offsetting mechanism of any kind**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 22 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **MORPHO at +3.89% net**: sell pressure of **25.55M MORPHO** against buy pressure of **zero**, on a circulating base of **657.07M MORPHO**. Our supply monitor reads **+3.93%** for the same window, a gap of **0.04 percentage points** — far inside the half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships on this build. That agreement is worth pausing on, because it only appears once the reward wallets are read correctly: booking the full **6.0M MORPHO** that governance moved into the reward multisig on **Jun 22 2026** would have pushed the framework to **+4.31%** and opened a gap in the wrong direction. Morpho is best labelled **capped by governance policy rather than by code, and inflationary on the float purely through treasury deployment**.

## Sell pressure: where new MORPHO comes from

Nowhere, in the literal sense — and the first job of any MORPHO reading is to prove that, because Morpho ships **two** token contracts. The original MORPHO is non-transferable and still carries its own near-1B balance; the live, tradable MORPHO is a separate contract, and a migration wrapper swaps one for the other at **1:1**. Read the wrong contract and a wallet can show **0.00** at both ends of a window while holding **10,000,000** coins in the other unit, which is exactly what five large holders do today. Read both, and the answer is clean: the supply figure on each contract is identical at both window ends, so Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**. It is tagged as a checked row rather than a permanent one for a reason: the live MORPHO contract is upgradeable and its implementation carries an owner-only mint function, with the Morpho governance multisig as the owner. The **1B** ceiling is a promise governance keeps, not a limit the code enforces.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **1.12M MORPHO**, and it is the realised figure rather than the calendar one. The migration wrapper is a genuinely readable escrow: a locked allocation is untradable until its holder swaps the legacy token for the live one, so the wrapper's balance is the gate. Over the window its live-token leg fell by **1,115,972** while its legacy leg rose by exactly the same amount — the two halves of one identity, which is what makes the number trustworthy. The published calendar says far more should have moved: a strategic-partner tranche of **67M MORPHO** alone runs a two-year linear vest ending **Nov 21 2026**, roughly **8.3M** a quarter, and third-party unlock trackers quote around **4M** a month. It did not move. **391.29M MORPHO** is still sitting inside the wrapper unswapped, so the vesting calendar describes an entitlement, not supply reaching the market, and the framework books what actually left.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **21.23M MORPHO** and is the whole story of this quarter. The Morpho Association treasury was granted **150M MORPHO** by the DAO for a multi-year support programme and received it on **Apr 30 2026**, on top of **30M** a week earlier. It then front-loaded the deployment hard: **52.2M** in April, **29.5M** in May, **12.4M** in June, **4.8M** in July and **1.4M** so far in August. Two independent reads agree to the coin — the treasury balance fell from **111.32M** to **90.10M**, and a complete sweep of its **80** transfers over the window nets to the identical **21.23M** across **41** destinations. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: Morpho has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution anywhere in its picture.

A fifth row carries the reward stream, because MORPHO rewards are a claim, not a payment. Governance funds a reward set and lenders and borrowers pull from it on their own timing, so the amount funded and the amount that reaches the market are different numbers. Governance moved **6.0M MORPHO** into the reward multisig on **Jun 22 2026** — a transfer between two Morpho-controlled wallets that reached no market at all. Reading the whole reward set instead, across both networks, shows it went from **1.99M** to **4.78M**, meaning it kept **2.79M** of that top-up. Sell #5 is therefore **3.21M MORPHO** claimed, and the **4.78M** still parked there is an overhang, not a flow.

## Buy pressure: where new MORPHO goes

It does not go anywhere. All four canonical buy rows are **zero**, and each is zero by mechanism rather than by a quiet quarter. Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **zero** because Morpho has no buyback and no revenue with which to run one: the Morpho Blue fee switch, capped at a quarter of borrower interest, has never been activated, and a protocol handling billions of dollars of deposits still routes none of it to the MORPHO token. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **zero** — there is no burn path in the Morpho design, and the supply figure on both token contracts was unchanged to the decimal across the window. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**: every Morpho-controlled wallet was a net sender, and the governance multisig's legacy holding sat flat at **290.42M MORPHO** throughout. Buy #4, a new long-term lock, is **zero** — no staking contract, no vote-escrow and no new escrow was deployed, and the **$175M** strategic round the Morpho Association announced on **Jun 9 2026**, structured as a MORPHO token purchase, published no lockup terms at all, so there is nothing verifiable to count as locked.

## Foundation and overhang

Four overhangs are tracked, and together they are larger than the counted float. The Morpho governance treasury holds **290.42M MORPHO** in the legacy, untradable token plus **13.58M** already in transferable form across two networks; the legacy leg did not move at all in the window, and it moves only by governance vote. The migration wrapper holds **391.29M MORPHO** against allocations that have not converted — the governance treasury's own legacy claim, plus founder, strategic-partner and contributor allocations vesting out to **May 17 2028**. The Morpho Association treasury still holds **90.10M** of its grant after a quarter of heavy spending. And the reward wallets hold **4.78M** that is funded, allocated and claimable at any moment. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 — or Sell #2 for the wrapper and Sell #5 for the reward wallets — at the next refresh.

## How MORPHO compares to other DeFi lending tokens

MORPHO sits in the same family as the other large lending-protocol governance tokens, and the family splits on one question: does protocol revenue reach the token? Tokens whose DAO has switched on a fee stream and routed it into open-market purchases can run a negative net even while unlocks continue, because the buy side is mechanical. MORPHO has the revenue but not the switch — the fee cap exists in the contract, the DAO has never turned it on, and commercial income has so far been directed to the Association rather than to token holders. That leaves MORPHO one-sided by design in a way that a fee-switched peer is not.

The second axis is the cap. MORPHO looks like a hard-capped asset next to an uncapped, continuously-emitting network token, and in flow terms it behaves like one — no coin has ever been minted beyond the original **1B**. But the cap is enforced by an owner-controlled mint function on an upgradeable contract, not by protocol code, which places MORPHO closer to a governance-capped exchange or foundation token than to a protocol-capped asset with a fixed emission schedule. The practical difference only matters if governance ever changes its mind; the practical similarity is that both classes run their real inflation through treasury and vesting release rather than through issuance.

The third axis is the wrapper, and here MORPHO is genuinely unusual. Most tokens have one contract and one float. MORPHO has a legacy contract, a live contract and a swap wrapper, so the market's circulating figure and the on-chain transferable figure disagree by a wide margin — **657.07M** counted against roughly **608.71M** that has actually been converted. Any comparison drawn from a headline supply number alone will mis-state MORPHO against its peers in both directions.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Morpho Association treasury balance. It stood at **90.10M MORPHO** on **Aug 22 2026** and its monthly spend has fallen every month since April; the forward figure of **13.1M** assumes the decay holds, and a single large batch would break it. Second, the strategic-partner tranche that completes its vest on **Nov 21 2026** — the calendar entitlement has been running far ahead of realised conversion all year, so the number to watch is the wrapper balance, not the tracker. Third, the lending fee switch: activating it is the single change that would move any buy row off zero, and the DAO has repeatedly deferred it on legal grounds. Fourth, the reward wallets, which held **4.78M** claimable at the window close and are topped up in irregular multi-million batches — the last on **Jun 22 2026**. Fifth, the terms of the **$175M** strategic round announced **Jun 9 2026**; if a lockup schedule is ever disclosed, part of this quarter's treasury outflow would reclassify.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads MORPHO at **+3.89%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 22 2026**, easing to **+2.65%** over the next 90. No MORPHO has ever been minted beyond the original **1B**, so every unit of that growth is already-created supply changing hands — **21.23M** deployed from the Morpho Association's multi-year treasury grant, **3.21M** of rewards claimed and **1.12M** of vesting actually converted. The key risk is that the offsetting side is empty by design: Morpho runs no buyback and no burn, and the fee switch that could fund either has never been activated, so nothing absorbs the release. The ceiling is **1B MORPHO**, but it is a governance ceiling on an upgradeable contract with an owner-only mint function, and **391.29M** of the supply still sits inside a migration wrapper waiting to become tradable.
