Monero mints new XMR through proof-of-work mining and nothing else, at a tail emission of **0.6 XMR** per block that never halves and never ends — which is why Monero has **no supply cap at all**. Over the last 90 days the Monero chain sealed **64,720** blocks and issued **38,832 XMR**, while every buy-side mechanism the framework looks for — buyback, fee burn, foundation buying, long-term lock — reads exactly **zero**, because Monero has no premine, no treasury and no protocol revenue. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+0.21%** net for the window and **+0.21%** for the next 90 days, against our supply monitor at **+0.44%** — a gap of **0.24 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no data flag ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 3 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **XMR at +0.21% net**, with the forward view also at **+0.21%**. Our supply monitor reads the realised change at **+0.44%**, so the gap is **0.24 percentage points** — inside the framework's tolerance, and no monitor-gap flag is warranted. The reconciliation is measured, not assumed. A chain read at both ends of the window puts Monero at block **3,689,427** on **Jun 5 2026** and block **3,754,146** on **Sep 3 2026**, a span of **64,720** blocks in **90.0 days**. That works out to **120.1 seconds** a block against Monero's **120-second** target, so the chain ran fractionally slow, and because the Monero reward is paid per block with nothing in the protocol rescaling it for pace, the target alone would have claimed **80** blocks that were never mined. Multiplying observed blocks by the observed block reward gives the issuance directly. XMR is a **quiet chain with permanent, flat, slowly-diluting tail emission**.

## Sell pressure: where new XMR comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **38,832 XMR** and it is the only non-zero row anywhere on the Monero ledger. Monero is proof-of-work, mined with RandomX, so the coinbase is the sole mint: no staking emission, no treasury issuance, no bridge minting. The reward itself was measured rather than looked up. Every one of the **64,720** block headers inside the window was read this build, and the minimum reward across the entire window is exactly **0.6 XMR** with no block paying less — the **3,709** blocks that carried no transactions at all pay that figure and nothing more. Everything above **0.6** in a Monero block is transaction fees, and Monero hands its fees to the miner rather than destroying them, so fees recycle coins that already exist and are not issuance. Multiplying **64,720** measured blocks by the **0.6 XMR** tail emission gives **38,832 XMR** of genuinely new supply, which is **+0.21%** of the float, or roughly **0.84%** a year — and that annual figure falls a little every year, because the tail emission is a fixed number of coins divided by a growing supply.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each for a reason written into Monero's history rather than into a schedule. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because Monero launched in **April 2014** as a fair, pre-announced launch with no premine, no instamine, no sale and no insider allocation — there was never anything set aside that could vest, which is also why no unlock tracker publishes a Monero calendar. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because no foundation, labs entity or DAO ever received an allocation; every XMR in existence was paid to a miner. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** because no estate, trustee distribution or escrowed tranche exists in Monero's history, and the chain has no contract layer in which a lock could even be written.

## Buy pressure: where new XMR goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **0**, and on Monero the zero has to be proved differently than on a transparent chain. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0** because Monero retains no protocol revenue whatsoever — the whole block reward and every fee go to the miner — so no income stream could fund a buyback and no entity holds a budget to buy with. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** for the same reason: the Monero project is volunteer contributors funded by donations, with no company balance sheet behind it. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** because a proof-of-work chain with no staking, no bonding and no contract layer offers nothing a coin could be locked into.

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the interesting zero. The usual test reads a dead address at both ends of the window, but Monero conceals amounts and uses one-time stealth output keys, so **no address on this chain publishes a balance** — a burn address included. That surface simply does not exist here, so the proof comes from the emission identity instead. Across the window the Monero coinbase paid out **39,567 XMR**, which is the **38,832 XMR** base reward plus **735 XMR** of transaction fees stacked on top of it. Had those fees been destroyed, the coinbase would have carried the base alone and the supply would have risen by less than the full reward. It rose by exactly the full reward, so the fees were moved to miners and stayed in circulation. Nothing on Monero burns.

## Foundation and overhang

Monero's overhang is close to genuinely empty, which is unusual enough to state plainly. There is no foundation treasury, no labs holding, no DAO treasury, no buyback accumulation wallet and no bankruptcy residual — those categories do not exist here rather than going unmeasured, because the fair launch never created them. The one community-controlled pool is the Monero crowdfunding system, which pays contributors from coins donated by users. At the last walk on **Aug 12 2026** it carried six open proposals seeking **371.4 XMR** in total, of which **84.3 XMR** had been raised — under **0.0005%** of circulating supply. It is donated, already-circulating XMR, so a payout changes hands rather than adding supply. Its balance cannot be read on-chain, again because Monero publishes no address balances, so it is tracked by walking the published proposal pages instead. If that pool's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How XMR compares to other privacy and hard-capped chains

The mechanism that separates Monero from a hard-capped chain is the tail emission. A capped chain halves its subsidy on a schedule until issuance reaches zero, at which point miners are paid only by fees; Monero deliberately refused that endpoint and fixed the reward at **0.6 XMR** per block forever. The trade is explicit. A capped chain buys a finite supply story and inherits a security-budget question; Monero buys a permanent, predictable security budget and gives up the finite supply story. Monero has **no maximum supply**, and any description of XMR as fixed-supply is simply wrong.

The size of the trade is small and shrinking. A fixed **0.6 XMR** per block against a float of roughly **18.80M** is about **0.84%** a year today, and because the numerator is a constant while the denominator grows, that percentage declines every year without any protocol change. The reason it can never be offset is the fee economy. Monero's transaction fees over this window ran at **735 XMR** — around **$1.5M** a year against a **$9.5B** market cap, roughly **0.016%**, which places Monero near the floor of the fee-to-market-cap field across everything the framework tracks. Fees are **1.9%** of the window's issuance, so even a hypothetical burn of every fee would offset under **2%** of new supply. That is the structural reason a fee-burning smart-contract chain can print a negative net number and Monero cannot.

Against the other privacy chains the difference is custody rather than emission. Several were launched with a founders' reward, a development fund or a block-reward split routed to an entity, which creates a schedule and a discretionary holder the framework has to enumerate. Monero has neither, so seven of its eight ledger rows are zero for reasons the protocol enforces, not for reasons a treasury has chosen. That is what makes this ledger unusually easy to verify and unusually hard to surprise.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the block pace. Issuance here is paid per block with nothing compensating for interval drift, so a sustained move away from the **120.1 seconds** measured this window moves the number directly — a chain running fast issues more than the target implies, and one running slow issues less. Second, the pending privacy upgrade. The Monero network is still running consensus version **16** with no activation height scheduled as of **Sep 3 2026**, despite several reports claiming a hard fork had already shipped; when it does activate it is emission-neutral by design, so it should change nothing on this page, and the value of the watch is confirming exactly that. Third, the crowdfunding pool, walked every couple of weeks — a large payout would be the first thing capable of putting a number in Sell #3. Fourth, the supply monitor's trailing column, which is derived from market data rather than from the chain and can drift by a couple of tenths on price noise alone; its own emission-based forward reading currently agrees with the framework to four decimal places.

## Summary

Monero is a fair-launch proof-of-work privacy chain whose only supply mechanism is mining, at a permanent tail emission of **0.6 XMR** per block that never halves and never ends — so XMR is uncapped, not fixed-supply. Measured block by block, that produced **38,832 XMR** over the last 90 days, **+0.21%** of supply, with an empty buy ledger and the same **+0.21%** projected forward because a constant reward makes the forward number equal the trailing one. The risk is that the number can only go one way: with no burn, no buyback and no lock, and with fees at **1.9%** of issuance, Monero has no mechanism capable of producing a negative net figure. The offsetting fact is that there is no ceiling to breach and no cliff to fear — the dilution is small, permanent, fully visible, and falls a little every year.
