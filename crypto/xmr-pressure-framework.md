# XMR Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Monero (XMR) adds new coins at a fixed pace and removes none. Every Monero block pays its miner a tail emission of **0.6 XMR**, and the Monero chain produced **64,751 blocks** in the last 90 days, so **38,850 XMR** of new supply reached miners, against **zero** removed — Monero has no burn, no buyback, no treasury and no vesting. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads XMR at **+0.21% net** over 90 days against a supply-monitor reading of **+0.17%**, a gap of **0.03 percentage points**. Monero supply grows by roughly **0.84% a year**, a share that shrinks slowly as the supply it is measured against keeps growing.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 24 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **XMR at +0.21% net**: the Monero tail emission added **38,850 XMR** to a circulating supply of **18.81M XMR**, and nothing on the buy side took any away. The independent supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **+0.17%**. The gap is **0.03 percentage points**, well inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so XMR ships with **no data-conflict flag**. The forward column reads **+0.21%**, because the Monero tail emission is fixed per block and the chain holds its two-minute rhythm, so the next 90 days look almost exactly like the last. The label for XMR is **perpetual low inflation by design**: a fair-launched proof-of-work coin that pays miners a small, permanent subsidy instead of ever reaching a hard cap.

## Sell pressure: where new XMR comes from

All of it comes from one place. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **38,850 XMR** — the Monero tail emission. Since the main emission curve ran out in May 2022, every Monero block has paid a base reward of **0.6 XMR**, fixed per block rather than per unit of time. That made the block count the thing to measure, not assume: the Monero chain produced **64,751 blocks** in the 90 days, a realised interval of **120.09 seconds** against the 120-second target, so the chain ran a hair slow and the window issued **49 fewer blocks** than the calendar implies. Monero does not compensate for that drift — difficulty retargets toward two minutes but no reward is scaled to make up missed blocks — so the measured count is the issuance. It was then read block by block rather than multiplied out. On **4,747 blocks** a miner filled the block past the size the protocol treats as free, and the protocol cut that block's new coins as a penalty; those cuts left **1.07 XMR** that the full rate would have paid never created at all. The figure booked is what was actually minted: ten blocks were re-checked coin by coin — new coins paid out minus the fees inside them — and every one matched to the last unit, and the total ties to the chain's own running emission count.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because Monero never had a vesting schedule: it launched in April 2014 with no premine and no allocation to a team, a foundation or investors, so there is nothing locked to release. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**. The Monero Project keeps a donation-funded General Fund that pays contributors through its community crowdfunding system, but every XMR in it was mined, spent into the float and then donated, and the whole issued Monero supply already counts as circulating — so a payout from that fund moves coins between holders and adds nothing new. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: no Monero estate distributes coins on a court schedule and no long-dated lock is unwinding.

## Buy pressure: where new XMR goes

Nowhere, and the empty buy ledger is itself the finding. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: Monero has no company, no revenue and no treasury to buy with. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** — every Monero transaction fee goes to the miner who includes it, and none is destroyed. The Monero miners collected **714 XMR** in fees across the window, and all of it stayed in circulation. The burn side was checked on both surfaces rather than assumed. There is no dead address to read: Monero hides balances, and no address is recognised as provably unspendable. The supply surface is the one that works — Monero coinbase amounts are public, so new supply can be read block by block, and the chain's running emission total rose by exactly the minted amount across the window. Had anything been destroyed, the float would have grown by less than the minted amount; it did not.

The penalty on oversized blocks is not booked here either. Those coins were never created, so they never entered the float and cannot leave it; they are netted inside Sell #1 instead, which keeps each coin counted once. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**: no entity accumulates XMR from the market on a programme. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**: Monero is mined, not staked, so there is no staking contract, no lockup and no validator bond to absorb supply.

## Foundation and overhang

Monero has no foundation treasury, no team allocation and no unscheduled reserve. The only project-linked balance is the Monero General Fund, a donation wallet the core team holds in escrow for crowdfunded work. Its incoming donations can be checked with a key the project has published, but outgoing payments cannot, so its balance is **opaque** and is tracked on a bi-weekly walk of the crowdfunding site rather than read from the chain. It does not change the ledger either way: every coin in it came out of circulation and goes back into circulation. The size check makes the same point from the other side — the whole issued Monero supply already counts as circulating, so no wallet on the chain sits outside the float. If that ever changed — a premine revealed, a treasury created by hard fork, or a fund holding newly issued coins — its outflows would enter Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How XMR compares to other proof-of-work and privacy coins

Monero sits between the two proof-of-work models. Bitcoin, Litecoin and Zcash follow halving schedules toward a hard cap: their issuance is higher than Monero's today and steps down every four years until it reaches zero, leaving miners paid by fees alone. Monero chose the opposite end state. Its tail emission never stops, so the dollar value of mining security does not depend on fee markets developing, but the coin never becomes fixed supply. Measured as a share of supply, Monero's tail emission is already low — about **0.84%** a year — and it keeps falling slowly, because the same fixed number of coins is spread over a larger base every year.

Against other privacy coins, the difference is structural rather than cosmetic. Zcash makes privacy optional and funds development from part of its block reward, while Monero makes privacy compulsory on every transaction and funds development only through donations — no protocol fund, no dev tax, no foundation cut. Against proof-of-stake chains that pay validators from a percentage-based mint, Monero's emission does not scale with supply or with how much is staked; it is a flat number of coins per block. And unlike chains that burn part of every fee, Monero destroys nothing: the fees go to miners in full, so the whole issuance is net issuance. That is the trade Monero made — a small, predictable, permanent sell pressure, with nothing on the buy side to offset it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the FCMP++ hard fork: the Monero Research Lab's **Sep 16 2026** meeting still had the final beta and code merges outstanding and set no activation date, so no fork is expected to land inside the window. FCMP++ changes how Monero transactions prove their inputs, not how much XMR is issued; when a date is set, the reading will be re-checked for any change to block size limits that could alter the block-size penalty. Second, block production: the Monero tail emission is paid per block, so a sustained move away from the two-minute rhythm — for example if a large miner joins or leaves — would move issuance in step. Third, mining concentration: after a large mining operation ran a selfish-mining campaign against Monero in 2025, Monero developers are working on defences, and a reorganisation or a change to difficulty rules would be re-measured, although neither creates new coins on its own. Fourth, access: the EU rule barring regulated platforms from handling privacy coins takes effect on **Jul 10 2027**, outside this window; further exchange delistings affect where XMR trades, not how much XMR exists.

The structural mechanism is a fair-launched proof-of-work chain with no premine, no treasury and no fee burn, paying miners a permanent subsidy in place of a hard cap. The key risk is not a surprise unlock — there is nothing to unlock — but that Monero's issuance can never be offset, so every coin mined is net new supply. There is no ceiling: Monero supply grows forever, at a rate of about **0.84%** a year that edges lower as the base grows.
