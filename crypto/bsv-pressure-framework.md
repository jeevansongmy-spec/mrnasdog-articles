# BSV Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing slowly, projected to keep easing

Bitcoin SV (BSV) minted **40.3K BSV** over the last 90 days — 12,891 blocks at a fixed proof-of-work subsidy of **3.125 BSV** — and absorbed none of it, because Bitcoin SV has no buyback, no fee burn and no treasury. The framework reads **+0.20% net** on a **20.07M** circulating base against a **21M** hard cap, about **95.5%** mined. The supply monitor reads **+0.15%** — a 0.055-point agreement, comfortably inside tolerance.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from May 7 2026 to Aug 5 2026, the framework reads **Bitcoin SV at +0.20% net inflation**: mining emission of **40.3K BSV** against exactly zero on the buy side. It projects the same **+0.20%** over the next 90 days — the block subsidy does not change again until the halving near block 1,050,000, expected around Apr 2028. The supply monitor reads **+0.15%**, leaving a gap of **0.055 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships on the BSV page. Bitcoin SV is a **quiet chain with no discretionary supply**: every BSV in existence came out of a coinbase output, the issuance curve has not changed since the 2018 fork from Bitcoin Cash, and nothing on the chain can vote a new coin into being.

## Sell pressure: where new BSV comes from

There is exactly one source, and it is the block reward. Sell #1 (protocol inflation) booked **40.3K BSV** — and that figure is a count, not an estimate. Bitcoin SV targets a ten-minute block, which would imply 144 blocks a day and 40,500 BSV over 90 days, but the chain does not run exactly on target. Reading the chain end to end for this window gives **12,891 blocks** between height 948,010 and the tip at height 960,901, or about **143 blocks per day** — the network ran about 0.5% slow, because Bitcoin SV shares its proof-of-work algorithm with Bitcoin and Bitcoin Cash and hashrate migrates between those chains as relative profitability shifts. Its difficulty algorithm then chases that moving hashrate, so real block timing drifts around the target in a way a nominal 144 never captures. At **3.125 BSV** per block — the subsidy since the Apr 2024 halving that opened the fifth issuance era — 12,891 blocks is 40,284 BSV. The tip sits well inside the 840,000-to-1,050,000 halving era, so no reward change happened inside the window.

Every other sell row is zero, and each is zero for a structural reason. Sell #2 (vesting unlocks) is **0** and always will be: Bitcoin SV forked from Bitcoin Cash on Nov 15 2018 by inheriting the existing ledger one coin for one coin. There was no premine, no team allocation, no investor round, and therefore no vesting contract to unlock. Sell #3 (Foundation and unscheduled unlocks) is **0** because Bitcoin SV has no protocol-level foundation allocation and no diversion out of the coinbase — the BSV Association, the Swiss non-profit that stewards the protocol, is funded by its backers rather than by a coin allocation, and publishes no treasury wallet. Sell #4 (long-term locked or bankruptcy) is **0** for the window, but it is the one row worth watching rather than dismissing, and the next section explains why.

## Buy pressure: where new BSV goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows are **0**, and Bitcoin SV is unusually clean about it. Buy #1 (programmatic buyback) is **0** because there is no protocol revenue and no treasury — the chain collects nothing and holds nothing, so there is no cash flow that could be pointed at the market. Buy #2 (protocol fee burn) is **0**: transaction fees on Bitcoin SV are paid straight to the miner inside the same coinbase output that carries the subsidy, so a fee is a transfer, never a destruction. The Apr 7 2026 Chronicle upgrade, which restored original-protocol scripting semantics and lifted script-size limits, activated before this window opened and changed what Bitcoin SV contracts can express while leaving the monetary rules untouched — no burn, no fund, no issuance change. Buy #3 (Foundation buy) is **0** because no foundation entity holds a protocol allocation or runs an accumulation programme. Buy #4 (new long-term lock) is **0** because Bitcoin SV is pure proof-of-work: there is no staking contract, no masternode collateral and no lockup to take coins off the float.

The result is that Bitcoin SV prints its inflation at face value. On a chain with a buyback or a fee burn, the headline emission number overstates what actually reaches the market. Here it does not. The **40.3K BSV** mined in the window is **40.3K BSV** of new float, and the net is simply the gross. In-window ecosystem activity — the Jul 21 2026 relaunch of the Twetch on-chain social app — drives transactions and fees to miners, but adds nothing to either side of the supply ledger.

## Foundation and overhang

Bitcoin SV has no team-controlled overhang in the usual sense. There is no foundation treasury, no labs multisig, no unscheduled allocation and no buyback accumulation wallet, because there was never an allocation to create one from. That leaves a single identified overhang, and it sits outside the protocol entirely: the **Mt. Gox rehabilitation estate**. When Bitcoin SV split from Bitcoin Cash in Nov 2018, every Bitcoin Cash balance — including the estate's roughly **142,846 BCH** — was duplicated into a matching Bitcoin SV balance, and the Tokyo court's rehabilitation plan directs the trustee to liquidate non-Bitcoin, non-Bitcoin-Cash crypto assets into cash for creditors.

The estate's remaining Bitcoin SV balance is not separately disclosed, and no dated BSV disposal has been announced. The trustee's repayment deadline has been extended to **Oct 31 2026** — just past the end of the next 90-day window. Because there is a single, ambiguous source and no observed on-chain movement, the framework treats the balance as opaque and the row as zero rather than inventing a quantum. The overhang is monitored by walking the estate's public disclosures. If that balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #4 at the next refresh.

## How BSV compares to other capped proof-of-work chains

Bitcoin SV belongs to the small family of hard-capped proof-of-work chains whose entire supply schedule is a halving curve: Bitcoin itself, Bitcoin Cash, and Litecoin. Within that family the mechanism is identical and only the position on the curve differs. Bitcoin SV is about **95.5% mined** with under **1M BSV** left to issue, which is why its quarterly inflation prints at **+0.20%** rather than the multi-percent figures uncapped chains post. Bitcoin SV and Bitcoin Cash share the same fork lineage and the same 3.125 subsidy, so they run almost the same rate; Litecoin, on a faster 2.5-minute block and a different halving calendar, runs roughly double. The structural point is that all of them are already close enough to their caps that new issuance is a rounding error against float — the supply question for a capped proof-of-work coin has largely been answered by arithmetic.

The sharper contrast is against chains that have a buy side at all. An uncapped smart-contract chain with a fee burn can run negative net supply in a busy quarter because the burn scales with usage; an exchange token with a quarterly buyback removes float on a schedule regardless of usage. Bitcoin SV has neither lever, so its net can never go below zero. Its floor is its ceiling: the best reading Bitcoin SV can ever produce is a small positive number shrinking toward zero as the halvings compound. That is a genuine structural difference, not a shortfall — a capped chain with no discretionary mechanism is maximally predictable, which is exactly what this metric rewards, but predictability is not the same as scarcity increasing.

The third comparison worth drawing is governance surface. A chain with a treasury, a DAO or a vesting cliff carries a standing risk that a vote changes the supply picture. Bitcoin SV carries almost none of that. Its protocol is stewarded rather than governed on-chain, and its upgrades — the Apr 2026 Chronicle restoration among them — have targeted scripting and node capacity, never issuance. For this metric, the absence of a lever is the finding.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Mt. Gox trustee deadline of **Oct 31 2026**. It falls just past the next window, but any Bitcoin SV disposal by the estate before then would be the single largest supply event available to Bitcoin SV and would populate Sell #4. Second, the observed block rate. This window ran at about 143 blocks a day against a 144 target; a sustained hashrate shift toward or away from Bitcoin SV would move the emission figure by a percent or two in either direction, which is the only real variable in the entire ledger. Third, any protocol upgrade following Chronicle — the item to check is whether a proposal ever touches issuance rather than scripting or node capacity; none has in the chain's history. Fourth, the halving at block height 1,050,000, roughly **89,099 blocks** away and expected around Apr 2028; it is far outside the window but it is the event that eventually halves this page's headline number. Fifth, any first appearance of a corporate treasury or buyback programme denominated in BSV — none exists today, and a search of the last 90 days found none.

## Summary

Bitcoin SV is a fair-launch, hard-capped proof-of-work chain whose only supply mechanism is the block subsidy, and the framework reads it at **+0.20% net** over 90 days — **40.3K BSV** mined across 12,891 observed blocks, with nothing absorbed on the buy side because no buyback, burn, treasury or staking lock exists. It projects the same **+0.20%** forward, since the subsidy holds until the Apr 2028 halving. The supply monitor reads **+0.15%**, a 0.055-point gap that stays inside tolerance. The structural mechanism is among the cleanest in the catalog: no premine, no vesting, no foundation allocation, no governance lever over issuance, and about **95.5%** of the **21M** cap already mined. The key risk is not protocol issuance but the undisclosed Bitcoin SV balance still held by the Mt. Gox rehabilitation estate, whose repayment deadline is **Oct 31 2026** — the only path by which a large block of BSV could reach the market without a single new coin being created.
