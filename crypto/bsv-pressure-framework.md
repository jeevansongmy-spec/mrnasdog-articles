# BSV Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Bitcoin SV has one of the cleanest supply ledgers the Pressure Framework tracks: mining is the only thing that makes a BSV, and nothing at all destroys one. Over the last 90 days the Bitcoin SV chain minted **40,322 BSV** across **12,903** counted blocks at a **3.125 BSV** block subsidy, against zero buyback, zero burn, zero vesting and zero new locked supply — a net **+0.20%** of circulating supply reaching the market, against **+0.28%** on the inflation monitor. With roughly **95.6%** of the **21M** hard cap already mined and the next halving not due until around **Apr 2028**, that figure is about as low as a chain that removes nothing can go.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework reads Bitcoin SV at **+0.20%** net new supply over the trailing 90 days and **+0.20%** over the next 90 days. The inflation monitor reads **+0.28%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.076** percentage points — comfortably inside the framework's tolerance, so no data-conflict chip is shown. The residual is not a mechanism disagreement at all: the monitor infers supply from market cap divided by price, and that inferred figure moved **33,485 BSV** in a single day at the end of Aug 2026, a swing larger than the entire quarterly signal. The framework instead counts blocks. Both sides of the BSV ledger are structural rather than discretionary: nobody decides how much BSV is created, and nobody can decide to destroy any. Bitcoin SV is a **quiet, hard-capped proof-of-work chain with a permanently empty buy side**.

## Sell pressure: where new BSV comes from

Every unit of sell pressure on Bitcoin SV comes from one place: the coinbase. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **~40.3K BSV** for the window, and it was measured on the chain rather than taken from a schedule. Between block height **952,021** on **Jun 4 2026** and block height **964,924** on **Sep 2 2026**, Bitcoin SV produced **12,903** blocks, each paying a fixed **3.125 BSV** subsidy — **40,322 BSV** in total. That measurement matters because Bitcoin SV runs a low hashrate relative to the rest of the Bitcoin lineage, and the realised block interval came in at **602.7** seconds against a **600**-second target. The chain ran slightly slow, so a naive read at the nominal ten-minute pace would have claimed **12,961** blocks and **40,502 BSV** — about **180 BSV** of issuance that never happened. Difficulty retargeting corrects the interval, never the reward, and the halving boundary is a fixed block height, so on Bitcoin SV a slow quarter genuinely issues fewer coins. The subsidy itself was confirmed from a real coinbase transaction inside the window, which paid **3.13254322 BSV** — the **3.125** subsidy plus recycled transaction fees.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each is zero for a structural reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because Bitcoin SV has no vesting schedule to unlock: the chain split from Bitcoin Cash in **Nov 2018** by inheriting the existing ledger one-for-one, so there was no premine, no token sale, no team allocation and no investor tranche — no unlock tracker even carries a page for BSV, because there is nothing to track. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because a fair-launch fork never created an allocation for anyone to hold; the reward output read at both window edges pays exactly one address, the miner, with no developer diversion, and the Swiss association behind the protocol publishes no wallet and no coin reserve. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero for a subtler reason covered below: the overhang exists, but no dated disposal does.

## Buy pressure: where new BSV goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows on Bitcoin SV are **zero**, and this is the defining structural fact of the coin. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero because Bitcoin SV captures no protocol revenue and holds no treasury — there is simply nothing to fund a buyback with. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero and was verified on both surfaces the framework requires. On the supply surface, published BSV supply is the halving schedule evaluated at block height, a figure that reconstructs to the satoshi and structurally cannot fall; it rose monotonically across the window by exactly **40,322 BSV**. On the dead-address surface, the three canonical unspendable addresses hold **2,206 BSV** of permanently keyless coins between them, and their combined inflow across the entire 90-day window was **0.04 BSV** — dust, not a burn. Neither surface moved by any material amount, which is the framework's test for "nothing was destroyed." Bitcoin SV has no fee-burn mechanism at all: transaction fees ride inside the coinbase output straight to the miner, which is why every in-window coinbase reads slightly above **3.125** rather than below it.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is zero because there is no protocol treasury doing the buying and no disclosed accumulation of BSV by the association behind the network, which is funded by private backers rather than by a coin allocation. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero because proof-of-work pays miners directly — Bitcoin SV has no staking, no bonding, and no lockup contract that could hold coins off the market. There is also no mint authority to worry about: on a proof-of-work chain the coinbase ceiling is a consensus rule, so no key can create BSV outside the schedule and no key can destroy it. Changing either would take a hard fork.

## Foundation and overhang

Bitcoin SV has exactly one identified overhang, and it is not a team-controlled one. A bankruptcy estate holds roughly **143K BSV** — about **0.7%** of circulating supply — matched one-for-one to the estate's Bitcoin Cash position at the **Nov 2018** split, and the rehabilitation plan states that assets outside the two main coins are to be liquidated into cash. That is a real, dated intention to sell. What does not exist is a dated sale: the trustee has never published a BSV wallet address, never published a remaining BSV amount, and never announced a disposal date. The creditor repayment deadline of **Oct 31 2026** does fall inside the forward window, but a repayment deadline is not a disposal date, and that deadline has already been extended three separate times. An intention to sell without a date is not a sale, so Sell #4 books **0** and the overhang is surfaced instead — on the overview page and in the upcoming strip. It is re-walked on a fortnightly cycle; if this overhang's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

Two other candidates were considered and excluded. The long-asserted founder-trust holdings associated with the chain's original promoter have never been tied to a verified on-chain address and the underlying claims did not survive court scrutiny — with no identifier and no observed firing, that is an unidentified holding, not a trackable overhang. The association behind Bitcoin SV publishes no BSV wallet and no reserve figure, because a fair-launch fork never created an allocation for it to hold. There is no foundation coin allocation, no labs multisig, no DAO treasury and no buyback accumulation wallet on Bitcoin SV.

## How BSV compares to other hard-capped proof-of-work chains

Bitcoin SV sits in the same structural class as Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash and Litecoin: a fixed hard cap, a block subsidy that halves on a fixed block-height schedule, and no mechanism anywhere in the protocol that removes supply. Within that class Bitcoin SV is nearly indistinguishable from Bitcoin Cash — the same **21M** cap, the same **210,000**-block halving interval, the same **3.125** subsidy in the current era, and the same next boundary at height **1,050,000**. Both land near **+0.20%** quarterly issuance for the same arithmetic reason: roughly **95.6%** of the cap is already mined, so the remaining flow is small relative to the float. The one variable that separates them is hashrate, and this is where Bitcoin SV's comparatively thin mining base actually shows up in the ledger — the realised interval must be measured, not assumed, because a chain that runs slow issues fewer coins than its schedule implies.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains the contrast is sharper. A proof-of-stake L1 with a perpetual staking-reward curve keeps issuing indefinitely and typically prints several percent a year, with the framework's sell side dominated by a rate that governance can change by vote. Bitcoin SV's issuance is not a policy variable at all: no vote, no committee and no foundation can raise or lower it, only a hard fork of the consensus rules. Against exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks and burns, the comparison runs the other way — those coins can post a negative net because their buy side is funded by real protocol revenue, whereas Bitcoin SV has no revenue to capture and therefore no route to a deflationary reading. Against fee-burn chains such as Ethereum, the same asymmetry holds: Bitcoin SV pays every fee to the miner rather than destroying a base fee, so activity on Bitcoin SV never reduces supply, however high transaction volume climbs.

The practical consequence is that Bitcoin SV cannot score at the top of the inflation metric no matter how quiet the chain gets. A hard cap with an empty buy side has a floor at slightly-positive net supply until the last coin is mined. That is a structural ceiling, not a criticism — it is the same ceiling Bitcoin itself carries.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the bankruptcy-estate creditor repayment deadline of **Oct 31 2026** — not because the deadline itself moves supply, but because any published BSV disposal figure or wallet address alongside it would immediately convert the **143K BSV** overhang into a booked Sell #4. Second, the realised block interval: Bitcoin SV ran **602.7** seconds per block this quarter, and a material hashrate move in either direction changes issuance directly, because the subsidy is paid per block and nothing in the protocol compensates for a drifting interval. Third, the halving at block height **1,050,000**, still around **85,076** blocks and roughly **593** days away — it will not fire inside this window, but it is the only scheduled event that changes the sell row. Fourth, any consensus change from the association behind the protocol: the Chronicle upgrade activated in **Apr 2026** and touched opcodes and size limits only, with no effect on issuance, and the Teranode scaling work in progress is likewise a throughput change rather than a monetary one — but a proposal touching the coinbase would be the one thing that reopens this ledger. Fifth, the emergence of any treasury, buyback or burn programme, of which there is currently no trace.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Bitcoin SV as a structurally quiet hard-capped proof-of-work chain: **~40.3K BSV** of block subsidy over 90 days from **12,903** counted blocks at **3.125 BSV** each, against a buy side that is zero on all four rows, for a net **+0.20%** of supply reaching the market and the same **+0.20%** projected forward. The mechanism is mining and nothing else — no vesting, no foundation allocation, no buyback, no fee burn, no staking lock — and it cannot be changed by governance, only by a hard fork. The key risk is not issuance but the **143K BSV** bankruptcy-estate overhang, which is roughly **0.7%** of circulating supply, is intended for liquidation, and carries no published disposal date. The ceiling is arithmetic: with **95.6%** of the **21M** cap already mined and nothing in the protocol able to remove a coin, Bitcoin SV's net supply can approach zero but cannot go below it.
