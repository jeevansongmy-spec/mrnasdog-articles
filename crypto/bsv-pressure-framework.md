# BSV Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Bitcoin SV is a fair-launch proof-of-work chain whose entire supply story is one number: the block subsidy. Over the last 90 days the Bitcoin SV chain issued **~40.4K BSV** from **12,917** blocks at **3.125** BSV each, against a buy ledger that is empty in all four rows — no buyback, no burn, no foundation bid, no lock. That is **+0.20%** of circulating supply reaching the market, against **+0.12%** on our supply monitor, a gap of **0.08 percentage points**. Nothing changes in the forward quarter either: the next halving on Bitcoin SV sits at block **1,050,000**, roughly **603** days away, so the same **3.125** subsidy runs the whole window and the forward read is also **+0.20%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework reads Bitcoin SV at **+0.20% net** supply growth over the trailing 90 days and **+0.20%** for the next 90. Our supply monitor reads **+0.12%** over the same window, leaving a gap of **0.08 percentage points** — inside tolerance, so no data-conflict chip is raised on the BSV page. The agreement is thinner than it looks: the monitor derives BSV supply by dividing market capitalisation by price, and on a coin this size that quotient swings across a band wider than the entire signal from one day to the next, so the framework's figure is anchored on block heights instead. On mechanism, BSV is a **hard-capped, halving-model proof-of-work coin with a permanently empty buy side**: the subsidy schedule is the only lever, it only ever moves one way, and it is not due to move for another two years.

## Sell pressure: where new BSV comes from

Every unit of Bitcoin SV sell pressure in this ledger is protocol inflation, and protocol inflation on Bitcoin SV is mining and nothing else. The chain produced **12,917** blocks between the two ends of the window — counted by differencing the block heights at each end of the full 90 days, never sampled from a short interval and never inferred from the nominal target. That works out to a real block interval of **10.03** minutes, slightly slower than the **10** minutes Bitcoin SV targets, so the quarter issued **43** fewer blocks than a target-based read would have claimed. At **3.125** BSV a block that is **~40.4K BSV** of new supply. The subsidy figure itself was read off the chain rather than trusted to a document: the coinbase at block **839,999** paid **6.25** BSV and the coinbase at block **840,000** paid **3.125**, so the Bitcoin SV halving fires at the round-number block and not one block later, which is exactly what the node software does when it divides block height by the halving interval.

The other three sell rows are zero, and each is zero for a different structural reason. Vesting unlocks are zero because Bitcoin SV has no vesting schedule at all — the chain split from Bitcoin Cash in **Nov 2018** and inherited the existing ledger one-for-one, so there was never a token sale, a team tranche, an investor tranche or a cliff for anyone to unlock, and circulating supply sits just **6.25** BSV below total supply as a result. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero because there is no foundation coin allocation: every unit of the Bitcoin SV block reward goes to the miner, and the association that maintains the protocol is funded by its private backers rather than endowed with a reserve. The bankruptcy row is zero on evidence rather than on structure, and it is covered below.

## Buy pressure: where new BSV goes

Nowhere. All four Bitcoin SV buy rows are zero, and on a UTXO chain the burn row in particular has to be proved from the address side rather than the supply side. The Bitcoin SV supply figure is the cumulative halving schedule evaluated at the current block height — it is a function of height, not a scan of spendable coins — so a coin paid to an address nobody holds the key to is still inside that total. Supply on Bitcoin SV can therefore never fall, and a burn can only ever show up as an address balance. The two well-known keyless addresses inherited from the pre-fork ledger hold **2,144** BSV between them that nobody can ever spend and that the supply figure still counts in full. Across the whole window those addresses received **0.000045** BSV in eight dust transactions — a real, non-zero, and entirely immaterial read.

There is no fee burn either. Bitcoin SV transaction fees ride inside the same coinbase output that pays the subsidy, which is why every block sampled in this window paid slightly more than **3.125** rather than less — fees on Bitcoin SV are recycled to the miner, never destroyed. Programmatic buyback is zero because Bitcoin SV keeps no protocol revenue to buy with: the subsidy and the fees both leave the protocol the instant a block is found. Foundation buy is zero for the same reason — there is no balance sheet to do the buying. And new long-term lock is zero because Bitcoin SV is pure proof-of-work, with no staking, no bonding and no lockup contract, so a Bitcoin SV holder has no protocol-level way to take coins off the tradable float.

## Foundation and overhang

Bitcoin SV has exactly one identified overhang, and it is not a team wallet. A defunct exchange's rehabilitation estate holds roughly **143K BSV** — about **0.7%** of circulating supply — inherited one-for-one alongside the estate's Bitcoin Cash position at the **Nov 2018** split. The trustee has stated an intention to sell the Bitcoin SV holding and repay creditors in cash rather than in coin, but no disposal date has ever been published, the estate reports its position in Bitcoin and Bitcoin Cash terms only, and the creditor deadline has been pushed back three separate times, most recently to **Oct 31 2026**. A deadline is not a disposal, so the framework books this at zero and carries it as a tracked overhang instead. If that balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the Bitcoin SV sell ledger at the next refresh.

Two other candidate overhangs were considered and excluded. The long-asserted early-mined founder holdings associated with the chain's original promoter have never been tied to a verified on-chain address and the underlying claims did not survive court scrutiny, so with no identifier and no observed movement they are an unidentified holding rather than a trackable one. And the BSV Association publishes no treasury wallet and no reserve figure, because a fair-launch fork never created an allocation for anyone to hold in the first place.

## How BSV compares to other capped proof-of-work chains

Bitcoin SV belongs to the smallest and most predictable class in the framework: hard-capped, halving-model proof-of-work coins with no burn, no buyback and no vesting. Against Bitcoin itself the mechanism is identical by inheritance — same **21M** cap, same **210,000**-block halving interval, same **3.125** subsidy in the current era — but Bitcoin SV sits further along its own schedule, because its chain has produced more blocks since the fork lineage diverged. That is why the next Bitcoin SV halving arrives at block **1,050,000** on a chain already past **963,000**, and why **95.6%** of the Bitcoin SV cap is already mined with only about **927K BSV** left to issue.

The contrast with fee-burning chains is the sharper one. A chain that burns its base fee can post a negative quarter when activity is high, so its net supply reading swings with usage. Bitcoin SV deliberately went the other way: it removed limits on transaction size and script complexity to chase throughput, but it routes every satoshi of fee to the miner, so no amount of on-chain activity can ever pull Bitcoin SV supply down. Against uncapped continuous-emission chains the contrast runs the other direction again — those set a target inflation rate that governance can revisit at any time, whereas the Bitcoin SV issuance curve is fixed in consensus code and its next change is already dated to a specific block. The practical result is that Bitcoin SV's best possible reading is a small positive number, and its worst possible reading is the same small positive number.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

There is no scheduled Bitcoin SV protocol event inside the forward window, which closes around **Nov 21 2026**. The next halving is at block **1,050,000**, roughly **603** days out at the measured pace and due around **Apr 2028**, when the Bitcoin SV subsidy falls to 1.5625. Watch the block interval instead, because it is the only free variable in this ledger: Bitcoin SV ran at **10.03** minutes a block this quarter, and a sustained shift in hashrate moves the forward figure directly. Watch the rehabilitation estate for any announcement that converts the **Oct 31 2026** creditor deadline into an actual dated Bitcoin SV disposal — that is the single event that would put a non-zero number in the sell ledger's fourth row. And watch for any successor to the Chronicle upgrade of **Apr 7 2026**: that one changed script limits and opcodes without touching issuance, but a consensus change is the only mechanism by which the Bitcoin SV subsidy schedule could ever move off its curve.

## Summary

Bitcoin SV runs one of the simplest supply mechanisms in the framework and one of the emptiest buy sides: **~40.4K BSV** mined over the trailing 90 days from **12,917** blocks, nothing bought back, nothing burned, nothing locked, for a net **+0.20%** that stays at **+0.20%** in the forward quarter because the subsidy does not change. The structural mechanism is Bitcoin's inherited halving schedule, encoded in consensus rules that both cut the subsidy every **210,000** blocks and reject any block that pays its miner more than the schedule allows — which is what makes the **21M** Bitcoin SV cap a protocol fact rather than a claim. The key risk is not a supply shock but the absence of any offset: with no burn and no buyback, Bitcoin SV can never post a negative quarter, and the only thing that shrinks the number is the next halving. With **95.6%** of the cap already mined and about **927K BSV** left, the remaining issuance is small in absolute terms and entirely knowable in advance.
