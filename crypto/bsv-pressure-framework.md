# BSV Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Bitcoin SV mints about **40.4K BSV** over the next 90 days through proof-of-work mining at a **3.125 BSV** block subsidy, against a buy ledger that is empty in every row — no buyback, no fee burn, no foundation buyer, no lock. That is a net of about **+0.20%**, essentially unchanged from the **+0.20%** realised over the last 90 days, and our supply monitor reads **+0.28%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.08 percentage points** that sits well inside tolerance, so no warning chip ships. BSV is a fair-launch, hard-capped proof-of-work coin with one supply force and no offsets, and **95.6%** of its 21M cap has already been mined.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 13 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **BSV at +0.20% net** over the last 90 days and **+0.20%** projected over the next 90. Our supply monitor reads **+0.28%** for the trailing window, a gap of **0.08 percentage points** — far under the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so no **monitor gap** chip is attached to the page. The small residual is rounding noise in a classified circulating series measured against a 20M base, not a disagreement about how much Bitcoin SV actually mined. Both readings agree on the shape: BSV is a **quiet, hard-capped proof-of-work chain** whose supply barely moves, because the only mechanism that can move it is a block subsidy already four halvings deep.

## Sell pressure: where new BSV comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the entire sell ledger for Bitcoin SV, at about **40.4K BSV** over the next 90 days and the same **40.4K BSV** realised over the last 90. Bitcoin SV is a proof-of-work chain that split from Bitcoin Cash on **Nov 15 2018**, inheriting the existing ledger one-for-one, and it kept Bitcoin's monetary policy untouched: a **21M** hard cap and a block subsidy that halves every **210,000** blocks. The chain tip sits at height **962,130**, inside the era that began at the **840,000** halving, so the subsidy was **3.125 BSV** for every block in the window and stays 3.125 through the next one. Mining the coinbase is the only mechanism in the design that creates new BSV.

Read live from the chain on **Aug 13 2026**, the arithmetic is direct rather than modelled. Bitcoin SV produced **12,914** blocks between height **949,216** and height **962,130**, a span of **89.99** days — a real rate of **143.5** blocks a day against the protocol's **144** target. Multiplied by the 3.125 subsidy, that is **40,356 BSV** of realised issuance, about **144 BSV** less than a nominal 144-blocks-a-day read would have booked. On a low-hash chain the realised rate is the number that matters, and here it ran roughly **0.34%** slow. The forward window projects the measured rate rather than the nominal one, giving **40,362 BSV**. Nothing re-bases that figure: the next halving sits at block **1,050,000**, some **87,870** blocks and roughly **612** days away, around April 2028, so it falls outside both windows.

Every other sell row is zero, and each is zero for a structural reason. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — cannot exist, because the November 2018 fork duplicated an existing UTXO set rather than issuing a new token: there was no premine, no sale and no investor allocation, so no vesting contract was ever written and no cliff can ever arrive. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is zero because a fair-launch fork never created an allocation for anyone to hold, and no share of the BSV block reward is diverted to a foundation, a labs entity or a DAO treasury. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — books zero this window, though it is the one row with a live watch item, covered below.

## Buy pressure: where new BSV goes

The buy ledger is empty in all four rows, and that is the single most important structural fact about BSV inflation. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero** because Bitcoin SV takes no protocol revenue beyond the block reward, and that reward is paid straight out to miners, so there is no revenue stream capable of funding a repurchase and no buyback contract exists to hold one. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**, and this build verified it by reading a coinbase transaction rather than asserting it: block **962,130** pays a single output of **3.1251933 BSV** to one miner address, which is the **3.125** subsidy plus that block's transaction fees, with nothing routed to a destroy output. Fees go to the miner. The protocol removes no BSV from supply, ever.

Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero** because no entity has published a BSV purchase programme, an amount bought, or a schedule for buying more. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** because Bitcoin SV is pure proof-of-work: there is no staking, no bonding, no masternode collateral and no protocol lock-up that can take BSV off the tradable float. The practical consequence is that BSV has no mechanism capable of turning net-deflationary. Its inflation can only fall toward zero as the halvings compound and the subsidy shrinks; it cannot go negative while the protocol stands as written.

## Foundation and overhang

There is exactly one identified team-controlled overhang for Bitcoin SV, and it is not a team's at all — it is a bankruptcy estate residual. The Mt. Gox rehabilitation estate held **142,846** Bitcoin Cash when the BCH chain split in November 2018, and that position was duplicated into an equal **142,846 BSV**, worth roughly **$2.1M** at today's price and about **0.71%** of circulating supply. The approved rehabilitation plan distributes Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash and cash to creditors while directing other forked coins to be liquidated into cash, so the estate's BSV is earmarked for sale in principle. In practice the trustee has never disclosed a BSV wallet, a quantum sold, or a date. The plan's repayment deadline was extended to **Oct 31 2026**, which does fall inside the next 90 days, but a creditor repayment deadline is not a dated disposal of BSV, so the framework books Sell #4 at zero and carries the balance as a watched overhang. The estate is opaque — there is no address to poll — so it refreshes on a bi-weekly walk of trustee and estate disclosures. If that balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

No other overhang qualifies. Bitcoin SV has no foundation coin allocation, no labs multisig, no DAO treasury and no buyback accumulation wallet, because a fair-launch fork never minted an allocation for any of them to hold. Long-running claims about large early-mined trusts associated with the chain's original promoters have never been tied to a verified address and have not survived court scrutiny, so they count as unidentified holdings rather than trackable team-controlled supply, and they stay out of the ledger.

## How BSV compares to other hard-capped proof-of-work chains

BSV belongs to the class of **hard-capped proof-of-work coins with a halving subsidy** — the same design family as Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash and Litecoin, and the exact opposite of an uncapped continuous-emission proof-of-stake L1. Because Bitcoin SV forked from Bitcoin Cash, which forked from Bitcoin, all three share one emission curve: **21M** cap, halving every 210,000 blocks, currently **3.125** per block. What separates them is not the schedule but the realised block rate. A chain with a small share of the SHA-256 hash pointed at it finds blocks off-target between difficulty adjustments, which is why Bitcoin SV issued at **143.5** blocks a day rather than 144 this window. Reading emission off the nominal target instead of the chain would have overstated supply growth here — small in percentage terms, but it is the difference between a measured number and an assumed one.

Against uncapped staking chains the contrast is much sharper. A Cosmos-style or Solana-style L1 mints new supply indefinitely to pay validators, at a rate governance can raise; BSV has a fixed ceiling, a fixed halving schedule, and no governance body that can vote issuance upward. Against chains with a base-fee burn such as Ethereum, BSV is the plainer instrument: Ethereum can swing net-deflationary when fee burn exceeds issuance, whereas BSV's fees flow to the miner inside the coinbase, so no burn offset exists at any level of network activity — and Bitcoin SV's design intent is explicitly high-volume, very-low-fee data throughput, which keeps fee revenue tiny relative to the subsidy. Against exchange tokens running quarterly buyback-and-burn programmes, BSV is the mirror image: those coins run net-deflationary because a revenue stream funds removal, while BSV has no revenue stream to repurchase with. Against privacy coins with a permanent tail emission such as Monero, BSV is stricter, because the ceiling here is a hard cap rather than a perpetual minimum subsidy. For an inflation lens, BSV reads as one of the quietest supply profiles in the catalogue — and one where the largest single risk is a legacy estate, not the protocol.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Mt. Gox rehabilitation estate and its **142,846 BSV**: the plan's repayment deadline of **Oct 31 2026** falls inside this window, and any trustee disclosure that names a BSV sale date or quantum would move Sell #4 off zero immediately. Second, the realised block rate, which ran **143.5** a day this quarter — a sustained fall would cut issuance below the projected **40.4K BSV**, and a hash-rate influx would push it back toward the **144** target. Third, node-software releases from the chain's maintaining association: the **Chronicle** upgrade activated at height **943,816** on **Apr 7 2026** and changed scripting rules only, with no effect on subsidy or issuance, and any successor release should be checked against the same test. Fourth, the halving at block **1,050,000**, still around April 2028 but worth tracking because the block rate is what sets its arrival date. Fifth, any proposal to introduce a fee burn or a treasury diversion, which would be the only way a new row could ever appear on either side of this ledger.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads BSV at about **+0.20%** net over the next 90 days, from roughly **40.4K BSV** of proof-of-work issuance and nothing whatsoever on the buy side. The structural mechanism is Bitcoin's original monetary policy carried across two forks intact: a **21M** hard cap, a subsidy halving every 210,000 blocks, currently **3.125 BSV** per block, and fees paid to the miner rather than burned. The key risk is not protocol dilution but the **142,846 BSV** sitting in the Mt. Gox rehabilitation estate with a liquidation instruction and no announced date — about **0.71%** of supply that could arrive at market faster than a quarter of mining does. With **95.6%** of the cap already mined, the ceiling is hard, the schedule is fixed, and nothing in the protocol can push supply the other way.
