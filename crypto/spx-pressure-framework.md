# SPX Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

**TL;DR:** SPX supply is flat and can never grow. From Aug 28 to Nov 26 2026 not a single new SPX was created, while two community contracts burned **157 SPX**, so the supply of **930.99M SPX** moved by **0.00%** in 90 days. The SPX6900 contract fixed the supply at 1 billion when it launched in August 2023 and has no way to make more; other trackers show about **-0.02%** for the same 90 days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

In the 90 days to Nov 26 2026, SPX6900 added **0 SPX** and lost **157 SPX** to burns, a net change of **0.00%** on a circulating supply of **930.99M SPX**, and the next 90 days should look the same at **0.00%**. Other supply trackers read **-0.02%** for the same stretch, a gap of only **0.02 percentage points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. The label that fits SPX today is **a fixed-supply meme coin with a tiny voluntary burn**: nothing comes in, and only a trickle goes out.

## Sell pressure: where new SPX comes from

**Protocol inflation is zero, and it is zero for good.** The SPX6900 contract on Ethereum set its total at 1 billion SPX in the code when the coin launched in August 2023, handed all of it to the launch wallet once, and has no function that can create more. The contract's owner key was given up, so nobody can change those rules either. There is no staking reward, no mining and no yearly issuance.

**Vesting unlocks are zero.** SPX6900 never set aside a team, investor or treasury share on a timer. The wallet that launched the coin holds no SPX today, so there is no locked batch waiting to open.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero.** SPX6900 has no foundation and, in its own words, no formal team or roadmap. The one pile near the launch setup is **63.93K SPX** held inside the token contract itself, left from early trades. Those coins already count as circulating, so a sale would move coins between holders without adding any.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is zero.** No court case or failed company holds SPX for a later payout. About **104.19M SPX** sit in a bridge on Ethereum, but they back the SPX copies people trade on Solana and Base one for one, so each coin is counted only once, on Ethereum.

## Buy pressure: where new SPX goes

**The programmatic buyback is zero.** SPX6900 earns no revenue and has no contract or treasury that buys SPX back from the market.

**The protocol fee burn is zero.** Trading SPX costs no fee to the coin itself, and the contract has no burn built in. The early launch tax has been off for a long time, so a trade destroys nothing.

**Foundation buying is zero.** No company or fund holds SPX as a treasury plan. Well-known holders and the community buy and sell coins that already exist, which leaves the supply count unchanged.

**New long-term locks are zero.** SPX has no staking and no lock-up, so no coins leave the market for a fixed time.

**Holder burns removed 157 SPX.** The only way SPX supply shrinks is when someone sends coins to the dead address, a wallet nobody can ever spend from. Between Aug 28 and Nov 26 2026 two community contracts did that: one sent **147 SPX** on Sep 1 and Sep 3 2026, and the other sent three small amounts of **0.36, 0.75 and 9.32 SPX** on Sep 1, Oct 1 and Nov 3 2026. The second contract has sent a few SPX at the start of almost every month since July 2026, so about **10 SPX** more should be burned in the next 90 days. The dead address now holds **69.01M SPX**, almost all of it from the big launch burn in 2023.

## Foundation and overhang

SPX6900 has very little overhang because it has almost no insiders on record. The launch wallet is empty, there is no foundation, no DAO treasury and no investor allocation. The only pile tied to the launch setup is the **63.93K SPX** sitting inside the token contract, which the launch wallet is allowed to sell. It is checked every day: if that balance falls, the drop shows up in the Foundation row at the next check. The **104.19M SPX** in the bridge is not an overhang, because it is the backing for coins already trading on other chains. The biggest single holders hold between about 20M and 28M SPX each, but they are private wallets with coins already in circulation, so their selling would push on the price without adding to the supply.

## How SPX compares to other meme coins

SPX sits in the plainest group of meme coins: a fixed supply set at launch, no team share and no tax. PEPE on Ethereum works the same way, with a supply fixed in its contract and no mint, so for both coins the supply side is close to silent and the price is set almost entirely by demand. Dogecoin is the opposite: it pays miners about 5 billion new DOGE every year with no end date, so DOGE holders absorb steady new supply that SPX holders never face.

Shiba Inu is closer to SPX in design, a fixed supply with no mint, but its community runs a much louder burn campaign and has destroyed a large share of the original supply over the years. SPX had one big burn at launch and only a trickle since, so its supply is effectively frozen. Newer meme coins such as TRUMP or PENGU still carry large team and investor shares that unlock on a schedule, which adds new coins every month. SPX has none of that, so the only supply risk it carries is the selling of coins that are already out.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**Early December 2026:** the monthly community burn is due again around Dec 1 2026; past sends were between 0.36 and 9.32 SPX, so it will not move the supply figure in any visible way.

**Early January and early February 2027:** the same contract should fire again around Jan 1 2027 and Feb 1 2027. If it stops, the burn row drops to zero and the next-90-day view stays at 0.00%.

**The 63.93K SPX in the token contract:** any sale would be a small sale of coins already circulating, worth watching only as a sign of activity from the launch wallet.

**Bridge flows:** coins have been moving from Solana back to Ethereum, and the bridge balance on Ethereum fell from **109.21M SPX** on Aug 28 2026 to **104.19M SPX** on Nov 26 2026. This changes where SPX trades, not how many exist.

## Summary

SPX6900 is a fixed-supply meme coin: its contract created 1 billion SPX once in August 2023, cannot make more, and its owner key has been given up. About **930.99M SPX** are in circulation after the launch burn, and in the 90 days to Nov 26 2026 the only change was **157 SPX** burned by two community contracts, a net **0.00%**. There is no team share, no unlock, no buyback and no staking, so the supply side puts almost no pressure on the price in either direction. The real risk for SPX holders is not new coins but large holders selling coins that already exist, since demand is the only thing that sets the price of a coin with a frozen supply.
