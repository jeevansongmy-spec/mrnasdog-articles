SPX6900 (SPX) cannot issue another token. The SPX6900 contract on Ethereum declares its total supply as a compile-time constant of **1,000,000,000 SPX**, the deployed bytecode contains no mint function, and ownership was renounced to the empty address — all three read from the chain for this build. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads SPX at **−0.001% net** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** over the next 90, against a circulating base of **930.99M SPX**, with the independent supply monitor at **+0.077%** — a gap of **0.078 percentage points**. SPX6900 is a fixed-supply memecoin with no vesting cliff, no unlock calendar, no buyback, no protocol fee burn and no foundation.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 30 2026**, the framework reads SPX6900 at **−0.001% net**: an entirely empty sell ledger against a buy ledger holding a single small entry. The independent supply monitor reads **+0.077%** over the same window, a gap of **0.078 percentage points** — well inside the half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap warning is raised on the SPX reading. That residual is snapshot noise rather than disagreement: the monitor infers supply from price and market capitalisation, while the SPX6900 contract itself moved by 5,746 tokens in ninety days. Both readings round to zero. SPX6900 is best labelled a **frozen float** — the supply side of this asset is finished, and every question about SPX from here is a demand question.

## Sell pressure: where new SPX comes from

Nowhere, and for once that is a statement about code rather than about intent. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0** because SPX6900 has no issuance path at all. The verified SPX6900 source declares its supply function as a pure function — one that cannot write anything — returning the constant **1,000,000,000 SPX** at eight decimals. It is not a stored balance that a mint could increase; it is a number baked into the bytecode. Read directly from the Ethereum chain at both ends of this window, the SPX6900 contract returned exactly 1,000,000,000 both times. A selector scan across the full deployed bytecode finds no mint entry point of any shape, no burn entry point, and no function to transfer ownership; the renounce function is present and has already been executed, so the owner slot returns the empty address. There is no key, and there is no lock for a key to fit.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** and can never be anything else. The entire billion SPX was created in the constructor when SPX6900 was deployed in **Aug 2023** and distributed immediately: no presale tranche, no investor allocation, no team block, no escrow contract holding anything back for later. No unlock tracker publishes a vesting schedule for SPX6900, and that absence is the finding rather than a gap in coverage — there is no schedule to publish. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well: SPX6900 has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution anywhere in its history.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** — SPX6900 has no foundation, no labs entity, no DAO and no treasury, and the original fee address holds **0 SPX** at both ends of this window. One overhang is still named rather than waved away, because it is the sort of thing a renounced contract is assumed to have closed and has not. The SPX6900 token contract holds **58,712.74 SPX** of its own, coins holders sent to the contract address by mistake, and a function in the source can sell that balance into the pool and forward the proceeds. Its access check names the fee address written in at deployment, not the owner, and that address has no setter — so renouncing ownership left the function reachable. The balance has only ever risen, from **47,546.01 SPX** at the start of this window, and has never once been drained, so under the framework's evidence rule it carries no figure and is watched instead.

## Buy pressure: where new SPX goes

The buy ledger holds one entry and it is not SPX6900's own. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**: SPX6900 charges no fee, earns no revenue and holds no treasury contract, so there is nothing to repurchase with and no code on Ethereum that could execute a repurchase. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0** and this build checked it from the code rather than assuming it — both tax constants in the SPX6900 source read zero, so no fee accrues on any trade, and the token has no burn function, so the supply figure it reports can never fall. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** because no organisation exists: the creator burned their own allocation and left the SPX6900 project two weeks after launch. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** — the launch liquidity is still held inside a third-party lock contract, but that lock was set in Aug 2023 and removes nothing new from supply today.

What is left sits in a fifth row, and it exists because a burn on this token can only ever be a transfer. Since the SPX6900 supply figure is a constant, destruction here means sending SPX to an address nobody holds the key to — so the burn was verified by reading the dead address at both ends of the window rather than by watching the supply figure, which cannot move. It gained **5,745.64 SPX** across the window, lifting its pile to **69,012,664.75 SPX**, about **6.9%** of every SPX ever made, almost all of it from the creator's own burn in **Aug 2023**. Because that flow comes from outsiders choosing to burn rather than from any SPX6900 mechanism, it cannot honestly sit under a protocol-burn heading. The forward column carries **0**: the balance was read at three separate points and has not moved since **Aug 3 2026**, still standing at the identical figure twenty-seven days later, with nothing announced and nothing scheduled.

## Foundation and overhang

There is no foundation overhang on SPX6900 because there is no foundation, and this is one of the few coins where that sentence survives a check. What the framework enumerates instead is three concentrations, none of them team-controlled. The first is the one described above: **58,712.74 SPX** stranded inside the SPX6900 token contract with a permissioned route to the market that outlived ownership renunciation, watched on-chain. The second is the cross-chain lockbox holding **109.09M SPX** — roughly **11%** of supply and the largest single SPX balance on Ethereum. It is excluded because it is not extra supply and not held by anyone connected to SPX6900: it is Ethereum-side collateral backing wrapped SPX elsewhere, and the wrapped balances it stands behind add to **109.06M**, matching to **0.024%**. It fell by **11.96M SPX** across the window, which is SPX coming home to Ethereum, not SPX being created. The third is the SPX/WETH pool, holding **12.26M SPX**, whose pool tokens are entirely held by a third-party lock contract — the on-chain part of the widely repeated claim that SPX6900 liquidity is locked for decades. Beyond those, SPX6900 ownership is genuinely dispersed: the largest individual wallets sit near **2.7%**, **2.3%** and **2.0%**, none of them identifiable as a coordinated group. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How SPX compares to other fixed-supply memecoins

SPX6900 belongs to the strictest supply class the framework covers: a renounced, fixed-supply ERC-20 with no issuance and no sink. Its structural analogues are the older Ethereum memecoins built the same way — a constructor mint, liquidity added once, ownership thrown away — and the mechanism comparison is mostly a comparison of what each one still has. Many memecoins in this class retain a transfer tax that routes a slice of every trade somewhere; SPX6900 does not, because both tax constants read zero. Many retain a burn function that lets the supply figure fall; SPX6900 does not, so its only path to scarcity is a voluntary transfer to a dead address. Many retain an owner who can flip parameters; SPX6900's owner slot is the empty address.

Against fee-burning smart-contract platforms and exchange tokens, the contrast runs the other way. Those assets can and do drive a negative net reading — base-fee burns, quarterly revenue buybacks, staking locks — because they capture revenue and can spend it on their own supply. SPX6900 captures nothing, so it has no mechanism capable of shrinking its float on purpose. Against uncapped emission chains, SPX6900 wins outright: those assets mint every block and the framework books the issuance every quarter, while SPX6900 books nothing in either direction. That is the honest ceiling on this reading. A frozen supply cannot dilute you, but it also cannot compound in your favour — it earns a flat score, not a deflationary one, and every remaining question about SPX6900 sits on the demand side of the book.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four watch lines, and none of them is a dated event, because SPX6900 has no calendar. First, the stranded contract balance: it stood at **58,712.74 SPX** on **Aug 30 2026** and has only ever grown — a fall would be the fee address exercising the one permissioned route the renounced contract left open, and it would enter Sell #3 immediately. Second, the burn address at **69,012,664.75 SPX**: it has been static since **Aug 3 2026**, and a resumption of third-party burns is the only thing that can push the SPX reading negative again. Third, the cross-chain lockbox at **109.09M SPX**: a divergence between that balance and the wrapped supplies it backs would be a real finding, though today they match to **0.024%**. Fourth, the liquidity lock: the pool tokens are held by an outside lock contract, and the release date behind that lock is a project claim this build could not independently confirm on-chain, so it stays a watch line rather than a stated fact. Market events sit outside all four — SPX6900 listings on two Korean venues on **Jun 16 2026** and the withdrawal of an SPX perpetual futures market on **Aug 26 2026** moved trading, not supply.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads SPX6900 (SPX) at **−0.001% net** over the trailing 90 days and **0.00%** over the next 90, against **930.99M SPX** circulating and a hard ceiling of **1,000,000,000**. The structural mechanism is the absence of one: SPX6900's supply is a compile-time constant, no mint function exists in the deployed bytecode, ownership is renounced, and the only removal on record is **5,745.64 SPX** that outsiders chose to burn before the flow stopped on **Aug 3 2026**. The key risk is not dilution but concentration and permission: **58,712.74 SPX** sits inside the token contract behind a function that survived ownership renunciation, and roughly **11%** of supply sits in one cross-chain lockbox. The ceiling is the point — SPX6900 will never issue another token, and it will never buy one back either.
