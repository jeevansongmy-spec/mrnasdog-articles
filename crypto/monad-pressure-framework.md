# MON Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Monad is an uncapped parallel-EVM Layer-1 whose MON token was minted **100B** at genesis in **November 2025** and keeps minting on every block, with only **11.83B** counted as circulating today. Over the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026** the Pressure Framework reads **179.81M MON** of sell pressure against **23.63M MON** of buy pressure from the base-fee burn, a net of **+1.32%** — modest, because most of Monad's block reward compounds inside Foundation-delegated stake and never reaches a seller, and because not one insider token has vested since launch. That second fact is the whole story: the first vesting cliff opens on **Nov 24 2026** and releases about **6.66B MON** in a single day, which pushes the next-90-day reading to **+57.56%**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.02%**, a gap of **1.30 percentage points**, so a monitor-gap flag ships on this build. MON is **a young chain with a quiet mint and an enormous, dated supply wall**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 29 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **MON at +1.32% net**: sell pressure of **179.81M MON**, entirely block rewards, against buy pressure of **23.63M MON** from the protocol's base-fee burn, on a circulating base of **11,825,165,000 MON**. The next 90 days read **+57.56%**, because the forward window reaches **Nov 24 2026** — the one-year cliff on the team, investor and treasury allocations — and books its full **6.66B MON**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.02%** for the trailing window, a gap of **1.30 percentage points**, far outside the half-point tolerance, so the flag is raised rather than suppressed. The reason is structural rather than disputed: the market-wide circulating count for MON has not moved off **11,825,165,000** since **Apr 21 2026**, because it tracks unlock milestones and cannot see block rewards, while the chain minted **475.66M MON** of them inside this window. The right label for MON is **structurally inflationary on a thin float, with a dated supply wall that dwarfs the mint**.

## Sell pressure: where new MON comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **179.81M MON** over 90 days and is the only sell row with a number on it. Monad mints a fixed block reward on every block, and the size of that reward changed inside this window: the network ran at a **0.40 second** block paying **25 MON** until **Jul 23 2026**, when a consensus upgrade cut the vote pace to **300 milliseconds** and cut the reward to **18 MON** in the same activation. Reading the reward straight off the chain confirms it: the staking contract's balance grows by exactly **18.000000 MON** per block today. Counting the real blocks on each side of that activation — **11,367,771** at 25 MON and **10,637,143** at 18 MON — gives gross issuance of **475.66M MON** for the window. The upgrade is a case where a faster chain does not mean more inflation: the protocol re-scaled the reward alongside the interval, so per-second issuance fell from **62.5** to **60.0 MON** rather than rising by a third.

Only part of that gross figure is sell pressure, and the split is measurable. Block rewards accrue to stake, and Monad's stake is dominated by the Foundation's own delegations, whose rewards accrue straight back to the Foundation — supply that never enters the float this page divides by. Enumerating every one of the **197** consensus validators and all **13,136** delegations gives total stake of **15.50B MON**, of which **10.99B** sits in delegations at the Foundation's own tier amounts. That leaves **29.13%** of stake in independent hands, plus a stake-weighted validator commission of **12.23%** that node operators take out of the Foundation's own rewards and can freely sell. Together, **37.80%** of the mint reaches the market — **179.81M MON** last quarter and **174.66M** next.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** for the trailing window and **6,662.5M MON** for the next one, and it is the largest single figure on this page. Not one team, investor or treasury token has vested since the **Nov 24 2025** launch — all three allocations sit behind one shared twelve-month cliff. When that cliff opens on **Nov 24 2026** it releases the twelve months already accrued: **4,925M MON** to investors, being 25% of their **19.7B**, **987.5M** to the development company's treasury on the identical schedule, and the team's first monthly tranche of **750M**. About **1.24B MON** a month follows for three years after that, to **Nov 24 2029**. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero**: no discretionary deployment registered anywhere in the window. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**, because MON has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to it.

## Buy pressure: where new MON goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the only real buy pressure MON has, and it is **23.63M MON** for the trailing window. Monad destroys the base component of every transaction fee outright — there is no dead address involved, the supply simply falls. Sampling **250** blocks on each side of the July upgrade gives a burn of about **0.97 MON** per block before it and **1.18 MON** after, with the base fee reading exactly **100 gwei** in every sample, so the whole variation is gas demand rather than price. Projected forward at the post-upgrade rate that is **30.27M MON** over the next 90 days. It is real, it is permanent, and against a mint of **475.66M** it retires about one token in twenty.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**. The development company disclosed on **Jan 21 2026** that it may repurchase up to **$30M** of MON on the open market, entirely at its own discretion — and since then no purchase has been confirmed, no quantity published and no wallet disclosed. Capacity is not the same thing as a programme, so nothing is booked. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is also **zero**, for a subtler reason: the Monad Foundation did run a repurchase this summer, offering early investors up to **$60M** to sell their locked MON back at a discount, and it was reported on **Aug 18 2026** that almost nobody accepted. Those would have been locked tokens, not market ones — the effect is to move an overhang from one holder to another, and the amount was never published. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**, because a MON staking withdrawal clears in a single epoch of about **four hours**; staking parks no supply across a 90-day horizon.

## Foundation and overhang

MON is nearly all overhang: **88.86B** of the **100.68B** issued so far sits outside the counted float. Five holdings are tracked. The largest is the Ecosystem Development allocation of **38.5B MON**, which was unlocked at genesis and has been stewarded by the Monad Foundation ever since; **10.99B** of it is measurably delegated to validators through the Foundation's tiered delegation programme and can be watched on chain, while the rest moves only when the Foundation deploys it. Next are the team allocation of **27B MON** and the investor allocation of **19.7B MON**, both untouched and both behind the **Nov 24 2026** cliff, and the development company's treasury of **3.95B MON** on the same schedule; none of the three publishes a wallet, so all three are walked by hand. Fifth is the buyback destination, which does not exist as an address at all — the **$30M** authorisation has never named one. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How MON compares to other uncapped Layer-1 chains

Monad belongs to the class of uncapped proof-of-stake Layer-1s that mint a validator reward forever and burn part of the transaction fee against it. Compared with the mature members of that class, the mechanism is conventional but the balance is not. A settled chain of this shape typically issues low single-digit percentages a year against a float that is most of its supply, so the mint lands on a wide base and the burn can plausibly offset it in a busy quarter. Monad issues about **1.9B MON** a year against a float of **11.83B** — a mint worth roughly **16%** of the tradable supply annually, on paper. What pulls the real figure down to **+1.32%** a quarter is not restraint in the emission schedule; it is that the Foundation holds most of the stake and therefore receives most of the reward, so the dilution accumulates inside a treasury rather than on the order book. That is a very different kind of safety from a chain whose issuance is small because its issuance is small.

The July upgrade is worth reading carefully against the same comparison set, because it is the sort of change that is routinely mispriced. Cutting the block interval from **400** to **300 milliseconds** raises the block count by a third, and on a chain that pays a fixed reward per block that alone would raise issuance by a third. Monad cut the reward from **25** to **18 MON** in the same activation, which more than absorbs it — issuance per second fell about **4%**. Chains that have made the same move have handled it in different ways, some re-scaling a protocol constant instead of the reward; the lesson is identical in both cases, which is that a faster chain is not automatically a more inflationary one, and the only safe way to read it is to count real blocks rather than trust a published target.

Where Monad separates most sharply from its peers is the vesting calendar. A mature uncapped Layer-1 has usually finished its insider unlocks, so its supply question reduces to issuance against burn — a question a quarterly framework answers well. Monad has released **11.83%** of its supply and has not started its insider schedule at all. On **Nov 24 2026** a single day releases more than half again the entire current float, and then **1.24B MON** a month runs for three more years. Against any chain whose cliffs are behind it, MON carries a structurally larger forward supply risk, and no burn at the current scale is remotely the right size to meet it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The date that matters is **Nov 24 2026**, when the twelve-month cliff opens and roughly **6.66B MON** becomes claimable in one day; the thing to watch is not the unlock itself but where the tokens move afterwards, because an unlocked token sitting in an investor wallet and an unlocked token arriving at an exchange look identical on an unlock calendar and mean opposite things. Second, watch the market-wide circulating count: it has been pinned at **11,825,165,000 MON** since **Apr 21 2026**, and how quickly it restates after the cliff will show whether that figure is being maintained at all. Third, watch the Foundation's ecosystem deployments — **38.5B MON** is unlocked and undeployed, and one grant programme announcement would move this page's reading more than a quarter of block rewards does. Fourth, watch for any confirmation of the **$30M** open-market repurchase, which has been an authorisation and nothing more since **Jan 21 2026**. Fifth, watch the governance queue for any further change to the block reward, since the **Jul 23 2026** upgrade proved the reward is a parameter the network is willing to move.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Monad's MON at **+1.32%** net over the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026** and **+57.56%** over the next 90, against a monitor reading of **+0.02%** and a flagged gap of **1.30 percentage points**. The structural mechanism is a fixed per-block mint on an uncapped supply — **18 MON** a block since the **Jul 23 2026** consensus upgrade, **475.66M** minted in the window — of which only **37.80%** reaches the market, because the Foundation holds most of the stake and collects most of the reward, set against a base-fee burn of **23.63M MON**. The key risk is not the mint at all: **88.86B MON** sits outside the float, none of the insider calendar has begun, and **Nov 24 2026** releases about **6.66B MON** in a single day followed by **1.24B** a month to **2029**. There is no ceiling to fall back on — MON has no maximum supply, so the only limit on dilution is the schedule, and the schedule has barely started.
