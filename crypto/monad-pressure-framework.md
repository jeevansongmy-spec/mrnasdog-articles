# MON Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

MON supply is growing, and the big jump has not happened yet. From Aug 9 to Nov 7 2026, Monad sent **437.69M MON** into the market through block rewards and Foundation payouts, while the fee burn removed **39.17M MON**, a net rise of **+3.37%** on a float of **11.83B MON**. On Nov 24 2026, one year after mainnet, **15.63B MON** of team and investor coins unlock, so the next 90 days could add about **+149.91%**. Other trackers show about **+0.00%** for the last 90 days, because the float figure they use did not move.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 7 2026, MON supply in the market grew **+3.37%**. Other trackers read the same window as about **+0.00%**, a gap of **3.37 points**, and the MON coin page carries a warning note about it. The reason is simple: the circulating figure those trackers use for MON stayed at 11.83B MON for the whole window and does not add the new MON paid to stakers or the MON the Foundation hands out. On-chain, both flows are plain to see. The forward view is far larger: with the first-anniversary unlock on Nov 24 2026 and two monthly releases after it, the next 90 days point to about **+149.91%**. Monad is a young proof-of-stake chain whose supply is set by its unlock calendar, not by its fee burn.

## Sell pressure: where new MON comes from

Protocol inflation added **176.45M MON** to the market. Every Monad block mints a fixed 18 MON reward, down from 25 MON since the Jul 23 2026 upgrade that also made blocks faster, and the network produced 25.69M blocks from Aug 9 to Nov 7 2026, so **462.42M MON** was created in all. Most of it went to the Foundation's own staked MON, and by the published Monad tokenomics those rewards stay inside the Foundation's Ecosystem Development allocation. Only the share paid to validators as commission and to outside stakers reaches the market. Because the Foundation raised its stake from 11.15B to 15.77B MON on Oct 23 2026, that outside share fell, and the next 90 days should bring about **154.2M MON** from block rewards.

Vesting unlocks added nothing in the last 90 days, because no locked MON was due before the first anniversary. That changes fast. On Nov 24 2026 about **10.7B** team MON and **4.93B** investor MON unlock at once, and about **863M MON** follows on Dec 24 2026 and again on Jan 24 2027. Together that is **17.35B MON** in the next 90 days, more than the 11.83B MON circulating today.

Foundation payouts added **261.24M MON**. The Monad Foundation's payout wallets sent MON to 42 outside wallets over the window, about 50.8M in late August, 107.3M in September and 128.3M in October, so we expect a similar amount in the next 90 days. There is no court estate or bankruptcy pile of MON, so the long-term locked row is zero.

## Buy pressure: where new MON goes

The fee burn is Monad's only built-in way to remove MON. The base part of every transaction fee is destroyed, and from Aug 9 to Nov 7 2026 that came to about **39.17M MON**. The base fee sat at its floor almost the whole time, so the burn grows only when people use more block space. It cancelled about a fifth of the new MON that reached the market, and it is tiny next to the Nov 24 2026 unlock.

Nothing in the Monad protocol buys MON back, so the programmatic buyback row is zero. The Foundation buy row is zero too: Category Labs, the company that builds Monad, may buy up to $80M of MON on the open market by the end of 2026 but has not said it bought any, and the Foundation's August 2026 offer to buy investors' locked MON was turned down by almost everyone, with any coins it did buy staying locked on the same schedule. The new long-term lock row is zero as well. About 20.31B MON is staked, but staked MON can be withdrawn and still counts as circulating, and locked team and investor MON cannot be staked at all.

## Foundation and overhang

The Monad Foundation still holds a large pool of MON that is not counted as circulating. Its main Ecosystem Development wallet holds **9.91B MON** after it moved 7.23B MON out on Oct 23 2026: 3.57B MON into its 20 validator-delegation wallets, 3.5B MON into a new Foundation wallet and 160M MON into its payout wallets. The 20 delegation wallets now stake **15.77B MON** with Monad validators, and the rewards on that stake stay with the Foundation. The payout wallets hold **182.4M MON**, and they are the ones whose spending fills the Foundation payouts row.

Category Labs holds its own **3.95B MON** treasury, locked until Nov 24 2026, when about 987.5M MON unlocks into the company's hands, followed by about 82.3M MON a month until late 2029. We do not count it as new market supply until the company pays it out. The Foundation also bought an undisclosed, small amount of locked investor MON in August 2026, which unlocks into Foundation hands on the investor schedule. We check the Foundation wallets on-chain and follow Category Labs and Foundation news at every update; if any of these balances falls between checks, the outflow enters the Foundation payouts row at the next check.

## How MON compares to other young proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Monad pays validators with a fixed reward per block, about 2% of the initial 100B MON a year at full speed, and burns the base part of each fee, much like Ethereum's design. The difference is weight. Ethereum has no insider vesting left, so its supply moves only with validator pay and its burn. Monad is one year old, and most of its supply is still locked or held by the Foundation: only 11.7% of all MON circulates today, so even a modest reward lands on a small float.

In that sense MON looks more like other recent Layer 1 launches with large team and investor allocations, where a one-year cliff is followed by years of monthly unlocks. Those chains taught the same lesson: the unlock calendar, not the fee burn, decides supply in the first years. What is unusual about Monad is the size of the first step: the Nov 24 2026 unlock alone is larger than the whole float, and the monthly releases after it run until late 2029.

Monad also routes a large part of its staking rewards to the Foundation, because the Foundation is the biggest staker. That keeps much of the new MON off the market today, but it grows a pool the Foundation can later spend, so the Foundation payouts row matters as much as the block reward.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

On **Nov 24 2026**, about 10.7B team MON and 4.93B investor MON unlock, the largest single supply event in Monad's history so far. On **Dec 24 2026** and **Jan 24 2027**, about 863M MON each follows from the same team and investor schedules. By **Dec 31 2026**, Category Labs' open-market buyback authorization of up to $80M runs out; any purchase it discloses would be the first real buy pressure beyond the burn. Through the whole window, watch the Foundation's payout wallets, which sent about 100M MON a month lately, and whether the outside trackers restate their MON float after the unlock.

## Summary

MON supply grew **+3.37%** in the 90 days to Nov 7 2026, from block rewards that reached outside stakers and from Foundation payouts, while the fee burn removed only 39.17M MON. The Nov 24 2026 unlock of about 15.63B team and investor MON, plus two monthly releases, could lift the circulating supply by about **+149.91%** in the next 90 days. Monad has no supply cap and no buyback, so the main risk is that unlocked holders sell into a small float. The ceiling on new supply in the coming years is set by the remaining locked team and investor MON and the Foundation's large Ecosystem Development pool.
