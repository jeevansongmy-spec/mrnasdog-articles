# ALGO Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Algorand cannot mint a single new ALGO. All **10,000,000,000** were created in the 2019 genesis, the protocol has no mint call, and it has no burn either — and ALGO is still one of the more inflationary assets in this coverage, because both of its supply taps run out of pools the **Algorand Foundation** already controls. Over the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026** the block-production bonus paid **18.7M ALGO** to block proposers, and another **99.3M ALGO** was transferred out of the Foundation's **74** published wallets, against **zero** buyback and **zero** burn. On a circulating base of **9.04B ALGO** that is **+1.31%** net, easing to **+0.97%** next quarter. ALGO is **a hard-capped chain whose entire inflation question is how fast one treasury spends**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 29 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ALGO at +1.31% net**: sell pressure of **118.0M ALGO** against buy pressure of **zero**, on a circulating base of **9.04B ALGO**. Our own supply monitor reads the same window at **+1.32%**, a gap of **0.01 percentage points**, far inside the half-point tolerance, so no data-conflict flag ships on the Algorand overview page. That agreement is worth naming precisely, because the two sides measure different things: the monitor divides a classified circulating-supply series by its own value 90 days earlier, while this ledger counts the individual transfers that left the Foundation's published wallets plus the block payouts that actually landed. They converge because the Algorand Foundation's published classification is tracking its real flows in near real time. The forward reading is **+0.97%**. Algorand is best labelled **capped by design, with no mint and no burn, and inflationary purely by treasury policy**.

## Sell pressure: where new ALGO comes from

Nowhere, in the literal sense — and this is the first thing most readings of Algorand get wrong. No ALGO has been created since 2019. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **18.7M ALGO**, and every one of those coins already existed: they sit in the fee sink, a protocol account outside the market, and the chain pays them to whoever proposes a block. The published rate is a per-block bonus that started at **10 ALGO** and shrinks **1%** every million blocks; on chain it read **8.600578 ALGO** when the window opened and **8.345131 ALGO** when it closed. Multiply the average by the **2,827,331** blocks Algorand actually produced and you get **23.9M** — which is the number a documentation-only reading ships, and it is wrong by about a quarter. Sampling **2,500** block headers at random across the window shows why: only **81.76%** of blocks pay anything at all, because a block pays nothing when its proposer is not eligible for rewards. The realised payout is **19.6M ALGO**. Two deductions follow. **3.92%** of it went to proposers that are themselves on the Foundation's published registry, so it never left custody, and users paid **0.12M ALGO** of transaction fees back into the sink over the same window. That leaves **18.7M ALGO**. The Foundation's own quarterly report books **20.1M ALGO** of validator rewards for the 91 days to **Jun 30 2026**, which scales to **19.9M** per 90 days — three independent angles inside four percent of each other.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is the larger tap at **99.3M ALGO**, and it was measured transfer by transfer rather than read off balances. All **74** addresses the Algorand Foundation publishes on its transparency page were swept for outgoing payments across the exact window — **74,268** transactions. Payments landing outside that set of wallets total **99.3M ALGO**; a further **118.4M** moved between the Foundation's own wallets and is netted out, because moving a coin from one treasury address to another adds nothing to the market. Reading balances alone would have missed most of it: the Fireblocks market-operations wallet rests at **3.6M ALGO** while passing multiples of that through itself. Three flows dominate. **51.3M ALGO** went to a single trading hub in **13** tranches on **13** separate dates between **Jun 1 2026** and **Aug 26 2026**, roughly one a week at **3M** to **6M** each — this is the structured selling the Foundation discloses, executed out of dedicated published wallets with automatic halts on a sharp price drop. **30.0M ALGO** left in one payment on **Jul 17 2026** to an account that is not on the published registry and that now holds **zero**, having dispersed the lot. The remaining **18.0M ALGO** went out in dated grant, ecosystem and xGov batches.

The other two sell rows are genuinely zero. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the early-backer and team release schedule finished in **2024**; there is no cliff, no escrow and no unlock calendar left to fire on Algorand. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: no estate, no trustee, no court-ordered ALGO distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new ALGO goes

Nowhere, and each of the four rows fails for its own reason. Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **zero**, and that is a measurement rather than an absence of announcements: the same sweep of the Foundation's **74** published wallets, run over inbound payments, found **zero ALGO** arriving from outside the set across the whole window. Every flow was outbound. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **zero** because Algorand destroys nothing. Half of each block's fees is paid straight to the proposer and half stays in the fee sink, which is the same pool the bonus is paid from, so fees recycle rather than disappear. Both surfaces of a burn were checked. Algorand has no canonical dead address for coins to be stranded at, and the ledger's own supply figure read **9,774,008,190.06 ALGO** at one block and **9,774,009,313.92 ALGO** two hundred blocks later — rising at the proposer-payout rate, never falling. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero**: the Algorand Foundation is a disclosed seller through published market-operations wallets, not a bidder. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** even though about **2.00B ALGO** takes part in consensus, because Algorand participation carries no bond, no waiting period to exit and no slashing. Those coins remain spendable throughout, which makes them custody rather than a lock; booking them would have invented **22%** of deflation that no supply figure anywhere reflects.

## Foundation and overhang

Four ALGO overhangs are tracked. The first is the Algorand Foundation treasury itself: **915.0M ALGO** across **73** published wallets, on no release plan at all, reconciled after the fact each quarter rather than scheduled in advance — the largest single address holds **140.6M ALGO**. The second is the fee sink at **Y76M3MSY6DKBRHBL7C3NNDXGS5IIMQVQVUAB6MP4XEMMGVF2QWNPL226CA**, which holds **18.1M ALGO** and pays out at roughly **19.7M** a quarter — about one more quarter of runway before the Foundation must top it up from treasury. The third is the pair of structured-selling wallets, **37VPAD3CK7CDHRE4U3J75IE4HLFN5ZWVKJ52YFNBX753NNDN6PUP2N7YKI** resting empty and **44GWRTQGSAYUJJCQ3GFINYKZXMBDVKCF75VMCXKORN7ZJ6BKPNG2RMGH7E** holding **3.6M ALGO** — balances that say nothing about throughput, since the pipe they feed moved **51.3M** this quarter. The fourth is the unlisted recipient of the **Jul 17 2026** payment, **F2IQ7LFJOWCJTPSX3TANDSXUOHFIHOOPS7R7URJFQXAEFYRXQ5TQJTQ3QI**, which received **30.0M ALGO** and has already emptied to **zero**. The rule is the same for all four: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ALGO compares to other hard-capped Layer 1 chains

Algorand belongs to the small class of Layer 1 chains with a fixed, fully-minted cap — the opposite pole from uncapped proof-of-stake chains that mint staking rewards out of thin air every block. Against those, Algorand's promise is genuine and permanent: an uncapped chain paying **7%** to **12%** a year creates coins that did not exist, forever, whereas Algorand's **+1.31%** quarter is bounded absolutely by the **933.1M ALGO** still in Foundation custody. When that pool is spent, ALGO's inflation goes to zero by construction and cannot restart. No uncapped chain can say that.

Against the other capped chains, though, Algorand looks weaker, and the reason is where the supply comes from. A halving-model chain with a hard cap pays its block reward from an unminted remainder on a fixed schedule that nobody can accelerate — the issuance is protocol policy, published years ahead, and no committee can decide to double it. Algorand's equivalent is a treasury decision. The block bonus is a Foundation-funded subsidy paid out of a pool the Foundation refills, and the far larger tap, the **99.3M** of wallet outflow, has no published release plan at all; the **30.0M** that left on **Jul 17 2026** is the clearest case, a single payment to an unlisted account, disclosed only after the fact. Against fee-burn chains the comparison is starker still. A chain with a base-fee burn converts usage directly into supply destruction, so heavy quarters shrink supply mechanically. Algorand routes its fees back to block proposers instead, which is good for validator economics and does nothing at all for holders. The upgrade that would change Algorand's standing in this class is not a smaller grant budget — it is routing some share of fees to destruction, which is the one edit that would give ALGO a counterweight it currently does not have.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the fee sink balance. It holds **18.1M ALGO** against a payout rate near **19.7M** a quarter, so the Algorand Foundation must top it up from treasury before roughly **Nov 2026**; the size of that top-up is the clearest forward signal on how long the subsidy runs. Second, the end of the bonus commitment. The Foundation committed to funding the block bonus for about **24** months from the **Jan 2025** launch, which points at **Jan 2027**, just past this window — reporting since suggests an extension, and confirming it either way changes the shape of Sell #1 for all of 2027. Third, whether the **Jul 17 2026** payment repeats. It is carried at zero in the forward column because a single firing is not a pattern; a second one of similar size would push the next-90-day reading from **+0.97%** back to about **+1.31%** and move the Inflation score down a band. Fourth, the Q3 2026 Algorand Foundation transparency report, expected around **Nov 2026**, which is where that **30.0M** should finally be named. Fifth, the structured selling rhythm out of the published market-operations wallets, last seen firing on **Aug 26 2026** — a pause would cut about half the sell side, and an acceleration would add to it directly.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ALGO at **+1.31%** net for the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026** and **+0.97%** for the quarter ahead, against a monitor reading of **+1.32%** — a **0.01** percentage-point gap and no data conflict. The mechanism is unusual and worth stating plainly: Algorand mints nothing and burns nothing, so all of that inflation is already-minted ALGO leaving Algorand Foundation custody, **18.7M** through block rewards and **99.3M** through transfers out of its published wallets, with no buyback, no burn and no lock on the other side. The key risk is that the pace is a policy rather than a schedule — **933.1M ALGO** remains in published custody with no release plan, and a single undisclosed **30.0M** payment on **Jul 17 2026** accounted for nearly a third of the quarter's sell side on its own. The ceiling is real, however, and it is the best thing about this asset: the **10,000,000,000 ALGO** genesis cap cannot be raised, so every quarter of spending permanently shrinks the amount of ALGO that can ever surprise a holder.
