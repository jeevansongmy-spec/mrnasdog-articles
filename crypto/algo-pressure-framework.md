# ALGO Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

ALGO supply is growing even though Algorand never creates a new coin. All **10B ALGO** were made at launch, and the Algorand Foundation still holds about **942M** of them outside the circulating count. In the last 90 days **117.36M ALGO** left those Foundation accounts — **98.44M** from its wallets and **18.91M** as staking payouts — while fees took back only **101.2K**. Net, circulating ALGO rose **+1.29%**, and we project about **+0.82%** for the next 90 days, capped by what the Foundation has left.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Sep 29 2026, Algorand added **117.36M ALGO** to the market and removed **101.2K**, a net rise of **+1.29%** on a circulating supply of **9.06B ALGO**. Our inflation monitor, which reads the circulating count day by day, shows **+1.33%** for the same stretch — a gap of **0.03 percentage points**, well inside our 0.5-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown. For the next 90 days we project **+0.82%**: the steady streams continue, but two large one-off Foundation payments are not repeated. In one line: ALGO is a fixed-supply coin whose float is still growing, because the Foundation's reserve is being released into it.

## Sell pressure: where new ALGO comes from

Protocol inflation, in the usual sense, does not exist on Algorand: there is no mint, and the 10B total has not changed since launch. But stakers are paid, and the money comes from a fee account the Foundation tops up — it moved **19.3M ALGO** into it this window. That account sits outside the circulating count, so each payout to an outside staker adds new coins to the market. Paid block by block, the payouts came to **18.91M ALGO** in 90 days, with a proposer paid on about 82% of blocks. The reward per block falls 1% every million blocks — from 8.51 ALGO at the start of the window to 8.26 ALGO now — so the next 90 days come to about **18.39M**.

Vesting unlocks add **0**. The launch-era vesting schedules ended in 2024, and no unlock tracker lists anything left to release.

Foundation releases are the largest row: **98.44M ALGO** left Foundation wallets for outside hands in 90 days. Of that, **43.28M** went through the Foundation's rules-based selling desk in tranches from Jul 2 to Aug 26 2026; **30.00M** went out in a single payment on Jul 17 2026 and was then passed on in smaller lots; **12.63M** went out in a single payment on Sep 22 2026 to a new wallet that still holds it; and **12.53M** paid for grants, community programs and running costs. July alone saw 66.14M leave. For the next 90 days we keep the recurring streams at their window pace, **55.81M**, and drop the two single payments, which had no schedule.

Long-term locks and bankruptcy add **0**: Algorand has no estate, no trustee schedule and no unwinding lock.

## Buy pressure: where new ALGO goes

There is no programmatic buyback — **0**. No contract or treasury buys ALGO back, and the Foundation received no ALGO from outside wallets in the window.

There is no fee burn either — **0**. Algorand fees are collected, not destroyed. The one address people send ALGO to as a "burn" does not keep it, and it sits inside the circulating count anyway.

Foundation buying is **0**: the Foundation sells ALGO to fund its work. New long-term locks are **0** too: about 2.03B ALGO is staked online, but Algorand staking has no lock-up, and staked coins stay in the circulating count.

The one real buy-side flow is fee collection. Every transaction fee goes into the fee account, outside the circulating count, which took about **101.2K ALGO** off the market in 90 days — around 0.036 ALGO a block, because Algorand fees are tiny. Half of it comes straight back out inside the staking payouts. Against 117.36M released, fees offset under 0.1% of the sell side.

## Foundation and overhang

Every ALGO not yet in circulation is held by the Algorand Foundation, across 304 published wallets. On Sep 29 2026 its current wallets held about **900.9M ALGO**, the largest single one about 140.6M; one old wallet held **29.5M** and has not moved; and the fee account that pays stakers held **11.6M**. Two wallets are its published selling wallets, holding about 3.64M between them. Together that is **942.1M ALGO**, and adding it back to the circulating count gives exactly 10B. None of it has a published release schedule. At this window's pace — about 117M a quarter — the reserve would last roughly eight more quarters. We read these balances on-chain at every rebuild: if any of them falls between checks, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How ALGO compares to other pre-mined proof-of-stake chains

Most proof-of-stake Layer 1s pay stakers by creating new coins. Ethereum mints new ETH for validators and burns part of every fee; Cardano pays stakers from a reserve that was created at launch and shrinks each epoch. Algorand is closest to the second model, but simpler: there is no minting at all, and the only source of new float is a foundation-held reserve. That makes ALGO's supply growth a management decision — how fast the Foundation sells, grants and funds staking — rather than a rule written into the protocol.

Compared with chains that release a pre-mined supply on a timetable, such as XRP's monthly escrow, ALGO has no published schedule for its remaining reserve. The selling desk follows written rules on how much it sells each day and at what price, but the grants and the one-off payments do not. And unlike chains with a fee burn, Algorand's fees are too small to matter: they offset under 0.1% of what leaves the reserve.

The hard cap is the one firm limit. Supply cannot pass 10B, and with about 942M still held by the Foundation, the float can grow by at most about 10.4% more from here. Once that reserve is spent, ALGO becomes a fixed-supply coin in practice — unless the Foundation's staking review adds a new way to pay stakers.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The staking reward per block steps down 1% at block 66M, around Oct 15 2026, then again around Nov 16 2026 and Dec 18 2026; each step trims the payouts slightly. The selling desk has not sent a tranche since Aug 26 2026 — if it restarts, it adds to the Foundation row. The Foundation's Q3 2026 transparency report, due around late October, should show how much it sold and granted in July to September. The Foundation's review of how to pay stakers after the current bonus funding ends is still with its advisory council; any change that funds rewards by creating new ALGO would change this reading. And the 12.63M ALGO paid out on Sep 22 2026 still sits in one wallet — it is already counted as circulating, so moving it adds nothing new.

## Summary

ALGO's circulating supply grew **+1.29%** in the 90 days to Sep 29 2026, and we project **+0.82%** for the next 90 days. Algorand never mints new coins, so all of that growth is the Algorand Foundation releasing its reserve: **98.44M ALGO** from its wallets and **18.91M** in staking payouts, against only **101.2K** taken back by fees. The key risk is the pace of Foundation selling and grants, which follows no public schedule for the remaining **942M ALGO**. The ceiling is firm: supply can never pass 10B.
