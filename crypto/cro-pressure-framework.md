Cronos carries the heaviest scheduled supply load in the Pressure Framework, and almost none of it comes from the blockchain. Over the last 90 days CRO added **3.73B** of new supply: **3.50B** released by a Strategic Reserve of 70B CRO that was burned in 2021 and re-created by a governance vote in 2025, plus **226.6M** of staking issuance. Nothing came off the other side — no buyback, no fee burn, no disclosed purchase. The framework reads **+7.88% net** against our supply monitor at **+8.54%**, a gap of **0.66 percentage points** that comes entirely from which supply base the percentage is taken against. The reserve pays out **1.167B CRO** every month until **Mar 2030**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Jul 27 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **CRO at +7.88% net**. Sell pressure is **3,726.6M CRO**, buy pressure is **zero**, against a circulating base of **47.28B CRO**. Our supply monitor reads **+8.54%**, so a monitor-gap note ships on the CRO overview — but the deep walk closed it cleanly. The monitor measured growth of **3,718.7M CRO**; this build measured **3,726.6M**. That is the same number to within 0.2%. The 0.66-point difference is only the denominator: the monitor divides the flow by the supply as it stood 90 days ago, **43.56B**, while the framework divides by today's **47.28B**. Looking forward, three more reserve releases and a smaller mint project **3,688.5M**, or **+7.80%**. Cronos is best characterised as **a chain whose inflation is a calendar, not a consensus mechanism**.

## Sell pressure: where new CRO comes from

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **3,500M CRO** and dominates the ledger by a factor of fifteen. In 2021 the exchange operator behind Cronos destroyed 70B CRO and marketed it as permanent scarcity. In March 2025 a Cronos governance vote re-created that same 70B and placed it in an escrow account on Cronos POS that vests linearly across 60 monthly periods of **1,166,666,667 CRO** each, ending **Mar 17 2030**. Three periods fell inside this window — **May 18 2026**, **Jun 17 2026** and **Jul 17 2026** — and reading the escrow's balance at both ends of the window confirms it: **54.83B** on **Apr 28 2026** against **51.33B** today, a fall of exactly **3,500,000,001 CRO**. The published schedule and the realised release agree to a single token, which is rare enough to be worth stating plainly.

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **226.6M CRO**, measured off the chain's own total-supply counter rather than inferred from a rate: **98,587,544,942** CRO at the start of the window against **98,814,111,496** today. That answers the first question anyone should ask about CRO — total supply is rising, not flat and not falling. It is, however, rising more slowly every month. A network upgrade activated on **May 20 2026** replaced the flat 1% staking mint with an emission that decays **6.8%** every month, and the chain now reports its rate at **0.857%**. Because that change landed mid-window, the forward figure is re-based on the post-change rate rather than the blended average: **188.5M CRO** over the next 90 days. The same upgrade wrote a hard ceiling of **100B CRO** into the chain itself, which leaves room for roughly **1.19B** more CRO to be minted, ever.

The other two sell rows are **zero**. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because nothing was released outside the published schedule — and because counting it would double-count the reserve tokens already booked in Sell #2. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero because Cronos has no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered CRO distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new CRO goes

All four buy rows are **zero**, and this is the reason CRO scores where it does. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero: Cronos operates no buyback contract and no revenue-funded purchase programme. What the 2026 tokenomics change delivers is a slower mint, not a bid — it reduces what is created rather than removing what exists, and those are different things. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is zero: Cronos does not burn transaction fees, and validators plus the community pool collect them exactly as they collect new issuance. This one deserved checking rather than assuming, because the announcement language around the 2026 change reads burn-adjacent. The chain's designated burn address held **200,000,621 CRO** at the start of the window and **200,005,122** today — an inflow of **4,501 CRO** across 90 days, which is dust. The **200M** sitting in that address comes from community burns that all pre-date this window, and the community pool that an earlier proposal floated as a burn source actually grew, from **179.8M** to **213.8M** CRO.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is zero: no open-market CRO purchase was disclosed inside the window. The widely-reported **684.4M CRO** treasury acquisition was bought over the counter directly from the issuer at about **15.3 cents** and closed before this window opened, so it never competed for the traded float. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero in effect: the **May 20 2026** upgrade added staking tiers with one-, two- and four-year exit waits and up to **7%** bonus yield, but take-up so far is **8 positions** holding **10,705 CRO** between them. Against a 47.28B float that is not a mechanism yet.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled pools are tracked for CRO. The largest is the Strategic Reserve escrow itself, still holding **51.33B CRO** on a published monthly schedule running to **Mar 2030**; it is read on-chain at every rebuild and its releases are already booked in Sell #2. The second is the reserve operating wallet, and it carries the most important caveat on this page. Every vested tranche is swept there, and it took in the full **3,500,000,001 CRO** across the window while paying out only **550M** — **500M** on **Apr 30 2026** and **50M** on **Jun 11 2026**. Its balance rose from **7.83B** to **10.78B**. So roughly **2.95B** of the supply this ledger books as released is, today, still parked in an identified team wallet rather than in traded hands. The framework ships the conservative reading — the full 3.50B — because the escrow genuinely released it and because the circulating figure used as the denominator already classifies it as circulating; netting it out of one side while leaving it in the other would mix two conventions. The third pool is the community pool at **213.8M CRO**, spendable only by a passing governance vote. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CRO compares to other exchange-adjacent chains

The natural comparison class for CRO is exchange-affiliated tokens that also run their own chain. The defining mechanical difference is direction of travel. The large exchange tokens in that class run quarterly buyback-and-burn programmes funded from exchange revenue, so their sell ledger is a small validator mint and their buy ledger is a real, recurring destruction of existing supply — supply shrinks. CRO is built the other way round: its buy ledger is empty by design, and its sell ledger is a fixed calendar that nothing in the market can slow. A quarterly burn responds to revenue; a vesting calendar responds to nothing at all.

Against ordinary proof-of-stake Layer 1s the contrast is different again. An uncapped staking chain typically runs somewhere between 3% and 12% a year of issuance, and every token it creates is genuinely new. CRO's **226.6M** mint is tiny by that standard — under **0.9%** a year and falling **6.8%** a month — and its hard cap of **100B** is now enforced by the chain rather than promised in a document. Judged only on consensus issuance, Cronos is one of the quieter chains in the catalog. The **3.50B** quarterly release sitting on top of it is not consensus issuance; it is a governance decision to un-burn a burn, and it is fifteen times larger than everything the protocol mints.

The closest structural analogue is not a chain at all but a token with a large treasury on a published multi-year unlock. Those assets share CRO's core property: the supply schedule is known years in advance, so the only genuine uncertainty is demand. What makes CRO unusual inside that group is that the schedule exists because previously destroyed supply was restored, which is why the total supply and the circulating supply have both been climbing while the protocol's own mint rate falls.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Three Strategic Reserve releases of **1.167B CRO** each are already dated: **Aug 17 2026**, **Sep 16 2026** and **Oct 17 2026**. The reserve operating wallet is the single most informative thing to watch — if its **10.78B** balance keeps climbing, the released supply is being warehoused rather than sold; if it starts draining faster than the **550M** it paid out this window, real supply is reaching the market ahead of the ledger. Watch the mint rate too: at **6.8%** monthly decay the reported **0.857%** should read near **0.70%** by late October 2026, and any governance move to pause the decay would show up there first. Watch take-up of the time-locked staking tiers, which would turn Buy #4 into a genuine offset if it ever reaches billions rather than thousands. And watch for any proposal that converts the community pool's **213.8M CRO** into an actual burn — announced in principle, never yet executed on-chain.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Cronos at **+7.88%** net supply growth over the last 90 days and **+7.80%** projected for the next 90, against a monitor reading of **+8.54%** whose only difference is the supply base used. The structural mechanism is a governance decision rather than a protocol: 70B CRO burned in 2021 was re-created in 2025 and now releases **1.167B** a month until **Mar 2030**, which is fifteen times the chain's own **226.6M** staking mint. The key risk is that this schedule is indifferent to price, and the buy side is empty — no buyback, no fee burn, no lock-up with meaningful take-up. The one genuine positive is the ceiling: the **100B** hard cap is now enforced in the chain's own code with only about **1.19B** of mintable headroom left, so once the reserve finishes vesting, CRO's supply story ends.
