Cronos added **3.72 billion** new CRO to the market in the last 90 days and removed **64.6 million**, a net of **+7.73%** of circulating supply — one of the heaviest sustained supply expansions in the top 50. Almost all of it comes from a single governance decision. The **70 billion** CRO burned in 2021 was re-created by a 2025 Cronos vote and now vests out of an on-chain escrow at **1,166,666,667 CRO** every 30.4 days until **March 2030**. Our supply monitor reads **+8.64%**, a gap of **0.92 percentage points** that is almost entirely a base-date convention, so the monitor-gap chip ships on the overview. Cronos has a real **100 billion** hard cap written into the chain's code — but the cap constrains minting, not the reserve, and the reserve is what is moving.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 11 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **CRO at +7.73% net**: sell pressure of **3,719.98M CRO** against buy pressure of **64.56M CRO**, on a circulating base of **47.31B CRO**. The next 90 days project to **+7.64%**. Our supply monitor reads **+8.64%** for the same window, and the deep walk resolved the difference: both measure the same **3.76B** of new supply but divide by different bases — the monitor by circulating supply as it stood 90 days ago (**43.55B**), the framework by today's (**47.31B**). That base difference alone is **0.69** of the **0.92** points; the remaining **0.23** is the newly time-locked CRO that the framework deducts and the wider market still counts as circulating, plus noise. CRO is best characterised as **inflationary by governance decision, not by protocol design** — the chain itself is quiet, and the reserve is loud.

## Sell pressure: where new CRO comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, contributed **220.0M CRO**. This is the Cronos POS staking mint, read straight off the chain's own supply counter: total supply went from **98.628B** to **98.848B** across the window. It is also the one number moving in the holder's favour. A governance upgrade that activated on **May 20 2026** introduced a compound decay of **6.8% per month** applied on top of the base issuance rate, driving the annual mint deterministically toward zero and pinning a **100 billion** maximum supply into the chain's code. The observed yearly rate has already fallen from **1.00%** at the window's open to **0.83%** today. Because that change landed mid-window, the trailing **220.0M** is a blend of the old and new mechanisms; the forward column is re-based on the post-change rate only, giving **180.0M** for the next 90 days.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, contributed **3,500.0M CRO** — sixteen times the mint, and the reason CRO scores where it does. In November 2021 the issuer burned **70 billion** CRO, cutting the maximum supply from 100 billion to about 30 billion. In March 2025 a Cronos governance proposal re-minted all **70 billion** into a Strategic Reserve escrow and restored the 100 billion cap. That escrow is a periodic vesting account holding 60 monthly periods of **1,166,666,667 CRO**, starting **Mar 18 2025** and ending **Mar 17 2030**. Three periods fired inside this window — **May 17 2026**, **Jun 17 2026** and **Jul 17 2026** — and the escrow balance fell by exactly **3,500,000,001 CRO**, matching the schedule to under one token. That is an important detail: on many chains a vesting calendar runs ahead of the realised outflow and the gross figure overstates the pressure. Here the released amount equals the scheduled amount, so there is no undrawn backlog and no overstatement. The escrow still holds **51.33B CRO** across 44 remaining periods.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, reads **zero**, because every reserve tranche is already booked in Sell #2 and double-counting it would inflate the ledger. It carries the most interesting finding of this build, though. Of the **3.50B CRO** that left the vesting escrow, **3.45B** went straight into a single reserve operating wallet that now holds about **10.78B CRO**; only around **50M** continued past it. The supply has been released, but most of it has not yet been spent into the market — it has changed hands from a contract to a treasury. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, reads **zero**: Cronos has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new CRO goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**. Cronos operates no buyback contract and the issuer has disclosed no CRO repurchase programme. The May 2026 upgrade is frequently described as making CRO deflationary; it does not. It decays issuance — the chain mints less every month — but it destroys nothing, so it can at best drive net new supply to zero, never below it. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is also **zero**, and this is a genuine structural difference from Ethereum. The Cronos EVM fee-market module implements dynamic fees but does not burn the base fee: both the base fee and the tip are collected by validators. On the staking chain, the address the protocol code treats as burned took in **4,501 CRO** across the entire 90 days — dust, and not a mechanism.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**, and the outlook for it worsened inside this window. The one identified would-be accumulator of CRO — a listed treasury vehicle billed at up to **$6.4 billion** of CRO — was mutually terminated on **Aug 7 2026**, along with the attached services agreement and prediction-market deal. The framework never booked that deal as buy pressure because no dated on-market purchase was observable, so its cancellation does not change the number; it removes the main path by which the number could have changed.

Buy #4, new long-term lock, is the one non-zero row on the buy side at **64.56M CRO**, and it is five days old. The same May 2026 upgrade introduced time-locked staking: delegators can commit CRO for 1, 2 or 4 years for a bonus yield of **2%**, **4%** or **7%**, with the lock enforced on-chain. Take-up was immaterial until **Aug 7 2026**, when it jumped: **64.5M CRO** entered across 93 positions in five days, **45.2M** of it on the four-year tier, taking the total to **64.56M CRO** in 104 positions. Because five days is too thin to extrapolate, the forward column repeats the measured total rather than extending the pace. If the observed pace did hold for a full quarter, the next-90-day net would fall from **+7.64%** to roughly **+5.3%** — meaningful, but still deeply inflationary.

## Foundation and overhang

Three team-controlled overhangs are tracked for Cronos. First, the Strategic Reserve escrow itself, holding **51.33B CRO** — the largest single overhang in the top 50 relative to float, equal to about **108%** of everything currently circulating. It is not unscheduled: 44 further periods of **1,166,666,667 CRO** are already written into the vesting account and run to **Mar 17 2030**, and the chain read confirms it pays on time. Second, the reserve operating wallet that receives each tranche, holding about **10.78B CRO** after taking in **3.45B** during this window. That balance is released supply sitting one hop from the market with no published release rule — capacity without a stated schedule, which is exactly what the framework treats as an overhang rather than as pressure. Third, the Cronos POS community pool at roughly **218.8M CRO**, up **33.0M** over the window and spendable only by a passing governance vote.

The trigger is the same for all three, and it is the reason they are enumerated even while their row value stays at zero: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. For the escrow that is already priced in through Sell #2. For the operating wallet it is not — a decision to deploy even a fraction of that **10.78B** would add sell pressure on top of the scheduled unlocks, and nothing about the current reading anticipates it.

## How CRO compares to other exchange-backed chain tokens

The natural peer set is exchange-backed chain tokens: BNB, OKB, GT, LEO. Structurally, CRO is the outlier — and it is the outlier in the wrong direction. The defining mechanism of that class is a quarterly or continuous buyback-and-burn funded by exchange revenue, which makes net supply shrink year after year; BNB has burned its way down from 200 million toward a 100 million target, and OKB and LEO run comparable revenue-funded destruction. Cronos has no buyback and no burn at all. It sits in the same commercial category but runs the opposite supply policy, and the framework reads the difference directly: peers with a live burn print negative net supply, while CRO prints **+7.73%**.

Against uncapped continuous-emission Layer-1s such as Solana or Cosmos Hub, the comparison flips in Cronos's favour on the mint but not on the total. Cronos's protocol issuance is unusually low — under **1%** a year, now decaying **6.8%** a month toward zero, with a real **100 billion** ceiling enforced in the chain's own code. On the mint alone, Cronos would score well; a chain that mints **220.0M** against a **47.31B** float is a quiet chain. What separates CRO from those peers is that its dominant supply force is not issuance at all. It is a vesting schedule created by a vote, and vesting is invisible to the emission rate that most comparisons cite.

The closest structural analogue is actually a young token still working through investor and team cliffs — a category CRO does not otherwise belong to, given it launched in 2018. The 2025 re-mint reset Cronos to that profile: a large locked allocation dripping into circulation on a fixed calendar for years, with the float rising faster than demand can plausibly absorb. The difference from a normal vesting token is that these tokens were once destroyed and then voted back into existence, which is why the March 2025 proposal drew heavy community opposition even as it passed on validator weight. The framework does not score intent; it scores flow. The flow is **1.17B CRO** a month, on schedule, for another 44 months.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the three scheduled Strategic Reserve releases on **Aug 17 2026**, **Sep 16 2026** and **Oct 17 2026**, each **1,166,666,667 CRO**. These are already in the forward column; the thing to verify is that realised keeps matching scheduled, because a shortfall would mean the escrow is accumulating a backlog. Second, the reserve operating wallet at **10.78B CRO** — any material outflow from it is pure additional sell pressure that no schedule announces in advance. Third, time-locked staking: whether the burst that began **Aug 7 2026** is a one-off migration or a sustained programme is the single largest swing factor on the buy side, worth roughly **2.3 percentage points** of forward net if it holds. Fourth, the decay path of the staking mint, which should keep stepping the yearly rate down about **6.8%** a month from today's **0.83%** — a governance change to that decay rate would move Sell #1. Fifth, any replacement for the terminated treasury vehicle: a credible, dated open-market CRO purchase programme is the only realistic route to a non-zero Buy #3.

## Summary

Cronos (CRO) is inflationary by governance decision rather than by protocol design. The chain itself is quiet — it minted **220.0M CRO** in 90 days on a rate now decaying **6.8%** a month toward zero, under a real **100 billion** cap enforced in code — but the **70 billion** CRO burned in 2021 and re-created by a 2025 vote releases **1,166,666,667 CRO** every 30.4 days until **March 2030**, and it released **3,500,000,001** of them in this window alone. Against that, buy pressure is **64.56M CRO** of five-day-old time-locked staking, with no buyback and no fee burn anywhere in the design, giving a net of **+7.73%** last 90 days and **+7.64%** projected. The key risk beyond the schedule is the **10.78B CRO** sitting in the reserve operating wallet — supply that has already been released but not yet spent — and the ceiling that matters is not the 100 billion cap, which minting will not reach, but the **51.33B** still queued inside the escrow.
