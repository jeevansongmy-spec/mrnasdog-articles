# BDX Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Beldex is an uncapped CryptoNote privacy chain, and almost none of its inflation is mining. Over the 90 days to **Aug 28 2026** the BDX block subsidy created just **1.64M BDX**, while a single scheduled tranche of **130.68M BDX** left the Beldex Ecosystem Development wallet on **Jun 30 2026** — the eighteenth such quarterly release. Against that, Flash transaction fees and Beldex Name Service fees burned **1.04M BDX**, and there is no BDX buyback of any kind. Net supply pressure is about **+1.67%** of the **7.87B** circulating BDX, and the nineteenth Beldex release on **Sep 30 2026** makes the next quarter read the same.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the window ending **Aug 28 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads BDX at **+1.67% net**, with the forward view also at **+1.67%**: a Beldex sell side of **132.32M BDX** against a buy side of **1.04M BDX**. Our own supply monitor reads **+1.66%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.01 percentage points** — far inside the framework's **0.5-point** tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships and no deep walk was required. The agreement is unusually tight for a privacy coin, and the reason is structural: one dated Beldex event accounts for **98.8%** of the quarter's new supply, so there is very little left for the two readings to disagree about. BDX is best labelled **a scheduled-release chain, not an emission chain** — its dilution arrives on a calendar, in identical steps, and can be predicted a quarter at a time.

## Sell pressure: where new BDX comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1.64M BDX**, and the interesting part is how small that is. Beldex runs a masternode-secured CryptoNote chain with a flat block subsidy, and this build measured both the block rate and the subsidy rather than assuming them. Reading the Beldex chain at both window ends gives **259,185 blocks** across **7,775,832 seconds**, an average interval of **30.00 seconds**, exactly the protocol target. The subsidy was taken from a raw coinbase transaction rather than a summary field: the coinbase output pays **6.25 BDX** to the atomic unit, while the explorer's headline reward figure reads about **6.26** because it folds in recycled transaction fees, which come out of existing supply and are not new BDX. That is **1,619,906 BDX** mined in a quarter, plus one anomalous coinbase of **18,906 BDX** paid when the Beldex network restarted at the Hardfork 21 fork block on **Jun 27 2026**. On a **7.87B** float, all the mining Beldex does in a quarter moves the supply by about two hundredths of one percent.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **130.68M BDX** — the entire story. Beldex releases a fixed tranche from its Ecosystem Development wallet on the last day of every quarter, and the **Jun 30 2026** firing was the eighteenth. This build did not take that from an unlock calendar, because a calendar records entitlement rather than movement. It took it from the reserve itself: the project publishes the wallet address, its view key and its remaining balance every quarter, and the Jun 30 2026 disclosure reports **1,607,760,000 BDX** left. That balance is also stated as **16.24%** of the Beldex tokenomics base, which pins the base at **9.9B BDX**, which makes the Ecosystem allocation **3,960,000,000 BDX** — and the amount already released divides into exactly eighteen identical tranches of **130,680,000 BDX**. The tranche is a constant, not an estimate, and the next one is dated.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** — not because nothing is held, but because nothing beyond the scheduled tranche was observed leaving. Booking the same Beldex release in both Sell #2 and Sell #3 would invent pressure that does not exist. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **0**: Beldex has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee, and no expiring lockup contract. The result is a Beldex sell ledger with exactly two live lines, one of which is 80 times the other.

## Buy pressure: where new BDX goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. Beldex publishes a full quarterly report enumerating every ecosystem action it took — exchange listings, code audits, wallet integrations, event sponsorships, product releases — and no purchase of BDX appears anywhere in it. The only buyback material that exists for BDX is a promotional post from early 2023 that never became a mechanism, and this build re-checked it rather than inheriting the previous conclusion. Buy #3, Foundation buying, is **0** for the same reason: Beldex funds itself out of the reserve it releases, not out of revenue, so it has no reason to be a buyer and has never disclosed being one.

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **1.04M BDX** and it is the only thing pushing back. Beldex burns are protocol-level: Flash transaction fees and Beldex Name Service subscription fees are destroyed, and the daemon subtracts them from the chain's own supply total, so the counter is destruction rather than a marketing dashboard. The chain-wide burn counter reads **11,703,911 BDX** cumulative today against **11.17M** reported at **Jun 30 2026**, a direct read of **533,911 BDX** over 59 days. Two independent cross-checks were run this session: a systematic sample of **3,988** Beldex blocks spread across the exact window scales to about **1.22M**, and Beldex Name Service registrations at **6,528** today, priced on a **650** to **4,000 BDX** ladder by subscription term, land in the same range. Growing as it is, the BDX burn still offsets under one percent of a single quarterly release.

Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **0**. Running a Beldex masternode requires posting **10,000 BDX**, and roughly **2,500** nodes hold about **25M BDX** that way — a real standing bond, but one already posted. No Beldex locking programme with a stated size was announced inside this window, and the framework does not book a lock whose quantum has to be inferred.

## Foundation and overhang

Three BDX overhangs are tracked. The first is the Beldex Ecosystem Development wallet at **1,607,760,000 BDX**, published with address and view key each quarter; it is partly scheduled, draining **130.68M** per quarter-end with roughly twelve more tranches of runway and no schedule for the remainder. The second is the Beldex Seed and VC wallet at **214,500,000 BDX**, which has released nothing and is watched on the same quarterly disclosure. The third is an unattributed residual of about **246.8M BDX**: the market classifier treats **2,068.94M** as non-circulating while Beldex names only **1,822.26M**, and that difference belongs on the page as a classification gap rather than being quietly ignored.

One new item joined the watch list this build. On **Aug 20 2026** Beldex announced an **$8M** raise led by Sigma Capital, with NTC, Nxgen, Digital Consensus Fund and EAK Ventures participating, taking total capital raised to **$36M**. The release discloses no BDX amount, no price, no vesting and no lockup, and does not even say whether the round is equity or a token sale — so it carries no value in this ledger. It is recorded because the Beldex Seed and VC wallet is where a token leg would come from, and it has been motionless for over a year. If any of these balances falls between refreshes beyond its scheduled tranche, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How BDX compares to other privacy chains

The obvious comparison is Monero, whose codebase Beldex descends from, and the comparison is where the two diverge rather than where they meet. Monero has no premine and no foundation reserve, so its entire supply schedule is the emission curve and its tail emission — every unit of Monero inflation is mined, visible in the block reward, and impossible for anybody to accelerate. Beldex inherited the privacy machinery but not that structure: it launched with an allocated tokenomics base of **9.9B BDX**, and its mining is so small at **1.64M** a quarter that it is a rounding error beside the **130.68M** quarterly reserve release. Two chains can share a cryptographic design and have completely different supply politics.

Against Zcash, the contrast is about who decides. Zcash's funding stream was a protocol-encoded slice of the block reward, argued over in public and re-authorised by consensus change; Beldex's is a wallet balance released on a calendar the project sets, with the amount fixed but the remaining schedule undeclared. Against Dash, the third masternode-secured privacy-adjacent chain, the difference is that Dash mints its treasury inside the block reward and mints nothing it does not spend, whereas Beldex's reserve was minted at genesis and enters the float on release. Beldex is closer in shape to an exchange token with a quarterly release schedule than to a mined privacy coin — which is exactly why reading BDX inflation off a mining curve gets it wrong by two orders of magnitude.

There is also a measurement contrast worth stating, because it constrains how confidently anything above can be claimed. On a transparent chain every one of these wallets would be readable directly. On Beldex, stealth addresses and confidential amounts mean no per-address balance is readable by anyone without the view key, and there is no dead-address balance to read either, so the burn has to be verified against the chain's own supply total instead. The chain-wide totals are exact and the reserve balances are published with the keys needed to check them — but they are a disclosure, and this page says so rather than dressing them up as a direct chain read.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The nineteenth quarterly Beldex release lands on **Sep 30 2026**, and at **130.68M BDX** it is the single event that sets the next reading; a tranche of a different size, or a schedule change, would be the most consequential news BDX could produce. The quarterly tokenomics disclosure that follows it in early October is the read on the Beldex Ecosystem wallet and Seed and VC wallet balances — the Seed and VC line is the one to check first, given the August raise. Any disclosure of BDX token terms attached to that **$8M** round would move a zero row off zero. On the buy side, the Beldex Name Service registration count and the chain's cumulative burn counter are the two numbers that decide whether the fee burn keeps growing past **1M BDX** a quarter. And the new cross-chain bridge that cleared audit on **Aug 6 2026** is worth watching after the June bridge incident, because how the wrapped representation is backed determines whether it ever becomes a supply question.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Beldex at **+1.67%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 28 2026** and the same again forward, against a monitor reading of **+1.66%** — a **0.01 point** gap and no data conflict. The structural mechanism is not mining: BDX block rewards add **1.64M** a quarter while a fixed **130.68M** tranche leaves the Beldex Ecosystem Development wallet every quarter-end, offset only by a **1.04M** Flash and Beldex Name Service fee burn and by no buyback at all. The key risk is that the reserve schedule is set by the project rather than by protocol: **1,607.76M BDX** remains in the Ecosystem wallet and **214.50M** in the Seed and VC wallet, and the August funding round has undisclosed token terms. There is no ceiling to fall back on — Beldex publishes no hard cap and the chain's gross emission of **9,950.99M BDX** keeps rising — so the constraint on BDX dilution is a calendar, not a maximum.
