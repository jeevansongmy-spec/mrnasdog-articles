Beldex is an uncapped CryptoNote privacy chain, and almost none of its inflation is mining. Over the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026** the BDX block subsidy created just **1.64M BDX**, while a single scheduled tranche of **130.68M BDX** left the Beldex Ecosystem Development wallet on **Jun 30 2026** — the eighteenth such quarterly release. Against that, Flash transaction fees and Beldex Name Service fees burned **1.11M BDX**, and there is no buyback of any kind. Net supply pressure is about **+1.67%** of the **7.87B** circulating BDX, and the nineteenth release on **Sep 30 2026** makes the next quarter read the same.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the window ending **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads BDX at **+1.67% net**, with the forward view also at **+1.67%**: a sell side of **132.32M BDX** against a buy side of **1.11M BDX**. Our own supply monitor reads **+1.68%** for the same trailing window, a gap of **0.015 percentage points** — far inside the framework's **0.5-point** tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships. The agreement is unusually tight for a privacy coin, and the reason is structural: one dated event accounts for **98.8%** of the quarter's new supply, so there is very little left for the two readings to disagree about. Beldex is best labelled **a scheduled-release chain, not an emission chain** — its dilution arrives on a calendar, in identical steps, and can be predicted a quarter at a time.

## Sell pressure: where new BDX comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1.64M BDX**, and the interesting part is how small that is. Beldex runs a masternode-secured CryptoNote chain with a flat block subsidy, and this build measured the block rate rather than assuming it — a trap that has produced wrong numbers on several chains. Reading the Beldex chain at both window ends gives **259,200 blocks** across **7,776,018 seconds**, an average interval of **30.00 seconds**, exactly the protocol target. Blocks with no transactions in them pay **6.25 BDX** to the decimal at both the old and the new hardfork version, so the base subsidy is flat; the small excess seen on busier blocks is recycled transaction fees rather than new issuance. That is **1,620,000 BDX** mined in a quarter, plus one anomalous coinbase of **18,906 BDX** paid when the network restarted at the Hardfork 21 fork block on **Jun 27 2026**. On a **7.87B** float, all the mining Beldex does in a quarter moves the supply by about two hundredths of one percent.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **130.68M BDX** and is effectively the whole ledger. Beldex releases a fixed **130,680,000 BDX** from its Ecosystem Development wallet on the final day of every quarter, and has done so eighteen times. The framework never books a scheduled release on the strength of the calendar, because a schedule describes capacity, not what actually moved — so this one was verified as released. The Beldex Ecosystem wallet held **1,738,440,000 BDX** at **Mar 31 2026** and **1,607,760,000 BDX** at **Jun 30 2026**, a fall of exactly the tranche, and the separate Seed & VC wallet released nothing at all, reading **214,500,000 BDX** unchanged at **Dec 31 2025**, **Mar 31 2026** and **Jun 30 2026**. A third, independent angle confirms the coins reached the market rather than merely changing hands internally: the tracked circulating supply stepped up by **129.83M** on **Jul 4 2026**, the only step in a series that is otherwise flat. One honest limit belongs on the record — Beldex hides individual balances by design, so a reserve wallet cannot be read from a block explorer the way an ordinary token contract can. Beldex publishes the wallet address, its view key and its balance each quarter precisely so the figure can be checked, and the float step is what turns that disclosure into a measurement.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** while carrying the overhang that matters most. It is zero because the only outflow anyone has observed from a Beldex reserve is the quarterly tranche already booked in Sell #2 — booking it twice would invent sell pressure — and because the Seed & VC wallet, the one reserve that could fire unscheduled, has not released a single BDX in over a year. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is also **zero**: Beldex has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee distribution and no expiring lockup contract.

## Buy pressure: where new BDX goes

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is the only live buy row at **1.11M BDX**. Beldex burns two fee streams: the fee on Flash, its fast-confirmation transaction layer, and the fees paid to the Beldex Name Service for registering, renewing and transferring human-readable names. Both are sent to addresses whose keys nobody holds, and the Beldex daemon subtracts them from the chain's own supply total, which is what makes this destruction rather than a dashboard claim — the cumulative burn counter reads **11,612,981 BDX** today against **11.17M** at **Jun 30 2026** and **9.69M** at **Mar 31 2026**. Beldex Name Service adoption is what drives it: registrations went from **5,242** to **6,217** across the second quarter and read **6,476** now, at **650** to **4,000 BDX** per subscription depending on term, with a name marketplace opening on **May 30 2026**. It is a real, growing burn — and it still cancels less than one percent of the quarterly release.

The other three buy rows are **zero**. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, does not exist: Beldex publishes an exhaustive quarterly report of listings, audits, integrations, partnerships and sponsorships, and no purchase of BDX appears anywhere in it; the only buyback material that exists at all is a promotional post from early 2023 that never became a mechanism. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero for the same reason — Beldex funds itself by releasing reserve BDX, not by earning revenue and buying BDX back, so there is nothing for a treasury to deploy. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero: running a Beldex masternode requires posting **10,000 BDX** of collateral, but that is a standing bond rather than a lock that removes float, and the node count did not grow this quarter, sitting around **2,500** against **2,680** at the end of the first quarter.

## Foundation and overhang

Two named Beldex reserves sit behind the float. The Ecosystem Development wallet holds **1,607,760,000 BDX** — about **16.2%** of everything ever created — and drains only on the quarterly step already counted in Sell #2; at **130.68M** a quarter it has roughly twelve more releases in it. The Seed & VC wallet holds **214,500,000 BDX** and has been completely static for over a year, so there is no observed pattern to project from: a reserve that has not moved tells us nothing about when it will. Together they are **1.82B BDX**, about **23%** of the traded float. A third figure deserves naming: the circulating count used as the denominator is **7.87B**, while Beldex's own accounting calls **81.6%** of supply circulating, leaving roughly **247M BDX** that the market classifier treats as non-circulating and the project does not attribute to either named wallet. Both reserves are re-read every rebuild, and if either balance falls by more than its scheduled tranche, the extra outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How BDX compares to other privacy chains

The obvious comparison is Monero, whose code Beldex descends from, and the comparison is instructive because the two are almost opposites on this metric. Monero has no premine and no reserve: every XMR that will ever exist comes out of the block reward, so its inflation is a smooth tail emission that nobody has to schedule and nobody can accelerate. Beldex kept the CryptoNote privacy primitives — ring signatures, stealth addresses, confidential amounts — but replaced proof-of-work with masternode consensus and paired it with a large allocated reserve. The result is that Beldex mines less than one fiftieth of what it distributes, and its dilution is a governance artefact rather than a mining artefact.

Against fee-burn chains the shape is different again. A chain whose burn scales with usage can flip deflationary when activity rises; Beldex's Beldex Name Service burn is genuinely usage-linked and genuinely growing, but it is measured in single-digit millions against a fixed release measured in hundreds of millions, so no plausible adoption curve makes it decisive in the next few quarters. And against a capped exchange token running a revenue-funded quarterly buyback-and-burn, BDX is the mirror image: same quarterly rhythm, opposite direction. The honest summary is that BDX behaves less like a privacy coin than like a token with a long linear unlock — the privacy technology is real, but the supply schedule is what the ledger is measuring.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the nineteenth quarterly release on **Sep 30 2026**: another **130.68M BDX** is expected out of the Ecosystem wallet, and the balance disclosed in the early-October report should read **1,477,080,000 BDX** if the pattern holds. Second, whether the Seed & VC wallet finally moves — it has been pinned at **214,500,000 BDX** for more than a year, and any release from it would open a Sell #3 row that has never been non-zero. Third, the Beldex Name Service registration count, which sets the burn rate: it grew by **975** names in the second quarter but only **259** in the seven weeks since, so the burn is currently decelerating. Fourth, the tail of Hardfork 21, activated **Jun 27 2026** — the network halted for about fifteen hours, a state rollback and reconciliation followed, and exchanges resumed between **Jun 30** and **Jul 1 2026**; the block subsidy came through unchanged, but a second reconciliation event would need re-measuring. Fifth, the migrated BDX-BSC contract, re-issued one-for-one to holders from a **Jun 10 2026** snapshot after a bridge incident, with the first phase completed **Jun 18 2026** — a wrapped representation on another chain creates no native BDX, but any change to how it is backed would matter.

## Summary

Beldex adds about **+1.67%** to its circulating BDX every 90 days, and it does so almost entirely through one scheduled event rather than through mining. The block subsidy contributes **1.64M BDX** a quarter, the Ecosystem Development wallet releases a fixed **130.68M BDX** on the last day of each quarter, and the Flash and Beldex Name Service fee burns take back **1.11M BDX** — under one percent of what the release adds, with no buyback anywhere on the buy side. The key risk is not a surprise: it is the arithmetic of a reserve that still holds **1.6B BDX** and pays out on a published calendar, which implies roughly twelve more quarters of the same dilution before the schedule runs dry. There is no hard cap to arrest it, which is why the ceiling here is the reserve balance rather than a protocol maximum.
