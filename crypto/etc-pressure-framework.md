# ETC Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

Ethereum Classic is a proof-of-work chain whose entire supply story is one number, the block reward — and that number moved inside this window. Over the last 90 days Ethereum Classic issued **~1.08M ETC** from **574,333** blocks, against a buy ledger that is empty in all four rows: no buyback, no burn, no foundation bid, no lock. That is **+0.68%** of circulating supply reaching the market, against **+0.93%** on the inflation monitor, a gap of **0.25** percentage points. The forward quarter is lower rather than flat: the fifth ECIP-1017 reduction cut the reward **20%** from **2.048** to **1.6384** ETC at block **25,000,000** on **Jul 22 2026**, so the next 90 days run entirely at the lower rate for **~0.944M ETC** and a net of **+0.60%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The framework reads Ethereum Classic at **+0.68%** net supply growth over the trailing 90 days and **+0.60%** for the next 90. The inflation monitor reads **+0.93%** over the same window, leaving a gap of **0.25** percentage points — inside tolerance, so no data-conflict chip is raised on the ETC overview. The agreement is looser than the number suggests: the monitor derives ETC supply by dividing market capitalisation by price, and that quotient swung from **156.13M** to **156.73M** inside a single week of this window, a band several times wider than the residual it is being asked to settle. The framework counts block rewards block by block instead. On mechanism, ETC is a **capped, reward-reduction proof-of-work coin with a structurally empty buy side**: the ECIP-1017 schedule is the only lever, it only ever moves one way, and it has just moved.

## Sell pressure: where new ETC comes from

Every unit of Ethereum Classic sell pressure in this ledger is protocol inflation, and protocol inflation on Ethereum Classic is mining and nothing else. The chain produced **574,333** blocks between the two ends of the window, counted by differencing block heights across the full 90 days rather than sampling a short interval or trusting the published target. That works out to a real block interval of **13.54** seconds against the **13** seconds Ethereum Classic targets — the chain ran **4.14%** slower than nominal, so a target-based read would have claimed **598,152** blocks and invented roughly **39,000 ETC** of issuance that never happened. This matters on ETC specifically because the ECIP-1017 reward is denominated per block and each reduction is pinned to a fixed block height, not a date: the difficulty adjustment nudges the interval back toward target but never rescales the reward, so a slow quarter genuinely issues less ETC and pushes the next reduction further into the future.

The reward itself was read off the chain rather than taken from the schedule. The coinbase at block **24,999,999** paid **2.048** ETC and the coinbase at block **25,000,000** paid **1.6384**, which places the fifth ECIP-1017 reduction at the round-number block and one block earlier than the proposal's own prose ranges imply. That block was mined on **Jul 22 2026**, **39** days before the window closed, so the trailing quarter is a blend: **323,002** blocks at the old reward and **251,331** at the new one. Uncle blocks add a further **2,791 ETC** — ECIP-1017 pays a thirty-second of the era reward to the uncle miner and another thirty-second to the including miner, verified on-chain at a zero-transaction block where the including miner's balance rose exactly **1.6896** and the uncle miner's rose exactly **0.0512**.

The other three Ethereum Classic sell rows are zero, each for a different structural reason. Vesting unlocks are zero because there is no vesting schedule, no cliff and no lock contract on this chain: the only allocation that ever existed is the 2014 genesis distribution of **72,009,990** units, which reached public buyers more than a decade ago, and circulating supply now sits just **967 ETC** below total supply. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero because Ethereum Classic has no foundation coin reserve and no protocol treasury at all — every unit of the block reward goes to the miner. The bankruptcy row is zero because no estate holds ETC, though two large blocks of coins sit outside the tradable float: a listed US trust held **10,850,162.72 ETC** at **Jun 30 2026** with no redemption program, and the largest dormant wallet on the chain carries about **3.36M ETC**, roughly **2.1%** of supply, and has never sent an outbound transaction in its entire history.

## Buy pressure: where new ETC goes

Nowhere, and the Ethereum Classic burn row in particular is a measured zero rather than an assumed one. The chain never adopted the fee-burning upgrade its larger sibling did, and that is provable directly from a block header: an Ethereum Classic header carries no base-fee field at all, so transaction fees are paid to the miner in full and none of them is destroyed. The keyless addresses were then read from the address side as well — across the whole window they took in **1.1 ETC** between them, in **408** dust transfers, and coins parked there stay inside the counted Ethereum Classic supply forever anyway, because that supply figure is genesis plus cumulative rewards rather than a scan of spendable coins.

Programmatic buyback is zero because Ethereum Classic keeps no protocol revenue to buy with: the block reward and the fees leave the protocol in the same instant, in the same output, to whoever found the block. Foundation buy is zero for the same reason — there is no balance sheet to do the buying, and the group that maintains the protocol is funded by donations rather than endowed with coins. New long-term lock is zero because Ethereum Classic is pure proof-of-work, with no staking, no bonding, no validator deposit and no lockup contract, so an ETC holder has no protocol-level way to take coins off the tradable float. One correction worth making plainly: the proposed fee-market upgrade widely described as adding a burn to Ethereum Classic does not burn anything. It redirects **100%** of the base fee to a treasury contract at the consensus layer, which would make it a future sell-side overhang rather than a buy row — wrong in sign as well as size if booked as a burn.

## Foundation and overhang

Ethereum Classic has no team-controlled overhang to enumerate, and that is unusual enough to state explicitly. There is no foundation reserve, no labs allocation, no DAO treasury and no buyback accumulation wallet, because none of those things has ever held a protocol allocation of ETC — the chain inherited a genesis distribution that was already public and has minted every coin since to miners. The one item on the watch list is prospective: the draft fee-market upgrade would create the chain's first protocol treasury, an immutable contract credited with the entire base fee, and it currently carries no activation block and no confirmed date.

Two large non-team holdings sit alongside it and are watched rather than booked. The first is a listed US trust, which held **10,850,162.72 ETC** at **Jun 30 2026** — about **6.9%** of supply — with **13,993,800** shares outstanding at both ends of the half-year, no creations, no redemptions, and a filing that states plainly that the trust does not operate a redemption program and has no intention of seeking approval for one; its only ETC outflow is an in-kind sponsor fee of **135,354 ETC** over the six months. A fund holding on behalf of shareholders is out of scope for the team-controlled enumeration, but with no redemption path those coins cannot become new Ethereum Classic float, which is why they are surfaced here rather than counted. The second is the **3.36M ETC** dormant wallet described above, watched because a balance that has never moved is not a pattern. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 or Sell #4 at the next refresh.

## How ETC compares to other proof-of-work chains

Ethereum Classic belongs to the capped, reward-reduction proof-of-work class, and inside that class its distinguishing feature is the shape of the step rather than the existence of one. A halving chain cuts the subsidy **50%** every four years; Ethereum Classic cuts it **20%** every five million blocks, which at the measured pace lands roughly every two and a half years. The result is a gentler, more frequent staircase — the same convergent geometric series, with an asymptotic ceiling ECIP-1017 puts near **210.7M ETC**, of which **75.0%** is already issued. Against a Bitcoin-style chain, ETC is at a similar point on the curve but arrives there in smaller increments, which is why a reduction inside the window changes the forward forecast by a visible **0.08** percentage points rather than halving it.

Against uncapped continuous-emission proof-of-stake chains, the contrast is starker and runs the other way. Those chains mint on a rate expressed in supply terms, so issuance scales with the float and the forward number is a policy parameter a validator set or a governance vote can change. Ethereum Classic's issuance is denominated in coins per block, fixed in consensus code, and indexed to block height — no vote can raise it, and nothing in the protocol compensates when block production drifts. The trade-off is that Ethereum Classic has no offsetting sink either: chains with an EIP-1559 base-fee burn can and do run net-negative in busy quarters, whereas ETC's buy ledger is structurally empty and its net reading can only ever be positive until the reward asymptotes toward nothing.

The closest comparison is to the other chain that shares its genesis. Ethereum kept the ledger and moved to proof-of-stake with a fee burn; Ethereum Classic kept proof-of-work and the original monetary policy. Both start from the same **72,009,990**-unit genesis, and the divergence since is entirely a monetary-policy divergence rather than a distribution one — which is why ETC has no vesting row to walk and no unlock calendar to watch, only a block clock.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the block interval. The chain ran **13.54** seconds a block this quarter against a **13**-second target; because Ethereum Classic issuance is block-indexed and uncompensated, a sustained swing in hashrate moves the forward number directly, and a return to target would raise next-quarter issuance by roughly **4%**. Second, the fee-market upgrade: it is a draft with a stated ambition of mainnet activation by the end of **2026** and no activation block, and if it lands it creates the chain's first protocol treasury and a new Sell #3 line, not a burn. Third, the treasury governance proposals that ride alongside it, which would decide who can spend that balance and how quickly. Fourth, the next reward reduction at block **30,000,000** — about **745** days away at the measured pace, around **Sep 2028**, so it is not a factor in this window but it is the next scheduled change to the Ethereum Classic emission curve. Fifth, the listed US trust's quarterly filings — the next one covers the period ending **Sep 30 2026** and would be the first place a redemption program or a change in its **10.85M ETC** position appears — and the **3.36M ETC** dormant wallet, both checked on every rebuild.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Ethereum Classic as a capped proof-of-work coin with a structurally empty buy side and a supply curve that has just stepped down. Mining issued **~1.08M ETC** over the last 90 days for a net of **+0.68%**, and the next 90 days run entirely at the post-reduction **1.6384** reward for **~0.944M ETC** and **+0.60%** — growth, but cooling growth, and the direction is protocol-guaranteed rather than discretionary. The key risk is not a supply event but a category error: the upgrade most often described as bringing a burn to Ethereum Classic routes the base fee to a treasury instead, which would add sell-side capacity rather than remove supply. The ceiling is the ECIP-1017 asymptote of about **210.7M ETC**, **75.0%** of which is already mined.
