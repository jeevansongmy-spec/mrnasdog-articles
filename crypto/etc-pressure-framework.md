Ethereum Classic is the cleanest ledger in this coverage and also one of the least forgiving: mining is the only thing on the sell side, and there is nothing whatsoever on the buy side. Over the 90 days to **Aug 11 2026** the ETC block reward minted **1.13M ETC** against **zero** burned, bought back or locked — a net of **+0.72%** of circulating supply, with our supply monitor reading the same window at **+0.84%**. The ECIP-1017 reward reduction on **Jul 22 2026** cut the block subsidy 20%, from **2.048** to **1.6384 ETC**, which takes the forward reading down to **+0.60%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 11 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **Ethereum Classic at +0.72% net**. Sell pressure totals **1.13M ETC** and buy pressure totals **0 ETC**, against a circulating base of **157.8M ETC**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.84%**, a gap of **0.13 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag ships with this page and no reconciliation walk was required. The two readings agree because there is almost nothing for them to disagree about: ETC has no treasury wallet, no escrow, no vesting contract and no classification question at all, so both numbers are measuring the same thing, which is the block reward. Ethereum Classic is best characterised as a **hard-capped proof-of-work chain with a one-sided ledger** — mildly inflationary today, and mechanically less so every era.

## Sell pressure: where new ETC comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **1.13M ETC** and it is the whole sell side of this page. Ethereum Classic is proof-of-work, and the block reward is the only issuance path in the protocol — there is no staking emission, no rebase and no mint function that any party can call. The monetary policy is ECIP-1017, universally called the **5M20** schedule: the block subsidy falls **20%** every **5,000,000** blocks, stepping 5 to 4 to 3.2 to 2.56 to 2.048 to 1.6384 ETC. Reading the chain directly over this window gives **574,429** blocks at an average of **13.5 seconds** each, split by the reduction into **448,809** blocks at the old reward and **125,620** at the new one, plus the small uncle and nephew payments that ECIP-1017 sets at a thirty-second of the block reward apiece. Those side payments are not a rounding artefact left to assumption: sampling clusters across the window puts the uncle rate at **7.0%**, which is what carries the figure to **1.13M**.

That reduction is the single most important fact on this page, and it landed inside the measurement window. Block **25,000,000** was mined on **Jul 22 2026** — the event the Ethereum Classic community calls the fifthening — and the per-block reward has read **1.6384 ETC** ever since. It was confirmed two ways rather than taken on trust from the schedule: the era boundary itself, and the per-block reward read at heights either side of the cut, which returns 2.048 before and 1.6384 after. Because a documented mechanism change fired mid-window, the trailing 90-day average blends a retired emission rate with the live one, and projecting that blend forward would over-forecast issuance by roughly a fifth. The forward column is therefore re-based onto the post-reduction rate alone: **0.94M ETC** over the next 90 days. The next 20% step comes at block **30,000,000**, roughly two years out, so nothing further changes inside this horizon.

Every other sell row is **zero**, for structural reasons rather than quiet quarters. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is zero because there is no vesting to unlock: the roughly **72M ETC** present at genesis came from the 2014 public sale and the early contributor allocation, all of which finished releasing before the chain split in 2016, and no unlock calendar for ETC exists anywhere. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is zero because there is no foundation allocation — the chain never had a premine reserve, and the non-profit that supports it is donation-funded and holds no protocol supply. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is zero because no estate, trustee or court-ordered distribution touches ETC.

## Buy pressure: where new ETC goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **zero**, and this is the defining feature of the Ethereum Classic ledger. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is the one worth stating precisely, because it is widely misreported. Ethereum Classic never adopted the EIP-1559 fee market: every block sampled this session, the chain tip included, carries **no base fee field at all**. There is no burn mechanism to be idle — the mechanism does not exist, so **100%** of the gas paid on Ethereum Classic goes to the miner who produced the block and not one ETC is destroyed.

The Olympia upgrade is often cited as the fix, and it is worth reading the specification rather than the coverage. ECIP-1111 would introduce EIP-1559 on Ethereum Classic, but its own text is explicit that the base fee **is not burned** — it is redirected at the consensus layer to the Olympia Treasury defined in ECIP-1112, to be governed by the DAO in ECIP-1113. Several current write-ups describing an **80% burn** that would make ETC deflationary are contradicted by the ECIP itself. That matters for the framework in both directions: if and when Olympia ships, Buy #2 stays at **zero** because nothing is destroyed, while a new treasury appears that can accumulate and spend — a future **Sell #3** line, not a buy one. As of this build, ECIP-1111 is a draft with activation heights marked to be determined for both testnet and mainnet, and its absence is confirmed on chain.

The rest follow. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero because Ethereum Classic collects no protocol revenue and holds no treasury, so there is nothing to fund a repurchase. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is zero: no project-controlled wallet was observed or disclosed buying ETC in the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is zero because a proof-of-work chain has no staking contract, no bonding and no lockup product — the protocol has no way to take coins off the market.

## Foundation and overhang

Ethereum Classic has no team-controlled overhang, which is unusual enough to state plainly. There is no foundation reserve, no DAO treasury, no labs allocation and no buyback accumulation wallet, because the chain never had a premine to create one. Two items are nevertheless tracked. The first is the proposed Olympia Treasury, an on-chain contract that would begin collecting redirected transaction fees if the upgrade activates; it holds nothing today. The second is the largest parked block of ETC anywhere, a listed US trust holding roughly **10.9M ETC** at its **Mar 31 2026** quarterly filing, which reported no creations and no redemptions in the period and has no redemption program at all — so those coins have no path to the market, which is why they sit as a note rather than a number. If that treasury goes live and its balance falls, or if the trust ever gains a redemption path, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ETC compares to other capped proof-of-work chains

Ethereum Classic belongs to the same structural family as Bitcoin, Litecoin and Bitcoin Cash: a fixed ceiling, a subsidy that steps down on a block-height schedule, and no discretionary issuance. The difference is granularity. Bitcoin halves — a **50%** cut every four years — while Ethereum Classic takes a **20%** cut roughly every two years, so its disinflation is smoother and slower, and the reductions land often enough that a 90-day window can straddle one, exactly as this window did on **Jul 22 2026**.

Against its own ancestor the contrast is sharper. Ethereum runs proof-of-stake with an EIP-1559 base-fee burn, so its net supply oscillates around zero and can turn negative when activity is high. Ethereum Classic kept proof-of-work and kept the legacy gas market, so it has the issuance without the offset — the same chain history, the opposite supply shape. Against uncapped emission chains it is far tighter: **+0.60%** forward is well under what an inflationary staking L1 pays out, with a real ceiling of **210.7M ETC** behind it. But against tokens with quarterly buybacks and burns, ETC has no mechanism to shrink at all, and that caps how good this reading can ever get.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether the Olympia upgrade receives a mainnet activation height. ECIP-1111 is still a draft and the community target is late 2026; a named block would move the Olympia Treasury from a watch item to a live Sell #3 line. Second, whether the treasury design changes during that finalisation — if a burn share were added to the base fee, Buy #2 would turn on for the first time in the chain's history. Third, block times: the forward projection assumes **13.6 seconds** per block, and a sustained hashrate exit after the reward cut would slow blocks and quietly reduce issuance below **0.94M ETC**. Fourth, the listed trust's next quarterly disclosure, for any change to its **10.9M ETC** holding or the introduction of a redemption path.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Ethereum Classic at **+0.72%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 11 2026**, easing to **+0.60%** forward after the ECIP-1017 reward reduction on **Jul 22 2026** cut the block subsidy from **2.048** to **1.6384 ETC**. The structural mechanism is as simple as this coverage gets: mining is the only source of new ETC, and no burn, buyback, staking lock or treasury purchase exists to offset it. The key risk is not a hidden unlock — there is none — but the fact that the buy ledger has no path to turn on, since even the proposed Olympia fee market redirects the base fee to a treasury rather than destroying it. The ceiling of **210.7M ETC** is real and the schedule is credible, but until something removes coins, Ethereum Classic can only grind toward neutral, never below it.
