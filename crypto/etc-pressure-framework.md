# ETC Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
**Main page:** the full ETC coin page — signal, price drivers and the buy/sell questions → [mrnasdog.com/research/etc](https://mrnasdog.com/research/etc)

**Ethereum Classic (ETC)** is mildly inflationary by design: proof-of-work miners received **988,974 new ETC** in the 90 days to Oct 3 2026, and nothing was burned or bought back, so the circulating supply of **158.36M ETC** grew **+0.62%**. The ETC block reward was cut by a fifth on Jul 22 2026, so the next 90 days should add about **942,462 ETC** (**+0.60%**). The ETC supply can never pass its hard cap of about **210.7M**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Jul 5 to Oct 3 2026, ETC supply grew a net **+0.62%**: miners were paid **988,974 ETC** and **0 ETC** left the float. Our inflation monitor read **+1.17%** for almost the same window, a gap of **0.54 points**. We checked it: the monitor's supply figure stood still near 156.5M ETC until Jul 16 2026 and then jumped about 932,000 ETC in one day on Jul 17 2026 to catch up with the chain, so its starting point sat about 824,000 ETC too low. That stale starting point explains the whole gap, so no warning chip is shown and our number stays. For the next 90 days we project **+0.60%**. Ethereum Classic is a steady proof-of-work chain whose only new supply is mining pay, and that pay shrinks in steps.

## Sell pressure: where new ETC comes from

Protocol inflation is the only source of new ETC. Every block pays its miner a fixed subsidy set by the ECIP-1017 monetary policy, which cuts the reward by 20% every 5,000,000 blocks. Block 25,000,001 arrived on Jul 22 2026, inside this window, and the Ethereum Classic block reward fell from **2.048 ETC** to **1.6384 ETC**. We read miner balances on empty blocks on both sides of the cut and saw exactly those two amounts. Miners also get a small extra for orphaned blocks: the miner of an orphan and the miner who records it each earn one thirty-second of the block reward. We counted every block of the window and found **23,662** such orphans. Adding it up, the 113,027 blocks before the cut paid 231,479 ETC, the 460,790 blocks after it paid 754,958 ETC, and orphans added 2,536 ETC: **988,974 ETC** in total.

For the next 90 days we use only the new, lower reward, because the old one no longer exists. ETC blocks have come about every 13.55 seconds since the cut, which gives about 573,700 blocks in 90 days and **942,462 ETC** of new supply, or about 10,470 ETC a day.

Vesting unlocks are **0**. Ethereum Classic has no team, investor or foundation schedule; the coins that existed at its 2015 start were spendable from the first block. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are also **0**: circulating supply is within about 838 ETC of total supply, so there is no locked reserve that could be released. Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is **0**, because no estate or trustee holds ETC to pay out.

## Buy pressure: where new ETC goes

Nothing on Ethereum Classic takes ETC out of the market. There is no programmatic buyback, so that row is **0**. The protocol fee burn is also **0**: ETC never adopted the Ethereum-style base fee, so every transaction fee goes to the miner instead of being destroyed. We also read the two common burn addresses at both ends of the window; together they gained just **1.10 ETC**, and those coins are still counted in the supply.

There is no foundation buy (**0**) and no new long-term lock (**0**). ETC is mined rather than staked, so no coins are locked away to earn rewards. The fee-market upgrades now being discussed would not change this: one sends the new base fee to a community vault and a rival plan pays it to miners. Neither burns ETC, and neither has a start block on the main network.

## Foundation and overhang

Ethereum Classic has no team-controlled overhang. No foundation, company or treasury wallet holds a reserve outside the float; the gap between total and circulating supply is only about 838 ETC. The largest known single holder is Grayscale's Ethereum Classic trust, which reported **10,850,163 ETC** on Jun 30 2026. It bought those coins in the market and has no redemption program, so they already count as circulating and selling them would add no new supply. If a fee vault is switched on in a future upgrade, it would collect fees that users already paid from circulating ETC. We re-check these balances at every rebuild; if any of them falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ETC compares to other proof-of-work chains

ETC sits in the same family as Bitcoin, Litecoin and Bitcoin Cash: a mined coin with a fixed cap and a reward that steps down on a schedule. The difference is the rhythm. Bitcoin halves its reward every 210,000 blocks, about four years; Ethereum Classic cuts by a fifth every 5,000,000 blocks, about two years at today's pace. So ETC's steps are smaller but come twice as often, and its supply curve bends more smoothly toward the 210.7M cap.

Against Ethereum, the chain it split from in 2016, the contrast is sharper. Ethereum pays stakers and destroys part of every fee, and has no cap. ETC pays miners, burns nothing and has a hard cap. Against Kaspa, another mined coin with a fixed cap, ETC lowers its reward in large steps while Kaspa lowers it a little every month. At about **+0.60%** per 90 days, ETC's new supply is higher than Bitcoin's today, because Bitcoin is further along its schedule; each future ETC era cut narrows that gap.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the fee-market vote: the base-fee vault plan (ECIP-1111 with its vault, ECIP-1112) and the miner-paid rival (ECIP-1120) are both drafts, and a main-network start block would be the first change to where ETC fees go. Second, the client split: a rival release was briefly run by some mining pools on Sep 15 2026 and then rolled back; a repeat would not change supply but could shake confidence in the network. Third, block speed: ETC is block-indexed, so if blocks come faster than the 13.55 seconds we measured, more ETC is paid each day. Fourth, Grayscale's next quarterly report, likely in early November 2026, shows whether the trust's 10.85M ETC is shrinking. No era cut is due: the next one, to 1.31072 ETC per block, comes at block 30,000,001, about two years away.

## Summary

Ethereum Classic's supply is growing slowly and on a known schedule: **988,974 ETC** of mining pay in the last 90 days, nothing burned or bought back, for a net **+0.62%**, and about **+0.60%** projected next after the Jul 22 2026 reward cut. There are no unlocks, no team reserve and no treasury outside the float. The main risk to buyers is that mining pay keeps reaching the market every day with no burn to offset it; the limit is the hard cap of about 210.7M ETC and a reward that drops by a fifth every 5,000,000 blocks.
