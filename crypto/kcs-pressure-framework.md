KuCoin Token has **no mint function** — the KCS contract creates coins once and can never create another, and total supply has sat at **142,155,021 KCS** all year — yet KCS reads inflationary. The reason is that KuCoin pays **2.5M KCS** out of a company reserve into the tradable float on the first day of every quarter, while the profit-funded buyback-and-burn that KCS is famous for has not settled a round since **Dec 2025**. Sell pressure **2.5M KCS** against buy pressure of **zero** gives a net of **+1.82%**, with our supply monitor at **+1.91%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 23 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **KCS at +1.82% net**. Sell pressure totals **2.5M KCS**, buy pressure totals **zero**, against a circulating base of **137.155M KCS**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+1.91%**, a gap of **0.09 percentage points** — well inside tolerance, so no data-conflict chip ships on the KCS overview page. The residual is a denominator difference and nothing more: both readings describe the single reserve release of **Jul 1 2026**, the monitor dividing it by the float as it stood 90 days earlier and the framework by the float as it stands today. The forward reading is also **+1.82%**, because exactly one further release, **Oct 1 2026**, falls inside the next 90 days. KCS is best characterised as an **exchange token that cannot mint and is inflating anyway, because its issuer is releasing custody faster than its burn removes float**.

## Sell pressure: where new KCS comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**, and for KCS that is a hard structural fact rather than a judgement. The verified KCS token source declares a contract that inherits a standard token plus a burn extension, mints once inside its constructor, and exposes no mint function of any kind in its published interface. It is not a proxy, so there is no implementation behind it that could be swapped for one. The second surface is KuCoin Community Chain, where KCS is the native gas coin, and that chain pays no block subsidy either — three consecutive empty blocks produced by three different validators each show the producing validator's balance rising by exactly nothing, because KCC validators are paid from gas fees out of existing KCS rather than from new issuance. No KCS has been created since deployment and none can be.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**. The 2017 KuCoin sale schedules expired years ago and no unlock tracker carries an active KCS calendar; what KuCoin still withholds sits at the company's discretion, not on a published cliff calendar, so it belongs one row down. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: KuCoin is a going concern with no estate, no trustee and no court-ordered token distribution attached to KCS.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **2.5M KCS**, and it is the entire story. The decisive arithmetic is one subtraction: total supply **142,155,021** less circulating supply **137,155,021** leaves exactly **5,000,000 KCS** — a company reserve, held back from the tradable float. KuCoin's own December 2025 burn announcement put that same reserve at exactly **12,500,000 KCS**, quoting circulating **129,655,021.74** against the identical total. Between those two dates the total supply did not move and the tradable float rose **7,500,000 KCS** — three tranches of 2.5M, released on **Jan 1 2026**, **Apr 1 2026** and **Jul 1 2026**. The published supply series shows five such steps in a row going back to **Jul 1 2025**, each on a quarter boundary and flat in between. Projected by date rather than averaged, the trailing window contains exactly one firing and the forward window contains exactly one, so the row reads **2.5M** on both sides.

This release leaves no on-chain trace, and that is not an evasion — it is what a custody reclassification looks like. Every large KCS holding was swept transfer by transfer across both chains, not merely read at both ends: a wallet holding **90.0M KCS** on Ethereum and **47.5M** on KuCoin Community Chain has not moved since **Mar 2022**; a second holding **60.5M** and **12.5M** has not moved since **Mar 2025**; a third holding **19.9M** has not moved since **Aug 2024**; and a fourth holding **56.0M** took 400 inbound transfers during the window and made zero outbound ones. The full Ethereum transfer log for the window — all **645,870** blocks and **10,239** transfers — shows the largest net movement of any single address at **38,887 KCS**. Nothing on chain released 2.5M. KuCoin simply reclassified it, and publishes the result itself.

## Buy pressure: where new KCS goes

All four buy rows read **zero**, and Buy #1 is the one that matters, because the KCS buyback-and-burn is the mechanism most readers assume is still running. It is not. The burn destination was read at both ends of the window and rose from **26,781,718.333** to **26,781,718.792 KCS** — a move of **0.44 KCS** in 90 days. Sweeping that address's own transfer log returns exactly two sends inside the window, **0.44 KCS** on **Jun 14 2026** from KuCoin's wallet and a stray **0.018 KCS** on **Aug 21 2026** from an unrelated address, which sum to the balance change to nine decimal places. The last real burn was the 66th round, **20,240 KCS** on **Dec 31 2025**, and a sweep of **2,448** KuCoin announcements from October 2025 forward finds no burn announcement anywhere in 2026. The quantum had been shrinking for two years before it stopped — **311,727 KCS** in April 2024 down to **20,240** in December 2025.

The critical detail is which ledger you read. On Ethereum, the KCS burn addresses hold **zero** at both ends of the window and the ERC-20 total supply is frozen at **165,641,743** and never falls. That is a clean, honest, completely wrong answer: KuCoin's burns are native KCS transfers to the destroy address on KuCoin Community Chain, so a reader checking Ethereum sees a flat zero and concludes either that the burn is dead or that it never existed. The burns are also traceable by sender rather than by watching a balance rise, and the sender is a live KuCoin exchange hot wallet that carries thousands of ordinary user flows and held **23,535 KCS** at the window open and **6,369 KCS** at the close. That matters for classification: the burn is funded from tokens already counted as circulating, so when a round does fire it is genuine deflation. It simply has not fired.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** because nothing is destroyed by usage — KuCoin Community Chain hands gas straight to the validator that produced the block, and the Ethereum token has no fee mechanism at all. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**: outside the dormant burn programme KuCoin discloses no open-market accumulation, and no company wallet took KCS in from outside during the window. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** because KCS staking runs inside KuCoin's own books rather than in an on-chain lock contract, so staked KCS never leaves the tradable float.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled overhangs are tracked for KCS. The first is the quarterly reserve itself, **5,000,000 KCS** as of **Aug 23 2026**, observable only as the gap between the published total and circulating supply — refreshed daily from the published supply fields, with a bi-weekly walk of KuCoin's announcements for any change to the release size. It has exactly two tranches left, **Oct 1 2026** and **Jan 1 2027**, after which the tap runs dry unless KuCoin reclassifies more. The second and third are the dormant custody wallets, one holding **90.0M KCS** on Ethereum and **47.5M** on KuCoin Community Chain, the other **60.5M** and **12.5M**, both read on chain every day; between them they are the capacity that could refill the reserve for years. The fourth is the burn funding wallet at **6,369 KCS**, tracked not for its size but because a refill is the earliest mechanical sign of a restarted burn, ahead of any announcement. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How KCS compares to other exchange tokens

Exchange tokens are usually judged on one mechanism — how aggressively the issuer burns — and KCS shows why that framing is incomplete. The strongest structures in this class run an **automatic** burn keyed to something the issuer cannot decline, so the destruction is a formula rather than a decision, and the burn address grows on a visible schedule whether or not the exchange is having a good quarter. KCS runs the discretionary version: **10% of quarterly profit**, repurchased on the market and burned, with the size and the timing both at the issuer's discretion. A discretionary burn is a real mechanism when it fires and worth exactly nothing when it does not, and the KCS destroy address has moved less than half a coin in nearly eight months.

The second structural difference is the direction of the float. Most exchange tokens have finished distributing: their circulating supply only falls, so the burn and the headline supply move together. KCS still has a company reserve outside the float that is being released on a fixed quarterly cadence, which means its tradable supply is growing from custody at the same time as its burn is dormant. Both taps point the same way, and that is why a token with a hard-coded inability to mint reads more inflationary than several uncapped chains in this coverage. The comparison also explains why **KCS** is easy to misread: peers settle their burns on the chain the token is best known on, whereas KCS is an ERC-20 on **Ethereum** that burns natively on **KuCoin Community Chain**, so the Ethereum ledger reports a permanent zero.

The third difference is the endpoint. KCS was launched with a **200,000,000** supply and a stated goal of reducing it to **100,000,000**; it stands at **142,155,021**, having covered roughly 58% of that distance and then stopped moving. Peers whose burn is formula-driven keep closing on their target through weak quarters as well as strong ones. KCS closes on it only when KuCoin decides to, and the reserve release means the tradable float is moving in the opposite direction in the meantime.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **KCS at +1.82% net** over the 90 days to **Aug 23 2026**, and **+1.82%** forward, against a monitor reading of **+1.91%**. The structural mechanism is unusual: KCS has no mint function on either of the two chains it lives on and its total supply of **142,155,021** has not risen all year, yet the tradable float keeps growing because KuCoin releases **2.5M KCS** from a company reserve every quarter. The key risk is that the offset everyone assumes exists has stopped — the buyback-and-burn has not settled a round since **Dec 2025**, and it settles on KuCoin Community Chain rather than Ethereum, so the ledger most readers check shows a permanently reassuring zero. The ceiling is the reserve itself: **5,000,000 KCS** remain, exactly two more releases, after which this source of supply is exhausted and the reading depends entirely on whether the burn restarts.
