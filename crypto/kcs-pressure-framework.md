KuCoin Token markets itself as deflationary, but the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **KCS at +1.8%** net supply over the last 90 days — the tradable float is growing. Total supply is flat at **142.16M KCS** because the profit-funded buyback-burn has been dormant since **December 2025**, yet circulating supply rose from **129.66M** in December to **137.16M** now as a company reserve drains into the market to fund the KCS Bonus. Sell pressure was **~2.5M KCS**, buy pressure was **zero**, and our supply monitor reads **+1.78%** — a gap of just **0.05 percentage points**. KCS is a **token that is deflationary on its ceiling and inflationary on its float**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window from **Apr 29 2026** to **Jul 28 2026**, the framework reads **KCS at +1.8% net**: sell pressure of **~2.5M KCS** against buy pressure of **zero** on a tradable base of **137.16M KCS**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.78%** for the same period — a gap of only **0.05 percentage points**, well inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so this page ships with no data-conflict flag. The framework and the monitor agree, and they agree on something the KCS marketing does not say: while the burn shrinks total supply toward a **100M** target, the **freely tradable float is expanding**. KuCoin Token is best labelled **deflationary on the ceiling, inflationary on the float** — a fixed-supply exchange token whose sellable supply is rising because a company reserve is being paid out to holders faster than any burn removes coins.

## Sell pressure: where new KCS comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and it cannot turn on. KCS is an ERC-20 on Ethereum with no mint function; the protocol cannot create a new KCS, and total supply only ever falls, via the burn. That is genuinely fixed supply at the contract level. Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is also **zero**: KuCoin Token launched in 2017 and its original team, advisor and private-sale allocations vested years ago, with no live vesting or escrow contract holding KCS on a forward calendar today.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **~2.5M KCS**, and it is the entire story of this page. A KuCoin-held reserve is draining into the tradable float to fund the KCS Bonus and staking subsidies. The evidence is in the classified supply itself: total supply minus circulating supply fell from **12.5M KCS** in December 2025 — a figure KuCoin published in its own **66th** burn announcement — to **5.0M** now, roughly **1M KCS** a month reaching holders. Across this window about **2.5M KCS** moved from the reserve into circulation. Those tokens are paid to holders and can be sold, so under this framework they are real sell pressure, not a bookkeeping footnote. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: KuCoin is a going concern and no estate or trustee schedule touches KCS.

## Buy pressure: where new KCS goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, which is the most surprising number on the page given the token's reputation. KuCoin runs a profit-funded buyback-and-burn: it uses roughly **10%** of platform trading-fee profit to buy KCS on the secondary market and send it to a black-hole address, targeting a long-run supply of **100M**. But the programme is negligible now and dormant on the 2026 record. The last confirmed round destroyed **20,240 KCS** in December 2025; the round before it removed **53,595 KCS**. No 2026 round could be located this session, and total supply has not moved a decimal in-window — so the realised burn is effectively nothing, and this row books **zero** rather than a quarterly quantum multiplied out.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**: KCS has no automatic, EIP-1559-style fee burn, only the discretionary buyback above. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**, with no disclosed KuCoin purchase of KCS beyond that programme. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: KCS staking is un-bonded revenue-sharing at a reference yield near **0.88%**, so it pays holders but does not contractually lock the token away. With every buy row at zero, nothing offsets the reserve release, and the net comes straight from Sell #3.

## Foundation and overhang

KCS carries one identified team-controlled overhang that matters: the KuCoin / KCS Management Foundation reserve, holding about **5.0M KCS** at the last read — the gap between total supply of **142.16M** and circulating supply of **137.16M**. That reserve funds the KCS Bonus and the staking subsidy, and it is visibly draining, from **12.5M** in December 2025 to **5.0M** now. At the current pace of roughly **2.5M** a quarter it has around **two quarters** of runway left. The two largest on-chain KCS balances — near **90M** and **60.5M** — are KuCoin exchange custodial wallets holding depositor coins, not a spendable Foundation treasury, so they are excluded from this enumeration; those tokens belong to users. If the reserve's balance falls further between refreshes, the outflow continues to enter Sell #3 at the next refresh — and the more interesting date is when it empties, because that is when the KCS Bonus must either be funded by open-market buys or slow down.

## How KCS compares to other exchange tokens

The peer group is exchange tokens with buyback-and-burn programmes, and KCS shows why the burn headline can mislead. The classic exchange-token burn is revenue-funded end to end: the venue takes a slice of profit, buys the token on the open market, and destroys it, so the burn is simultaneously a bid and a supply cut and both land on the tradable float. KCS is meant to work that way, and historically did, but in 2026 the burn has gone quiet while a second mechanism — the KCS Bonus — pushes the float the other way. That makes KCS the mirror image of the sector tokens whose burns eat a reserve the float never counted: those shrink total supply without touching the market, whereas KCS is barely shrinking total supply at all and is actively growing the float.

Against a hard-capped, no-emission exchange token, KCS looks less deflationary than its branding: its contract supply is fixed, but its circulating supply is rising because company-held coins are being distributed as rewards. Against an uncapped emitter, KCS looks better, because the source of new float is a finite reserve of about **5.0M** rather than an open-ended issuance curve — once the reserve is spent, the float growth stops unless KuCoin funds bonuses by buying on the market. So KCS is neither a clean deflation story nor a perpetual inflation story. It is a fixed-supply token temporarily inflating its float from a draining reserve, with a dormant burn that could resume and change the sign.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the KCS Management Foundation reserve, at about **5.0M KCS** — track total supply minus circulating each refresh; when it stops falling, the float-inflation on this page ends. Second, the buyback-and-burn: a resumed 2026 round would be the first real buy pressure in over half a year, and the number to check is the KCS destroyed and the date. Third, the KCS Bonus distribution rate — if KuCoin trading volume rises, more fee revenue is paid out as KCS, accelerating the reserve drain. Fourth, any announcement that the Foundation will fund bonuses by market purchase rather than from the reserve, which would flip the float toward neutral. Fifth, the **100M** long-run supply target — progress toward it depends entirely on the burn restarting.
