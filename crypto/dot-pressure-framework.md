Over the last 90 days Polkadot minted about **14.0M DOT** in staking rewards on a **~1.70B** circulating base, and nothing offset it — no buyback, no burn. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads DOT at **+0.83%** net for the window and **+0.83%** forward; our independent supply monitor reads **+0.95%**, a gap of **0.12 percentage points** — inside tolerance, no flag. Since March 2026 DOT has been hard-capped at 2.1 billion and issues at a sharply reduced rate, but with the old treasury burn switched off it is still slowly, structurally inflationary.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending Aug 5 2026, the Pressure Framework reads **Polkadot at +0.83% net** on both the last-90-day and the forward view, because the same fixed issuance rate governs both. Our supply monitor independently reads the realised change at **+0.95%**, putting the gap at **0.12 percentage points** — comfortably inside tolerance, so there is **no monitor-gap flag** on this build. The two readings agree closely because newly minted DOT flows almost entirely to stakers and is counted as circulating, so realised supply growth tracks gross issuance; the small residual is measurement noise in the float-derived monitor rather than a real divergence in mechanism. Total sell pressure was **14.0M DOT** against **0** of buy pressure. The label that fits **Polkadot** is **structurally inflationary under a hard cap** — the 2.1 billion ceiling is real and permanent, but DOT still mints and no longer burns, so supply drifts up at a low, one-directional pace.

## Sell pressure: where new DOT comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, contributed **14.0M DOT** over the window, and it is the only active sell row on the page. Polkadot is a nominated proof-of-stake chain that mints new DOT as staking rewards for validators and nominators. In March 2026 two OpenGov referenda, **#1710** and **#1828**, shipped in runtime v2.1.0 and rewrote the monetary policy on **Mar 14 2026**: annual issuance was cut **53.6%** in a single step, from roughly **120M DOT** a year to about **56.88M DOT** a year, and a permanent hard cap of **2.1 billion DOT** replaced the old uncapped model. Applying that ~56.88M annual rate across 90 days gives the ~14.0M figure. Because this change activated more than four months before the current window opened, the whole window — and the next 90 days — run at the post-cut rate, so the forward reading is not re-based. The issuance rate steps down again under the network's Pi Schedule in 2028, cutting a further 13.14% of the remaining-to-cap supply.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**, and structurally so. Polkadot has no live vesting contract and no unlock calendar — the 2020 genesis allocations and the original DOT sale tranches finished releasing years ago, so there is no cliff inside the window or beyond it. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero** for this window: although Polkadot has sizeable governance-controlled reserves, none of them was observed distributing into the market during the window, and unused capacity is not a release. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** — there is no bankruptcy estate, no trustee distribution and no locked tranche attached to DOT.

## Buy pressure: where new DOT goes

Polkadot's buy side is empty, and after March 2026 that is a structural fact rather than a temporary lull. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: Polkadot runs no buyback contract and no open-market purchase has been observed on-chain. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and this is the single most important structural change to Polkadot's supply this year: **Polkadot no longer burns**. The old model periodically burned unspent treasury, but the March 2026 overhaul removed that burn and introduced a **Dynamic Allocation Pool** (DAP) that collects transaction fees, coretime sales and slashes and lets governance reallocate them across validators, nominators, the treasury and a strategic reserve. Value that used to be destroyed is now recycled, so however busy the network gets, activity removes no DOT from supply.

Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** — the Web3 Foundation has disclosed no open-market DOT purchase programme and no accumulation wallet has been identified. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is also **zero**: staking on Polkadot uses an unbonding period rather than a permanent supply-locking contract, so even though a large share of DOT is bonded for staking, it is not removed from supply in the way a burn or a multi-year lock would be, and no new lockup contract was deployed in the window. With every canonical buy row at zero, there is no offset anywhere on the page to the ~14.0M of fresh issuance.

## Foundation and overhang

Polkadot has three team-controlled overhangs worth tracking, all governance-controlled rather than sitting on a dump schedule. The first is the **on-chain Treasury**, funded automatically and spent only through ratified governance proposals — every disbursement is dated and readable on-chain, and its balance is read at each rebuild. The second is the **Dynamic Allocation Pool**, the new permanent account that now collects newly minted DOT plus fees, coretime revenue and slashes and holds them for governance to allocate across validators, nominators, the treasury and a strategic reserve. The third is the **Web3 Foundation's** genesis holdings. None of the three fired a market distribution in the window, so Sell #3 books zero — but if any of their balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How DOT compares to other capped proof-of-stake chains

Polkadot now sits in an unusual structural spot. Against **halving-model chains with hard caps** such as Bitcoin, DOT has, as of March 2026, a fixed **2.1 billion** ceiling and a stepped issuance curve that trims emission every two years — a deliberate echo of a halving schedule, timed to Pi Day. But Bitcoin's subsidy is set in code that no vote can touch, while Polkadot's issuance parameters were set by governance and could in principle be changed by governance again. More importantly, Bitcoin has no treasury and no reallocation pool: every coin issued goes straight to miners. Polkadot routes issuance through the DAP, giving governance a lever over where new DOT lands that a pure proof-of-work chain does not have.

Against **uncapped continuous-emission proof-of-stake L1s** such as Solana or the pre-2026 version of Polkadot itself, today's DOT looks far better on the ceiling and about the same on the offset. Ethereum burns its base fee, so heavy usage can push its net supply negative; Solana burns half of every transaction fee. Polkadot **burns nothing at all** since March 2026 — the DAP recycles fees and coretime revenue instead of destroying them — so there is no usage-linked mechanism that can offset issuance and drive DOT deflationary. The practical result is that Polkadot's supply growth is **low, capped and one-directional**: it cannot inflate past 2.1 billion and the rate keeps stepping down, but without a burn it also cannot go negative on demand. Compared with **exchange tokens running quarterly buybacks and burns**, which can post genuinely deflationary quarters, Polkadot offers a hard ceiling and a decaying issuance curve in place of active supply removal — scarcity by schedule rather than by demand.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, coretime demand: coretime sales now flow into the DAP for reallocation, and if governance ever votes to burn a share of that revenue again, Buy #2 could go non-zero for the first time since March. Second, any OpenGov referendum touching the issuance rate or the Pi Schedule — a governance change to the ~56.88M annual figure would re-rate Sell #1 directly. Third, DAP allocation votes: how governance splits newly minted DOT between the strategic reserve, the treasury and stakers determines how much issuance actually reaches the float versus sitting in a controlled bucket. Fourth, any large treasury spend, which would move DOT from the on-chain Treasury into circulation and lift Sell #3. Fifth, the next Pi Schedule step in **March 2028** — distant, but the next scheduled cut to issuance and the single most predictable future change to this page.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Polkadot at **+0.83% net supply growth** over the last 90 days and **+0.83%** projected forward — supply growing, and projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is a newly hard-capped 2.1 billion DOT supply that still mints roughly **56.88M DOT a year** in staking rewards, down 53.6% since March 2026, with no buyback and — after the same overhaul removed the treasury burn — no burn to offset it. The key risk is that this is a purely one-directional supply curve: it is bounded and decaying, but there is no usage-linked burn that could turn DOT deflationary, and governance retains a lever over issuance and allocation through the Dynamic Allocation Pool. The ceiling is the real guarantee: Polkadot cannot exceed 2.1 billion DOT, its issuance steps down again in 2028, and it therefore offers a bounded, slowly-decaying, low-inflation supply.
