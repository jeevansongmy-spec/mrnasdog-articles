# XTZ Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Tezos has no supply cap, no vesting schedule left to unwind and no buyback — and the Pressure Framework still reads XTZ at **+0.74%** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.74%** over the next 90. All of it is adaptive issuance: Tezos recomputes its own printing rate every cycle from the share of XTZ that is staked, and over these 90 days that produced **8.19M XTZ** of new coins against **61.5K XTZ** removed. The constraint is not a ceiling but a feedback loop — the rate falls as staking rises, and it fell from **3.12%** to **2.95%** a year across this window.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating base of **1,095.6M XTZ**, the framework books **8.19M XTZ** of sell pressure and **61.5K XTZ** of buy pressure over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+0.74%** — and projects **+0.74%** for the next 90 days. The inflation monitor reads **+0.75%** for the same window, a gap of **0.01 percentage points**, comfortably inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so no monitor-gap warning ships on the overview page. That agreement is structural rather than impressive: both sides are ultimately counting the same circulating number. What the framework adds is the decomposition, and the decomposition holds two things a headline rate does not — **30.0K XTZ** of forfeited rollup bonds, which is half of the entire buy side and was an accident rather than a policy, and **16.67M XTZ** that moved into staking across the window and removed nothing at all. The label for XTZ is a **chain that governs its own issuance and has voted to keep it on**: nothing forces the printing to stop, and nothing currently offsets it.

## Sell pressure: where new XTZ comes from

It comes from one place, and that place is Sell #1, protocol inflation, at **8.19M XTZ**. Tezos pays bakers, stakers and data-availability attesters with newly minted XTZ, and under adaptive issuance the size of that payment is not a constant a document can quote — it is a function the protocol re-evaluates every cycle from the ratio of staked XTZ to total supply, steering toward a target near half the supply staked. Below the target the rate rises to pull more XTZ into staking; above it, the rate falls. So the honest way to read Tezos issuance is to count what was actually minted, and the chain's own coinbase counter did that: it stood at **345,960,558 XTZ** at the start of the window and **354,147,689 XTZ** at the end, a rise of **8,187,132 XTZ**, or about **91,000 XTZ** a day. Annualised on the closing base that is **2.95%** a year, and it was **3.12%** a year on the opening day. The rate fell because the staked ratio rose, from **29.97%** to **31.09%** — the feedback loop doing exactly what it is designed to do, slowly.

Because Tezos amends itself by on-chain vote, the parameter alone is never the whole answer, so the ballot was read as well as the number. The protocol running at the start of this window was **Tallinn**; the protocol running at the end was **Ushuaia**, which activated on **Jun 30 2026** at block **13,857,889** — inside the window, out of the one voting epoch in the last twelve that carried a proposal at all. Every monetary constant is identical across the two protocols: the block interval, the cycle length, the minimum stake, the zero block reward, the staking ratios. Ushuaia raised data-availability bandwidth and reworked rollup governance; it moved no issuance parameter. The trailing window is therefore not a blend of two mechanisms, and the mint's three-day dip around the activation is a transition artefact rather than a regime change.

Every other sell row on Tezos is zero, and each is zero for a different reason. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** because the four-year schedule covering the founding allocations expired on **Sep 17 2022** and no new lockup replaced it — there is simply nothing left on a calendar. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0** because all fourteen labelled Tezos Foundation wallets were swept transaction by transaction across the window and returned zero outbound transfers between them; the row is zero on the absence of any observed release, not on a claim that a release is impossible. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0**: XTZ has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution attached to it. The one extra row, Sell #5, carries **4.6K XTZ** of 2014 fundraiser coins claimed on **Jul 28 2026** — the single such claim in the window, and immaterial against the **19.98M XTZ** of fundraiser allocation still unclaimed.

## Buy pressure: where new XTZ goes

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **0**. Tezos runs no programme that spends treasury money repurchasing XTZ on the open market, and none was announced or executed inside the window. This is structural rather than an oversight: transaction fees on Tezos are paid to bakers as income, not routed into a protocol treasury, so there is no revenue stream a buyback could be built on without a governance change to the fee split itself.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **21.4K XTZ**, and it is worth being precise about what Tezos does and does not burn. Ordinary transaction fees are not destroyed — they are baker revenue. What is destroyed is storage: writing data to the Tezos chain costs XTZ per byte, plus a fixed charge for allocating a new account, and that XTZ is gone. It ran continuously all window at about **238 XTZ** a day, rising with transaction volume. Against **8.19M XTZ** minted, it offsets roughly **0.26%** of issuance. The mechanism exists; it does not matter at this size.

The largest single removal of the window is not a burn mechanism at all. Buy #5, forfeited rollup bonds, is **30.0K XTZ**: rollup operators post a **10.0K XTZ** bond and forfeit it when a refutation game proves their commitment wrong, with half paid to the challenger and half destroyed. Six of those games were lost in three days — on **Aug 15 2026**, **Aug 17 2026** and **Aug 18 2026** — destroying **30.0K XTZ**, which is close to half of the entire buy side. It is carried at zero going forward, because six forfeitures inside a three-day cluster is an incident, not a pattern. Buy #6, coins sent to a dead address, adds **10.1K XTZ** across **5,584** transfers, all of them from a single automated liquidity contract — mechanical, steady, and the one buy-side line with a plausible forward run rate.

Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **0**: the Tezos Foundation wallets grew across the window, but only from baking rewards arriving on their own, which is income rather than market demand, so the framework tracks that growth as overhang on the sell side rather than crediting it here. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0** — and this is the row most likely to be misread on Tezos. Staked XTZ rose by **16.67M** across the window, a genuinely large number, and it counts for nothing in this reading because the chain's own circulating-supply definition already treats staked XTZ as float. Delegation removes even less: a delegated coin never leaves its owner's wallet at all. Staking on Tezos changes who is paid, not how much is tradable.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang on XTZ is moderate and fully readable. The dominant item is the Tezos Foundation itself: fourteen labelled wallets — four bakers and ten delegators — holding **83.2M XTZ** between them, roughly **7.6%** of circulating supply, on no published release plan. Every one was read on the transfer log rather than on a balance comparison, and all fourteen returned zero outbound transactions across the window. That method matters: on these wallets a balance-history read disagrees with the account read by exactly each wallet's staked portion, so a balance comparison would invent movement that did not happen. The balances are refreshed from the chain at every rebuild.

The second item is not team-controlled but is real supply sitting outside the float: **19.98M XTZ** of 2014 fundraiser commitments that were never claimed. That figure is not an estimate — it reconstructs exactly as the chain's total supply minus its circulating supply, and it is the only bucket the two counters differ by. Anyone holding an original fundraiser key can claim at any time with no expiry and no schedule, which is why one claim of **4.6K XTZ** appeared on **Jul 28 2026**. There is no buyback accumulation wallet to track, because there is no buyback, and there is no bankruptcy estate residual. The trigger sentence applies to both items: if the Foundation wallets' balance or the unclaimed commitment pool falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 or Sell #5 at the next refresh.

## How XTZ compares to other self-governing proof-of-stake chains

XTZ belongs to a small class: chains that hold a vote on their own monetary policy and can change it without a hard fork. That is the opposite commitment from a halving-model chain. Bitcoin mints on every block at a rate written into the code and stepped down on a known clock, so its inflation reading is positive, shrinking, and not up for discussion. Tezos mints at a rate the protocol recalculates from the staking ratio, inside bounds the bakers themselves voted for — and those bounds can be voted again. On the pure issuance axis Tezos is currently in the ordinary range for a proof-of-stake L1 at **2.95%** a year, but the number is soft in a way a halving schedule is not, and the softness cuts both ways: adaptive issuance has already carried the rate down as staking rose, and the same mechanism would carry it back up if stakers left.

Against uncapped continuous-emission chains the comparison is close but the governance is not. Most of that class fixes its emission curve in code and changes it only by upgrade; Tezos changes it by a scheduled on-chain ballot that runs whether or not anyone submits a proposal. In the last twelve voting epochs, exactly one carried a proposal at all, which says something worth knowing: the machinery is live but rarely used, so the practical volatility of Tezos monetary policy has been lower than its theoretical volatility.

The sharpest contrast is with exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks and burns. Those chains offset issuance with a demand-linked removal that scales with usage, and their inflation readings can go genuinely negative. Tezos has nothing of that shape. Its three removal paths together took **61.5K XTZ** out of a supply that gained **8.19M** — a coverage ratio under **1%** — and half of that came from six rollup operators losing disputes. For the burn side to matter to this reading, Tezos storage usage would need to rise by roughly two orders of magnitude. That is the gap between a mechanism existing and a mechanism mattering.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the staking ratio, because it is the only input that moves the number: it rose from **29.97%** to **31.09%** across this window and carried issuance down with it, so a continued climb toward the protocol's target lowers the next reading without anyone voting on anything. Second, the governance calendar itself — the proposal period that closed on **Sep 9 2026** received zero proposals, as did the four before it, so no amendment is in flight today; the moment one is submitted, the issuance parameters are formally on the table again. Third, the proposed protocol-native liquid staking token, which is running on testnet behind a feature flag and would reach mainnet only through a future vote: it removes no supply directly, but by making staking liquid it would likely raise the staked ratio and therefore lower issuance. Fourth, the rollup refutation games, which produced **30.0K XTZ** of destruction in three days in August and are carried forward at zero because there is no schedule for them — any repeat cluster shows up immediately in the burn counter. Fifth, the fourteen Tezos Foundation wallets at **83.2M XTZ**, which have not sent a single coin in this window and are read on the transfer log at every rebuild.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads XTZ at **+0.74%** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.74%** projected forward: supply growing, projected to keep growing. The structural mechanism is adaptive issuance — Tezos mints **8.19M XTZ** a quarter at a rate it recomputes from its own staking ratio, and removes only **61.5K** through storage burn, forfeited rollup bonds and a dead address. The key risk is that none of the usual brakes exist here: there is no supply cap, no vesting to run out, no buyback, and no fee burn of any size, so the reading falls only if staking rises or bakers vote the rate down. The one genuine comfort is that the rate is already falling on its own, and that the chain's own numbers reconcile exactly — the mint, the claims and the three removal paths account for the full change in circulating supply with nothing left over.
