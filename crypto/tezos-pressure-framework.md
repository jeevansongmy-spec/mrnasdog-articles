# XTZ Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Tezos minted **8.41M XTZ** over the last 90 days and destroyed **60.5K**, a net **+0.76%** of the **1.09B** circulating supply — and our own monitor reads **+0.75%**, a gap of **0.02** percentage points, so there is no data conflict to flag. Tezos is an uncapped proof-of-stake L1 whose adaptive issuance mechanism re-tunes the staking-reward rate every cycle from the staked ratio, and that dial is working: the staked share of XTZ climbed from **27.6%** to **31.2%** this quarter and the realised mint fell **10.6%** with it. There is no cap and no halving to stop the dilution, only a curve that slows it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 19 2026** the Tezos sell ledger totals **8.41M XTZ** and the buy ledger **60.5K XTZ**, for a net of **+0.76%** against a circulating supply of **1,093,817,062 XTZ**. Our inflation monitor, which measures the same chain from the opposite direction, reads **+0.747%**; the gap is **−0.02** percentage points, far inside the half-point tolerance, so no warning chip is shown. That agreement is worth stating carefully, because agreement is not the same as proof — both numbers are ultimately reading the chain's own circulating counter. What the framework adds is the breakdown, and the breakdown is where the interesting parts live. Tezos is **inflationary by design on an uncapped float**, but at a rate the protocol itself is steering downward.

## Sell pressure: where new XTZ comes from

Almost all of it is one row. Protocol inflation on Tezos means baking, attesting and data-availability rewards minted under adaptive issuance, and the chain's own mint counter rose **8.41M XTZ** over the window. The published yearly issuance rate read **3.049%** on **Aug 19 2026**, inside a protocol band of **0.25%** to **10%** a year — but the coins actually issued work out to **2.99%**, because attestation rewards for unfilled slots are never minted at all. A published parameter is a ceiling, not a measurement, and on a chain whose rate is a curve that distinction is the whole exercise. The realised pace fell from **102,347 XTZ** a day at the start of the window to **91,542** at the end, and the forward column carries the recent measured rate of **90,893** a day, giving **8.18M XTZ** for the next 90 days.

Every other Tezos sell row is zero, and each for a different reason. Vesting unlocks are zero because there is no unlock calendar: all **32** on-chain vesting contracts from the 2018 fundraiser read a balance of zero this session, as do the eight retired Foundation baker accounts, so that schedule is physically spent and cannot fire again. Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero because a full sweep of every transfer touching the **12** identified Tezos Foundation baker and delegator wallets across the 90 days found eleven with no transactions at all and one with a single **8.54 XTZ** inbound. Bankruptcy is zero because Tezos launched from a public fundraiser and has never been through an insolvency. The one small extra row is fundraiser claims: **20.0M XTZ** of 2017 commitments have still never been claimed and sit outside the counted supply, and **4.6K** of them were claimed on **Jul 28 2026**.

## Buy pressure: where new XTZ goes

Tezos runs no buyback. Transaction fees on Tezos are income for bakers, not fuel for a contract that buys XTZ on the market, no governance vote has proposed changing that, and there is consequently no accumulation wallet to watch. Foundation buying is zero for the same sweep that zeroed the sell side — no purchase reached any identified Foundation wallet. New long-term locks are zero too, and this is the trap on Tezos: staked XTZ rose **36.8M** over the quarter, by far the largest single flow on the chain, and it removes nothing. Staked XTZ is already inside the counted circulating figure, the chain defines circulating as total supply minus unclaimed 2017 commitments, and unstaking finalises in about four days. Booking that as buy pressure would invent **36.8M XTZ** of demand that did not exist.

Destruction on Tezos is real but small, and it arrives through three separate channels the chain counts separately. The protocol fee burn — **250 mutez** per byte of storage plus a fixed charge to allocate a new account — destroyed **17.3K XTZ**, about a five-hundredth of what the same window minted. A second, far stranger channel produced **30.0K XTZ**, and it is in nobody's tokenomics page: between **Aug 15** and **Aug 18 2026**, six disputes over the state of the Etherlink rollup were lost, and each loss slashes the operator's **10K XTZ** commitment bond and destroys half of it. Six games at exactly **5,000 XTZ** apiece, roughly two thirds of the quarter's entire burn, in four days. The third channel is the chain's null address, which took in **13.2K XTZ** — and sweeping the log shows every single inflow came from one contract, the liquidity-baking DEX, as an automatic part of its trades rather than people burning coins by hand.

## Foundation and overhang

The Tezos Foundation is the one identified group-controlled holder on the chain. Four labelled Tezos Foundation baker accounts hold about **32.7M XTZ** between them, and seven labelled Foundation delegator wallets a further **46.0M**, for roughly **78.7M XTZ** — about **7.2%** of supply — with no published release schedule of any kind. That is the overhang, and it was checked the hard way this session, because on this chain the obvious method is broken: the indexer's balance-at-a-past-block lookup silently returns today's balance whatever block you ask for, so a build that reads two balances is reading the same day twice. The transfer log is the only evidence that counts, and it is unambiguous — eleven of the twelve wallets moved nothing, and the twelfth received **8.54 XTZ**. Alongside it sits a second, non-team overhang: the **20.0M XTZ** of unclaimed 2017 fundraiser commitments, which convert to tradable supply the moment an original participant claims them. If either overhang's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the sell ledger at the next refresh.

## How XTZ compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake L1s

The right comparison for Tezos is not a hard-capped chain. A capped, halving-model coin has a schedule you can read years ahead, and its dilution is a known decay curve; Tezos has no cap and no halving, so its dilution never terminates. What it has instead is unusual among uncapped L1s: the issuance rate is not a governance parameter that someone has to vote to change, it is a function of the staked ratio, recomputed every cycle and bounded at **0.25%** and **10%** a year. Most uncapped proof-of-stake chains hold a flat per-block mint until a vote moves it, which means their rate is stable but their dilution only ever falls by political decision. Tezos's falls by arithmetic, and this quarter it did: the staked share moved from **27.6%** to **31.2%** and the realised mint dropped **10.6%** without any human involved.

Against chains that pair emission with a fee burn, Tezos looks weaker. An L1 that burns a base fee has a buy row that scales with usage, and at high activity it can offset or overtake issuance. Tezos routes fees to bakers as income instead, so its only destruction is a storage charge — **17.3K XTZ** against **8.41M** minted, a ratio of about one to four hundred and eighty. Against exchange tokens with quarterly revenue buybacks, the comparison is starker still: Tezos has no buyback at all, so there is no mechanism by which its supply can shrink. The most honest structural label is a chain that is mildly, predictably and permanently dilutive, with a self-correcting brake on how fast.

The Etherlink slashing burn is the one thing that makes Tezos genuinely unusual here, and it is worth naming because it is a category most supply models miss entirely. On a chain that hosts smart rollups, every rollup operator posts a bond, and a lost dispute destroys half of it. That makes rollup conflict a real, if erratic, deflationary channel — and on Tezos this quarter it out-burned the protocol's entire fee mechanism by nearly two to one.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the staked ratio. Adaptive issuance targets roughly **50%** staked, and every point the ratio climbs above **31.2%** pushes the mint rate lower — the single biggest lever on this reading, and it moves without any announcement. Second, the **Protocol V** governance vote on protocol-native liquid staking: sTEZ shipped behind a feature flag in the **Jun 30 2026** Ushuaia upgrade and is switched off pending that vote. It would not remove supply on its own, but it would very likely accelerate the staked ratio, which would cut issuance. Third, whether Etherlink rollup disputes continue — six fired in four days after none for eighty-six, so the forward column carries zero and any repeat is upside. Fourth, the **20.0M XTZ** of unclaimed 2017 commitments, which arrive in unpredictable lumps between **480** and **30,000 XTZ**. Fifth, any Foundation transfer at all: those twelve wallets have been completely still, and the first outflow would be the news.

## Summary

Tezos added **+0.76%** to its XTZ supply over the last 90 days and is projected to add **+0.75%** over the next — mild, steady dilution from an uncapped proof-of-stake chain with no halving, no buyback and a burn worth a five-hundredth of its mint. The structural mechanism that makes Tezos distinctive is adaptive issuance: the protocol reads its own staked ratio and lowers the reward rate as staking rises, which is why the realised mint fell **10.6%** this quarter with nobody voting on anything. The key risk is that this is a brake, not a stop — there is no cap, no schedule that ends, and a **78.7M XTZ** Foundation overhang with no published release plan sitting behind the float. The ceiling on how bad it can get is the protocol's own **10%** issuance bound; the floor, at full staking participation, is **0.25%**.
