# XTZ Inflation Analysis · October 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
➜ Start with the Tezos coin page for the short answer (should you buy XTZ?) and its price drivers: [mrnasdog.com/research/tezos](https://mrnasdog.com/research/tezos)

XTZ supply is growing, by **+0.73%** in the 90 days to Oct 29 2026 and about the same in the next 90. Tezos pays its bakers and stakers in newly made XTZ every block, and the coin has no maximum supply. In the window the chain created **8.03M XTZ** and buyers from the 2017 sale claimed **36.00K XTZ**, while burns destroyed **67.54K XTZ**; other trackers show **+0.72%** for the same 90 days.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Net new XTZ in the last 90 days came to **+0.73%** of the circulating supply of **1.10B XTZ**, and the next 90 days project to **+0.73%** as well. Other trackers put the same window at **+0.72%**, a gap of **0.01 percentage points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. Almost all of the new supply is staking pay; the burns on Tezos and on Etherlink, its fast add-on chain, take back less than one coin in a hundred. Tezos is a steady, mildly inflationary staking chain: new XTZ arrives every few seconds, and nothing on the chain removes it at the same pace.

## Sell pressure: where new XTZ comes from

Protocol inflation is the whole story. Between Jul 31 and Oct 29 2026 Tezos created **8,028,118 XTZ** as rewards for baking blocks, attesting to them and keeping the data layer available, about **89,200 XTZ a day**. Tezos sets this pay with adaptive issuance: the yearly rate moves with the share of XTZ that is staked. That share rose from 30.71% to 31.26% in the window, and the rate fell from 3.11% to **2.80%** a year, so today's pace is nearer 85,800 XTZ a day. We still project the full 90-day amount forward, because no rule change cut the pay. The separate liquidity-baking subsidy, which mints XTZ for the chain's built-in exchange, has not been paid since Jun 2 2026 and added nothing.

Vesting unlocks add **0**. The original allocations to the founders and the Tezos Foundation finished vesting years ago, so no calendar of team unlocks is left.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks add **35,996 XTZ**. About 19.95M XTZ bought in the 2017 Tezos sale has never been claimed by its buyers, and unclaimed coins do not count as circulating. Five claims came in during the window, on Sep 30 (two), Oct 7, Oct 13 and Oct 22 2026, the largest worth 24,900 XTZ. Claims arrived in every quarter of the past year, 118,428 XTZ in all, so we expect about **29,607 XTZ** in the next 90 days. The Tezos Foundation's own sales and grants add 0 here, because its wallets already count as circulating.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply adds **0**. No court estate or seized pile of XTZ is waiting to be paid out, and staked XTZ can leave staking at any time but is already in the count.

## Buy pressure: where new XTZ goes

Tezos has no programmatic buyback: no contract or treasury buys XTZ off the market, so that row is **0**.

The protocol fee burn removed **37,542 XTZ** in 90 days. Every time a user stores new data on Tezos, a small storage fee is destroyed; that came to 27,968 XTZ. The liquidity-baking exchange also burns 0.1% of every trade, which destroyed 9,574 XTZ. Together they cancel under 0.5% of the new XTZ paid out over the same days, and we project the same amount for the next 90.

Foundation buying is **0**. The Tezos Foundation spends XTZ on grants and holds it; it has no programme to buy it back, and no listed company has announced XTZ purchases.

New long-term locks remove **0**. Staked XTZ grew from 341.52M to **350.13M XTZ**, but staked coins still count as circulating, so a bigger stake shrinks nothing in the count; it only lowers the reward rate.

Lost rollup bonds removed **30,000 XTZ**. Between Aug 15 and Aug 18 2026 six operators of Etherlink lost disputes over the rollup's state. Each had posted a 10,000 XTZ bond; half of each went to the winner and half was destroyed. These were the first such losses on record and come with no schedule, so the next 90 days count 0 for this row.

## Foundation and overhang

The Tezos Foundation is the one team-controlled holder that matters. Its four labelled baker wallets and ten labelled delegator wallets hold about **61.68M XTZ**, and two newer wallets, funded on Oct 7 2026 and staking with Foundation bakers, hold another 30.00M XTZ. The 16 together hold **91.68M XTZ**, up from 73.08M at the start of the window. The Foundation's latest activity report valued its XTZ at USD 76 million on Dec 31 2025, about 146M XTZ at that day's price, so part of its holding sits outside the wallets we can name. We read the named wallets again at every rebuild.

The second pool is the **19.95M XTZ** of unclaimed 2017 sale coins. It belongs to the original buyers, not the team, and can be claimed at any time without an end date. If either the Foundation wallets or the unclaimed pool shrinks between our checks, the outflow enters the Foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next rebuild.

## How XTZ compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Tezos sits in the group of uncapped proof-of-stake chains that pay stakers with new coins. Its twist is adaptive issuance: instead of a fixed yearly rate, the XTZ rate drifts with the staked share, so more staking means slower supply growth. Ethereum also has no cap and pays validators in new ETH, but its fee burn can cancel a meaningful slice of that pay when the chain is busy. On Tezos, storage fees and the exchange burn remove under 0.5% of new XTZ, so the burn is close to a rounding line rather than a counterweight.

Cardano takes a different route: it pays stakers from a reserve that already exists under a hard cap, so its supply can never pass a fixed number. Solana cuts its issuance rate on a fixed schedule each year. Tezos has neither a cap nor a schedule; its rate only changes when the staked share moves or when bakers vote in a new protocol.

Tezos does share one feature with few other chains: a pool of unclaimed sale coins outside the circulating count. Most chains distributed all their sale coins at launch. On Tezos, 19.95M XTZ from 2017 still waits for its owners, which adds a small, irregular trickle on top of staking pay.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The current Tezos proposal period ends on **Nov 4 2026** with no upgrade proposed; an upgrade proposed in the next period could, at the earliest, go live around mid-January 2027, and the planned next protocol is expected to switch on sTEZ, the built-in liquid staking token.

The Tezos Foundation's activity report for January to June 2026 has not appeared yet; when it does, it will give a newer value for the Foundation's XTZ.

All Bakers Attest, a consensus change that turns on once half of all active bakers use the new tz4 keys, had **83 of 195** bakers switched in late August 2026, 15 short of the line; we will check whether it changes reward pay when it activates.

The staked share, now **31.26%**, sets the reward rate of **2.80%** a year; if staking keeps rising, the next 90 days could come in below the 8.03M XTZ we project.

Claims from the 19.95M XTZ unclaimed sale pool are irregular, and one large claim could move the row well above our **29,607 XTZ** estimate.

## Summary

XTZ supply grew by **+0.73%** in the 90 days to Oct 29 2026 and is projected to grow by about the same in the next 90 days. Tezos creates new XTZ every block to pay bakers and stakers, about **8.03M XTZ** in the last quarter, and its storage-fee and exchange burns remove under 0.5% of that. The chain has no maximum supply and no vesting left, so the main risk to holders is steady staking pay that only slows as more XTZ is staked. The Tezos Foundation's roughly **91.68M XTZ** in named wallets and the 19.95M XTZ of unclaimed 2017 sale coins are the piles to watch.
