**TL;DR.** Tezos has no hard cap and mints new XTZ only as staking rewards, tuned by adaptive issuance to how much of the supply is staked. About **8.77M XTZ** was created over the trailing 90 days and roughly **8.38M** is projected for the next 90 at the live **3.11%** yearly rate, against a buy side of only **~94K XTZ** of storage burn and slashing. That leaves the MrNasdog Pressure Framework at **+0.79%** net, a quiet, steadily inflationary chain with no offsetting mechanism.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window opening **Aug 2 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **Tezos at +0.76% net** forward, after a measured **+0.79%** over the trailing 90 days. Our supply monitor reads **+0.77%**, a gap of just **0.02 percentage points** — well inside the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap chip ships on the XTZ overview. The two readings agree because the Tezos ledger is fully legible on-chain: the created, burned and banished counters close on an exact identity, where created minus burned minus banished equals the change in total supply to the last mutez. Tezos is best characterised as **a mildly inflationary staking chain with no cap and no burn worth the name** — the issuance is modest and slowly easing, but nothing on the protocol takes XTZ back out.

## Sell pressure: where new XTZ comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is the entire story on Tezos, at about **8.77M XTZ** over the trailing 90 days. New XTZ is created only as baking and attesting rewards, and the pace is set by adaptive issuance: rather than a fixed schedule, the protocol raises or lowers the yearly reward rate toward a target based on the fraction of supply that is staked. Read live on-chain this session from two independent nodes, the yearly rate stands at **3.113%**, and that figure is confirmed a second way by the coinbase counter, which minted **8,769,749 XTZ** across the window. Roughly **31%** of supply is staked, and because staking has been rising the adaptive rate has drifted down over the window — the trailing blend annualises near **3.28%** while the current rate is **3.11%** — so the next 90 days are projected from the live rate, giving about **8.38M XTZ**.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**. Tezos ran an uncapped fundraise in 2017 rather than selling a fixed allocation on a cliff schedule, and there is no vesting contract releasing XTZ inside the window or beyond it; everything that trades is already unlocked. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is a near-zero **~5K XTZ** for the window, and the small figure hides two overhangs. About **19.98M XTZ** of 2017 fundraiser commitments are still unactivated, claimable at any time by the original contributors, and only about **5K** was claimed in the window — a sporadic trickle with no schedule, so the framework projects zero forward. The Tezos Foundation, separately, holds a large endowment of already-circulating XTZ and spends it on grants and operations, but in this window it acted as a delegator rather than a seller: its January 2026 arrangement with a validator operator delegated stake instead of selling reserve, and no reserve sell-down was observed on-chain. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**, because no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holds XTZ.

## Buy pressure: where new XTZ goes

The buy side on Tezos is almost empty, and that is the decisive fact for the reading. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**: Tezos has no protocol mechanism that repurchases XTZ and no ecosystem entity has disclosed one. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is real but tiny, about **94K XTZ** over the 90 days. Tezos burns storage and allocation fees as users write to the chain, and it banishes — permanently removes — stake when a baker is slashed for misbehaviour; the window recorded **17,013 XTZ** burned plus **77,206 XTZ** banished. Set against **8.77M** created, that is under **1.1%** of issuance, and the banishing is sporadic and slashing-driven, so it cannot be relied on as a steady offset.

Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**: no open-market XTZ purchase by the Tezos Foundation has been disclosed and no accumulation wallet exists to read, because the Foundation is a spender and delegator of reserve rather than a buyer. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero** as well. Staking on Tezos is liquid: delegated XTZ never leaves the holder's own wallet and stays spendable, and even the tighter staking role can be exited after a short delay, so growth in staked XTZ does not remove supply from the tradable float the way a bonded-stake chain's unbonding period would. No new lockup contract with a committed quantum was deployed in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on Tezos has two parts. The first is on-chain and exact: about **19.98M XTZ** of 2017 fundraiser commitments remain unactivated — the gap between the chain's committed and activated counters — and original contributors can claim them at any time. This is a genuine unscheduled unlock, but it drains slowly and sporadically: only about **5K XTZ** was activated across this 90-day window, read on-chain each day. The second is the Tezos Foundation, the Swiss non-profit that holds the network's endowment; its wallets hold a large block of already-circulating XTZ, last quantified in its own reporting at roughly **181M dollars** of XTZ at the end of 2024, with no published wallet-level release schedule and walked on the web roughly every two weeks. Because this XTZ is already counted in circulating supply and is spent into grants and operations rather than unlocked on a cliff, it registers as pressure only when the Foundation actually sells, and no sale was observed this window. There is no separate labs treasury holding a second block, no DAO treasury distinct from the Foundation, and no bankruptcy residual. If either balance falls between refreshes through an observed outflow, it enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How XTZ compares to other uncapped proof-of-stake chains

Tezos belongs to the uncapped continuous-emission class, alongside the large smart-contract layer-1s that mint staking rewards with no ceiling — but it differs from most of them in two ways. First, adaptive issuance means the rate is not a fixed constant: where a typical staking chain pays a set curve, Tezos targets a staked ratio and moves the yearly rate to reach it, so as staking rose across this window the rate eased from about **3.28%** toward **3.11%** on its own. Second, and more important for the framework, Tezos has no meaningful fee burn. A chain that burns its base fee can turn net deflationary when demand is high, so its headline emission overstates real dilution; Tezos burns only storage fees and slashed stake, about **94K XTZ** a quarter, which is rounding error against issuance. The net figure the framework books is therefore very close to the gross mint.

Against a hard-capped chain, Tezos is the opposite structure: a capped asset has a fixed ceiling and its dilution ends when the reserve or subsidy runs out, while Tezos has no cap and issues indefinitely, so its supply grows forever at whatever rate adaptive issuance settles on. Against an exchange token that buys back and burns from revenue, Tezos again has no offset — those assets convert cash flow into supply reduction, while Tezos converts staked participation into new supply. The right mental model for XTZ is a **low, self-regulating drip**: the issuance is small by layer-1 standards and gently self-correcting, but it is one-directional, and the June 2026 Ushuaia upgrade — which raised data-availability bandwidth fifteenfold and improved rollup governance — did not touch it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Watch the staked ratio, currently about **31%**: because adaptive issuance moves the yearly rate toward a target, a rise in staking pushes the **3.11%** rate lower and a fall pushes it higher, and that single number is the main lever on Sell #1. Watch for any activation of protocol-native liquid staking, sTEZ, which shipped behind a feature flag with **Ushuaia** on **Jun 30 2026** and is slated for a future Protocol V vote — a change to the staking model could shift the staked ratio and, with it, the issuance rate. Watch the Tezos Foundation for any disclosed reserve sale, which would be the first thing to lift Sell #3 above zero. Watch the next self-amending protocol proposal in Tezos on-chain governance, since Tezos can change its own issuance parameters by stakeholder vote with no fork. And watch the slashing and banishing counters, which are small today but would rise if a large baker were penalised.

## Summary

Tezos is an uncapped liquid-proof-of-stake chain whose supply grows only through staking rewards, set by adaptive issuance at a live **3.11%** a year. About **8.77M XTZ** was minted over the trailing 90 days and roughly **8.38M** is projected forward, against just **~94K XTZ** of storage burn and slashing, leaving the framework at **+0.79%** measured and **+0.76%** forward while the supply monitor reads **+0.77%** — a **0.02-point** gap well inside tolerance. The key feature is the absence of any offset: there is no buyback, no real burn and no vesting cliff, so the net reading tracks the gross mint almost exactly. The main risk, and the main lever, is the staked ratio, because it — not a fixed schedule — is what sets how fast new XTZ arrives on a chain with no cap to stop it.
