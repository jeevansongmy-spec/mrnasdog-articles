# XTZ Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Supply growing · projected to keep growing

<!-- main-page -->
➜ Start with the Tezos coin page for the short answer (should you buy XTZ?) and its price drivers: [mrnasdog.com/research/tezos](https://mrnasdog.com/research/tezos)

**Tezos (XTZ)** supply is growing and is set to keep growing at about the same pace. Tezos pays its bakers and stakers in brand-new XTZ, and it has no supply cap: from Aug 29 to Nov 26 2026 that staking pay created **7,767,556 XTZ**, and claims of old 2017 fundraiser coins added **38,997 XTZ**, while burns destroyed **41,134 XTZ**. Net, XTZ supply rose **+0.70%** in 90 days, against **+0.73%** on other trackers.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days from Aug 29 to Nov 26 2026, the number of XTZ in circulation grew by **+0.70%**, or about **7.77M XTZ** on a base of 1,102.50M. Other trackers show **+0.73%** for the same stretch, a gap of only **0.03 percentage points**, so the two readings agree and the Tezos coin page carries no warning note. For the next 90 days, to Feb 25 2027, Tezos is on course for about **+0.70%** again, because staking pay runs every block and nothing on the calendar changes it. The label that fits Tezos is simple: **inflationary by design, at a slow and steady pace**.

## Sell pressure: where new XTZ comes from

Protocol inflation is almost all of it. Tezos pays bakers, and the people who stake with bakers, for every block they make and every vote they cast, and that pay is newly created XTZ. Between Aug 29 and Nov 26 2026 Tezos created **7,767,556 XTZ** this way, about **86,300 XTZ a day**. The yearly rate is not fixed: Tezos sets it from the share of XTZ that is staked, and pays less when more is staked. It stood at **2.97%** a year at the start of the window and **2.80%** at the end, as the stake grew. Daily pay fell from about 89,000 XTZ in September to about 84,000 XTZ from mid-October for the same reason. The liquidity-baking subsidy, which once minted extra XTZ for a built-in trading pool, is switched off by a baker vote, so it adds nothing today.

Vesting unlocks are zero. The only XTZ outside the circulating count are unclaimed coins from the 2017 fundraiser, so no team, investor or Foundation batch is waiting on a lock to open.

Unscheduled unlocks come from that old fundraiser. People who bought XTZ in 2017 and never claimed it can still claim it at any time, and a claim moves those coins into circulation. Six claims between Sep 30 and Nov 6 2026 added **38,997 XTZ**, the biggest **24,900 XTZ** on Oct 22 2026. Claims came in 9 of the last 12 months, and their 12-month average is about **29,700 XTZ** per 90 days, which is what the next 90 days carry. About **19.94M XTZ** is still unclaimed.

Long-term locks and bankruptcy estates are zero. No court case, collapsed lender or long lock is holding XTZ for a later payout.

## Buy pressure: where new XTZ goes

Tezos has no programmatic buyback. No contract or treasury buys XTZ back on the network's behalf, so that row is zero.

The fee burn is the only thing that takes XTZ away, and it comes in two parts. First, Tezos burns the fee people pay for new storage and new accounts: **31,236 XTZ** from Aug 29 to Nov 26 2026. Second, the Sirius DEX, the built-in liquidity-baking pool, burns 0.1% of every trade by sending it to an address nobody controls: **9,898 XTZ** over 8,164 transfers. Together that is **41,134 XTZ**, about half of one day's staking pay. Tezos can also burn part of a baker's stake as a penalty for signing twice, but no baker was caught doing that in the window, so that part was zero.

The Tezos Foundation does not buy XTZ back. It spends on grants and its own work, and the XTZ it moved between its own wallets in the window, about 22M, changes nothing.

New long-term locks remove nothing either. About **350M XTZ** is now staked, up from 338M at the start of the window, but staked XTZ still counts as circulating, so a bigger stake does not shrink the supply.

## Foundation and overhang

The Tezos Foundation is the one team-side holder that matters. Its known wallets held **91.99M XTZ** on Nov 26 2026, up from 83.10M on Aug 28: four Foundation bakers with **33.77M XTZ**, ten Foundation delegator wallets with **28.22M XTZ** (six of them were emptied in the window), and two staking wallets that first appeared in late September with **30.00M XTZ**. The Foundation's last report put its XTZ at $76M on Dec 31 2025, close to 146M XTZ at that day's price, including loans and trading liquidity outside these wallets. All of these coins already count as circulating, so a Foundation sale moves XTZ from one holder to another without adding any. We read these wallets at each rebuild; if their balance falls between refreshes, the outflow is shown at the next refresh.

The other overhang is the **19.94M XTZ** of unclaimed 2017 fundraiser coins. It is not team money and has no schedule; it enters circulation only when an old buyer claims it.

## How XTZ compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Tezos belongs to the group of proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains that pay validators in new coins with no hard cap. What sets Tezos apart is how the rate is chosen. Tezos ties its rate to the staked share, paying more when too little is staked and less when plenty is, much like the Cosmos Hub ties its rate to its bonded share. Solana instead lowers its rate on a fixed yearly schedule, and Cardano pays from a fixed reserve under a hard cap, so its supply can never pass a set number.

On the burn side, Tezos is closer to the chains with small burns than to Ethereum, where a busy chain can burn a large share of new issuance. Tezos burns the storage fee and a slice of one pool's trades, which together offset only about half a percent of the new XTZ. Tezos also has no unlock calendar, unlike younger proof-of-stake chains where team and investor batches open every month. For XTZ, almost all of the change in supply is the staking pay itself.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

On Dec 2 2026 the main Tezos core teams move their day-to-day work on the Tezos protocol and Etherlink to a private repository, with released code still public. That changes no supply rule, but it shapes how future upgrades are built and reviewed.

The current on-chain proposal period ends on Dec 3 2026 with no upgrade proposed. Each Tezos vote runs in five stages of about two weeks, so even a proposal in the next period could not go live before about Feb 11 2027, and none that changes XTZ issuance has been announced.

Watch the staked share. The yearly rate was **2.80%** on Nov 26 2026; if more XTZ is staked, Tezos pays less per coin and the rate keeps sliding, and if stakers leave, the rate rises.

Watch for a large claim from the **19.94M XTZ** of unclaimed fundraiser coins, and for the Tezos Foundation's next report covering the first half of 2026, which will show how much XTZ it still holds.

## Summary

Tezos (XTZ) supply is growing at about **+0.70%** every 90 days, almost all of it new XTZ paid to bakers and stakers. The Tezos burns, from storage fees and the Sirius DEX trade burn, took back **41,134 XTZ**, a tiny slice of the **7.77M XTZ** created. Tezos has no supply cap and no unlock calendar, so the main risk is steady dilution, with the Foundation's 92M XTZ and 19.94M unclaimed fundraiser XTZ as the holders to watch. The Tezos staking rate, 2.80% a year and falling as more is staked, is the one dial that sets how fast new XTZ arrives.
