# VVV Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Venice Token (VVV) is an **uncapped** ERC-20 on **Base** whose emission Venice.ai has now cut four times in five months, down to **3 million VVV a year** — and yet VVV supply pressure still reads **+2.83%** over the last 90 days, because the team's on-chain vesting contracts released **1.22M VVV**, more than the **781.9K** the mint added to the tradable float. Against that, Venice's revenue-funded buy-and-burn destroyed **106.4K VVV** and its own treasury wallets absorbed another **544.8K** off the market. Our inflation monitor reads **+2.57%** for the same window, a gap of just **0.26 percentage points**, so the framework reading ships verified with no data-conflict warning.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 29 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Venice Token supply pressure at **+2.83%** of circulating VVV, rising to **+3.22%** over the next 90 days. Our inflation monitor reads **+2.57%** for the identical window, a gap of **0.26 percentage points** — inside the framework's half-point tolerance, so no monitor-gap warning is shown on the VVV overview page. The agreement is not luck: VVV's classification arithmetic closes on-chain to **0.004%** once the null address, the three Venice treasury wallets and the two vesting escrows are subtracted from total supply, which means every VVV that entered or left the tradable float this window is individually accounted for. The honest label for Venice Token is **a shrinking mint attached to a larger, unfinished vest** — the emission story is real, and it is not the biggest number on the page.

## Sell pressure: where new VVV comes from

Protocol inflation is the first tap and no longer the largest. VVV is minted continuously and paid to stakers as yield, and the Venice Token contract on Base shows total supply rising from 113,914,162.09 to 114,739,713.96 across the window — **825.6K VVV** created. Of that, **43.6K** was minted straight into a Venice company wallet and never entered the counted float, so the framework books **781.9K**. The emission is time-indexed rather than block-indexed: every fourteen-day slice after Jul 1 2026 minted exactly **115,068.11 VVV**, an annual rate of 3,000,000 regardless of how many Base blocks fell inside it. Venice cut the rate from 5M to 4M on Jun 1 2026 and from 4M to 3M on Jul 1 2026, both inside this window and both visible on the chain, and announced on Aug 5 2026 that it falls to **2.5M on Sep 1 2026** and **2M on Oct 1 2026**. Projecting the forward window by those dated steps rather than by the blended trailing rate gives **516.4K**.

Vesting unlocks are the bigger tap, and this is the number that decides the page. Venice's team and contributor allocation does not sit on a calendar — it sits in two readable streaming contracts on Base, a SablierV2LockupLinear escrow and a SablierLockup escrow, which pay out a little every day. Read at both ends of the window, the two together fell from 3,507,032 to 2,288,677: a realised release of **1.22M VVV**. The framework uses that realised outflow rather than the published entitlement, because what has actually left the escrow is what has actually reached the market. Sampled at six intermediate dates the drain is close to linear, between 11.6K and 15.3K a day with no step, so the forward column holds the same **1.22M**; the undrawn **2.29M** remaining covers roughly 169 more days, so the stream does not run dry inside the next 90.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero this window. All three Venice treasury wallets grew rather than sold — a release from any of them would land here, and none happened. Long-term locked or bankruptcy is zero for the simplest possible reason: no bankruptcy estate, trustee schedule or court-ordered distribution holds VVV, so there is nothing in that category to release.

## Buy pressure: where new VVV goes

The programmatic buyback is real, on-chain, and traceable to the transaction. Venice.ai spends part of its platform revenue buying VVV on the open market and sending it to the null address, and that address's balance rose from 33,738,954.16 to 33,845,380.17 across the window — **106.4K VVV** burned. Reading the burn required checking both surfaces: Venice burns by transferring to the null address, so total supply never falls when a burn lands, and a build that watched total supply alone would have reported the coin's entire buy side as zero. The programme runs on two legs. A small burn fires on every subscription — $2 for Pro, $5 for Pro+, $10 for Max — and, from Jul 18 2026, on every $100 of API credits purchased; these appear as roughly nineteen thousand individual transfers of about 0.89 VVV in a single week, swapped out of the Aerodrome pool and burned in the same transaction. On top of that a larger batch is bought through CoW Protocol and burned about once a month: **9.2K on Jun 8 2026**, **23.2K on Jul 9 2026** and **27.2K on Aug 7 2026**. The burner wallet already holds 20.6K toward the next one. Because the Jul 18 2026 credits rule roughly doubled the burn rate mid-window, the forward column is re-based on the post-change pace only, giving **158.9K**.

Protocol fee burn is zero: network fees on Base are paid in ETH, not VVV, and the Venice Token contract has no fee hook, so every VVV destroyed comes from the revenue buyback above. Foundation buy is zero as well — every inbound leg into the three Venice treasury wallets was traced this window, and none of them is an open-market purchase made to support the price; they are fee income, internal transfers and the buyback's own working balance.

New long-term lock is zero, and it is worth saying why, because Venice's staking design invites the opposite conclusion. Staked VVV entitles the holder to a daily allowance of Venice API compute, which sounds like exactly the kind of utility that locks a token away, and the staking contract did take in a real **1.52M VVV** and now holds 35.46M. But unstaking clears on a **7-day cooldown** — an exit notice, not a lock — and staked VVV is already inside the counted circulating float, so booking it would manufacture roughly 1.6% of deflation that no supply figure reflects. The one genuinely locked surface is sVVV pledged against DIEM, which can only be freed by burning the DIEM it minted, and DIEM supply **fell** from 37,309 to 37,039 across the window. Locking went backwards, so the row ships zero. Venice does, however, get a fifth buy row the canonical four do not cover: its three treasury wallets sit outside the tradable count, so VVV moving into them leaves the market exactly as a burn does, and they absorbed **544.8K** beyond what was minted to them. Only the steady fee income repeats, so that row projects to **41.0K** forward.

## Foundation and overhang

Venice.ai controls four readable pools outside the tradable float, and every one of them is watched at each rebuild through a direct on-chain balance read. The main company treasury holds **20.78M VVV** and grew by 253.8K over the window, partly because about 5% of every emission is minted straight into it. A second company wallet holds **8.64M** and took in 275.0K, entirely as two round internal transfers. The buy-and-burn working wallet holds **1.54M** and wobbles with the buyback cycle, taking in 59.6K net. The two Sablier vesting escrows together still hold **2.29M** undrawn, draining at roughly 13.5K a day toward an end of stream in early 2027. There is no DAO treasury, because Venice has no DAO — VVV carries no voting rights and Venice sets token policy unilaterally by announcement — and there is no bankruptcy residual. Together the four pools hold **33.25M VVV** against a 47.65M float, and that is the honest measure of Venice Token overhang. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh; if the escrow balances fall, it enters Sell #2.

## How VVV compares to other AI-compute tokens

Venice Token belongs to the small class of AI-compute tokens whose emission is deliberately scheduled downward toward zero rather than fixed by protocol code. That is a different animal from a halving chain like Bitcoin, where the cut is encoded and unarguable, and different again from an uncapped continuous-emission L1 like Solana or Celestia, where a disinflation curve is written into the protocol and governance changes it only by vote. Venice cuts by announcement — 14M a year at launch, then 6M, 5M, 4M, 3M, and 2.5M then 2M ahead — which means the schedule is credible only as far as the company is, and can move again in either direction at any time. The chain has matched every announced step exactly so far, which is the strongest evidence available that the next two will land as stated.

On the buy side, VVV resembles an exchange token more than an infrastructure token. Like BNB or OKB it routes a fixed slice of real platform revenue into open-market purchases and destroys what it buys, and like them the burn scales with usage rather than with a governance budget. The structural difference is size relative to the mint: Venice's buy-and-burn removed 106.4K VVV against 825.6K minted, roughly one token destroyed for every eight created, where a mature exchange token routinely burns multiples of its issuance. Venice's stated endgame is net deflation, and the arithmetic for it is visible — the mint is falling toward 2M a year while the burn rate roughly doubled in July — but that crossover has not happened yet and is not inside the next 90 days.

Where Venice differs from both classes is the vest. Bitcoin has no team allocation at all; most exchange tokens finished their unlock calendars years ago. Venice Token is still eighteen months from the end of a team stream that currently releases more VVV than the protocol mints, and that single fact is why four emission cuts have not produced a deflationary page. Any comparison that stops at the emission schedule reaches the wrong answer for VVV.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The emission rate falls from 3M to 2.5M a year on **Sep 1 2026** and from 2.5M to 2M on **Oct 1 2026**; the chain has matched every prior step to within half a percent, so a miss on either would be the first real break in Venice's record. The DIEM supply cap rises from 38,000 to 40,000 by **Sep 14 2026**, which is the only mechanism on the page that could turn Buy #4 non-zero — it needs DIEM supply to actually rise against that new headroom, and it has been falling. The next monthly buy-and-burn batch is due around **Sep 7 2026**; the burner wallet already holds 20.6K toward it, and a batch materially above the 27.2K burned on Aug 7 2026 would confirm the revenue growth Venice reported when it announced a $100M annualised run rate on **Aug 17 2026**. Finally, the two Sablier escrows still hold 2.29M VVV and drain at a near-linear 13.5K a day — a step change in that rate, in either direction, would move this page more than anything else on this list.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Venice Token supply pressure at **+2.83%** over the 90 days to Aug 29 2026, rising to **+3.22%** over the next 90, against an inflation monitor reading of **+2.57%** — a 0.26 percentage point gap that ships verified. The structural mechanism is a genuine, chain-confirmed emission taper on an uncapped token, now down to 3M VVV a year and falling to 2M by October, paired with a revenue-funded buy-and-burn that destroyed 106.4K VVV and treasury wallets that absorbed a further 544.8K off the market. The key risk is that none of that is the biggest number: two on-chain vesting escrows released **1.22M VVV** this window, more than the mint itself, and they hold 2.29M more that drains into the float at roughly 13.5K a day until early 2027. VVV has no supply cap and no DAO, so both the emission schedule and the burn rules are company policy rather than protocol law — the chain has honoured every announced step so far, which is the reason to take the next two seriously.
