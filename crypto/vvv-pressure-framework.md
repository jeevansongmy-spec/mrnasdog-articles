# VVV Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Venice Token is uncapped, and over the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026** the VVV contract on Base minted **0.84M** new coins into the tradable float while the team vesting contracts released a larger **1.12M**. Against that **1.96M** of sell pressure, Venice.ai's revenue-funded buy-and-burn destroyed **0.10M** and its treasury wallets absorbed another **0.40M**, for buy pressure of **0.50M**. On a circulating base of **47.3M VVV** the Pressure Framework reads **+3.08%** net against our supply monitor's **+2.62%** — a gap of **0.46 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so no monitor-gap flag ships. VVV is **a shrinking mint attached to a vesting stream that is not shrinking with it**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **VVV at +3.08% net**: sell pressure of **1.96M VVV** from the daily staking emission plus the team vesting release, against buy pressure of **0.50M VVV** from Venice.ai's buy-and-burn and treasury absorption, on a circulating base of **47.3M VVV**. Our supply monitor reads **+2.62%** for the same window, a gap of **0.46 percentage points**, inside the half-point tolerance, so no flag is raised on this build. The reason the two agree is that the VVV ledger closes arithmetically on-chain: total supply minus the null address, minus Venice's three treasury wallets, minus both vesting escrows gives **47,335,402** against a classified circulating figure of **47,335,253** — **149 VVV** apart. Venice Token is best labelled **structurally inflationary on the active float, with an issuance schedule bending down faster than its unlock schedule**.

## Sell pressure: where new VVV comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **0.84M VVV**, and it is the row Venice has been working hardest on. Every newly minted VVV is paid to stakers as yield — there is no validator subsidy, no liquidity-mining programme and no separate team emission — and Venice.ai has cut the annual issuance rate four times in a row, from **5M** a year to **4M** on **Jun 1 2026** and to **3M** on **Jul 1 2026**. The chain confirms the schedule rather than the press release: total supply on Base moved from **113,748,685** to **114,640,405**, a gross mint of **0.89M**, and the stretch after the Jul 1 cut ran at exactly **3.00M** a year. Of that gross figure, **0.05M** was minted straight into a Venice treasury wallet and never entered the circulating classification, which is why the row books **0.84M** rather than the headline mint.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **1.12M VVV** — larger than the mint, and the single most important number on this page. The VVV team allocation streams out of two on-chain Sablier lock contracts, and the framework reads what actually left them rather than what the calendar entitled: the linear lockup fell from **3,271,559** to **2,196,905** and a second lockup from **366,654** to **316,382**. The published schedule — a **10M** team allocation with **7.5M** streamed over 24 months to **Jan 27 2027** — implies roughly **0.93M** a quarter, so the escrows released about **20%** more than the calendar promised. That direction matters: on most tokens the realised figure sits far below the schedule because vested tokens go unclaimed, and here the backlog is draining instead of building. **2.51M VVV** remains inside the two contracts.

The remaining sell rows are zero, each for a checked reason. Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** — no public evidence of release in window. This was not taken from a calendar: Venice's three treasury wallets were read at both ends of the window and all three *grew*, holding **20.78M**, **8.64M** and **1.54M VVV** at the close. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: VVV has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new VVV goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **0.10M VVV**. Venice.ai spends part of its subscription and API revenue purchasing VVV on the open market and sending it to the null address, and the mechanism has been widened twice this year: a per-signup burn scaled by tier — **2 dollars** for Pro, **5 dollars** for Pro Plus, **10 dollars** for Max — and then, from **Jul 17 2026**, a further **5 dollars** of every **100 dollars** of credits purchased, API usage included. The effect is visible in the data rather than merely announced: the null address took in about **401** VVV a day before Jul 17 and about **1,752** a day after it, across **165,633** individual transfers reflecting the hourly cadence of the buying engine. One measurement note that decides this row: VVV burns are transfers to the null address, not supply-reducing burns, so total supply does not fall when a burn lands and the quantum has to be read off the null balance. Cumulative burns there now stand at **33.84M VVV**, most of it the unclaimed portion of the 2025 airdrop.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**: VVV is an application token on Base and network fees are paid in ETH, so usage destroys no VVV — every coin burned has to be bought first, which is Buy #1. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is also **zero**, and this is the trap VVV sets. The staking contract holds **35.07M VVV** and grew by **1.23M** over the window, which looks like an enormous lock, but staked VVV remains inside the circulating classification and can be withdrawn at will; booking it would invent buy pressure to which no holder is committed. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — no open-market purchase into a Venice wallet could be identified. What the treasury wallets did take in is carried as an extra row, Buy #5, at **0.40M VVV**: those coins genuinely left the tradable float, but tracing every inbound leg shows Aerodrome pool-fee income, settlement output from the buy-and-burn engine, and two round internal transfers of **200,000** and **75,000** — fee capture and bookkeeping, not demand. Only the fee income of roughly **0.04M** a quarter is steady enough to project forward.

## Foundation and overhang

Venice's overhang is unusually legible, because it is all on-chain and all readable. The largest holding is the treasury wallet at **0x2D8CB8DC596daD0e1E34E2042E7ae6Df93B11524** with **20.78M VVV**, up from **20.66M** at the start of the window. A second treasury wallet at **0x4665883F3Adb708F301BA75764D39AD0cD2a4D84** holds **8.64M VVV**, up from **8.36M**, and it sent nothing at all over the window. A third at **0xb3c89592D84ae6Adb6A1aa41515ac14eC822B175** holds **1.54M VVV** and functions as the working balance for the buy-and-burn engine, wobbling rather than trending. Together these three represent about **30.96M VVV** of company-controlled supply against a **47.3M** float — a genuinely large overhang with no published release schedule, refreshed from the chain at every rebuild.

The second overhang is the vesting remainder. The two Sablier lock contracts at **0x4CB16D4153123A74Bc724d161050959754f378D8** and **0xe261B366f231B12FCB58D6BbD71e57fAEE82431D** still hold **2.51M VVV** undrawn, and at the observed release pace that is about two more quarters of supply, with the streams contractually finishing around **Jan 27 2027**. Unlike the treasury, this one is scheduled and its end is in sight — the vesting row on this page should fall away entirely during the first half of 2027. The rule for both pools is the same: if either the treasury wallets' or the escrows' balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh, at whatever size the chain shows. There is no DAO treasury to add to this, because Venice sets VVV policy unilaterally and there is no governance vote.

## How VVV compares to other revenue-burn application tokens

VVV belongs to the revenue-burn application-token class — uncapped tokens issued to stakers, where the offsetting mechanism is the operating company spending real revenue to buy the token back. The natural comparison is an exchange token running a quarterly buyback out of trading fees, and the structural difference is stark. An exchange token's buyback typically retires a meaningful fraction of its own float each quarter because the fee base is enormous relative to the market cap; Venice's burn retired **0.10M VVV** against **1.96M** of new and unlocked supply, about a twentieth. Venice is closing that ratio from the issuance side rather than the revenue side, which is the honest reading of four emission cuts in five months.

Against a hard-capped, halving-schedule asset the contrast is one of discretion rather than magnitude. A halving chain's issuance is written into code and cannot be changed by the issuer, so its inflation path is knowable years out. VVV's emission rate is a company decision, revised by announcement — which has cut in the holder's favour every time so far, but is a policy and not a guarantee, and could be revised the other way with the same instrument. Against other AI-sector tokens with a staking emission and no burn at all, VVV is clearly the better-constructed ledger: it has a real revenue line pointed at its own supply, and the direction of every mechanism on this page is toward less issuance and more burning. The reason the net still reads **+3.08%** is not the mint, which is now modest at **0.84M** a quarter and falling. It is the vesting stream at **1.12M**, which is a fixed contract and will not respond to any announcement.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the emission cut to **2.5M** a year on **Sep 1 2026**, announced **Aug 5 2026**. Second, the follow-on cut to **2M** a year on **Oct 1 2026**; together these two steps take the forward Sell #1 figure down to about **0.57M** for the coming quarter, and the chain has matched every announced step to date, so the thing to verify is delivery rather than intent. Third, the release pace of the two Sablier lock contracts — at **1.12M** a quarter and **2.51M** remaining, any acceleration pulls the whole schedule forward into this window and any pause pushes the overhang past **Jan 27 2027**. Fourth, the burn rate under the credits rule that started **Jul 17 2026**: it lifted the null-address intake more than fourfold, and whether it holds at roughly **1,752** VVV a day is the single fastest way for VVV to reach the net-deflationary state Venice has publicly targeted. Fifth, the three treasury wallets holding **30.96M VVV** between them, which have never published a release schedule and are the largest unpriced risk on this page.

## Summary

Venice Token is an uncapped application token on Base whose issuance is falling fast and whose unlock schedule is not. Over the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026**, VVV added **0.84M** of freshly minted supply paid to stakers and **1.12M** released from the team vesting contracts, against **0.10M** bought and burned with Venice.ai revenue and **0.40M** absorbed into company treasury wallets, for a net of **+3.08%** on a **47.3M VVV** circulating base — projected at **+3.15%** next quarter as the vesting stream outruns the shrinking mint. Our supply monitor reads **+2.62%**, a **0.46-point** gap that raises no flag because the on-chain ledger closes to **149 VVV**. The key risk is the **30.96M VVV** of company-controlled supply with no published release schedule, sitting against a float only half again its size; the key opportunity is that both the emission cuts and the widened burn point the same way, and VVV's arithmetic turns negative if the burn rate holds once vesting ends in **Jan 2027**.
