# FET Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

The Artificial Superintelligence Alliance token exists on two ledgers, and only one of them is capped. The Ethereum ERC-20 held its total at **2,714,384,546 FET** at both ends of the quarter, minting nothing and burning nothing; the uncapped native Fetch chain paid stakers **10.70M FET** of fresh issuance over the same 90 days, and the merger conversion escrows released a further **0.68M FET** of already-minted supply. Against **zero** buy pressure — the announced buy-and-burn did not fire on either surface — the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads FET at **+0.50% net** over the trailing window and **+0.50%** forward, against a supply-monitor reading of **−0.08%**. FET is **inflationary by design on a supply most sources describe as capped**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads the ASI Alliance token at **+0.50% net**: **11.38M FET** of new and released supply against **zero** on the buy side, on a circulating base of **2,261.43M FET**. The supply monitor reads the same window at **−0.08%**, a gap of **0.58 percentage points** — outside the framework's half-point tolerance, so this build ships a **monitor-gap chip** and ran the full reconciliation walk. The walk found the cause and sized it: the monitor's supply concept is the Ethereum ERC-20 alone, and that contract genuinely did not move, so the Fetch chain's **10.70M FET** of staking emission is invisible to it. That single mechanism accounts for **0.47pp** of the **0.58pp** gap; the remainder is smaller than the monitor's own day-to-day spread, which ranged from **+0.14%** to **−0.10%** across its last three readings. FET is an **uncapped proof-of-stake emitter wearing a capped token's tokenomics page**.

## Sell pressure: where new FET comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **10.70M FET**, and it is the whole reason this page disagrees with the popular description of the ASI Alliance token. The Ethereum ERC-20 at **0xaea46A60368A7bD060eec7DF8CBa43b7EF41Ad85** reported a total supply of **2,714,384,546.672 FET** on **May 29 2026** and the identical figure on **Aug 28 2026**, with five intermediate samples flat in between — no mint, no burn, nothing. The native Fetch chain is a different animal: it runs a mint module with a fixed **3%** annual inflation parameter and no cap of any kind. Read directly from the chain, its supply of FET rose from **1,659,736,593.67** to **1,660,897,228.69** in **9.77** days — **1,160,635 FET**, or **118,850** a day, which annualises to **43.38M FET** and comes to **10.70M FET** across a 90-day window. Three independent angles agree: the measured supply growth, the chain's own mint parameters once real block time is corrected, and a public staking-data site that publishes FET inflation at **1.93%** and computes its staking ratio against the full **2,714M** supply. The market's own convention already treats this as FET inflation; most tokenomics summaries simply omit it.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **0.68M FET**, and the classification matters more than the size. The merger that created the ASI Alliance token converts legacy AGIX and OCEAN holdings into FET at fixed ratios, and the FET reserved for holders who have not yet converted sits in two readable on-chain escrows. Across the window the larger conversion contract fell from **13.7732M** to **13.1510M** and the smaller migration contract from **0.9083M** to **0.8509M**, a combined realised release of **679,600 FET**. Because the Ethereum total supply did not change by a single unit over the same blocks, this conversion minting is **not organic new supply** — it is a redenomination of coins that already existed, moving out of escrow into the float. It belongs in the vesting row and is deliberately kept out of protocol inflation. One unlock tracker still models the remaining migration reserve as a cliff calendar with a release date of **Sep 28 2026**, while another reports the vesting schedule complete since **Feb 28 2025**; neither calendar is used, because the escrows can be read and the realised outflow is what reaches the market.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero**, and it is a measured zero. Eight project-controlled balances were read at both ends of the window and every one is identical to the unit. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** as well: the ASI Alliance has no bankruptcy estate, no trustee schedule and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new FET goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, and this is the finding most likely to surprise a reader. The ASI Alliance has publicly announced a revenue-funded buy-and-burn intended to take tens of millions of FET out of supply. An announcement is not a flow, so this build checked the two places a burn can leave a mark, exactly as the framework now requires. First, the token's own total supply: unchanged at **2,714,384,546.672 FET** at both ends. Second, the address burned coins are sent to: it held **0.469999 FET** on **May 29 2026** and **0.469999 FET** on **Aug 28 2026**, and the null address held nothing at either end. Neither surface moved, so no FET was bought back and destroyed inside this window. This is not a claim that the mechanism is impossible: a search back through the contract's history dates its last supply-reducing burn to **Nov 5 2025**, a cut of **109,350 FET**. The engine exists and has been used. It just did not run this quarter.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**. Neither ledger destroys FET when it is spent: fees on the Fetch chain are paid out to the validators and delegators securing it, and the Ethereum token has no fee-burn at all. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero**, because no project-controlled wallet grew during the window; every one of them was flat. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**. The Fetch chain has **615.37M FET** bonded to validators, but bonded FET can be unbonded and sold, and it is the reason new FET is being printed rather than a reason less of it is available. Staking here is the emission mechanism, not a lock.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang on FET is large and, this quarter, completely motionless. The largest project safe holds **277.55M FET**, unchanged at both ends. Two further multisig safes hold **81.21M** and **32.20M**, the Fetch.ai foundation wallet holds **26.69M**, the merged SingularityNET treasury holds **7.60M**, a proxy holds **7.80M**, and the Ethereum staking contract holds **80.62M** of depositor funds — every single one identical at **May 29 2026** and **Aug 28 2026**. The two merger conversion escrows hold a remaining **14.00M** between them and are draining slowly, which is the Sell #2 row above. One balance that is often misread as an overhang is the cross-chain bridge at **0x947872ad4d95e89E513d7202550A810aC1B626cC**, which fell from **266.98M** to **264.43M**: that contract is chain location, not a holding, and every FET inside it is live on the other ledger. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How FET compares to other multi-chain proof-of-stake tokens

The useful comparison for FET is not other artificial-intelligence tokens; it is other proof-of-stake networks whose token also lives as an ERC-20. Chains with a single ledger — a hard-capped proof-of-work coin, or a fee-burning smart-contract chain — publish one supply number that means one thing. FET publishes a capped ERC-20 total of **2,714,384,546** while a second, uncapped ledger issues **3%** a year underneath it. That is the same shape as a Cosmos-family chain with a bridged wrapper, and it produces the same recurring measurement error: aggregators anchor on the wrapper because the wrapper is where price discovery happens, and the emission on the sovereign chain goes uncounted.

Against a fee-burning chain, FET has no offset at all. Chains that destroy a share of every transaction fee can run a positive emission and still print a negative net; the ASI Alliance token burns nothing on either ledger, so its emission passes through undiluted. Against an exchange token with a revenue-funded quarterly buyback, the contrast is sharper still: those tokens have a mechanism that fires on a schedule and can be verified by watching supply fall. FET has the announcement without, this quarter, the flow. And against a hard-capped mining asset, FET's capped ERC-20 looks superficially similar and behaves very differently — a real cap constrains total issuance across every venue the coin exists on, while FET's cap constrains one contract on one chain.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether the buy-and-burn actually fires: the test is simple and public, because the Ethereum total supply falls directly when it does. Second, the migration reserve release that one public unlock calendar places on **Sep 28 2026** — the framework books nothing for it today, because the escrows are readable and the realised drawdown is running near **0.68M** a quarter. Third, the mint parameter on the Fetch chain: it is governed on-chain and a proposal to change it would re-base Sell #1 immediately. Fourth, the long-pending phase-two rename of FET to ASI at a **1-to-1** ratio, which is a redenomination rather than a supply event but will move every ticker and every aggregator entry when it lands. Fifth, the six static project wallets: **433.06M FET** sits across them with no published schedule, and the first outflow from any of them is a Sell #3 event.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads the ASI Alliance token at **+0.50% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.50%** forward, driven almost entirely by **10.70M FET** of uncapped native-chain staking issuance that the capped Ethereum contract never sees. The structural mechanism is a token split across two ledgers with two different supply rules, and the key risk is that the widely-quoted **2,714,384,546** ceiling describes only one of them. The buy side is empty: an announced burn programme produced no realised on-chain flow in this window, verified on both the supply surface and the burn address. Until either that programme fires or the mint parameter changes, FET adds roughly half a percent to its float every quarter with nothing removing it.
