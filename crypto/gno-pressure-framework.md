Gnosis (GNO) creates no new tokens on either of the two chains it lives on — the Ethereum GNO contract reported a total supply of exactly **10,000,000** at both ends of the trailing 90 days, and the Gnosis Chain copy of GNO is a one-for-one mirror of GNO locked in the bridge rather than a second issuance. The quarter's entire supply story is GIP-151, a one-time pro-rata treasury redemption that took **111,074 GNO** out of holders' hands between **Jul 3 2026** and **Jul 17 2026** — **4.2%** of the tradable float — and returned it to GnosisDAO with every right stripped. Against a validator-reward stream of about **6.6K GNO** paid out of a DAO-funded reserve, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads GNO at **−3.96%** net supply for the trailing 90 days and **+0.25%** forward, because the redemption was a single opt-in window and it has closed.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Aug 18 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Gnosis at **−3.96%** net supply against a circulating float of **2,639,589 GNO**, and projects **+0.25%** forward now that the GIP-151 redemption window is spent. Our supply monitor reads **−0.02%** over the same window — a gap of **3.94 percentage points**, well outside tolerance, so GNO ships with a data-conflict chip. The gap is mechanical rather than a disagreement about facts. The supply monitor treats a single lock contract holding **360,411 GNO** as the only non-circulating GNO; the GnosisDAO treasury safe that received every redeemed token is still counted as circulating, so **111,074 GNO** leaving the market and landing in that safe is invisible to the monitor and fully visible to the framework. GNO is best characterised as a **hard-capped token with no issuance, whose float shrinks only when the DAO decides to shrink it**.

## Sell pressure: where new GNO comes from

Almost nowhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **6.6K GNO** — and the interesting part is that not one unit of it is newly created. Gnosis Chain is a proof-of-stake network secured by validators who stake GNO and are paid consensus rewards in GNO, which normally implies issuance. It does not here. The Ethereum GNO contract returned a total supply of **10,000,000** at both window ends, unchanged to the last decimal, and Gnosis Chain GNO tracked the Ethereum bridge lockbox to within **233 GNO** at both ends, drifting by only **1.31 GNO** across the whole quarter. Validator rewards are instead paid from a pre-funded reserve sitting inside the Gnosis Chain deposit contract, which GnosisDAO refills: a plain transfer of **65,000 GNO** from the DAO treasury into that contract landed on **Jun 9 2026**. That transfer is roughly two and a half years of funding in one movement, so the framework does not size the row from it. It sizes the row from the reward curve at the observed stake — the deposit contract held **268,301 GNO** at the window open and **318,603 GNO** at the close, and at that stake the published curve pays about **28,462 GNO** a year, which is **7,018 GNO** per quarter. The published validator yield of roughly **8.54%** gives **6,180 GNO** over the same stretch. The two land **12%** apart, and the row ships at their midpoint.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **0** — and this is the row a calendar would have got wrong. Gnosis locked most of its original supply into two disbursement contracts in **Nov 2020**. The eight-year contract still holds **3,852,258 GNO** and runs to **Nov 23 2028**; the five-year contract holds **360,411 GNO** and matured on **Nov 23 2025**. Read at both window ends, the eight-year contract's vested-and-claimable figure rose from **1,337,040** to **1,583,113 GNO**, and the five-year contract's entitlement now exceeds its entire remaining balance. So **1,943,524 GNO** was contractually withdrawable during this quarter. The amount actually withdrawn was **0**: both contracts' balances and their withdrawn-to-date counters were identical at both ends. Vesting on paper is not supply reaching the market, and the framework books the realised flow.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **0**. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **0** as well: Gnosis is a going concern with no estate, trustee or court-ordered distribution touching GNO, so the row cannot turn on.

## Buy pressure: where new GNO goes

Buy #5, a one-time treasury redemption, carries the whole ledger at **111,074 GNO**. GIP-151 passed on **Jun 26 2026** with **157,749 GNO** in favour — **97.53%** of the **161,740 GNO** that voted, against a **75,000 GNO** quorum — and opened a single opt-in window in which any holder could hand back GNO for a pro-rata share of GnosisDAO's liquid treasury, valued around **$223M**, at fair value. The redemption contract on Gnosis Chain took in **111,074.29 GNO** across **97** holder deposits between **Jul 3 2026** and **Jul 17 2026**, then swept every unit of it to the GnosisDAO treasury safe on **Jul 17 2026**, alongside **48,261 osGNO** that the framework deliberately leaves out of the count as the conservative reading. The proposal is explicit that redeemed GNO is returned to GnosisDAO and removed from circulation, losing every right and utility it had — retired, not burned. That distinction is what the burn read confirms.

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **0**. GnosisDAO's treasury manager bought **12,597 GNO** at a **$125.75** average in the first quarter of 2026 and a further **$1.46M** of GNO between **Apr 16 2026** and **May 8 2026** — both before this window — and then halted buying while the redemption proposals were live, stating in its **Jul 13 2026** report that buybacks stay paused while redemption is open. The chain agrees. The only GNO the treasury received from a pool inside the window was **2,561 GNO** on **Jun 1 2026**, the morning of the Gnosis Pay exploit, when the same report says DAO liquidity was pulled out of the affected pools — a withdrawal, not a purchase. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **0**: GNO has no automatic fee burn, and although GnosisDAO burned **3.15M GNO** under GIP-116 in **Jan 2026**, the burn address held the identical **3,147,806 GNO** at both ends of this window and the Ethereum total supply never moved, so nothing was destroyed by either route. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **0** because the redemption is the only acquisition GnosisDAO made and it is counted at Buy #5. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **0**: the deposit contract grew by **50,302 GNO**, but **65,000** of that was the reward-reserve top-up, and the contract pays out freely — one recent five-day stretch shows **748 GNO** leaving against **19 GNO** arriving, while the validator set fell from about **76,000** to **52,000** during July. Staked GNO is custody, not a lock.

## Foundation and overhang

The GNO overhang is unusually large relative to the float. The eight-year disbursement contract holds **3,852,258 GNO**, of which **1,583,113** is already vested and withdrawable, on a schedule running to **Nov 23 2028**. The matured five-year contract holds a further **360,411 GNO**, all of it claimable since **Nov 23 2025**. The GnosisDAO treasury safe holds **1,238,799 GNO** across Ethereum and Gnosis Chain — **824,331** on Gnosis Chain and **414,467** on Ethereum — a balance that grew this quarter from the redemption sweep and from consolidating an older DAO safe. Gnosis Ltd holds a further **250,000 GNO**, disclosed to governance in **Apr 2026** and excluded from the redemption's eligible base. The redemption contract itself is empty. Every one of these is read on-chain each rebuild, and the working rule is simple: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The historical pattern is encouraging rather than alarming — the disbursement draws that have happened went to the burn address under GIP-116, not to the market — but capacity of this size is worth naming plainly.

## How GNO compares to other fixed-cap staking chains

Most proof-of-stake networks pay for security with issuance: the validator reward is newly minted, the cap is soft or absent, and the holder pays for the chain through dilution. Gnosis inverts that. GNO is hard-capped, nothing mints it, and the validator reward is funded from a DAO reserve — so the cost of securing Gnosis Chain lands on the treasury's balance sheet rather than on the holder's share. Against an uncapped continuous-emission L1, that is a categorically different supply profile: there is no emission curve to out-run, only a reserve that can be spent or not spent.

Compared with the fixed-supply governance tokens that also shrink through treasury action, GNO's mechanism is closer to a corporate tender offer than to a market buyback. A buyback pays market price and depends on the treasury choosing to bid; the GIP-151 redemption paid a pro-rata slice of net asset value and let the holder choose to sell, which is why **4.2%** of the float retired in two weeks — far faster than any GNO buyback has moved. The trade-off is that a redemption is one-time by design, so the effect does not repeat, whereas a programmatic buyback compounds quarter after quarter. Both routes share the same limitation and it matters here: the tokens end up held by the DAO rather than destroyed, so a future vote could in principle put them back.

Compared with halving-model chains that carry a hard cap, GNO has the cap without the schedule. A halving chain's supply path is knowable years ahead; GNO's is a governance question every quarter, which makes it harder to forecast and — as this quarter shows — capable of moving several percent in a fortnight. The multi-year GnosisDAO plan to reduce total supply by **70%** is the closest thing to a schedule, and it is a plan, not protocol code.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, whether GnosisDAO restarts the GNO buyback now that the GIP-151 redemption window has closed — the treasury manager said buying stays paused only while redemption is live, and a resumption would put Buy #1 back on the ledger. Second, whether any further GNO is drawn from the eight-year disbursement contract and, critically, whether it goes to the burn address as GIP-116's **3.15M GNO** did in **Jan 2026** or somewhere else. Third, the matured five-year contract's **360,411 GNO**, which has been fully claimable since **Nov 23 2025** and has still not been touched. Fourth, GIP-153 and the proposed move of Gnosis Chain into the Ethereum Economic Zone, which would change what validators are paid for and therefore what the reward reserve costs. Fifth, GIP-154, in which Gnosis Ltd asked the DAO for **$15M** in stablecoins over twelve months from **Sep 1 2026** — funded in stablecoins rather than GNO, but a direct claim on the same treasury that backs the redemption value.

## Summary

Gnosis reads **−3.96%** net supply over the trailing 90 days and **+0.25%** forward. Nothing mints GNO on Ethereum or on Gnosis Chain, both vesting contracts sat untouched through a quarter in which **1,943,524 GNO** was legally withdrawable, and the only outflow is a validator-reward stream of about **6.6K GNO** paid from a DAO-funded reserve rather than from issuance. The move was GIP-151: **111,074 GNO** retired in two weeks, **4.2%** of the float, at holders' own choosing. The key risk is that the same governance that shrank the float controls an overhang several times larger than the shrink, and that redeemed GNO is held by the DAO rather than destroyed. The ceiling is fixed at **3,000,000 GNO** and cannot rise; everything else about GNO's supply is a vote.
