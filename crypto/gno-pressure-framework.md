# GNO Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply was shrinking, trend softening

Gnosis created no new GNO in the last 90 days — the Ethereum token contract reported the same **10,000,000** total at both ends of the window — and yet the tradable float fell hard, because a one-time GnosisDAO treasury redemption took **111,074 GNO** back off holders between **Jul 3 2026** and **Jul 17 2026**. Against roughly **7,086 GNO** of validator rewards paid out of a DAO-funded reserve, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads GNO at **−3.94% net** over the trailing quarter and **+0.27%** forward, against a supply-monitor reading of **−0.03%**. GNO is capped at **3,000,000** and nothing on either chain can mint another one.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the last 90 days the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Gnosis at **−3.94% net**: **7,086 GNO** of validator reward release against **111,074 GNO** retired through the GIP-151 treasury redemption, on a circulating base of **2,639,589 GNO**. The supply monitor reads the same window at **−0.03%** — essentially flat — a gap of **3.91 percentage points**, far outside the half-point tolerance, so this build ships a **monitor-gap flag**. The reconciliation is mechanical rather than mysterious. Redeemed GNO is not burned; GIP-151 returns it to GnosisDAO, and the classifier behind the monitor excludes exactly one address from circulating supply — a disbursement contract that held a flat **360,411 GNO** all quarter. The GnosisDAO treasury safe is not on that exclusion list, so 111,074 GNO moving from ordinary holders into the DAO changes the float without changing the counted supply by a single unit. The monitor is right on its own definition and blind to the event. GNO is **deflationary by treasury redemption, not by burn** — and that is a one-time door which has now closed.

## Sell pressure: where new GNO comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is small, and the reason is structural: Gnosis Chain does not mint GNO to pay its validators. The Ethereum GNO contract reported a total supply of exactly **10,000,000** at both ends of the window, and the Gnosis Chain copy of the token is a mirror of a bridge lockbox rather than a second issuance — the Gnosis Chain balance and the Ethereum lockbox tracked each other to about **232 GNO** at both ends, and the **39,725 GNO** the Gnosis Chain leg gained was matched almost to the coin by the lockbox. What validators are actually paid comes out of a pre-funded reserve inside the deposit contract, which GnosisDAO tops up from treasury: a bare transfer of **65,000 GNO** landed in that contract on **Jun 9 2026**. Because a multi-year top-up is not a 90-day rate, the framework sizes this row off the published reward curve instead, at the measured mean stake of **292,021 GNO** — about **28,738 GNO** a year, or **7,086 GNO** over the quarter. A second angle, the roughly **2.3%** annual dilution the Gnosis governance forum quotes against the **1,364,058 GNO** eligible float, gives **7,737 GNO** — nine percent apart, close enough for the row to stand.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**, and this build settles it by reading the escrows rather than the calendar. The two disbursement contracts left over from the 2020 vesting schedule hold **3,852,258.91 GNO** and **360,411.00 GNO**, and both reported those figures unchanged at both ends of the window. A third, much smaller lock released **32.75 GNO** — a rounding error against a 2.64M float. The entitlement is enormous and the realised flow was nil, which is exactly the divergence the framework exists to catch: an unlock calendar says one thing, an escrow balance says another, and the escrow wins.

Sell #3 — foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **zero** because no team-controlled wallet released GNO to the market. The largest movement of the quarter looks alarming and is not: **414,932 GNO** left an older GnosisDAO safe on **Jul 21 2026**, draining it to nothing, and arrived the same block in the main GnosisDAO safe. That is a consolidation between two DAO addresses, not a sale. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero** and structurally so: Gnosis is a going concern with no bankruptcy estate and no trustee distributing GNO on a court schedule.

## Buy pressure: where new GNO goes

The whole buy side of Gnosis this quarter is one event, and it is not any of the four canonical rows. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **zero**, because GnosisDAO paused its open-market buying while the redemption proposals were live. This build re-tested that on-chain rather than taking the report at its word: the DAO's buying wallet last acquired GNO on **May 7 2026**, before the window opened, and made exactly one move inside the window — **325 GNO** sent out, none bought. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**, and was checked on both surfaces the way a burn must be. Gnosis Chain charges its fees in xDAI, not GNO, so there is no fee burn by design; the burn addresses held a flat **3,147,806.35 GNO** and **0.07 GNO** at both ends, and the total supply figure was identical at both ends. Neither the dead-address balance nor the supply call moved, so the zero is measured, not assumed. Buy #3 — foundation buy — is **zero** for the same reason as Buy #1.

Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**, and it is the row that most nearly went wrong. The Gnosis Chain validator deposit contract genuinely grew, from **267,842 GNO** to **316,201 GNO**, a rise of **48,359 GNO**. Read alone, that looks like nearly fifty thousand GNO being locked away from the market. It was not: **65,000 GNO** of the inflow was GnosisDAO topping up the reward reserve on **Jun 9 2026**, so once the treasury's own money is removed, the market-sourced net flow into the staking contract is negative. Booking the balance rise as a buy would have counted a treasury transfer as market demand.

That leaves the mechanism that actually moved GNO, booked as an extra row because it fits none of the four: the one-time GIP-151 treasury redemption, **111,074 GNO**. Holders voted on Snapshot between **Jun 19 2026** and **Jun 26 2026** — **157,749 GNO** in favour, **97.53%** of the vote — to let any holder hand GNO back in exchange for a pro-rata share of the DAO's liquid treasury. The intake contract opened on **Jul 3 2026**, took deposits from **97** separate addresses, and closed on **Jul 17 2026**, sweeping everything to the DAO the same day. The detail that matters for reading this page correctly: that intake contract reported a balance of **zero** at both ends of the window, because it filled and emptied entirely inside it. Only the transfer log shows the event at all. And under GIP-151 the redeemed GNO is returned to GnosisDAO and treated as non-circulating — held, not burned.

## Foundation and overhang

Gnosis carries one of the largest identified overhangs in the framework, and it is unusually well-lit. The GnosisDAO treasury safe holds **824,332 GNO** on Gnosis Chain and **414,468 GNO** on Ethereum — **1,238,799 GNO** together, plus **56,257** staked-GNO receipts — and it is refreshed against the chain every day. It grew twice inside the window, once from the redemption sweep and once from the internal consolidation described above, and it shed nothing. The 2020 vesting escrows add **3,852,259 GNO** and **360,411 GNO**, both read directly from the chain daily and both perfectly still; the second matured in November 2025 and has still not been drawn. Gnosis Ltd holds roughly **250,000 GNO** on a governance disclosure from **Apr 17 2026**, with no wallet address published, so it is tracked by web walk rather than by chain read and is the one genuinely opaque item on the list. The redemption intake contract sits at **zero** with its window closed. Two large Gnosis Chain wallets that grew this quarter trade against automated market-maker pools and could not be attributed to any identified group, so they are deliberately left out rather than guessed into the Foundation row. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How GNO compares to other DAO-treasury tokens

The structural class GNO belongs to is not the L1 with an emission curve — it is the governance token sitting on a treasury worth a large fraction of its own market capitalisation. Against a chain like Ethereum or Solana, where a fixed protocol rule mints new units every block and the interesting question is how fast, Gnosis has no mint at all: the ceiling of **3,000,000 GNO** is enforced by there being no instruction to raise it, and the only supply that moves is supply changing custody. That makes GNO structurally closer to a closed-end fund than to a commodity chain, and it explains why the single biggest supply event of its year was a governance vote rather than a halving.

Against the other end of the class — exchange tokens that run a programmatic buyback-and-burn out of revenue — the difference is destination, and it is the difference that produces this page's monitor gap. When a buyback sends tokens to a burn address, the total supply falls and every classifier in the market sees it immediately. GnosisDAO's redemption sends the tokens to a treasury safe instead. Economically the float shrank by **4.2%**; arithmetically the counted supply did not move at all, because the safe is inside the circulating definition. A reader comparing GNO to a burn-based token on headline supply change would conclude nothing happened. A reader comparing the two on float would see one of the largest quarterly contractions in the catalog.

The third comparison is the one that decides the next year. Gnosis Chain is a proof-of-stake network that pays its validators in GNO out of a treasury subsidy rather than out of protocol issuance — a model very few chains run, because it is expensive and finite. Chains that mint their own rewards can subsidise security indefinitely at the cost of dilution; Gnosis has been paying for security out of capital instead, which keeps supply flat and drains the treasury. GIP-153 ends that arrangement.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the fate of the redeemed tranche. The **111,074 GNO** now sits in the GnosisDAO safe rather than a burn address. If the DAO burns it, the counted supply finally catches up with the float and this page's monitor gap closes on its own; if the DAO ever redeploys it, the same tokens re-enter the float as a Sell #3 outflow. Second, GIP-153, approved on **Aug 20 2026** by **123,158 GNO** to **115**: Gnosis Chain will retire its own validator set and settle to Ethereum as a rollup, with genesis targeted for **Dec 2026** or **Jan 2027**. At that genesis roughly **350,000 GNO** of staked collateral is released and the treasury-funded staking subsidy ends — the first removes the largest single lock on the token, the second removes this page's only sell row. Both land just outside this window, which is precisely why the forward reading is **+0.27%** and not something larger.

Third, whether the open-market buyback restarts now the redemption window has shut; it has been paused since **May 8 2026** and its restart would be the first recurring buy mechanism GNO has had this year. Fourth, the two 2020 disbursement escrows, one of which has been fully mature and undrawn since **Nov 23 2025** — a first draw from either would be the largest scheduled sell event available to this token. Fifth, the promised follow-up proposal on connecting GNO to network fee revenue, which the rollup proposal explicitly defers; until it lands, GNO has no revenue link at all.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Gnosis at **−3.94% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.27%** forward, on a circulating base of **2,639,589 GNO** under a hard **3,000,000** ceiling that nothing on either chain can raise. The contraction is not a burn and not a buyback: it is a one-time, governance-approved treasury redemption that retired **111,074 GNO** — **4.2%** of the float — into the DAO's own safe over two weeks in July 2026, against a modest **7,086 GNO** of validator rewards paid out of a treasury-funded reserve rather than minted. The key risk is that none of this is recurring: the redemption window is closed, the market buyback is paused, and the redeemed tokens are held rather than destroyed, so they remain a governance decision away from returning to the float. The ceiling is real and permanent; the contraction was an event.
