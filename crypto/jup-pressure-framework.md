## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 24 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **JUP at −1.00% net**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **33.05M JUP** of buy pressure, on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.15%**, a gap of **1.14 percentage points**, which is far beyond the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the JUP overview. The gap is structural rather than an error: the Litterbox Trust buys JUP on the open market and locks it, but a supply count still sees those coins inside the circulating float, so it reads roughly flat while the framework credits the **33.05M JUP** the trust took out of trading hands. That is the textbook **buyback-and-hold** gap, and it is worth naming precisely, because the Litterbox Trust is a three-year lock and not a burn — the JUP still exists. The honest label for Jupiter today is **deflationary by structural buyback against a switched-off sell side**.

## Sell pressure: where new JUP comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the single most important fact about Jupiter's tokenomics in 2026. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** and permanently so. JUP is a plain Solana token whose mint authority reads null on-chain — there is no function anyone, Jupiter included, can call to create a new JUP. Total supply began at a **10B** genesis cap, was cut to **7B** by the community supply reduction, and now reads **6,862.43M JUP** after roughly **137.57M** of further burns. From here the number can only go down. Jupiter does pay Active Staking Rewards of **50M JUP** a quarter to stakers who vote, with the latest batch opening for claims on **Jul 8 2026**, but the DAO's own net-zero emissions package states those coins are drawn from previously unclaimed airdrop allocations that the circulating count already holds. The circulating count held near **3,320M JUP** straight through that claim opening, and a genuine 50M release would have shown up as a **+1.5%** step. Two independent angles, one documentary and one measured, agree: the ASR distribution is float-neutral.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Jupiter DAO voted it away. The net-zero emissions proposal went to a vote from **Feb 17 2026** to **Feb 22 2026** and did three things at once: it halted team vesting after the **Jan 27 2026** unlock, it settled the remaining founding-stakeholder allocation with an accelerated final tranche on **Feb 25 2026**, and it postponed the Jupuary airdrop, returning that allocation to community custody. All three landed before this window opened. An unlock tracker now reads JUP as fully unlocked with the vesting schedule ended, and no cliff falls between today and **Nov 22 2026**. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** on flow even though the capacity is enormous — nothing left any identified team-controlled wallet inside the window. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the plain reason that Jupiter has no bankruptcy estate and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new JUP goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, carries the entire buy side at **33.05M JUP** over the last 90 days. The Litterbox Trust routes **50%** of Jupiter's on-chain revenue into open-market JUP purchases and locks what it buys for three years. This build measured the row directly on the Solana chain rather than from any dashboard: the trust's JUP balance read **126.52M** on **May 25 2026**, **140.83M** on **Jun 25 2026**, **151.33M** on **Jul 25 2026** and **159.57M** on **Aug 24 2026** — a clean **33.05M JUP** of accumulation, monotonically rising across 154 checkpoints, with not one outflow. The arithmetic ties out against the documented fee split. At the price paid across each interval those purchases cost about **$6.47M**. Jupiter's revenue over the same 90 days was about **$14.22M**, so a documented half-share would be **$7.11M**. The trust realised **91%** of that. The quoted 50% is being realised, not merely authorised.

The half-share is a share of Jupiter's **revenue**, not of its gross fees, and the difference is large enough to be worth stating. Gross fees across the same window were roughly **$46.39M**; the buyback consumed only **13.9%** of that. Applying the headline 50% to gross fees instead of to revenue would have overstated the JUP buyback by more than three times. On pace, the buyback ran at **0.37M JUP a day** across the window, dipping to **0.21M** in mid-July before recovering to **0.31M** through August, so the trailing rate is the honest forward predictor and gives **33.0M JUP** for the next 90 days. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**: holders did vote once, in **Nov 2025**, to burn about **130M JUP** out of the trust, but that was a one-off, and total supply has not moved since. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**, because no discretionary open-market buying is disclosed outside the automatic revenue buyback. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well, because the trust's three-year lock is already counted inside Buy #1 and is not booked twice.

## Foundation and overhang

Jupiter's overhang is the largest single risk in this reading. Total supply is **6,862.43M JUP** and circulating supply is **3,320.31M JUP**, so roughly **3,542M JUP** — more than the entire float — sits outside the market with no published release date. Broken out, that is the postponed Jupuary airdrop reserve of about **1,400M JUP**, the strategic reserve and wider community allocation of about **1,372M JUP**, the halted team allocation of about **428M JUP**, the settled founding-stakeholder pool of about **82M JUP**, and the Litterbox Trust's own **159.57M JUP**. Jupiter does not publish the individual multisig addresses for the first four, so those are refreshed by a walk of the project's own disclosures and the DAO forum roughly every two weeks; the Litterbox Trust is read straight off the chain from its JUP account, address **DpVH8xQZ4aapxwZ6KW9nuEUs9zEePa8HQvXny9Ajj93T**, every day. None of these carries a value today, because enumeration is about scope rather than forecast. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How JUP compares to other DeFi application tokens

JUP is not a Layer 1 and should not be read like one. A Solana or an Ethereum pays a continuous, protocol-encoded issuance to validators — a permanent sell row that no vote can fully switch off, only tune. Jupiter has no such row at all: the mint authority is renounced, so the entire question of protocol inflation for JUP is closed, permanently, in a way that no proof-of-stake Layer 1 can match. Where a Layer 1's deflation depends on a fee burn racing its own issuance, JUP's starting point is a supply that mathematically cannot grow.

The closer structural analogue is the exchange-token model: a business that earns fees and recycles a fixed share into its own token. The difference that matters is destination. An exchange token running a quarterly buyback-and-burn destroys the coins, and the burn shows up in every supply count on earth, so the framework and the monitor agree. Jupiter buys and **locks** instead, for three years, into the Litterbox Trust. Economically the float effect is the same — **33.05M JUP** cannot be sold by anybody for three years — but a supply count cannot see it, which is precisely why the JUP reading carries a **1.14 percentage point** gap and an exchange token running the same size buyback-and-burn would not.

The third comparison is to app tokens with no buyback at all, where the entire deflation case rests on unlocks eventually finishing. Jupiter is past that point on the sell side but not on the overhang: with **3,542M JUP** uncirculated against a **3,320.31M JUP** float, the governance decision to keep those tokens parked is the load-bearing assumption in this whole reading. A vote could reverse it, and the framework would book the outflow the moment it happened.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Q2 staking-rewards claim window closes on **Oct 8 2026**, after which unclaimed JUP returns to the treasury — float-neutral on our reading, but the point at which a change in the funding source would first show up. Second, a proposal to raise the Litterbox Trust's share of revenue from **50%** to **70%**, posted **May 30 2026**, remains discussion-stage with no vote as of **Aug 24 2026**; if it passes, the buyback row re-bases upward by about two fifths. Third, a separate proposal posted **Jul 31 2026** asks Jupiter to burn a slice of every buyback instead of sending all of it to the trust; if that passes, the monitor gap on this page closes by itself, because burned coins leave every supply count. Fourth, the Litterbox Trust now holds **159.57M JUP**, more than the **130M** the DAO voted to burn in **Nov 2025** — a repeat burn vote is the single largest deflationary catalyst available to Jupiter. Fifth, the buyback is a share of revenue, so Jupiter's trading volume is the real variable: the rate already dipped to **0.21M JUP a day** in mid-July before recovering, and a sustained revenue decline would pull the next-90-day figure down with it.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Jupiter (JUP) as **deflationary by structural buyback**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **33.05M JUP** bought and locked over 90 days, a net of **−1.00%** on a **3,320.31M JUP** float, projected at **−0.99%** for the next 90 days. The mechanism is unusually clean — a renounced mint authority means new JUP is impossible, and a governance vote closed the vesting schedule — so the entire reading rests on the buy side continuing. The key risk is the **3,542M JUP** of uncirculated supply, more than the float itself, parked by governance decision rather than by code. And the ceiling is now real in both directions: total supply of **6,862.43M JUP** is a hard maximum that can only fall, while the buyback's size is capped by Jupiter's revenue, not by ambition.
