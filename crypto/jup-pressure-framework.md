**TL;DR.** Jupiter added **zero** new JUP to the market over the last 90 days, while the Litterbox Trust spent half of the protocol's on-chain revenue buying **35.83M JUP** on the open market and locking it away for three years. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so total supply of **6,862.43M** can only fall. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads JUP at **−1.08% net** over the last 90 days on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP** — but the buyback is decelerating with revenue, and on the current run rate the next 90 days project to **−0.74%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 4 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **JUP at −1.08% net**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **35.83M JUP** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **−0.09%**, a gap of about **1 percentage point**, which is beyond the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the JUP overview. The gap is structural, not an error: the buyback buys JUP on the open market and locks it in a trust, but the supply monitor still counts those coins as part of the circulating float, so it reads roughly flat while the framework credits the **35.83M JUP** the trust removed. This is the classic **buyback-and-hold** gap. The honest characterisation of JUP today is **deflationary by structural buyback with a switched-off sell side — but with the buy side cooling**.

## Sell pressure: where new JUP comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the single most important fact about Jupiter's tokenomics in 2026. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** and permanently so. JUP is a Solana SPL token whose mint authority reads null on-chain — there is no function anyone, including the Jupiter team, can call to create a new JUP. Total supply began at a **10B** genesis cap, was cut to **7B** by the community supply reduction, and now reads **6,862.43M JUP** after roughly **137.57M** of cumulative burns. From here the number can only go down. Jupiter does pay Active Staking Rewards of **50M JUP** a quarter to voters and stakers, but those coins are drawn from a community allocation the circulating count already includes, so they change who holds JUP without changing how much of it exists in the market.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Jupiter DAO voted it away. The "Going Green" Net-Zero Emissions proposal closed on **Feb 22 2026** with roughly **75%** in favour, and it did three things at once: it halted team vesting after the **Jan 27 2026** unlock and replaced it with balance-sheet credits, it settled the remaining founding-stakeholder allocation through an accelerated final tranche on **Feb 25 2026**, and it postponed the Jupuary airdrop, sending **700M JUP** back to the community cold multisig. Unlock trackers now show JUP as fully unlocked with the vesting schedule ended, and no cliff falls between today and **Nov 2 2026**. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the plain reason that Jupiter has no bankruptcy estate and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new JUP goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, carries the entire buy side at **35.83M JUP** over the last 90 days. The Litterbox Trust routes **50%** of Jupiter's on-chain revenue into open-market JUP purchases and locks what it buys for three years. This build measured the row directly on-chain rather than from any dashboard: the trust's JUP balance read **117.67M** on **May 6 2026**, **132.03M** on **Jun 5 2026**, **145.30M** on **Jul 5 2026** and **153.50M** on **Aug 4 2026** — a clean, monotonic **35.83M JUP** of accumulation with not one outflow. Cross-checked against the mechanism, the half-share of protocol revenue over the same window buys about that much JUP at the window's average price of roughly **$0.19**, so the row ships as measured rather than estimated.

The important detail this quarter is the **pace**. The trust added **14.36M** JUP in the first month of the window, **13.28M** in the second, and only **8.19M** in the last — the daily buyback rate has roughly halved, from about **0.48M/day** in early May to **0.27M/day** into August, tracking a fall in Jupiter's trading revenue. Because the buyback is a fixed **50%** share of revenue and that carve-out has not changed, this is revenue decay rather than a policy change. Projecting the current run rate forward gives roughly **24.58M JUP** for the next 90 days, or **−0.74% net** — still deflationary, but no longer past the −1% mark. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**: Jupiter did burn roughly **130M JUP** in the "Burn the Litterbox" vote of **Nov 2025**, but that was a one-off well outside this window, and today's buyback holds rather than destroys. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero** — no discretionary open-market buying is disclosed outside the automatic revenue buyback. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well: JUP staking is user-initiated and withdrawable, and the trust's three-year lock is already counted inside Buy #1 rather than booked twice.

## Foundation and overhang

JUP's overhang is the largest single risk in this reading. Roughly **3,542M JUP** — total supply of **6,862.43M** less circulating of **3,320.31M** — sits outside the market with no published release schedule. Three buckets account for it. The community cold multisig holds the **700M JUP** Jupuary allocation that the net-zero vote returned rather than cancelled outright; the proposal explicitly preserved the original snapshot so a future DAO could still distribute it. The strategic reserve and the wider DAO and community allocation hold the rest. None of them is on a calendar, which is precisely why the framework books Sell #3 at **zero** today: having tokens is not the same as releasing them, and the circulating count has held near **3,320M** through the window.

The fourth tracked holding is the Litterbox Trust itself, now **153.50M JUP** — about **4.6%** of circulating supply, bought with real protocol revenue. It is read by chain RPC on every rebuild, and it has only ever grown. Because the trust holds rather than burns, that stack is a reversible overhang: the DAO has already shown, in **Nov 2025**, that it is willing to vote on what happens to it, and burning, redeploying and selling are all live options. If the trust's balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The same trigger applies to the community cold multisig, the strategic reserve and the DAO allocation.

## How JUP compares to other revenue-buyback DeFi tokens

JUP's closest structural relatives are not other Solana assets but tokens whose supply story is a revenue-funded buyback. Exchange tokens that run quarterly buy-and-burn programmes convert revenue into permanent destruction: the coins leave the ledger, total supply falls, and every observer agrees on the number. Jupiter sits one step short of that today. The revenue conversion is arguably stronger — it runs continuously at **50%** of on-chain revenue rather than quarterly at management discretion, a far higher share than the **12%**-of-fees model common among Solana DEX tokens — but the destination is a trust wallet, not a burn address. The effect on the tradable float today is identical; the durability is not, and that is the trade the DAO keeps revisiting.

Against uncapped, emission-driven DeFi and Layer-1 tokens, JUP looks unusually clean. A renounced mint authority, a vesting schedule that governance closed rather than merely finished, a supply already cut from **10B** to **6,862.43M**, and a buy side funded by real trading revenue is a combination very few tokens can show at once. But the genuine risk is exactly the flip side of that revenue link. The buyback is a fixed share of fees, so a revenue slowdown is a buyback slowdown — and that is what the last 90 days show, the deflation easing from **−1.08%** toward **−0.74%** as volume cooled. On the other side, the **3,542M JUP** outside circulation is larger than the float itself; a future governance decision could put a large share of it back into the market, and the framework would swing from deflationary to sharply positive in a single window.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the buyback pace itself: it has decelerated from **0.48M** to **0.27M** JUP a day as revenue fell, and whether it stabilises or keeps sliding is what decides between a **−0.74%** and a flatter next quarter. Second, the Litterbox Trust balance, **153.50M JUP** on **Aug 4 2026** and never yet fallen — any decline is the first real sell signal this ledger would register. Third, the proposal to raise the buyback allocation from **50%** to **70%**, posted **May 30 2026** and still in discussion — if it reaches a vote and passes, the buy row rises by roughly **40%** at the same revenue. Fourth, the companion tokenomics revamp posted **Jun 22 2026**, which would switch the buyback from accumulate to direct burn; that would not change the float effect but would close most of our monitor gap permanently. Fifth, the Q2 staking-reward claim deadline of **Oct 8 2026**, after which unclaimed JUP returns to the community allocation.

## Summary

Jupiter is one of the few large tokens whose sell ledger is genuinely empty. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so no new JUP can ever be created; the Feb 2026 net-zero vote halted team vesting and postponed the airdrop; and the only measurable flow in the last 90 days was the **35.83M JUP** the revenue buyback removed from the market and locked for three years, a framework reading of **−1.08% net**. But the buyback is a fixed share of falling revenue, so its pace has roughly halved over the window, and the same mechanism projected forward gives **−0.74%** for the next 90 days — still shrinking, just more slowly. The key risk is not inflation but discretion: **3,542M JUP** sits outside circulation with no schedule, the buyback holds instead of burning, and both are governance decisions rather than protocol guarantees. Read JUP as deflationary today, cooling, on a mechanism the DAO could change tomorrow.
