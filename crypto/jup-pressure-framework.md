**TL;DR.** Jupiter added **zero** new JUP to the market over the last 90 days, while the Litterbox Trust spent half of the protocol's on-chain revenue buying **36.57M JUP** on the open market and locking it away for three years. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so total supply of **6,862.43M** can only fall. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads JUP at **−1.10% net** on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**. Our supply monitor reads **−7.77%**, but almost all of that is a one-day reclassification rather than a real flow.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Jul 27 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **JUP at −1.10% net**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **36.57M JUP** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **−7.77%**, a gap of **6.67 percentage points**, which is far beyond the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the JUP overview. The gap resolves on inspection rather than on arithmetic. The monitor's circulating count did not drift down over 90 days; it fell about **280M JUP** in a single step between **Apr 28 2026** and **Apr 29 2026** and then sat flat for the remaining 89 days. Nothing happened on-chain that day: the JUP mint has a renounced mint authority and total supply held at **6,862.43M** throughout, so no coin was created and none was destroyed. That step is a bucket being re-tagged, not supply leaving the market. The honest characterisation of JUP today is **deflationary by structural buyback with a switched-off sell side**.

## Sell pressure: where new JUP comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the single most important fact about Jupiter's tokenomics in 2026. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** and permanently so. JUP is a Solana SPL token whose mint authority reads null on-chain — there is no function anyone, including the Jupiter team, can call to create a new JUP. Total supply began at a **10B** genesis cap, was cut to **7B** by the community supply reduction, and now reads **6,862,431,306 JUP** after roughly **137.57M** of cumulative burns. From here the number can only go down. Jupiter does pay Active Staking Rewards of **50M JUP** a quarter to voters and stakers, but those coins are drawn from a community allocation the circulating count already includes, so they change who holds JUP without changing how much of it exists in the market.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Jupiter DAO voted it away. The "Going Green" Net-Zero Emissions proposal closed on **Feb 22 2026** with roughly **75%** in favour, and it did three things at once: it halted team vesting after the **Jan 27 2026** unlock and replaced it with balance-sheet credits, it settled the remaining founding-stakeholder allocation through an accelerated final tranche on **Feb 25 2026**, and it postponed the Jupuary airdrop, sending **700M JUP** back to the community cold multisig. Unlock trackers now show JUP as fully unlocked with the vesting schedule ended, and no cliff falls between today and **Oct 25 2026**. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the plain reason that Jupiter has no bankruptcy estate and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new JUP goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, carries the entire buy side at **36.57M JUP** over 90 days. The Litterbox Trust routes **50%** of Jupiter's on-chain revenue into open-market JUP purchases and locks what it buys for three years. This build measured the row directly on-chain rather than from any dashboard: the trust's JUP balance read **115.04M** on **Apr 28 2026**, **126.68M** on **May 27 2026**, **142.53M** on **Jun 27 2026** and **151.61M** on **Jul 27 2026** — a clean, monotonic **36.57M JUP** of accumulation with not one outflow. Cross-checked against the mechanism, Jupiter booked **$13.63M** of protocol revenue over the same 90 days; the documented half-share is **$6.82M**, which buys **36.6M JUP** at the window's implied average price of about **$0.19**. Two independent readings landing on the same number is why this row ships as measured rather than estimated.

Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and the distinction matters. Jupiter did burn — the "Burn the Litterbox" vote passed with **86%** support and destroyed roughly **130M JUP** in **Nov 2025** — but that was a one-off event well outside this window, and the trust has been re-accumulating ever since. Today's buyback holds rather than destroys, which removes JUP from the tradable float without reducing total supply. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**: Jupiter discloses no discretionary open-market buying outside the automatic revenue buyback. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well — JUP staking is user-initiated and withdrawable, so it locks nothing permanently, and the trust's own three-year lock is already counted inside Buy #1 rather than booked twice.

## Foundation and overhang

JUP's overhang is the largest single risk in this reading. Roughly **3,542M JUP** — total supply of **6,862.43M** less circulating of **3,320.31M** — sits outside the market with no published release schedule. Three buckets account for it. The community cold multisig holds the **700M JUP** Jupuary allocation that the net-zero vote returned rather than cancelled outright; the proposal explicitly preserved the original snapshot so a future DAO could still distribute it. The strategic reserve and the wider DAO and community allocation hold the rest. None of them is on a calendar, which is precisely why the framework books Sell #3 at **zero** today: having tokens is not the same as releasing them, and the circulating count has not moved since **Apr 29 2026**.

The fourth tracked holding is the Litterbox Trust itself, now **151.61M JUP** — about **4.6%** of circulating supply, bought with real protocol revenue. It is read by chain RPC on every rebuild, and it has only ever grown. Because the trust holds rather than burns, that stack is a reversible overhang: the DAO has already shown, in **Nov 2025**, that it is willing to vote on what happens to it, and burning, redeploying and selling are all live options. If the trust's balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The same trigger applies to the community cold multisig, the strategic reserve and the DAO allocation.

## How JUP compares to other revenue-buyback DeFi tokens

JUP's closest structural relatives are not other Solana assets but tokens whose supply story is a revenue-funded buyback. Exchange tokens that run quarterly buy-and-burn programmes convert revenue into permanent destruction: the coins leave the ledger, total supply falls, and every observer agrees on the number. Jupiter sits one step short of that today. The revenue conversion is arguably stronger — it runs continuously at **50%** of on-chain revenue rather than quarterly at management discretion, a far higher share than the **12%**-of-fees model common among Solana DEX tokens — but the destination is a trust wallet, not a burn address. The effect on the tradable float today is identical; the durability is not, and that is the trade the DAO keeps revisiting.

Against uncapped, emission-driven DeFi and Layer-1 tokens, JUP looks unusually clean. A renounced mint authority, a vesting schedule that governance closed rather than merely finished, a supply that has already been cut from **10B** to **6,862.43M**, and a buy side funded by real trading revenue is a combination very few tokens can show at once. The genuine risk is not dilution, it is two-sided. On one side, the buyback is a fixed share of fees, so a revenue collapse is a buyback collapse and the deflation measured here would fade with it. On the other, the **3,542M JUP** outside circulation is more than the float itself — a future governance decision could put a large share of it back into the market, and the framework would move from **−1.10%** to sharply positive in a single window.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Litterbox Trust balance: it stood at **151.61M JUP** on **Jul 27 2026** and has never fallen. Any decline is the first real sell signal this ledger would register. Second, the proposal to raise the buyback allocation from **50%** to **70%**, posted **May 30 2026** and still in discussion — if it reaches a vote and passes, the buy row rises by roughly **40%** at the same revenue. Third, the tokenomics revamp posted **Jun 22 2026**, which would switch the buyback from accumulate to direct burn; that would not change the float effect but would close most of our monitor gap permanently. Fourth, the Q2 staking-reward claim deadline of **Oct 8 2026**, after which unclaimed JUP returns to the community allocation. Fifth, protocol revenue itself, running at **$13.63M** per 90 days — the buyback tracks it one for one.

## Summary

Jupiter is one of the few large tokens whose sell ledger is genuinely empty. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so no new JUP can ever be created; the Feb 2026 net-zero vote halted team vesting and postponed the airdrop; and the only measurable flow in the last 90 days was the **36.57M JUP** the revenue buyback removed from the market and locked for three years. That produces a framework reading of **−1.10% net**, and the same mechanism projects forward, so supply is shrinking and is expected to keep shrinking. The key risk is not inflation but discretion: **3,542M JUP** sits outside circulation with no schedule, the buyback holds instead of burning, and both are governance decisions rather than protocol guarantees. Read JUP as deflationary today on a mechanism the DAO could change tomorrow.
