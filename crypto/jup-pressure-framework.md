**TL;DR.** Jupiter added **zero** new JUP to the market over the last 90 days, while the Litterbox Trust spent half of Jupiter's on-chain revenue buying **34.42M JUP** on the open market and locking it away for three years. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so the total supply of **6,862.43M JUP** can only fall from here, and no vesting unlock is scheduled at all. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads JUP at **−1.04% net** over the last 90 days on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**, and projects the same **−1.04%** for the next 90 days on the measured buyback rate.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 31 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **JUP at −1.04% net**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **34.42M JUP** of buy pressure, on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **−0.06%**, a gap of **0.98 percentage points**, well beyond the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the JUP overview. The gap is structural rather than an error: the Litterbox Trust buys JUP on the open market and locks it, but a supply count still sees those coins inside the circulating float, so it reads roughly flat while the framework credits the **34.42M JUP** the trust took out of trading hands. That is the textbook **buyback-and-hold** gap, and it is worth naming precisely, because the Litterbox Trust is a three-year lock and not a burn — the JUP still exists, and it is surfaced on the sell side of the ledger as an overhang for exactly that reason. The honest label for Jupiter today is **deflationary by structural buyback against a switched-off sell side**.

## Sell pressure: where new JUP comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the single most important fact about Jupiter's tokenomics in 2026. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** and permanently so. JUP is a plain Solana token whose mint authority reads null on-chain — there is no function anyone, Jupiter included, can call to create a new JUP. Total supply began at a **10B** genesis cap, was cut to **7B** by the community supply reduction, and now reads **6,862.43M JUP** after roughly **137.57M** of further burns; the wider market has caught up with the chain and now lists that same figure as JUP's maximum supply. Jupiter does pay Active Staking Rewards of **50M JUP** a quarter to stakers who vote, with the current batch open for claims until **Oct 8 2026**, but those rewards are paid in staked form out of allocations the circulating count already holds. The circulating count held near **3,320M JUP** straight through the claim opening, and a genuine 50M release would have shown up as a **+1.5%** step. Two independent angles, one documentary and one measured, agree: the staking-rewards distribution is float-neutral.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Jupiter DAO voted the schedule out of existence. The net-zero emissions proposal closed on **Feb 22 2026** with roughly three quarters of the vote and did three things at once: it ended team vesting after the **Jan 27 2026** unlock, it settled the remaining founding-stakeholder allocation with an accelerated final tranche of **77.97M JUP** on **Feb 25 2026** in place of the remaining monthly unlocks, and it cancelled the Jupuary allocations outright, leaving them in custody with no release date. All three landed before this window opened. An unlock tracker now returns JUP as fully unlocked with the schedule ended and nothing upcoming through **Nov 30 2026**. This point deserves emphasis because listings elsewhere still republish the retired monthly vesting tranche of about **53.47M JUP** against fresh dates: that unlock is dead, and governance cancelled it faster than the trackers noticed. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** on flow even though the capacity is enormous — the three wallets holding essentially the entire uncirculated bucket were read at both ends of the window and none of them moved. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the plain reason that Jupiter has no bankruptcy estate and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new JUP goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, carries the entire buy side at **34.42M JUP** over the last 90 days. The Litterbox Trust routes **50%** of Jupiter's on-chain revenue into open-market JUP purchases and locks what it buys for three years. This build measured the row directly on the Solana chain rather than from any dashboard: the trust's JUP balance read **130.06M** at the window open on **Jun 2 2026** and **164.48M** at the window close on **Aug 31 2026**, with the balances either side of the window edge matching exactly, which is what proves the boundary is real rather than a truncated page of history. The arithmetic ties out against the documented fee split. Jupiter's revenue over the same 90 days was about **$15.54M**, so a documented half-share would be **$7.77M**, which at the window's mean JUP price of **$0.1965** implies **39.55M JUP** — against **34.42M** actually measured on-chain, or **87%** of the documented share. The quoted 50% is being realised, not merely authorised, and the shortfall is execution timing rather than a different rate.

The half-share is a share of Jupiter's **revenue**, not of its gross fees, and reading it off the gross line would overstate the buyback by several times. On pace, the buyback ran at about **0.38M JUP a day** across the window and has been accelerating inside it — one widely-reported single-day figure of roughly **0.20M JUP** on **Jul 26 2026** against about **1.51M JUP** on **Aug 26 2026**. That acceleration is revenue variation rather than a change in the mechanism, so the trailing-90-day average is the conservative forward predictor and gives **34.42M JUP** for the next 90 days. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and this build checked both surfaces a burn could show up on: the unspendable address that would collect burned JUP holds effectively nothing at either end of the window, and total supply did not move. Holders did vote once, in **Nov 2025**, to destroy about **134M JUP** out of the trust, but that was a one-off outside this window. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**, because no discretionary open-market buying is disclosed outside the automatic revenue buyback. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well, because the trust's three-year lock is already counted inside Buy #1 and is not booked twice.

## Foundation and overhang

Jupiter's overhang is the largest single risk in this reading. Total supply is **6,862.43M JUP** and circulating supply is **3,320.31M JUP**, so roughly **3,542M JUP** — more than the entire float — sits outside the market with no published release date. This build located it rather than estimating it: three wallets hold essentially all of it, at **1,700.0M JUP**, **1,682.7M JUP** and **159.9M JUP**, and all three read identical at both ends of the 90-day window. In allocation terms that bucket is the cancelled Jupuary reserve, the strategic reserve and wider community allocation, the halted team allocation of about **428M JUP** and the settled founding-stakeholder pool of about **82M JUP**. Separately, the Litterbox Trust itself holds **164.48M JUP**, which the market still counts inside the float; that balance is read straight off the chain from its JUP account, address **DpVH8xQZ4aapxwZ6KW9nuEUs9zEePa8HQvXny9Ajj93T**, on a 24-hour cycle, while the reserve wallets are re-read on each rebuild. None of these carries a value today, because enumeration is about scope rather than forecast. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How JUP compares to other DeFi application tokens

JUP is not a Layer 1 and should not be read like one. A Solana or an Ethereum pays a continuous, protocol-encoded issuance to validators — a permanent sell row that no vote can fully switch off, only tune. Jupiter has no such row at all: the mint authority is renounced, so the entire question of protocol inflation for JUP is closed, permanently, in a way that no proof-of-stake Layer 1 can match. Where a Layer 1's deflation depends on a fee burn racing its own issuance, JUP's starting point is a supply that mathematically cannot grow.

The closer structural analogue is the exchange-token model: a business that earns fees and recycles a fixed share into its own token. The difference that matters is destination. An exchange token running a quarterly buyback-and-burn destroys the coins, and the burn shows up in every supply count on earth, so the framework and the monitor agree. Jupiter buys and **locks** instead, for three years, into the Litterbox Trust. Economically the float effect is the same — **34.42M JUP** cannot be sold by anybody for three years — but a supply count cannot see it, which is precisely why the JUP reading carries a **0.98 percentage point** gap where an exchange token running the same size buyback-and-burn would carry none.

The third comparison is to app tokens with no buyback at all, where the entire deflation case rests on unlocks eventually finishing. Jupiter is past that point on the sell side but not on the overhang: with **3,542M JUP** uncirculated against a **3,320.31M JUP** float, the governance decision to keep those tokens parked is the load-bearing assumption in this whole reading. A vote could reverse it, and the framework would book the outflow the moment it happened.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the quarterly staking-rewards claim window closes on **Oct 8 2026**, after which unclaimed JUP returns to the treasury — float-neutral on our reading, but the point at which a change in the funding source would first show up. Second, a proposal to raise the Litterbox Trust's share of revenue from **50%** to **70%**, posted **May 30 2026**, remains discussion-stage with no vote as of **Aug 31 2026**; if it passes, the buyback row re-bases upward by about two fifths. Third, a separate proposal posted **Jul 31 2026** asks Jupiter to burn a slice of every buyback instead of sending all of it to the trust; if that passes, the monitor gap on this page closes by itself, because burned coins leave every supply count. Fourth, the Litterbox Trust now holds **164.48M JUP**, more than the **134M** the DAO voted to burn in **Nov 2025** — a repeat burn vote is the single largest deflationary catalyst available to Jupiter. Fifth, the buyback is a share of revenue, so Jupiter's trading volume is the real variable: the pace rose sharply through August, and a sustained revenue decline would pull the next-90-day figure down with it.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Jupiter (JUP) as **deflationary by structural buyback**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **34.42M JUP** bought and locked over 90 days, a net of **−1.04%** on a **3,320.31M JUP** float, projected at **−1.04%** for the next 90 days. The mechanism is unusually clean — a renounced mint authority means new JUP is impossible, and a governance vote cancelled the vesting schedule rather than merely pausing it — so the entire reading rests on the buy side continuing. The key risk is the **3,542M JUP** of uncirculated supply, more than the float itself, parked by governance decision rather than by code. And the ceiling is now real in both directions: total supply of **6,862.43M JUP** is a hard maximum that can only fall, while the buyback's size is capped by Jupiter's revenue, not by ambition.
