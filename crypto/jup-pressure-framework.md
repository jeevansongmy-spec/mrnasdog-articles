**TL;DR.** Jupiter added **zero** new JUP to the market over the last 90 days, while the Litterbox Trust spent half of the protocol's on-chain revenue buying **31.75M JUP** on the open market and locking it away for three years. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so total supply of **6,862.43M** can only fall. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads JUP at **−0.96% net** over the last 90 days on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP** — but the Jupiter buyback is cooling with revenue, and on the current run rate the next 90 days project to **−0.63%**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 17 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **JUP at −0.96% net**: **0 JUP** of sell pressure against **31.75M JUP** of buy pressure on a circulating base of **3,320.31M JUP**. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **+0.19%**, a gap of about **1.14 percentage points**, which is far beyond the framework's half-point tolerance, so a **monitor-gap chip ships** on the JUP overview. The gap is structural, not an error: the Litterbox Trust buyback buys JUP on the open market and locks it, but the supply monitor still counts those coins inside the circulating float, so it reads roughly flat while the framework credits the **31.75M JUP** the trust removed. This is the classic **buyback-and-hold** gap. The honest characterisation of Jupiter today is **deflationary by structural buyback with a switched-off sell side — but with the buy side cooling**.

## Sell pressure: where new JUP comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the single most important fact about Jupiter's tokenomics in 2026. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** and permanently so. JUP is a Solana token whose mint authority reads null on-chain — there is no function anyone, including the Jupiter team, can call to create a new JUP. Total supply began at a **10B** genesis cap, was cut to **7B** by the community supply reduction, and now reads **6,862.43M JUP** after roughly **137.57M** of cumulative burns. From here the number can only go down. Jupiter does pay Active Staking Rewards of **50M JUP** a quarter to stakers, with the latest batch opening for claiming on **Jul 8 2026**, but the DAO's own emissions package states those coins are drawn from previously unclaimed airdrop tokens the circulating count already includes. The circulating count held near **3,320M** straight through that claim window, which is exactly what a float-neutral distribution looks like.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** because the Jupiter DAO voted it away. The "Going Green" net-zero emissions proposal went to a vote in **Feb 2026** and did three things at once: it halted team vesting after the **Jan 27 2026** unlock and replaced it with balance-sheet credits, it settled the remaining founding-stakeholder allocation through an accelerated final tranche of **77.97M JUP** on **Feb 25 2026**, and it postponed the Jupuary airdrop, sending **700M JUP** back to the community cold multisig. Unlock trackers now show JUP as fully unlocked with the vesting schedule ended, and no cliff falls between today and **Nov 15 2026**. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** on flow even though the capacity is enormous — nothing left any of those wallets in the window. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** for the plain reason that Jupiter has no bankruptcy estate and no court-ordered distribution.

## Buy pressure: where new JUP goes

Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, carries the entire buy side at **31.75M JUP** over the last 90 days. The Litterbox Trust routes **50%** of Jupiter's on-chain revenue into open-market JUP purchases and locks what it buys for three years. This build measured the row directly on-chain rather than from any dashboard: the trust's JUP balance read **125.03M** on **May 18 2026**, **138.19M** on **Jun 16 2026**, **149.44M** on **Jul 16 2026** and **156.78M** on **Aug 17 2026** — a clean **31.75M JUP** of accumulation with not one outflow. The arithmetic ties out against the documented fee split: at the window's average JUP price those purchases cost about **$6.0M**, implying roughly **$12.1M** of on-chain revenue at a half-share, which matches Jupiter's measured revenue run rate. The quoted half-share is being realised, not merely authorised.

The important detail this quarter is the **pace**. The Jupiter buyback rose through June, peaking near **0.55M JUP a day** as trading volume spiked, then cooled to about **0.23M a day** by late July and August as revenue fell back. Because the buyback is a fixed **50%** share of revenue and that carve-out has not changed, this is revenue decay rather than a policy change. Projecting the current run rate forward gives roughly **21.05M JUP** for the next 90 days, or **−0.63% net** — still deflationary, but no longer near the −1% mark. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero**: Jupiter did burn roughly **130M JUP** from the trust after a vote in **Nov 2025**, but that was a one-off, and today's buyback holds rather than destroys — total supply has not moved since. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**: no discretionary open-market buying is disclosed outside the automatic revenue buyback. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero** as well, because JUP governance staking carries only a seven-day cooldown and the trust's three-year lock is already counted inside Buy #1 rather than booked twice.

## Foundation and overhang

JUP's overhang is the largest single risk in this reading. Roughly **3,542M JUP** — total supply of **6,862.43M** less circulating of **3,320.31M** — sits outside the market with no published release schedule. Four buckets account for it. The community cold multisig holds the **700M JUP** Jupuary allocation that the net-zero vote returned rather than cancelled outright, with the original snapshot preserved so a future DAO could still distribute it. A four-of-seven team cold multisig, the strategic reserve, and the wider DAO and community allocation hold the rest. None of them is on a calendar, which is precisely why the framework books Sell #3 at **zero** today: holding tokens is not the same as releasing them, and the circulating count has held near **3,320M** through the window.

The fourth tracked holding is the Litterbox Trust itself, now **156.78M JUP** — about **4.7%** of circulating supply, bought with real Jupiter revenue. It is read by chain RPC on every rebuild, and it has only ever grown. Because the trust holds rather than burns, that stack is a reversible overhang: the DAO showed in **Nov 2025** that it is willing to vote on what happens to it, and burning, redeploying and selling are all live options. If the trust's balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh. The same trigger applies to the community cold multisig, the team cold multisig, the strategic reserve and the DAO allocation.

## How JUP compares to other revenue-buyback DeFi tokens

JUP's closest structural relatives are not other Solana assets but tokens whose supply story is a revenue-funded buyback. Exchange tokens that run quarterly buy-and-burn programmes convert revenue into permanent destruction: the coins leave the ledger, total supply falls, and every observer agrees on the number. Jupiter sits one step short of that today. The revenue conversion is arguably stronger — it runs continuously at **50%** of on-chain revenue rather than quarterly at management discretion, a far higher share than the low-double-digit fee carve-outs common among rival Solana DEX tokens — but the destination is a trust wallet, not a burn address. The effect on the tradable float today is identical; the durability is not, and that is the trade the Jupiter DAO keeps revisiting.

Against uncapped, emission-driven DeFi tokens and continuous-issuance Layer-1s, JUP looks unusually clean. A renounced mint authority, a vesting schedule that governance closed rather than merely finished, a supply already cut from **10B** to **6,862.43M**, and a buy side funded by real trading revenue is a combination very few tokens can show at once. But the genuine risk is exactly the flip side of that revenue link. The buyback is a fixed share of fees, so a revenue slowdown is a buyback slowdown — and that is what the last 90 days show, the deflation easing from **−0.96%** toward **−0.63%** as volume cooled. On the other side, the **3,542M JUP** outside circulation is larger than the float itself; a future governance decision could put a large share of it back into the market, and the framework would swing from deflationary to sharply positive in a single window.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the buyback pace itself: it has cooled from a June peak near **0.55M** to about **0.23M** JUP a day as revenue fell, and whether it stabilises or keeps sliding is what decides between a **−0.63%** quarter and a flatter one. Second, the Litterbox Trust balance, **156.78M JUP** on **Aug 17 2026** and never yet fallen — any decline is the first real sell signal this ledger would register. Third, the proposal posted on **May 30 2026** to raise the buyback allocation from **50%** to **70%**, still discussion-stage with no vote scheduled; if it passes, the buy row rises by roughly **40%** at the same revenue. Fourth, any fresh move to burn the trust rather than hold it — that would not change the float effect but would close most of our monitor gap permanently. Fifth, the next quarterly Active Staking Rewards cycle around **Oct 2026**, which should again be float-neutral but is worth confirming against the circulating count.

## Summary

Jupiter is one of the few large tokens whose sell ledger is genuinely empty. The JUP mint authority is renounced, so no new JUP can ever be created; the Feb 2026 net-zero vote halted team vesting and postponed the Jupuary airdrop; and the only measurable flow in the last 90 days was the **31.75M JUP** the Litterbox Trust buyback removed from the market and locked for three years, a framework reading of **−0.96% net**. But the buyback is a fixed share of falling revenue, so its pace has cooled from a June peak, and the same mechanism projected forward gives **−0.63%** for the next 90 days — still shrinking, just more slowly. The key risk is not inflation but discretion: **3,542M JUP** sits outside circulation with no schedule, the buyback holds instead of burning, and both are governance decisions rather than protocol guarantees. Read JUP as deflationary today, cooling, on a mechanism the Jupiter DAO could change tomorrow.
