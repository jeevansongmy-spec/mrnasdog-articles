Orca creates no new ORCA. The Solana exchange token's on-chain supply held at **75.00M** across the last 90 days, its vesting schedule ran out in **2024**, and the one pool of tokens still outside the market — the DAO's **14.20M** community treasury — has not moved since **Mar 17 2026**. Against zero sell pressure, a trading-fee buyback bought roughly **0.36M ORCA** on the open market and dropped it into the xORCA staking vault, so the Pressure Framework reads ORCA at about **−0.59%** net on a circulating base of **~60.8M ORCA**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.09%** for the same window, a gap of **1.69 percentage points** that traces to how that monitor derives supply rather than to any token movement. ORCA is a **fully-unlocked exchange token whose float only shrinks**, at a pace set entirely by trading volume.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Jul 25 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **ORCA at about −0.59% net**: sell pressure of **0 ORCA** against buy pressure of **~0.36M ORCA** on a circulating base of **~60.8M ORCA**. Our supply monitor reads **+1.09%** for the same period — a gap of **1.69 percentage points**, outside the half-point tolerance, so a monitor-gap flag ships with this page. The gap is not a disagreement about Orca's mechanics. The ORCA token's circulating figure is defined by exactly two readings: the on-chain supply of **74,999,550** and the balance of the single community treasury account holding **14,200,768**. The first did not change, and the second has only twelve transactions in its entire life, the newest dated **Mar 17 2026**. Subtract one from the other at both ends of the window and the float is flat. ORCA is best labelled **deflationary by structural buyback on a fully-unlocked float** — no issuance to outrun, and a bid funded by the exchange's own fees.

## Sell pressure: where new ORCA comes from

It does not come from anywhere. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and for ORCA that is a property of what the token is. ORCA is the governance token of Orca, a concentrated-liquidity exchange built on Solana; it is not a chain's native coin, so there is no block reward, no staking emission and no validator subsidy paid in ORCA. An on-chain read of the ORCA token returned **74,999,550.16** tokens, matching the **75M** total supply the project documents. The one caveat worth naming: the authority to mint more ORCA has never been renounced. It still sits with the DAO's community treasury wallet. That wallet was walked transaction by transaction across the window — **May 29 2026** through **Jul 24 2026** — and not one of its transactions touches an ORCA balance. Capacity exists; issuance does not.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero**, and permanently so. Orca allocated its supply across a token treasury, team, private sale, advisory, contributor grant and three trader and liquidity-provider buckets, and every one of those schedules completed in **2024**. The unlock trackers agree: ORCA is fully unlocked, with no future cliff on the calendar. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is also **zero**, but for a different reason, and it is the row worth watching. The DAO's community treasury holds **14.20M ORCA**, the only meaningful block of tokens outside the market, and it has no published release schedule. Its observed history is sporadic rather than scheduled: **0.20M** released on **Oct 1 2025**, then nothing until **Mar 17 2026**, when **0.64M** went out in a single day across two transfers. Gaps of five weeks and then five and a half months give no pattern to project from, and nothing at all fired inside this window, so the row is booked at zero and monitored. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: no estate, no trustee distribution, no expiring lock-up contract touches ORCA.

## Buy pressure: where new ORCA goes

Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **~0.36M ORCA**, and it is the only number on this page that moves. Orca routes **12%** of every trading fee to the protocol as revenue. Since a governance Council update on **Jan 13 2026** raised the share from **20%** to **40%**, forty cents of every protocol dollar is spent buying ORCA on the open market. Protocol fee revenue measured across the window came to **$1.18M**; forty percent of that is **$0.47M** of purchases, which at the window's average ORCA price of **$1.32** is about **0.36M ORCA**. A second reading, built from the exchange's own published pool parameters — a volume-weighted fee of **0.0582%** and a protocol share of **13%** applied to **$18.28B** of trading volume — lands at **0.42M ORCA**, seventeen percent apart. The lower figure ships.

The destination matters as much as the quantum. Bought-back ORCA is deposited into the xORCA staking vault, which held **7.59M ORCA** against an xORCA supply of **4.78M** at the last read — a redemption rate of **1.5887** ORCA per xORCA, and every point of that premium is buyback already delivered to stakers. The vault is readable on-chain and has only ever grown; the buyback does not burn ORCA, it re-parks it inside a contract with a seven-day exit. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **zero**, because Orca has no burn at all: fees are revenue, spent on buybacks and grants, and the on-chain supply did not fall by a single token. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **zero**. A **2025** vote authorised the DAO to deploy roughly **55,000 SOL** and **$400,000** of stablecoins into open-market ORCA purchases and validator staking, but no dated execution from that pot landed in this window, and the buying that did happen is the fee-funded programme already counted in Buy #1. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **zero**: the staking vault is the buyback destination, so booking it again would count the same tokens twice.

## Foundation and overhang

Four holdings sit under identified control, and the largest is the whole story. The **community ORCA treasury**, account `5ooCx5vKiV2ZxAEKNNHAJjAJ7BARfLUZPGvgiApZjgFD`, holds **14,200,768 ORCA**. It is the entire difference between the on-chain supply and the circulating float, it has no release schedule, and it is refreshed on every chain read. Beside it, a **climate fund** wallet holds **93,025 ORCA**, and four grant and operations wallets seeded by the **Oct 1 2025** and **Mar 17 2026** distributions hold about **0.62M ORCA** between them — those four are already inside the circulating count, so they are watched for selling rather than for dilution. The xORCA staking vault's **7.59M ORCA** is the buyback destination and is read the same way.

The mint authority is the fifth item, and it has no number. It has never been renounced, and it belongs to the same wallet that owns the community treasury, so the DAO can in principle create ORCA beyond the documented **75M**. Nothing has been minted, and the window shows no attempt, but a fixed supply held by convention is not the same as a fixed supply held by code. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and for the mint authority specifically, a rise in the on-chain total is the trigger.

## How ORCA compares to other exchange tokens

Against the exchange tokens that run quarterly buybacks funded from a corporate balance sheet, ORCA sits at the opposite end of the transparency scale. Those programmes are decided in a boardroom, announced after the fact, and sized from revenue no outsider can audit; the ORCA buyback is a published percentage of a published fee, executed against an on-chain vault whose balance anyone can read. The trade-off is direction: a discretionary buyback can be scaled up when management wants a signal, while ORCA's scales only with trading volume. A quiet quarter on Solana shrinks the bid automatically.

Against other decentralised-exchange governance tokens, the difference is the vesting cliff. Most exchange tokens launched in the same cohort are still working through multi-year investor and team unlocks, which means their real dilution is set by a calendar and not by usage. ORCA finished that calendar in **2024**. What is left is a pure usage token: the only sell pressure that could ever appear is a discretionary treasury release, and the only buy pressure is fee revenue. That makes ORCA far simpler to read than an uncapped continuous-emission chain, where issuance runs whether anyone trades or not, and simpler than a halving-model coin, where the buy side is absent entirely.

The comparison that flatters ORCA least is against tokens that burn rather than accumulate. A fee burn removes supply permanently; ORCA's buyback moves supply into a staking vault that any holder can exit after seven days. Both count as buy pressure, and both tighten the float today, but only one is irreversible. On the framework's reading that distinction shows up not in the **−0.59%** figure but in the overhang list, where the vault's **7.59M ORCA** is tracked rather than written off.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the community treasury account — **14.20M ORCA**, untouched since **Mar 17 2026**. A single transfer out of it is the only event that can turn this page positive, and it needs no vote schedule to happen. Second, the buyback share itself: the Council moved it from **20%** to **40%** on **Jan 13 2026** without a token-holder vote, so the same body can move it back. Third, trading volume, which was **$18.28B** across this window and produced **$1.18M** of protocol revenue; the buyback is a fixed fraction of that, so a halving of volume halves the only buy row on the page. Fourth, the permissioned pools for tokenised real-world assets that Orca launched on **May 27 2026** — those fees feed the same buyback, and a meaningful ramp would show up as a larger Buy #1 in the next rebuild. Fifth, the on-chain token supply itself, which must stay at **75.00M**; any rise is the mint authority being used for the first time.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ORCA at about **−0.59%** net supply change over 90 days and projects the same for the next 90. The structure behind that number is unusually clean: Orca issues no new ORCA, its vesting finished in **2024**, and **40%** of the protocol's **12%** fee share buys roughly **0.36M ORCA** a quarter on the open market. The key risk is not dilution but discretion — the DAO's **14.20M** treasury has no release schedule, and the mint authority behind the **75M** supply has never been renounced, so ORCA's scarcity rests on governance restraint rather than on code. The ceiling on the buy side is equally plain: this buyback is a fraction of trading fees, so ORCA's float shrinks exactly as fast as Orca is used, and no faster.
