JasmyCoin is an Ethereum **ERC-20** that cannot mint a token and cannot burn one — and this time we proved it from the published contract source rather than from a flat supply figure. JasmyCoin mints once, inside its constructor, and its mint and burn routines are internal with no function anywhere able to call them; there is no owner, no minter role and no upgrade path. Total supply read exactly **50,000,000,000 JASMY** at both ends of the 90-day window, and **49.44B** of it already trades. All eight Pressure Framework rows for JASMY are **zero**, so the framework reads **0.00% net** for both the trailing and the forward window against a monitor of **−0.10%** — a gap of **0.10 percentage points**, inside tolerance, so **no monitor-gap chip** is attached.

JasmyCoin is **flat by construction, not flat by coincidence** — a fixed-cap ERC-20 with no issuance mechanism and no destruction mechanism.

## Sell pressure: where new JASMY comes from

Nowhere. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero** because JasmyCoin has no protocol inflation to have. The verified source shows a plain ERC-20 whose constructor calls the internal mint routine once and whose entire external surface is transfers, approvals and the read-only name, symbol, decimals, total supply and balance calls. There is no mint function, no minter role, no owner and no proxy behind which an implementation could be swapped, so "never minted" and "cannot mint" are the same statement for JASMY.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**. No vesting or escrow contract holds JASMY at all: we probed the two issuer-era custody contracts for the usual lock interface — beneficiary, release time, cliff, duration, released — and every call came back empty, so there is no schedule to draw down and no escrow whose realised outflow could differ from a published calendar. The original JasmyCoin distribution ran as ordinary wallet transfers, its weekly tranches ended in **Nov 2023**, and the last isolated release was **Jan 16 2025**.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **zero** — and this is the row that earned the work. One reserve wallet holds **555.0M JASMY**, which is precisely the whole non-circulating balance, matched to seven decimals. It held the same figure at both ends of the window, but a level balance has several innocent-looking causes, so we swept its transfer log as well: **36** lifetime transfers, none of them inside this window, and the forwarding wallet it once used sits at zero. Meanwhile **3.25B JASMY** genuinely did move in the window — **2,875,000,000** leaving a custody contract on **Jul 15 2026** and **378.7M** more two days later. That is the kind of event that looks like a hidden unlock, so we classified every address on both sides of it by what else it holds: all of them carry the same wide multi-asset exchange basket, and the receiving wallet holds stablecoins and 103 ETH beside its JASMY. It is exchange custody being reshuffled, not supply leaving the issuer. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: JasmyCoin has no estate, no trustee and no court distribution, because its issuer is an operating Japanese company.

## Buy pressure: where new JASMY goes

Also nowhere, and for a symmetrical reason. Buy #2, protocol fee burn, is **zero** because burning JASMY is impossible: the source has no external burn function, and an ordinary transfer to the null address is rejected outright by the code, so total supply cannot fall by any route. The standard dead address holds **315 JASMY** in total across the token's entire life, unchanged at both ends of the window. JasmyChain, the project's own Arbitrum Orbit layer-2, does burn its whole base fee — but that destroys bridged units on that network and can never reach the canonical Ethereum supply, and the chain has produced about **6,400 blocks** and **13,000 transactions** since launch, so the quantum is a fraction of a single JASMY.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: there is no buyback contract and no repurchase programme disclosed by the issuer. A widely-shared "buyback and burn" offer circulating under an unofficial third-party account is refuted by the chain itself — a real buyback-and-burn would have moved total supply off **50,000,000,000** at least once, and it never has. Buy #3, foundation buy, is **zero**: the only accumulating balance we measured in the window was **213.0M JASMY**, and that address holds more than a thousand different tokens alongside **1,654 ETH**, which makes it exchange float rather than a buyer. Buy #4, new long-term lock, is **zero**: no lockup was deployed, no staking removes JASMY from circulation, and the layer-2 gateway — the one lock-shaped destination that exists — corresponds to a total layer-2 supply of about **1.2M JASMY**, a rounding error against a 50B cap. Bridging moves custody; it does not remove supply.

## Foundation and overhang

JasmyCoin has exactly one identified issuer-controlled overhang, and it is small and old. The reserve wallet holds **555.0M JASMY**, about **1.11%** of the 50B cap, and it is the entire difference between the cap and the circulating figure the market reads. Its release pattern died in stages: weekly tranches through **Nov 20 2023**, a single **95M** release on **Sep 27 2024**, a final **50M** on **Jan 16 2025**, and silence for nineteen months. Holding tokens is not the same as releasing them, so the row stays at zero. The rail it used to forward tokens holds nothing. Everything else large on the JasmyCoin holder list — including four wallets of **3.5B to 3.6B** each that were batch-funded in Oct and Dec 2025 and have not moved a unit since — belongs to exchanges, not to the issuer, and exchange balances belong to depositors. We re-read this wallet against the chain every refresh: if the reserve's balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How JASMY compares to other fixed-supply corporate tokens

JasmyCoin belongs to the smallest and quietest class in the framework: capped tokens with no mechanism on either side of the ledger. Most capped tokens are only capped on the sell side. An exchange token with a quarterly auto-burn is capped and shrinking, so it scores above JASMY because real supply is destroyed. A vote-locked DeFi token is capped and often still emitting, because the cap governs the mint schedule rather than forbidding it.

The nearer comparison is the group of tokens whose supply looks frozen but is not. Several assets in this catalogue read identical supply at both ends of a quarter while a claim function sat live behind the number, with emissions accruing unclaimed inside the contract; others keep a mint authority that has simply never been called, or an issue path behind a timelock. Those are dormant mints, not absent ones, and the difference is the whole point of reading source rather than supply. JasmyCoin is on the other side of that line — the routine that could create tokens exists only as internal code with no caller, and there is no owner or proxy to add one. That earns the permanent tag on both its issuance and its burn rows, which most capped tokens do not deserve.

The trade-off is on the buy side. A token that cannot burn also cannot use a burn to defend its float, and a token with no protocol revenue routed into a buyback has nothing structural bidding for it. JASMY is not deflationary and never will be; the ceiling on its inflation score is the flat band, because a fixed but unshrinking supply tops out there. The layer-2 launch in **Jan 2026** gave JASMY a gas-token role, which is the first mechanism in its history that could ever lock or consume units — but at **1.2M JASMY** of layer-2 supply it is not yet a supply mechanic, only the option of one.
