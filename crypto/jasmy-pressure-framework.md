Paying for ecosystem services or moving tokens between networks does not create additional canonical JASMY. There is no block reward or validator-emission curve to extrapolate for this token.

A reconstructed chart is not an executed lock contract or a commitment to distribute on those dates. ## Buy pressure: where new JASMY goes

Programmatic buyback is **0 JASMY**: no issuer-confirmed programme and measurable purchase-and-removal flow were established. A promotional claim is not enough to forecast a purchase, and a purchase would still need a destination that actually removes counted supply. Merely moving tokens into another ordinary holder’s wallet would not establish destruction.

Protocol fee burn is **0**. The Ethereum token’s dead-address balance remained approximately **315 JASMY**, with no increase over the quarter. Its total-supply balance also remained **50B**. Those checks answer different questions: sending tokens to an inaccessible address can remove them from use without changing the contract’s total-supply field, so a flat total alone would not prove the absence of destruction. Here, neither checked surface changed.

JasmyChain uses JASMY for gas, but paying gas is not automatically burning it. Its configured fee recipients receive the payment, and a checked transaction’s recipient balance increased by exactly the gas charge. Bridge deposits likewise back spendable representations on another network. They are not a second issuance to add, nor a permanent removal to subtract. Foundation buys are **0** without a verified qualifying purchase, and new long-term locks are **0** without evidence of an additional exclusion from circulating supply.

## Foundation and overhang

The key issuer-controlled balance is the **555.0M JASMY reserve**, approximately **1.11% of the fixed supply**. It accounts for the gap between the **50B** total and approximately **49.445B** circulating. That makes it the important remaining supply boundary: a real distribution from this excluded reserve can increase the amount circulating even though total issuance cannot rise.

The reserve has no confirmed release date. Its balance and outgoing transfers should be checked daily, while corporate announcements and distribution policy should be reviewed every two weeks. If the reserve’s balance falls and tokens enter circulation, the released amount enters Sell #3 at the next refresh; an internal relocation that preserves the exclusion would not.

Large holders are not automatically company treasuries. Exchange custody and unidentified investor balances already lie within the circulating base and should not be relabeled as unreleased issuer allocations merely because they are large. JasmyChain’s fee-recipient balances represent operating receipts in bridged units, not another pool of unissued Ethereum tokens. The practical risk is an unscheduled release from the identified reserve, not the creation of another supply ceiling.

## How JASMY compares to other fixed-supply tokens

JASMY and FLOKI both have fixed token issuance, but fixed issuance does not guarantee the same supply trend. FLOKI’s current ledger includes verified burns from early staking exits and fee-funded purchases. JASMY’s checked reserve and destruction surfaces are unchanged, so its supply reading stays flat. A marketing description such as scarce or capped cannot substitute for an actual removal mechanism.

Ethereum differs again: its validators receive new ETH and transaction base fees destroy ETH. That combination can produce growth or contraction as network conditions change. JASMY has no comparable ongoing issuance, and paying ETH to transfer the Ethereum token does not destroy JASMY. Its separate gas-token role on JasmyChain also requires checking where the fees go before treating activity as deflation.

Compared with a newly launched token whose majority allocation remains unreleased, JASMY has relatively little remaining circulation headroom: about **98.89%** of the fixed supply is already counted. That reduces the size of the identified remaining release relative to total supply. It does not establish decentralised ownership, stronger demand or a price floor, and the **555.0M** reserve still matters if its policy changes.
