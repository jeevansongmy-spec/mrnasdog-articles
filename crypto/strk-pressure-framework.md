## Sell pressure: where new STRK comes from

**Protocol inflation: 39.0M STRK.** Starknet pays its stakers in brand-new STRK. The coins are minted on Ethereum, where the STRK token lives, in blocks of 1.3M about every three days and sent to the staking rewards contract. We counted 30 such mints in the window, and the token's total supply rose by exactly 39.0M STRK, from 10.135B to 10.174B. The yearly rate follows a minting curve that grows with the square root of the share staked: it read about 151.8M STRK a year at the start of the window and about **159.7M** now, because staking grew from 1.42B to 1.57B STRK. We carry the measured 39.0M forward.

**Vesting unlocks: 381.0M STRK.** Early contributors and investors receive up to 127M STRK on the 15th of each month, a schedule that started at this size on Apr 15 2025 and ends on Mar 15 2027. Three of these unlocks fell inside the window (Jul 15, Aug 15 and Sep 15 2026) and three more fall inside the next 90 days (Oct 15, Nov 15 and Dec 15 2026), so the row is **381.0M** both ways. About 762M STRK is still locked after September.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks: 300.0M STRK.** A team reserve wallet, funded straight from the original STRK supply, sent out 100M STRK on Jul 28 2026 and 200M on Sep 10 2026. The coins went through two middle wallets and across the bridge onto Starknet, where the receiving accounts passed most of them on. This wallet has released coins seven times in the last 12 months, 849.6M STRK in all, so for the next 90 days we carry its yearly pace, about **209.5M**.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy: 0.** No estate, trustee or court case holds STRK. The only coins still locked are the contributor and investor coins on the monthly calendar above.

## Buy pressure: where new STRK goes

**Programmatic buyback: 0.** No contract, foundation or company buys STRK back. Starknet fees are paid in STRK, but they go to the network's operator rather than to a buyback.

**Protocol fee burn: 0.** Fees are not destroyed. We checked both ways: the dead addresses on Ethereum and on Starknet held the same tiny balances at both ends of the window, and the total supply only went up, by exactly the staking mint. A fee burn has been discussed in the Starknet community, but none is live.

**Foundation buy: 0.** No announcement and no on-chain flow in the window shows the foundation or any team wallet buying STRK.

**New long-term lock: 0.** Staking grew by about 145M STRK and a liquid staking option opened on Jul 28 2026. But staked STRK can be withdrawn in 7 days and still counts as circulating, so a bigger stake takes nothing out of the float. It only raises the staking mint above.

## Foundation and overhang

Four team-controlled piles could still reach the market. The team reserve wallet that released 300M STRK this window still holds **950M STRK**. A team safe holds **1.0B STRK**; it sent 1.5B into the reserve wallet on Apr 21 2026 and did not move in this window. A second team safe holds about **83.6M STRK** and has not moved. And about **762M STRK** of contributor and investor coins is still locked on the monthly calendar. There is no buyback wallet, because there is no buyback. We read the three wallets on-chain at every rebuild and check the calendar by hand. If any of these balances falls between rebuilds, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next rebuild.

## How STRK compares to other Ethereum Layer 2 tokens

STRK belongs to the class of Ethereum Layer 2 tokens launched with a large team and investor allocation that unlocks over several years. Like most tokens of this class, its biggest supply source is not the protocol but the calendar: 381.0M STRK of unlocks in 90 days is about ten times the staking mint. What sets STRK apart is that it also mints new coins. Most Layer 2 tokens of its era are fixed at the supply they launched with, while Starknet pays stakers with fresh STRK, so its chain total is already above the 10B it started with.

The other difference is the buy side. Starknet fees are paid in STRK, which gives the token real use, but none of those fees are burned or used to buy STRK back. Ethereum itself burns part of every fee; STRK has nothing like it today. So every new or released coin stays in the market, and the net reading equals the gross sell side: 720.0M in, 0 out.

Compared with a hard-capped chain whose supply only changes on a fixed schedule, STRK has three moving parts: a calendar that ends on Mar 15 2027, a reserve wallet that releases when its holders decide, and a staking mint that grows as more STRK is staked. The first is known in advance; the other two are not.
