Starknet added about **136.4M STRK** to the market in the 90 days to **Jul 25 2026** and took none of it back. **36.4M** of that is newly minted staking reward; **100.0M** is the Starknet treasury spending tokens it already held. The vesting lock itself released **nothing** — its entire remaining unlock calendar was drawn out in a single **1,500M** transfer on **Apr 21 2026**, five days before the window opened. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **+2.02% net**; our inflation monitor reads **+15.19%**, a **13.16 percentage point** gap, because the monitor books the unlock calendar while the framework books what actually moved.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Jul 25 2026 the framework reads **STRK at +2.02% net** — **136.4M STRK** of sell pressure against **zero** buy pressure, on a circulating base of about **6.75B**. Our inflation monitor reads **+15.19%** for the same window, a gap of **13.16 percentage points**, far outside the half-point tolerance, so a data-conflict chip ships on the STRK overview page. The deep walk explains the gap precisely: the monitor's supply series moves in discrete jumps on the 1st and 16th of each month and sits perfectly flat in between, and it did not move at all on **Apr 21 2026** when 1,500M STRK genuinely left the Starknet lock contract — it is tracking an unlock calendar, not the on-chain float. Starknet is **one-way inflationary on the active float**: there is no buyback, no fee burn and no lock absorbing supply, so every STRK the treasury spends is a permanent addition to the tradable stock.

## Sell pressure: where new STRK comes from

**Protocol inflation** is booked at **36.4M STRK**. Starknet pays its stakers in newly minted STRK under a community-approved minting curve that scales with how much of the supply is staked, held under a **1.6%** annual ceiling. The measurement is direct: the STRK token contract on Ethereum reported **10,106.6M** total supply on Apr 26 2026 and **10,143.0M** on Jul 25 2026, a rise of 36.4M, or about **1.45%** a year. None of that minting happens on the Starknet rollup itself — the rollup-side STRK balance is an exact mirror of the Ethereum bridge escrow, so the Ethereum contract is the whole story. Inverting the minting curve puts the implied staked share near **13%** of supply, which lines up with Starknet's published staking figures.

**Vesting unlocks** read **zero**, and this is the finding that separates STRK from every aggregator's reading of it. The on-chain lock contract holding Starknet's vesting allocation sat at **2,501.3M STRK** from July 2025 all the way to Apr 15 2026, then released **1,500M in one transfer on Apr 21 2026** into a treasury wallet, and has held a flat **1,001.3M** ever since. At the published rate of roughly 127M a month, that single draw covers about **11.8 months** — effectively the entire remaining vesting calendar through March 2027, pulled forward into one transaction. The unlock trackers still print a cliff on the 15th of every month, but those dates no longer take STRK out of the lock, so booking them as fresh sell pressure would invent supply that does not exist.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks** carries the real flow: **100.0M STRK**. Six identified Starknet treasury addresses hold **2,715.6M STRK** between them, and their combined balance fell from **2,815.6M** to **2,715.6M** across the window. Two independent methods agree exactly on the figure: differencing those six balances at both window ends returns 100.0M, and scanning every outgoing transfer from all six addresses across the window returns exactly one event — **100.0M STRK on May 10 2026** — which was then bridged onto the Starknet rollup to fund ecosystem and grant spending. That is a marked slowdown from the 254.5M, 242.7M and 219.6M the same wallets released in the three preceding quarters. **Long-term locked or bankruptcy** is zero — no bankruptcy estate holds or distributes STRK.

## Buy pressure: where new STRK goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows read **zero**, and that is the defining fact about STRK's supply picture. **Programmatic buyback** is zero: Starknet routes no share of sequencer revenue into repurchasing STRK, and no such programme has been proposed with a quantum attached. **Protocol fee burn** is zero: transactions on Starknet are paid for in STRK, but the fee flows to the sequencer rather than to a burn address, and both burn addresses held identical balances at each end of the window. The **v0.14.3** fee redesign that reached mainnet on **Jun 22 2026** introduced a dynamic gas base fee and cut the gas target by 30%, which prepares the ground for an Ethereum-style burn, but none is live. **Foundation buy** is zero — no open-market accumulation was disclosed or observed. **New long-term lock** is zero: staked STRK can be withdrawn after a short exit delay, so a growing staked balance is not supply taken off the market, and no new lockup contract with an announced size was deployed. With nothing on the buy side, Starknet's net reading is simply its gross sell reading.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled overhang for STRK is large and, unusually, fully readable on-chain. Four buckets make it up. The **vesting lock contract** still holds **1,001.3M STRK**, unchanged since Apr 21 2026. The **distribution wallet** that received that April draw holds **1,250.0M STRK** and is the wallet actively feeding Starknet's ecosystem pipeline — it is the source of the 100.0M that moved in May 2026. Four **reserve wallets** hold a combined **464.3M** and have not moved in a year. Together that is **2,715.6M STRK** — roughly 40% of the circulating float — sitting in addresses the framework reads at every rebuild. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh; that is exactly how this quarter's 100.0M was captured, and it is why STRK's overhang is a measured flow rather than an unquantified risk.

## How STRK compares to other Ethereum rollup chains

STRK belongs to the class of **Ethereum rollup tokens with a large genesis allocation and a multi-year unlock calendar** — the same structural family as the other major Layer-2 governance tokens. Members of that class share three features: a fixed genesis supply distributed heavily to early contributors, investors and a foundation; a vesting schedule that releases that supply over three to five years; and a token that is not required to secure the chain the way a Layer-1 staking asset is. That last point is why almost none of them run a meaningful buy-side mechanism, and STRK is no exception.

Where Starknet differs from most of its rollup peers is that it mints. A rollup token whose supply is fixed at genesis has a hard ceiling: once vesting completes, issuance stops forever. Starknet added staking with newly minted rewards, so STRK's supply grows past genesis — it stood at **10,143.0M** in July 2026 against a 10,000M genesis, and rises about **1.45%** a year. That is modest in absolute terms, but it means STRK has no true cap where a purely vesting-driven rollup token does. Set against a fee-burning smart-contract chain the contrast is sharper still: Ethereum burns base fees against its issuance and can run net-negative, while Starknet collects the same fees in STRK and hands them to the sequencer. Starknet's own fee redesign points toward a burn, and adding one would be the single largest structural change available to this reading.

The comparison that matters most is against an exchange token with a scheduled buyback. Those assets pair distribution with a company-funded repurchase, so the float can shrink even while allocations unlock. STRK has the distribution without the offset. On the framework's arithmetic that is the whole difference between a mixed verdict and a negative one: **136.4M STRK** of quarterly sell pressure would be manageable against a buyback of similar size, but against zero it compounds every quarter, and the readable overhang behind it is still **2,715.6M**.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things would move this reading. First, the **lock contract** — it holds 1,001.3M STRK and has been flat since Apr 21 2026; a second bulk draw would be visible immediately and would rebuild the overhang in the distribution wallet. Second, the **distribution wallet's 1,250.0M**: this quarter's 100.0M was well below the 220M-plus pace of the three quarters before, and a return to that pace roughly doubles the reading, so the pace of Starknet ecosystem and grant spending is the single largest swing factor. Third, a **fee burn** — the v0.14.3 upgrade of **Jun 22 2026** built the fee mechanics a burn would sit on, and any governance step that starts destroying sequencer fees would create Starknet's first genuine buy-side row. Fourth, the **minting curve**: the rate scales with the staked share, so a large rise in staking participation lifts the 36.4M figure toward the 1.6% ceiling. A separate forum post from **Jul 6 2026** proposing to cut STRK's maximum supply to 1B by burning treasury tokens has no vote, no quorum and no execution behind it, and changes nothing today.

## Summary

STRK is an Ethereum rollup token with no real cap and no buy side. Over the 90 days to **Jul 25 2026** Starknet added **136.4M STRK** to the market — **36.4M** newly minted for stakers and **100.0M** spent from treasury wallets — against **zero** buyback, **zero** burn and **zero** new lockup, for a net reading of **+2.02%**. The framework's disagreement with the monitor's **+15.19%** comes down to one verifiable fact: Starknet's vesting lock released nothing in the window because its entire remaining calendar was drawn in a single 1,500M transfer on **Apr 21 2026**, so the calendar and the float have stopped agreeing. The key risk is that the readable overhang is enormous — **2,715.6M STRK**, about 40% of the float, still sits in identified Starknet wallets — and nothing in the protocol removes supply to offset it.
