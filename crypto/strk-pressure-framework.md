Starknet's STRK token is inflationary on both axes at once, and the Pressure Framework reads it at **+7.44%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 22 2026**. **518.7M STRK** reached the market: **381M** from a vesting cliff that releases **127M STRK** on the 15th of every month until **Mar 15 2027**, **100M** out of a Starknet project wallet on **Jul 28 2026**, and **37.7M** of genuinely new coins minted to pay staking rewards. Buy pressure is **zero** on all four rows — Starknet runs no buyback, no burn, and no lock. On a circulating base of **6,975.5M STRK** the forward read is **+6.00%**. STRK is an uncapped token whose float is expanding on a published calendar with no offsetting mechanism of any kind.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 22 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **STRK at +7.44% net**: sell pressure of **518.7M STRK** against buy pressure of **zero**, on a circulating base of **6,975.5M STRK**. Our supply monitor reads **+10.75%** for the same window, a gap of **3.32 percentage points**, which is well outside the half-point tolerance and ships with a data-conflict flag. The gap has a specific, testable cause rather than a vague one: the monitor's float steps on the **1st** and the **16th** of every month and sits dead flat in between, which is the signature of a calendar rather than of coins moving. Meanwhile the two largest Starknet-controlled wallets, holding **2,151.3M STRK** between them, were either completely still or moved exactly once across the whole window. The framework books the coins it can see minted or traced; the monitor books every genesis pool the calendar frees. Starknet is best labelled inflationary on the active float by unlock schedule rather than by issuance.

## Sell pressure: where new STRK comes from

Before any STRK figure can be trusted, one question has to be settled: STRK exists on two chains at once. The main ledger is an Ethereum contract; the Starknet rollup runs its own STRK token, and the bridge holds Ethereum-side coins against every rollup-side coin. Add the two together and you double-count **3.75B STRK** — more than half the counted float. The test that settles it is the residual at **both** ends of the window, because a mirror leaves a constant offset while a missing leg leaves a moving one. Starknet's residual was **35,204 STRK** on **May 24 2026** and **35,186 STRK** on **Aug 22 2026** — a drift of eighteen coins across ninety days, which is withdrawals in flight and nothing else. So the Ethereum contract carries the whole supply, and every mint is measurable there.

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **37.7M STRK**, and it is measured rather than modelled. Supply on the Ethereum contract went from **10,117.0M** to **10,154.7M** across the window, and a full sweep of the mint log accounts for the difference exactly: **29** mints of precisely **1.3M STRK** each, roughly one every three days, every single one handed to Starknet's reward-supplier contract and pushed across the bridge to pay staking rewards. That is **1.51%** a year. Starknet's minting curve scales issuance with the square root of the staking rate against a governance-set ceiling of **4%**, so the realised mint is **37.7%** of the ceiling today — and it rises automatically as staking participation rises, which it has been doing since liquid staking launched on **Jul 29 2026**. Note what this rules out: STRK cannot be tagged as a permanently fixed supply. The minter is live, it sits behind an upgradeable proxy, and the **10B** figure most price sites still publish as a maximum is the genesis number, not a cap.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **381M STRK** — three firings of the monthly cliff on **Jun 15**, **Jul 15** and **Aug 15 2026**. Starknet's own documentation sets it out plainly: early contributors hold **20.04%** of supply and investors **18.17%**, and from **Apr 2025** to **Mar 2027** those allocations release **1.27%** of the entire **10B** genesis supply every month, which is **127M STRK** a firing. The framework prefers a realised on-chain read over a calendar whenever one exists, and that test was run properly here rather than waved through. Starknet's lock manager is a factory, not a vault: it deployed **167** separate grant contracts, and each one was read individually at both window ends. Together they vested **47.7M STRK** and released only **14.4M**, leaving **414.8M STRK** unlocked-but-unclaimed and growing. That is a real and important finding — but those **167** contracts hold just **774.7M** against a **3.82B** contributor-and-investor entitlement, and they are already **93.2%** unlocked with only **53.0M** left to run. They cannot be the vehicle paying a **127M** cliff into **2027**. Where no readable escrow covers the allocation, the published schedule stands, so the calendar governs and the escrow read is logged beside it.

Sell #3, Foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is **100M STRK**, and unlike the cliff it is traceable end to end. Starknet's project distribution wallet — an address holding STRK and no other token, funded with **2,505M STRK** back in **Apr 2023** — fell from **1,250.0M** to **1,150.0M**. Its entire transfer log for the window is three entries: two zero-value test transfers and one **100M STRK** move on **Jul 28 2026**. Following the coins onward, they landed in a bridging wallet that pushed **50M** onto the Starknet rollup the next day and another **28.5M** on **Aug 21 2026**, holding only **30.1M** at the window close. Roughly **78.5M** of it is therefore already loose on the network. The forward column is **zero**, because the same wallet's full history shows only two firings in twelve months — **140M** on **Aug 22 2025** and this one — with an eleven-month gap between them and no published date for the next. Being able to move coins is not the same as a schedule to move them. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero**: there is no estate, no trustee and no court-supervised distribution anywhere in the Starknet picture.

## Buy pressure: where new STRK goes

Nowhere. All four canonical buy rows read **zero**, and each is zero by mechanism rather than by a quiet quarter. Buy #1, the programmatic buyback, is **zero** because Starknet has never deployed a buyback contract, announced a buyback programme, or disclosed an open-market purchase — so there is no destination to track and no accumulation wallet to watch. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and this is the row most often mis-stated for STRK. Starknet fees are now payable only in STRK, which sounds like a burn mechanism and is not one: fees are forwarded to sequencers and stakers, never destroyed. Two independent reads confirm it. Supply on the Ethereum contract only ever rises, and a sweep of the burn side returns nothing at all; and the rollup-side supply tracks its bridge escrow to within **35,186 STRK** at both window ends, which is precisely the check that would expose a burn hiding on the rollup. Buy #3, Foundation buy, is **zero** — no Starknet-controlled wallet accumulated a coin over the window. Buy #4, a new long-term lock, is **zero**: staking does hold STRK, but the mainnet exit is a **7-day** delay after signalling intent, which is a withdrawal queue rather than a term lock, and staked STRK is counted inside the tradable float regardless. No new escrow or lockup contract was deployed in the window.

## Foundation and overhang

Four overhangs are tracked, and together they are close to half the counted float. The Starknet Foundation safe holds **1,001.3M STRK** and did not send a single transfer across the entire ninety days — it is the largest still, quiet block of supply in the picture, on no published schedule at all. The project distribution wallet still holds **1,150.0M STRK** after its **Jul 28 2026** release, and it fires in large irregular lumps rather than on a calendar. The **167** grant contracts hold **467.7M STRK**, of which **414.8M** is already vested and claimable the moment a recipient chooses to take it — a backlog that grew by **33.3M** over this window rather than draining. And subtracting every wallet we could name from the gap between the on-chain total of **10,154.7M** and the circulating figure of **6,975.5M** leaves **448.5M STRK** in wallets no public source identifies, most likely rollup-side foundation and community addresses. Separately, the Starknet Foundation has been distributing **1.7B STRK** of governance voting power to community delegates — that is delegation, not ownership, and no coins change hands, so it is not booked as flow. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How STRK compares to other Ethereum layer-two tokens

The layer-two token class splits on one question, and it is not throughput: does the network's fee revenue reach the token? Rollups that route sequencer revenue into a burn or a buyback can run a negative net even while unlocks continue, because the buy side is mechanical and does not require anyone to decide anything. STRK sits on the other side of that line. Starknet made its token the mandatory fee asset — a genuine demand improvement — but the fees go to sequencers and stakers as income, not to a burn address, so the mechanism that would offset the unlock schedule simply does not exist in the design. That is the single most important structural fact about STRK's supply, and it is the reason all four buy rows are zero rather than small.

The second axis is issuance. Most layer-two tokens are fixed-supply governance assets with no protocol-level mint at all; their entire inflation story is the vesting calendar. STRK is unusual in the class because it also mints: staking rewards are paid in new coins on a curve that scales with participation, against a governance-set ceiling of **4%** a year. That puts STRK closer to an uncapped proof-of-stake base layer than to a typical rollup token — with the important difference that a base layer usually pairs its issuance with a fee burn, and Starknet does not. STRK therefore carries a base-layer's issuance profile and a rollup token's empty buy side at the same time, and the two effects add rather than cancel.

The third axis is the unlock calendar, and here STRK is at the harsh end of its class. A layer-two token whose contributor and investor cliffs have already run their course prices a known float; STRK still has monthly **127M** firings scheduled through **Mar 15 2027**, on top of **3,179.2M STRK** of supply that is not yet circulating and, for the large foundation blocks, publishes no release dates whatsoever. Any comparison built from the **10B** maximum-supply figure that price sites still display will understate STRK against its peers in both directions — the real on-chain total is **10,154.7M** and rising, and the schedule that governs the float runs for another seven months.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the three cliff dates themselves — **Sep 15 2026**, **Oct 15 2026** and **Nov 15 2026** — each releasing roughly **127M STRK**, with the programme running to **Mar 15 2027**. Second, the unclaimed backlog inside the grant contracts, which stood at **414.8M STRK** on **Aug 22 2026** and has been accumulating rather than draining; a wave of claims would convert paper entitlement into real float without any calendar event at all. Third, the Starknet Foundation safe at **1,001.3M STRK**, silent for the whole window — its first outflow would be the single largest discretionary event available in this ledger. Fourth, the staking mint rate, currently **1.51%** a year against a **4%** ceiling; the minting constant is adjustable by governance inside a **1.0%** to **4.0%** band, and adoption of liquid staking since **Jul 29 2026** pushes the realised rate up on its own. Fifth, any proposal that would route sequencer fees into a burn or a buyback — that is the one change that would move a buy row off zero.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads STRK at **+7.44%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 22 2026**, easing to **+6.00%** over the next 90 as the one-off project-wallet release drops out of the window. The structural mechanism is a published vesting calendar rather than issuance: **127M STRK** unlocks on the 15th of every month through **Mar 15 2027**, and Starknet mints a further **37.7M** a quarter to pay staking rewards on top. The key risk is that the offsetting side is empty by construction — Starknet has no buyback and no burn, and its fee revenue is paid out as income to sequencers and stakers rather than destroyed, so nothing absorbs the release. And there is no ceiling to fall back on: the **10B** figure widely quoted as STRK's maximum supply is the genesis mint, the chain already reads **10,154.7M**, and **3,179.2M STRK** of it has yet to reach the market.
