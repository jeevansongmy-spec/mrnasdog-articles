# QNT Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

QNT supply is roughly steady: no new QNT has been made since June 2018, and the only coins that joined the circulating count in the 90 days to Nov 11 2026 were **12,002 QNT** released from a Quant company wallet between Oct 6 and Oct 10 2026. Against that, about **15 QNT** were lost for good when people sent them to the token contract by mistake, so supply rose about **+0.08%**. Other trackers show **+0.07%**, close to the same, and the next 90 days look close to flat unless the company releases more.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Net new QNT in the 90 days from Aug 13 to Nov 11 2026 came to **+0.08%** of the circulating supply, and the forecast for the next 90 days, to Feb 9 2027, is about **0.00%**. Other trackers read **+0.07%** for the same 90 days, a gap of only **0.02 points**, so the coin page carries no warning note. All of the change came from one company release, not from any built-in source of new coins. The label that fits QNT is a quiet, fixed-pool token whose only real supply risk is the company itself.

## Sell pressure: where new QNT comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**. Every QNT that exists today was handed out in the token sale of June 2018, and nobody is paid in new QNT for running the network. One detail matters, though: the old 2018 sale contract can still create new QNT if its owner key acts again. That key has not touched the contract in more than eight years, so it adds nothing today, but it means the supply is not fixed by code.

Vesting unlocks are **zero**. Team and staff coins vested over the 12 months after the 2018 sale and have been free since 2019, so no locked batch of QNT is waiting to open.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks came to **12,002 QNT**. Quant keeps company coins in two wallets that sit outside the circulating count. Between Oct 6 and Oct 10 2026 the second of those wallets sent **12,002 QNT** in five transfers to a brand-new wallet, which still holds them. That move put the coins into the circulating count. It was the wallet's first release, there is no public plan for another, so the next 90 days carry zero for this row.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy coins are **zero**. No court case, bankrupt firm or time lock is holding QNT that will be paid out later.

## Buy pressure: where new QNT goes

Programmatic buyback is **zero**. Quant has no buyback plan and has never announced one, and no contract takes QNT off the market on a schedule.

Protocol fee burn is **zero**. Using Quant's network does not burn QNT. The one big burn happened on Sep 14 2018, when about **9.55M** unsold sale coins were sent to the token contract, where nothing can move them. That old burn is already outside today's supply, so it does not count again.

Foundation buying is **zero**. There is no public sign that the company buys QNT back, and funds or traders who buy QNT take coins that are already counted.

New long-term locks are **zero**. No lock that takes QNT out of the circulating count has started, and coins that node operators stake still count as circulating, so staking removes nothing.

The one small buy-side flow is coins stuck in the token contract: **15.17 QNT** from 32 mistaken sends between Aug 13 and Nov 11 2026. No code can move them out, so they are gone for good. The same small trickle is expected in the next 90 days.

## Foundation and overhang

Quant Network, the London company behind QNT, holds the only coins that sit outside the circulating count. The first company wallet, publicly labelled as Quant's Team Wallet 1, holds **25.94K QNT** and did not move in the window. The second company wallet, filled from the first in October 2025, fell from **42.38K** to **30.38K QNT** after the October release. Together they hold about **56.31K QNT**, under 0.4% of the circulating supply. The new wallet that received the **12,002 QNT** is already counted as circulating, so if it sells, the count does not change.

The bigger overhang is not a wallet but the 2018 sale contract. Its counters still leave room for about **6.47M QNT** of unsold presale coins and about **21.04M QNT** of unsold sale coins, and its owner key can still turn that room into new QNT. It has not done so since 2018, and nothing points to it now. Both company wallets and the sale contract are read again at every rebuild. If either wallet's balance falls between rebuilds, the outflow enters the foundation row at the next rebuild, and any new QNT from the sale contract would enter the protocol inflation row.

## How QNT compares to other fixed-supply infrastructure tokens

Most network tokens add supply in one of two ways: they pay validators or stakers with new coins, as proof-of-stake chains do, or they release a big team and investor pool on a monthly schedule, as many newer tokens do. QNT does neither. It has no block rewards, no staking rewards paid in new coins and no vesting left, so in a normal quarter its supply barely moves. That puts QNT in the same quiet group as older Ethereum tokens whose whole supply was handed out at launch.

The difference from the strictest fixed-supply tokens is control. A token with no mint function at all cannot grow, whatever its team decides. QNT's 2018 sale contract still has a working path to create coins through one owner key, and the company decides when its own wallets release coins. So QNT's supply is steady by choice rather than by code, and the company's actions are the thing to follow.

QNT also differs from tokens that use fees to shrink supply. Networks with a fee burn or a revenue buyback take coins out every day as usage grows. Quant has no such link: more use of Overledger does not remove QNT, and the only coins that disappear today are the small amounts people send to the token contract by accident.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the two company wallets through Feb 9 2027: any further release from the **25.94K** or **30.38K QNT** balances would be new circulating supply, as the October 2026 batch was.

Second, the wallet that received the **12,002 QNT** on Oct 6 to Oct 10 2026: a move to an exchange would add selling but not new supply, because those coins are already counted.

Third, the 2018 sale contract through Feb 9 2027: any call from its owner key that creates QNT would be the first new QNT since June 2018 and would change this reading at once.

Fourth, The Clearing House network that picked Quant on Sep 24 2026 is due to open for banks in the first half of 2027. Neither side has said banks must buy or lock QNT, so any word on that would change the buy side.

## Summary

QNT supply grew about **+0.08%** in the 90 days to Nov 11 2026, a mixed reading with supply roughly steady. No new QNT has been made since June 2018; the whole rise was a **12,002 QNT** release from a Quant company wallet in October 2026, against about **15 QNT** lost in the token contract. The company still holds about **56.31K QNT** outside the circulating count, and an old 2018 sale contract could still create new coins through one key, so the main risk is a company decision, not a schedule. Without another release, the next 90 days should be close to flat.
