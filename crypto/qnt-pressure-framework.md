# QNT Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Mixed flows, supply roughly steady

Quant issues no new QNT and destroys none: every one of the eight framework rows reads **0**, so the net supply change is **0.00%** over the last 90 days and a projected **0.00%** over the next 90, while our supply monitor reads **+0.05%** for the trailing window — a gap of **0.05 percentage points** that sits far inside tolerance, so no warning chip ships. QNT is an **Ethereum** token issued once at the 2018 Quant Network sale, with the supply figure on the token contract identical at both ends of this window and unchanged for years. The mechanism people usually credit for shrinking QNT — Overledger licence fees — is billed in pounds and settles into treasury-held tokens that come back at the end of the term, so it locks nothing permanently and burns nothing at all.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Aug 14 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads **QNT at 0.00% net** over the last 90 days and **0.00%** projected over the next 90. Our supply monitor reads **+0.05%** for the trailing window, a gap of **0.05 percentage points** — an order of magnitude under the framework's 0.5-point tolerance, so no **monitor gap** chip is attached to the page. The residual is rounding drift inside the **68.3K QNT** company reserve that separates the counted total from the counted float, on a base of **14.54M** tokens; it is not a disagreement about whether Quant minted anything, because the chain is unambiguous that it did not. The cite-able label for QNT is **a genuinely inert supply** — not a slow-inflation token, not a deflationary one, but a token whose sell ledger and buy ledger are both empty by construction.

## Sell pressure: where new QNT comes from

It does not come from anywhere, and that is the whole sell section. Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **0**. Quant Network runs no blockchain of its own; Overledger is an interoperability layer that sits above other chains, so there is no block reward, no validator set, no staking issuance and no emission curve anywhere in the QNT design. Every QNT that exists was issued at the April 2018 crowdsale. Reading the token contract on Ethereum at both ends of this window, the supply figure returns **45,467,000** on **Aug 14 2026** and the identical **45,467,000** on **May 15 2026**, with the same value at sample blocks going back several years — a delta of exactly zero.

That **45,467,000** deserves one sentence of care, because it is a constant left in the contract rather than the amount of QNT that exists. About **24.16M QNT** was actually issued when the Quant Network sale closed, and on **Sep 14 2018** roughly **9.55M** of it was pushed into the token's own address, which is why the recognised total supply is **14,612,493** — the two figures reconcile to the token, exactly. One correction this build makes to earlier readings of Quant: the popular claim that minting was permanently switched off by a **finishMinting** call in 2018 is not verifiable on this contract, because the accessors that pattern requires are simply absent and revert when called, while a mint entry point does appear in the deployed code. The framework therefore claims only what the chain shows — no mint has ever been exercised and the supply figure is frozen — and tags the row from the live read rather than as a permanent structural guarantee.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **0** because Quant's vesting is long spent: team and staff tokens ran a twelve-month schedule that finished in 2019, founder and advisor allocations expired alongside it, and Quant's own token disclosure states there is no remaining vesting overhang. No unlock tracker carries a QNT calendar, because there is nothing left to put on one. Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is **0** with no dated release observed in the window; the pools it watches are covered below. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **0** because no bankruptcy estate holds QNT and no court-supervised distribution names the token, so there is no trustee schedule that could push a block of it onto the market.

## Buy pressure: where new QNT goes

The buy ledger is empty in all four rows, and the reason matters more than the zeros. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is **0**. Quant bills Overledger as an annual licence fee denominated in fiat, originally **GBP 100**, so the revenue that reaches the company arrives as pounds rather than tokens. The Quant treasury may buy QNT over the counter to settle a licence, but that is a company decision taken at its discretion, not a protocol contract: no treasury address is published, no purchase size is disclosed, and no release schedule exists to project forward. Under the framework's evidence rules a discretionary flow with none of history, schedule or dated disclosure books zero.

Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is **0**, and this build verified it in the code rather than asserting it: the deployed QNT token carries no burn entry point of any kind, the zero address holds nothing, and the conventional dead address holds a rounding fraction. No amount of Overledger usage can shrink QNT supply, because the token has no mechanism to destroy a unit. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is **0**; Quant has never published a balance-sheet purchase programme, and the only buying it describes is the over-the-counter conversion already covered above. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is **0**, and this is the row Quant's model is most often credited with. QNT settled into an Overledger licence is held by the treasury for the licence term, commonly twelve months, and then returns to the treasury's disposal. A hold that reverses is not a removal, and it is certainly not a burn; with no lock contract address published and no size disclosed, nothing is booked while the model stays on watch.

## Foundation and overhang

Two Quant-controlled pools are enumerated and tracked. The first is the company reserve: **68,317 QNT**, the difference between the recognised total of **14,612,493** and the counted float of **14,544,176** on **Aug 14 2026**. That is under half a percent of supply, it has no published release calendar, and Quant's own token-transparency disclosure independently puts company holdings at roughly the same **68,000 QNT**. It is refreshed by chain read each rebuild. The second is the **9,550,882 QNT** parked inside the token's own address since the 2018 sale. That pool moved during this window, but only inwards — from **9,550,870** to **9,550,882**, about **12 QNT** of stray user sends, which is **0.00008%** of the float and rounds to zero in the ledger. It has never sent anything out, and it structurally cannot: the QNT token has no owner accessor and no withdrawal path in its deployed code, so tokens that arrive there stay there. If either pool's balance falls between refreshes, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How QNT compares to other fixed-supply utility tokens

QNT belongs to the smallest and quietest supply class in crypto: an enterprise-software token with a one-time issuance, no chain of its own, and no protocol-level mechanism on either side of the ledger. Against uncapped continuous-emission layer ones — the Solanas, Avalanches and Cosmos chains that pay a validator set every block — QNT has no inflation floor at all to out-earn. Against halving-model proof-of-work coins such as Bitcoin or Bitcoin SV, which are hard-capped but still mint a shrinking subsidy every ten minutes, QNT is further along the same road: its issuance is not merely capped, it is finished. In framework terms, a proof-of-work coin still reports a positive net because the subsidy keeps arriving, while QNT reports nothing.

The more instructive comparison runs the other way, against exchange tokens like BNB or OKB that pay quarterly buyback-and-burn out of platform revenue. Those tokens convert business revenue into a permanent supply reduction, and the framework reads them as deflationary because the burned units cannot come back. Quant collects real enterprise revenue too, but routes it differently: the fee is charged in pounds, the treasury converts it, and the tokens are held for the licence term and released. The distinction between a burn and a hold is the entire difference between a negative net and a flat one, and it is the reason QNT does not score as a deflationary asset despite an economically comparable revenue model. Quant's roadmap points at a Trusted Node programme and staking for the Overledger Fusion network in 2026, which would be the first mechanism able to move this ledger — in either direction, depending on whether rewards are paid from fees or from newly issued tokens.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four items would change this reading. First, the Trusted Node and staking programme announced for 2026: if it goes live with a published bond size it becomes a Buy #4 lock, and if rewards are ever funded by issuance rather than fees it becomes a Sell #1 row for the first time in QNT's history. Second, the Overledger Fusion Rollup, which reached mainnet on **Jun 2 2026** connecting 74 networks — watch whether its institutional usage brings a published treasury policy with it. Third, any disclosure of a Quant treasury address or a dated over-the-counter purchase, which would turn Buy #1 and Buy #3 from unmeasurable into measurable. Fourth, the **68,317 QNT** company reserve, which is checked on every rebuild and would land in Sell #3 the moment a release is observed. Absent all four, the next reading is the same reading.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Quant at **0.00% net supply change** over both the trailing and forward 90-day windows, against a monitor reading of **+0.05%** and a gap of **0.05 percentage points** that needs no warning chip. The structural mechanism is that QNT was issued once in 2018 and has no issuance, no vesting, no burn and no buyback since: the supply figure on the Ethereum token contract is identical at both ends of the window, and the token carries no way to destroy a unit. The key risk is definitional rather than numerical — Overledger licence fees are billed in fiat and settle into treasury tokens that return at the end of the term, so a model widely described as deflationary removes nothing permanently. The ceiling is a recognised total of **14,612,493 QNT** against a counted float of **14,544,176**, leaving **68,317 QNT** of company reserve as the only supply that can still reach the market.
