CHIP is the fixed-supply governance token of USD.AI, the protocol that finances AI and GPU hardware. USD.AI mints **no** new CHIP and burns **none**, so the whole story is where the existing 10B sits: the only live movement is the Flatiron rewards season paying CHIP out of the pre-minted ecosystem bucket, of which about **6M** actually reached the tradable float — a net **+0.30%** of supply, with nothing on the buy side to offset it. Our supply monitor reads the same window at **−0.05%**, so the two agree the float is essentially flat, a gap of **0.35 percentage points** that ships with no data-conflict chip. The real event — the investor and contributor vesting cliff in **2027** — is still several quarters away, with **8,000M CHIP (80% of supply)** waiting behind it.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **August 10 2026**, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CHIP at **+0.30% net** — a small, one-sided drip of supply onto the market — and projects the same **+0.30% net** for the next 90 days. Our supply monitor reads the window at **−0.05%**, a gap of just **0.35 percentage points**, inside the half-point tolerance, so this build ships with **no monitor-gap chip**. The framework books the realised ecosystem release into the float, which the monitor confirms is tiny — the gross rewards program is far larger, but most claimed CHIP is staked straight back into sCHIP rather than sold. CHIP is best characterised as **a hard-capped token whose float is quietly settling, releasing a little and absorbing nothing**.

## Sell pressure: where new CHIP comes from

Sell #1 — protocol inflation — is **zero**, and structurally so. A live read of the CHIP token contract returns exactly **10,000M** CHIP, unchanged across the window; the entire CHIP supply was minted at launch in **2026**, and the protocol has no emission schedule, no staking issuance and no mint that fires. Points and airdrops are paid out of the pre-minted ecosystem allocation, so even they redistribute coins that already exist rather than creating new ones. Nothing USD.AI does adds a CHIP to the cap, so every movement in this ledger is a transfer of supply that already exists — unlike a continuous-emission layer-1, CHIP's inflation question is entirely about custody, never about issuance.

Sell #2 — vesting unlocks — is **zero** for both windows. The investor allocation (**29.6%**) and the Permian Labs core contributor allocation (**23.5%**) — **5,310M** CHIP together — sit behind an identical 12-month vesting cliff measured from the 2026 launch: nothing vests before month twelve, **33%** unlocks at the cliff around **March–April 2027**, and the remaining **67%** releases in equal monthly instalments over the following 24 months. The framework checked whether those allocations sit in a readable on-chain escrow that could release ahead of the calendar. They do not: the large CHIP allocation holders are ordinary wallets rather than vesting contracts, so the published calendar governs, and it releases nothing before 2027.

Sell #3 — Foundation and unscheduled unlocks — is the one live sell row, at about **6M** CHIP. USD.AI runs the Flatiron Season 2 rewards season out of the pre-minted ecosystem bucket, and it is the only mechanism putting CHIP onto the float right now. On paper the program pays out far more, but the realised amount reaching the tradable float is small — most claimed rewards are staked straight into sCHIP, the protocol's first-loss backstop, or left unclaimed — so the framework books the realised **6M** rather than the gross flow, and the supply monitor confirms the float stayed flat. For the next 90 days the framework repeats that same realised quantum, because the Flatiron season settles by **October 14 2026**, inside the window. The ecosystem, reserve, investor and contributor buckets that fund this release still hold roughly **8,000M** CHIP between them. Sell #4 — long-term locked or bankruptcy — is **zero**: CHIP has no bankruptcy estate and no court-ordered distribution schedule.

## Buy pressure: where new CHIP goes

Every buy row is **zero**, which is the defining weakness of CHIP's current ledger. Buy #1 — programmatic buyback — is zero because CHIP does not entitle holders to protocol revenue, so there is no revenue-funded buyback. The one mechanism that could remove CHIP from the float is the USD.AI Level Up protected sale, which lets buyers hand their CHIP back and be settled in stablecoins — a downside-protection right rather than a buyback. But that right is a put, and it is now **out-of-the-money**: CHIP trades around **$0.032**, above the protected settle levels struck at the **$270M and $190M** fully-diluted valuations, so buyers have little reason to redeem at the final maturity on **October 14 2026**. The framework therefore books no custody return, where the prior build had assumed one.

The remaining rows are zero for structural reasons. Buy #2 — protocol fee burn — is zero because no CHIP is ever destroyed; the token design contains no burn address. Buy #3 — Foundation buy — is zero: no open-market CHIP buying has been disclosed, so the row stays monitored rather than estimated. Buy #4 — new long-term lock — is zero as a booked offset. Staking CHIP into sCHIP is live and does hold coins behind a governance-set cooldown, but the position can be unwound and no lock quantum has been announced, so the framework books nothing rather than crediting a reversible lock as permanent absorption.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is the dominant fact about CHIP, and it is enormous: **8,000M** CHIP, **80%** of the fixed 10B supply, sits outside the **2,000M** circulating float. It breaks into four tracked blocks. First, the investor and core contributor allocations, **5,310M** CHIP across a set of identified holding wallets, cliff-locked until roughly **March–April 2027** and then released monthly for two years — refreshed on a bi-weekly walk against the published vesting schedule. Second, the USD.AI ecosystem allocation, roughly **27.5%** of supply, which funds the Flatiron rewards season and is the source of the live release — read on-chain each rebuild. Third, the reserve allocation, roughly **19.5%** of supply, discretionary and undated. Fourth, the protocol custody wallet tied to the protected sale, read on-chain each rebuild. Exchange custodial balances are deliberately excluded, because they belong to depositors rather than to the team. One rule governs all four: if any of these balances falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CHIP compares to other fixed-cap governance tokens

CHIP belongs to the fixed-cap, fully pre-minted governance token class. Against a continuous-emission layer-1, CHIP looks structurally clean: no block reward, no staking issuance, no validator subsidy, so the supply curve is flat by construction and no amount of network growth dilutes anyone. Against a hard-capped proof-of-work coin, the comparison inverts. A halving-model chain distributes its remaining supply to the open market over decades on a schedule nobody controls; CHIP distributed its entire supply on day one into identified buckets, and its release calendar is a corporate document rather than a consensus rule. The cap protects against issuance; it does nothing about custody.

The closer analogues are other recently launched protocol tokens with heavy vesting cliffs — the cohort where 15% to 25% of supply floats at launch and insiders wait a year. In that group CHIP sits at the tighter end at **20%** circulating, which flatters the near-term reading and sharpens the 2027 one. What used to separate CHIP from that cohort was the protected-sale redemption right, the one structural way CHIP could be pulled off the float. With the token now trading above its protected settle levels, that right sits idle, so CHIP looks like most of its peers again: a small ecosystem drip onto the float and no structural absorption to counter it. When the final maturity passes on **October 14 2026**, the buy side of the ledger is empty by design.

Against exchange tokens that run a quarterly buyback and burn out of revenue, CHIP is structurally weaker on the buy side and should be read that way. USD.AI captures real fees on origination and net interest margin, but CHIP carries no claim on that cash flow at all — the token is governance-only, so routing any of it into a CHIP buyback would be a governance decision that has not been made. A token whose absorption depends on a future vote is not the same asset as one whose absorption is written into the protocol.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things would move this reading. First, **October 14 2026** — the second and final USD.AI protected-sale maturity. If CHIP falls back below its protected settle level before then, the put comes back into the money and redemptions would put a real buy-side row back on the ledger; at the current price it stays empty. Second, the same date closes the Flatiron Season 2 rewards season, whose realised payout into the float sets Sell #3; how much of the gross program is sold rather than staked is the open question. Third, the ecosystem and reserve buckets themselves — an unscheduled release from either would raise Sell #3 immediately at the next refresh. Fourth, and largest by far, any governance move touching the **2027** vesting cliff, either by routing protocol fees into a CHIP buyback that offsets it or by amending the release schedule. Nothing supply-affecting has been executed in USD.AI governance to date.
