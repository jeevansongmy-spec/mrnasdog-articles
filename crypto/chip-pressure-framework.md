USD.AI's governance token CHIP has a hard cap of **10B** and issues nothing new, yet it is one of the most inflationary assets in this catalogue: the USD.AI treasury and its allocation vaults released about **273.8M CHIP** into third-party hands over the 90 days to **Aug 17 2026**, against only **9.2M** newly staked, for a net of **+13.23%** of the counted float. A fixed cap is not scarcity when **80%** of the supply is still sitting with the issuer and is actively being handed out. The vesting cliff that everyone is watching — investors and core contributors, **5,310M CHIP** between them — has not even opened yet; it starts **Mar 30 2027**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CHIP at **+13.23%** net new supply over the last 90 days and the same **+13.23%** over the next 90, measured against a circulating base of **2,000.0M CHIP**. Our supply monitor reads **+0.26%** for the same window, a gap of **12.97 percentage points**, and that gap triggers a **monitor gap** flag on the CHIP overview. The reason is not a disagreement about the chain — it is a disagreement about what counts as circulating. The monitor's float figure has been pinned to the **2.00B** classification set at listing and has not moved since, so it cannot see a treasury distribution that is plainly visible in the transfer log. Read the chain instead of the label and CHIP is **structurally inflationary on the active float**: a capped token whose issuer is still the market's largest supplier.

## Sell pressure: where new CHIP comes from

Protocol inflation is **zero**, and genuinely so. The full **10B CHIP** supply was minted at the token generation event on **Mar 30 2026**; a live read of the token contract on its home network still returns exactly that figure, and the balances visible on the other networks CHIP trades on are bridged mirrors held inside lock adapters, not extra coins. USD.AI pays no staking emission and runs no reward mint. Vesting unlocks are also **zero**, and this is where the framework's released-beats-scheduled rule earns its keep: the investor allocation (**29.6%**) and the core-contributor allocation (**23.5%**) share an identical 12-month cliff that opens on **Mar 30 2027**, and every one of the allocation vaults holding them moved not a single coin across the last 90 days. Schedule and chain agree at nothing.

All of the sell pressure sits in the third row, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, at **~273.8M CHIP**. The USD.AI treasury safe split the cap into allocation vaults on **Apr 24 2026**; adding the safe and those vaults together, the cluster held **8,533.6M CHIP** on **May 19 2026** and **8,259.8M** on **Aug 17 2026**. That difference is a realised handover, not a calendar entitlement — reallocations that moved between vaults inside the cluster are netted out and are not counted as sell pressure. Two mechanisms drive it: a live distribution wallet that ran down from **225.7M** to **41.9M** paying out hundreds of individual recipients, and the Allo Game Season 2 reward programme, which pays every reward as a CHIP airdrop and runs through **Oct 14 2026**. On **Aug 4 2026** the treasury opened a fresh **100M CHIP** tranche, of which **44.4M** is still undistributed. An independent check lines up: one large exchange's hot wallet went from nothing to **210.0M CHIP** over the identical window, fed by thousands of ordinary deposits. The fourth sell row, long-term locked or bankruptcy, is **zero** — CHIP launched in **Mar 2026** and no estate or court-ordered distribution exists against it.

## Buy pressure: where new CHIP goes

There is no programmatic buyback. CHIP is a governance token that gives holders no claim on USD.AI's lending revenue, and routing protocol fees into a buyback is a decision the DAO has the power to take but has not taken. There is no protocol fee burn either: the token contract carries no burn address and the protocol destroys none of the fees it collects, so the hard cap can only ever be a ceiling, never a shrinking supply. Foundation buying reads **zero**, with one important footnote. In **May 2026** Permian Labs, the core-contributor entity, bought **338.8M CHIP** back — but out of **locked investor allocations**, not the open market. Combined with an earlier **31.7M** airdrop buyback that is **370.5M CHIP**, or **3.71%** of the cap, retired from the future unlock queue. It is genuinely good news for 2027; it is not buy pressure in 2026, because those coins were never trading. The only real offset is new long-term locking at **~9.2M CHIP**: the staked-CHIP contract grew from **18.6M** to **27.8M** across the window, coins sitting behind a withdrawal cooldown. That absorbs roughly one coin for every thirty the treasury released.

## Foundation and overhang

The overhang is the story behind the story. The USD.AI treasury safe still holds **709.6M CHIP** and refreshes on every chain read. The investor and core-contributor vaults hold **5,310M CHIP** between them, dormant until the **Mar 30 2027** cliff, at which point a third releases at once and the remainder streams monthly for two years. The ecosystem bootstrapping bucket (**27.5%**) and the reserve (**19.5%**) carry no published release schedule at all — capacity with undecided cadence, which is exactly what the last 90 days were spending. The **370.5M CHIP** Permian Labs bought back is held, not destroyed, so it is an overhang too, simply one with a longer fuse. A live distribution wallet holds **41.9M** and the newest tranche wallet **44.4M**, both actively paying out. Separately, an unclaimed-rewards contract holds **27.8M CHIP** whose window movement could not be measured this build and is excluded, which is the conservative direction. If any of these balances falls between refreshes, the outflow enters the foundation and unscheduled unlocks row at the next refresh.

## How CHIP compares to other RWA credit tokens

The instructive comparison is not with a chain, it is with other governance tokens sitting on top of real-world credit protocols. Tokens like ONDO and MORPHO share CHIP's basic shape: a fixed or effectively fixed cap, no meaningful burn, no revenue claim for the holder, and a supply schedule dominated by treasury and vesting decisions rather than by protocol issuance. MORPHO is the closest analogue in this catalogue — also a fixed **1B** cap that cannot be minted, also reading strongly inflationary because an association deployed treasury tokens into the market. The mechanism class is the same, and it is the one investors most often misprice: with no mint, the token markets itself as non-inflationary, while the actual float grows every time a treasury wallet opens.

What separates CHIP from that peer group is timing and proportion. USD.AI is roughly five months from its token generation event with **80%** of supply still issuer-held, whereas a halving-model chain with a hard cap distributes new coins to a diffuse set of miners on a schedule nobody controls, and an exchange token with a quarterly buyback actively shrinks its float. CHIP has neither property. It also lacks the fee-burn offset that makes an uncapped chain like ETH sometimes deflationary — USD.AI's lending revenue flows to the USDai and sUSDai side of the product, not to CHIP. So the correct structural label is a capped token with an unconstrained distributor: the **10B** cap binds only at the very end, while the distribution binds every single month until the last locked coin is out.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

Four things move this reading. First, **Oct 14 2026** is the end of the Allo Game Season 2 reward programme — if the remaining reward budget is paid out in a final burst, the next-90-day figure rises rather than cools. Second, **Oct 14 2026** is also the second and final Level Up protected-sale maturity, whose released quantum has never been published; the first maturity on **Jun 17 2026** moved very little on-chain, which is the base case. Third, the treasury safe at **709.6M CHIP** and the two active distribution wallets at **86.3M** combined are the direct tell — a new tranche the size of the **Aug 4 2026** one would keep the run rate intact. Fourth, watch for a governance vote that routes protocol fees into a CHIP buyback or a burn; USD.AI's DAO has the authority and has not used it, and that single decision is the only thing that could put a real number in the buy column before **Mar 30 2027**.

CHIP is a hard-capped governance token that mints nothing and burns nothing, and it is still growing its tradable supply at **+13.23%** per 90 days, because the USD.AI treasury released **273.8M CHIP** into third-party hands between **May 19 2026** and **Aug 17 2026** while only **9.2M** was newly locked. Our supply monitor reads **+0.26%** and misses it entirely, because its float has been pinned to the **2.00B** figure set at listing — a **12.97pp** gap that is a classification artefact, not a disagreement about the chain. The key risk is that none of this is the main event: **5,310M CHIP** of investor and core-contributor supply is still behind a cliff that opens on **Mar 30 2027**, and the ceiling on all of it is the fixed **10B** cap, of which **80%** remains issuer-held today.
