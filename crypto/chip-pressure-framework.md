# CHIP Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply was growing, trend cooling

CHIP, the governance token of USD.AI on Arbitrum, has a fixed supply of **10B CHIP** with no mint and no burn, so CHIP supply only reaches the market when a locked wallet pays out. Over the last 90 days the locked USD.AI team, investor and reserve wallets paid **48.08M CHIP** to ten new wallets in two payout runs, on **Aug 24 2026** and **Sep 14 2026**, while nothing was bought back, burned or locked. Against the **2.00B CHIP** counted as circulating, the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CHIP at **+2.40% net** over the last 90 days and **+0.35%** over the next 90, when only small repeating monthly payouts are booked, against a supply-monitor reading of **−0.11%** — a gap of **2.52 percentage points** that comes from a circulating figure that has not moved since listing. The big CHIP vesting cliff is still ahead, around **Mar 30 2027**.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 18 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **CHIP at +2.40% net**: **48.08M CHIP** left locked allocation wallets for wallets outside the project, and **zero** CHIP came off the market. The independent supply monitor reads the same window at **−0.11%**. The gap is **2.52 percentage points**, well past the framework's half-point tolerance, so CHIP ships with a **data-conflict flag**. A full five-source deep walk found the cause and could not close it: the circulating figure the monitor divides has sat at exactly **2.00B** since listing on **Apr 21 2026**, so it cannot show a locked wallet opening, while the chain shows seventeen of them falling from **7,741.03M** to **7,651.29M CHIP**. The forward column reads **+0.35%**, well below the trailing one, because only **6.92M CHIP** of repeating monthly payouts from the locked wallets carry a schedule; the larger one-off payments do not repeat on any published date. The label for CHIP is **a fixed-supply token whose float grows through early payouts from locked wallets**.

## Sell pressure: where new CHIP comes from

Not from new coins. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **zero**: all **10B CHIP** were minted once on **Mar 31 2026**, the CHIP total supply read the same at both ends of the window, and the live USD.AI token contract has no mint function. That was checked rather than assumed — the supply figure sits in storage the contract could change, and it did not change. The token contract can still be upgraded by its admin, so a fixed CHIP supply is a policy the framework re-reads on every refresh, not a guarantee. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** for the last 90 days: USD.AI team, investor and reserve CHIP unlock nothing before month 12, then a third at once and the rest monthly over 24 months, and that first CHIP vesting date lands around **Mar 30 2027**. The official USD.AI unlock chart also grows the ecosystem pool before then — about **61.1M CHIP** between **Sep 18 2026** and **Dec 17 2026**. That was tested on-chain rather than booked on faith. The USD.AI ecosystem wallets did pay **143.63M CHIP** to outside holders this window, but the ecosystem coins now in outside hands total **1,356.2M**, still below the **2.00B CHIP** the project counted as circulating at launch. Every one of those coins was already counted, none of the scheduled growth beyond the launch float has reached the market, and about **643.8M** of counted float still sits in project wallets. So Sell #2 stays at zero in both columns and is monitored.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, carries the whole CHIP ledger at **48.08M CHIP**. Seventeen wallets hold the locked team, investor and reserve allocations; every one was traced from the single CHIP mint and read at both ends of the window. Together they fell by **89.74M CHIP**, and the log of every transfer they made adds up to the same figure with nothing left over. **41.67M CHIP** of that was one round split into a new vault of the same kind, which still holds it, so it stays inside the locked group. The other **48.08M CHIP** went out in two payout runs — **23.22M** on **Aug 24 2026** to Aug 27 and **24.85M** on **Sep 14 2026** to Sep 15 — to ten fresh wallets that have not moved them yet. These runs are off the published CHIP vesting schedule, which is why they sit in Sell #3 and not Sell #2. Only two amounts repeat from run to run — **1,636,728** and **671,419 CHIP**, each paid by the same wallet to a different new wallet every month — and the August run carried five months of back-pay on both. That gives **2.31M CHIP** a month, so the forward column books three months: **6.92M CHIP**. The larger one-off payments have no schedule and project at zero; the last one fired on **Sep 14 2026**. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: no estate or trustee holds CHIP, and the **338.8M** locked investor CHIP the developer bought back in May 2026 changed hands before the window without touching the market.

## Buy pressure: where new CHIP goes

Nowhere, on every canonical row. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero**: CHIP carries no claim on USD.AI protocol revenue, and no programme routes USD.AI fees into buying CHIP. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is **zero**, and both burn surfaces were read to prove it: the two burn addresses held nothing at either end of the window, total CHIP supply did not fall, and the contract has no burn function at all. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**: the USD.AI liquidity wallet rose **2.99M CHIP** from pool rebalancing, which moves CHIP inside the float rather than out of it.

Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero**, and this is the row most readers will expect to be positive. The CHIP staking module grew from **25.26M** to **53.87M CHIP** across the window. The USD.AI docs describe waiting periods and a slashable backstop for staked CHIP, but they mark those terms as still being explored, and the contract actually running today is a plain vault: staked CHIP can be withdrawn in the same block, nothing can be slashed, and the vault's share price read exactly **1.0000** at both ends, so staking CHIP pays nothing on-chain. Coins that can leave at any moment are not locked, so none of that growth is booked. If a real waiting period ships, the row will change at the next refresh.

## Foundation and overhang

The largest CHIP overhang is the **7,651.29M CHIP** still in the seventeen locked allocation wallets — close to four times the counted float. Most of it waits for the month-12 CHIP cliff, but two payout runs have now shown that these wallets can pay out early, and each one is re-read on every refresh. The USD.AI treasury holds **697.26M CHIP** and paid out **112.06M** this window, including a **100M** partner tranche on **Aug 4 2026**. That spending is not booked as new supply, because it came from the **2.00B CHIP** the project counted as circulating at launch — the ICO, the airdrop and the reserve slice unlocked for liquidity — and the ecosystem wallets have not yet handed out more than that launch float: **1,356.2M** of the **2.00B** is in outside hands, and about **643.8M** of counted float remains inside project wallets. Three smaller wallets are tracked the same way: the airdrop claim contract at **27.84M**, a grants wallet at **41.92M** and a partner wallet at **30.56M**. The bridge lockers that back CHIP on Ethereum, Base and Solana hold coins that already exist and are not overhangs. If any locked wallet's balance falls between refreshes and the CHIP lands outside the project, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh — and once ecosystem payouts pass the launch float, those outflows will enter too.

## How CHIP compares to other governance tokens with locked allocations

CHIP belongs to the large class of fixed-supply governance tokens where nothing is minted after launch and the float grows only as locked allocations open. Against a proof-of-stake Layer 1 that pays validators in new coins every block, CHIP has no issuance leg at all — its supply risk is a calendar of unlocks rather than an emission rate. Against a standard vesting design, where team and investor coins sit in an on-chain vesting contract that releases on fixed dates, CHIP is looser: its allocations sit in ordinary wallets, so they can pay out before the published cliff, and this window shows they do. That makes the CHIP ledger harder to forecast than a contract-enforced schedule and easier to move without notice.

Against governance tokens that capture protocol revenue, the contrast is on the buy side. A token that routes fees into buybacks or burns has a steady bid pulling supply off the market; CHIP has neither, because USD.AI states the token has no claim on protocol revenue, and USD.AI keeps its lending income inside the protocol. A token whose staking locks coins behind a waiting period or earns a real yield also removes float; the CHIP staking module does neither in its current form. The result is a CHIP ledger with a live sell side and an empty buy side — every coin that leaves a locked wallet adds to supply, and nothing on the other side of the ledger offsets it.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, a **250M CHIP** liquidity-provider tranche. The project's EU filing says 2.5% of supply unlocks 8 months after launch, but launch can be read three ways — **Oct 19 2026**, **Nov 30 2026** or **Dec 21 2026** — and the newer official unlock chart shows no such step, so it is not booked; if it lands inside the window and reaches holders, the forward reading jumps far higher. Second, the ecosystem pool: its wallets paid out **143.63M CHIP** this window, all from the launch float, with about **643.8M** of counted float left; at that pace the cushion lasts more than a year, and once it runs out ecosystem payouts become new supply. Third, the monthly payouts: after **Aug 24 2026** and **Sep 14 2026**, a larger run like those would add to the **6.92M CHIP** booked. Fourth, **Oct 14 2026**, when USD.AI Season 2 rewards end and the second batch of protected ICO CHIP matures — paid from coins already counted as circulating. Fifth, the CHIP staking module: an upgrade that adds a waiting period would turn its **53.87M CHIP** into a real lock and open Buy #4.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CHIP at **+2.40% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.35%** over the next 90, with an empty buy side and a sell side made entirely of early payouts from locked wallets. The structural mechanism is a fixed **10B CHIP** supply with no mint and no burn, so the USD.AI float grows only as locked allocations open, and **48.08M CHIP** opened this window in two payout runs. The key risk is that those allocations sit in ordinary wallets rather than an enforced vesting contract, so they can keep paying out before the published schedule, with **7,651.29M CHIP** still behind them. The ceiling is the fixed supply itself: CHIP can never exceed **10B**, but **8B** of that is not yet counted as circulating, and the first scheduled cliff arrives around **Mar 30 2027**.
