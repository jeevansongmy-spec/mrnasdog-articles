Ethereum minted about **257K ETH** to validators over the last 90 days and destroyed only about **4.0K ETH** through the EIP-1559 base-fee burn, leaving net supply **+0.21%** across the window. Validator issuance is now the whole story: the burn that once cancelled it out has fallen to roughly **44 ETH a day** — and to **21 a day** in the last week — because rollup data and everyday activity sit on cheap Layer 2 blob space rather than on Ethereum mainnet. Ethereum has no hard cap, no vesting schedule and no buyback, so the only levers that move ETH supply are validator issuance and the base-fee burn, and issuance is winning by roughly sixty to one.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to **Aug 13 2026** the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ETH at **+0.21%** net supply growth — **257K ETH** of validator issuance plus **3.2K ETH** of Ethereum Foundation treasury spending against **4.0K ETH** of base-fee burn, on a circulating base of **120.68M ETH**. The next 90 days project to **+0.22%**: issuance drifts up to about **261K ETH** because the staked pool keeps growing, while the burn is carried forward unchanged. The inflation monitor reads **-0.02%** over the same window, a gap of **0.24 percentage points** — inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so no data-conflict flag applies. That monitor series derives circulating supply from market value divided by price, which carries enough rounding noise on an asset this large to land anywhere in the near-zero band; the framework instead reads the ETH supply directly off the chain, where it plainly rose from **121.70M** to **121.95M** across the window. Both readings agree Ethereum is close to steady. ETH in August 2026 is **mildly inflationary on a fading burn** — not the "ultrasound money" of 2022, and not a runaway emitter either.

## Sell pressure: where new ETH comes from

Ethereum has exactly one mint. Validator issuance under proof-of-stake produced about **257K ETH** over the window, roughly **2,850 ETH a day** averaged, and the live rate now runs near **2,900 a day**. The pay rate is not a fixed number: Ethereum pays a total reward each epoch proportional to the square root of the total staked balance, so issuance rises as more ETH is staked while the yield each validator earns falls. The staked pool is at an all-time high — about **41.9M ETH**, close to **35%** of the float, secured by roughly **897K validators** after post-consolidation balance limits cut the validator count — and the staking ratio crossed **33.33%** for the first time on **Jul 28 2026**. Because the pool is still growing, the forward projection for Ethereum is slightly higher at **261K ETH** for the next 90 days.

The second live sell row is the Ethereum Foundation. Four **1,000 ETH** tranches left the Foundation's main multisig inside this window — on **May 25**, **Jul 15**, **Jul 27** and **Aug 11 2026** — into two paying safes, which then sent roughly **2,150 ETH** onward. Reading the whole identified Foundation group as one unit, its balance fell from about **12.7K ETH** to about **9.5K ETH**, so **3.2K ETH** genuinely left Foundation hands and reached the market. That is what the ledger books; the ETH still sitting inside a Foundation-controlled safe is tracked as overhang rather than counted as a sale.

The other two sell rows on Ethereum are zero, and zero by design. Vesting unlocks are zero for good: the 2014 crowdsale and both founding endowments finished distributing by 2017, and no vesting contract, cliff or team lock exists on Ethereum today — a genuinely rare position for an asset of this size. Long-term locked and bankruptcy supply is zero because the large 2022–23 estates settle their ether claims in cash at petition-date valuations rather than in coin; the FTX recovery trust's fifth distribution, which began **Jul 31 2026**, paid dollars, not ETH, so no estate schedule runs against the market.

## Buy pressure: where new ETH goes

The EIP-1559 base-fee burn is Ethereum's only removal mechanism, and over this window it destroyed just **4.0K ETH** — roughly **44 ETH a day**, and falling fast: the last 30 days ran at **33 ETH a day** and the last seven at **21**. Ethereum mainnet gas is effectively at the floor, and the reason is the blob market. Since the Fusaka upgrade and the blob parameter-only forks that followed it, Ethereum targets far more blob capacity per block, so rollups post their data for almost nothing and the fee pressure that used to burn thousands of ETH a day never reaches the execution layer. The EIP-4844 blob base fee itself sits at its minimum, contributing about **3 ETH** of burn across the entire 90 days. The upshot is blunt: the base-fee burn no longer comes close to matching validator issuance, and the deflation Ethereum was famous for in 2021–23 is off for as long as activity stays on Layer 2.

The remaining buy rows are zero. There is no programmatic buyback — Ethereum mints validator rewards rather than purchasing them, and the protocol holds no treasury it could bid with. There is no Foundation buy: the Ethereum Foundation funds itself by selling ETH, not accumulating it, and no accumulation programme exists. And there is no new long-term lock, because while about a third of all ETH is staked, withdrawals have been open since 2023 and stakers can exit within days — that is holder-driven yield, not a programme that removes coins from the market with an announced size.

## Foundation and overhang

The one team-controlled overhang on Ethereum is the Ethereum Foundation treasury. The identified group of Foundation wallets — the main multisig, two disbursing safes and one secondary wallet — held about **9.5K ETH** in liquid form at the close of this window, down from **12.7K ETH** at the start, and those balances are read from the chain at every rebuild. Alongside it sits the roughly **70,000 ETH** the Foundation staked into validators through a programme completed in **April 2026**, which is withdrawable but not liquid today; that position is walked by hand at each refresh rather than read live.

The direction of travel matters more than the size. The Ethereum Foundation's published treasury policy targets annual operating spend of roughly **15%** of treasury value against a 2.5-year buffer, and the whole point of the staking programme was to fund research, grants and donations out of yield instead of out of sales. Vitalik Buterin has since said the Foundation will sell **less** ETH going forward, prioritising long-term sustainability over the breadth of its spending. So the Foundation row carries a downward bias, not an upward one — and even at the pace observed here, a full quarter of Foundation selling is about **1.2%** of a single quarter of validator issuance. The trigger stands either way: if the Foundation group's balance falls again between refreshes, that outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How ETH compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Against the capped, halving-model chains, ETH sits in a different category entirely. A hard-capped proof-of-work coin has a schedule that is knowable years ahead and immune to usage; Ethereum has no cap, and its issuance floats with how much ETH is staked. That makes ETH's supply path a function of validator behaviour rather than of a calendar. The compensation is the burn — capped chains have no removal mechanism at all, whereas Ethereum can in principle run negative. In 2021–23 it did. In 2026 it does not, because the EIP-1559 burn depends on demand for mainnet execution and that demand has migrated to Layer 2 rollups.

Against other uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1s, ETH is at the quiet end. Chains that mint on a fixed percentage curve typically run supply growth north of 3% a year regardless of activity; Ethereum's **+0.21%** per 90 days annualises to roughly **0.86%**, an order of magnitude milder, and it is partly self-limiting because a growing staked pool raises issuance only with the square root of stake. The structural risk is the mirror image of that strength: an emitter whose only offset is fee burn is hostage to where the fees are earned. Ethereum deliberately routed its own transaction volume to rollups, which is good for users and bad for the burn. Exchange tokens with quarterly buybacks funded by revenue, and Layer 1s that burn a fixed share of every fee, do not have that dependency — their removal mechanism is tied to revenue rather than to congestion on one execution layer.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

The Glamsterdam upgrade — headlined by EIP-7732 enshrined proposer-builder separation, EIP-7928 block-level access lists and EIP-7904 gas repricing — forks the Hoodi testnet on **Aug 17 2026** and targets Ethereum mainnet around **Sep 16 2026**, inside this forward window. It changes neither the issuance curve nor the burn rule, but its gas repricing and a 200-million gas-limit path would push mainnet fees, and therefore the burn, lower still. EIP-8363 (also circulating as EIP-8361), the "Tapered Issuance Burn" filed on **Aug 4 2026**, would burn a rising share of validator rewards until issuance stops entirely at a 50% staking ratio; it is a draft that never reached proposed-for-inclusion and missed the Hegotá window, and two of the largest staking businesses are campaigning against it — but it is the single proposal that would rewrite this ledger, so watch its status. Total staked ETH is the most important number on the sell side: every million ETH added to the pool raises annual issuance by roughly **13K ETH**, and the pool just set an all-time high near **41.9M**. The next Ethereum Foundation tranche out of the main multisig would move the Foundation row. And a sustained recovery in mainnet base fees above roughly **1 gwei** is the only realistic path back toward neutral ETH supply.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Ethereum at **+0.21%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 13 2026** and projects **+0.22%** for the next 90 — mildly inflationary, and steady. The mechanism is simple and unusually clean: validator issuance of about **257K ETH** is the only mint, a further **3.2K ETH** of Ethereum Foundation spending is the only discretionary sell, the EIP-1559 base-fee burn of about **4.0K ETH** is the only removal, and there is no vesting, no unlock schedule, no buyback and no bankruptcy estate anywhere in the ledger. The key risk is that the burn keeps shrinking: Ethereum mainnet is already near the floor, and the Glamsterdam gas repricing pushes it lower. The ceiling on the other side is the square-root issuance curve, which means even a much larger staked pool cannot push ETH supply growth far above **1% a year**.
