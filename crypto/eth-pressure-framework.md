> Originally published at **[mrnasdog.com/research/eth/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/eth/inflation)** by MrNasdog.

Ethereum minted about **257.0K ETH** to validators over the last 90 days and destroyed only about **3.9K ETH** through the EIP-1559 base-fee burn — the burn now covers barely one and a half percent of what the protocol pays out. Add roughly **3.0K ETH** drained from Ethereum Foundation wallets and the net is **+0.212%** of supply to market over 90 days, with **+0.216%** projected next. ETH has no hard cap and no buyback; the only brake on issuance is the burn, and the burn has been shrinking since rollup data moved to blobspace.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Against a circulating supply of **120.68M ETH**, the Pressure Framework reads sell pressure of **260.0K ETH** and buy pressure of **3.9K ETH** over the trailing 90 days — a net of **+0.212%**. Projected forward on the live protocol run rate, the next 90 days come to **+0.216%**. The inflation monitor reads **+0.012%** for the same window, a gap of **0.20 percentage points** — inside the framework's 0.5pp tolerance, so no data-conflict flag is raised. That monitor figure derives supply from market cap divided by price and carries roughly ±0.05% of rounding noise on an asset this size, which is why it prints near zero while Ethereum's own supply plainly rose by about **253,000 ETH** across the window. The label for Ethereum today: **mildly inflationary on a collapsed burn** — a proof-of-stake Layer 1 whose issuance is small, square-root-damped and predictable, but whose only offset has quietly stopped working.

## Sell pressure: where new ETH comes from

Ethereum has exactly one mint. Consensus-layer issuance pays validators for attesting, proposing and sync-committee duty, and it is set by a curve rather than a schedule. Working it out from the protocol spec rather than quoting it: the base reward per increment is 64 divided by the square root of the total active balance, and the five reward weights — source 14, target 26, head 14, sync 2, proposer 8 — sum to exactly the weight denominator of 64, so nothing is withheld and issuance per epoch collapses to 64 multiplied by the square root of the active balance. In annual terms that is **166.32 × √(staked ETH)**. A fresh consensus-layer read this session puts the active balance at **42.39M ETH**, about **34.8%** of supply, which the curve turns into **1.08M ETH** a year — roughly **266.8K ETH** per 90 days at today's stake, or **261.2K ETH** once the curve is integrated over a window in which the staked pool grew from about 38.9M ETH in May. What the chain actually delivered was **257.0K ETH**, about **2,856 ETH a day**: **96.3%** of the flat-stake ceiling and **98.4%** of the integrated one. The residual is exited validators still sitting inside the balance sum while earning nothing, and attestation participation that is never a perfect 100%. The measured number is the one the framework ships.

Vesting unlocks contribute nothing to Ethereum, and cannot. The 2014 crowdsale and both founding endowments finished distributing in 2017; there is no vesting contract, no cliff, no team lock and no unlock-calendar entry for ETH anywhere. The Foundation row does carry a figure. The Ethereum Foundation wallet cluster — a main multisig, two disbursing safes and one operational wallet — fell from **12,486 ETH** to **9,495 ETH** across the window, a net drain of **2,992 ETH**. Three separate 1,000-ETH tranches left the main multisig on **Jul 15 2026**, **Jul 27 2026** and **Aug 11 2026**. Each was located by bisecting the multisig's balance down to its exact firing block and then traced through its internal transfers into a Foundation safe and out again to the operational wallet, which passes it on in grant-sized sends. Because **700 ETH** of that flow is still sitting inside a Foundation-controlled safe, only the cluster net is booked. The bankruptcy row is zero: the large crypto estates settle creditor claims in cash, so none of them sends ether to market on a schedule.

## Buy pressure: where new ETH goes

There is no programmatic buyback on Ethereum, and there cannot be one — validator pay is minted, not purchased, and the protocol holds no treasury it could bid with. There is no Foundation buy either; the Ethereum Foundation sells ETH to fund research, grants and donations, and has announced no accumulation programme. That leaves one buy row, the **EIP-1559 base-fee burn**, which destroys every base fee paid on Ethereum plus the blob base fees introduced by EIP-4844. Priority fees and MEV are not burned — they go to the block proposer — so the burn is strictly the L1 base fee, never a bundled fee figure. Measured across the exact 90-day window it came to **3,940 ETH**, about **44 ETH a day**, against issuance of 257.0K. Two independent measurements back it: a direct per-block read of base fee multiplied by gas used across 601 sampled blocks, and an independent full-population burn index that never touches that sample, which agrees to **8.5%** on the overlapping 30 days and **1.3%** on the last 7.

The trend is the story. The burn ran **44 ETH a day** across the full 90-day window, **36 a day** over the last 30, with a brief bounce to **61 a day** in the last week — a floor, not a recovery. The L1 base fee sat near **0.1 gwei** against a gas limit of **60M**, and rollup data now lives on cheap blobspace rather than in calldata, with the blob target at 14 and the limit at 21 since January 2026. The fourth buy row, new long-term lock, is zero and was re-tested this window. Staked ETH keeps growing, but staking here is not a lockup contract with a stated size and not a staking cap: the exit queue is effectively empty, anyone can leave at will, and liquid staking tokens keep the position tradable throughout. The growing stake is counted where it actually acts — on the mint side, raising issuance through the square-root curve.

## Foundation and overhang

Two team-controlled overhangs are tracked on Ethereum, and both belong to the Ethereum Foundation. The first is the liquid wallet cluster above, holding **9,495 ETH** at the close of the window and read directly from chain state at both ends. It runs on no published calendar: the Foundation's treasury policy sets annual spending as a share of treasury value with a multi-year cash buffer, and sales fire when the fiat side falls short of that test rather than on fixed dates. The observed rhythm this window was one 1,000-ETH tranche roughly every two weeks. The second is the Foundation's staked position of about **70,000 ETH**, a programme completed in April 2026 and designed to fund operations from yield instead of sales. Ethereum has no other qualifying overhang — no unscheduled-unlock pool, no DAO treasury, no bankruptcy-estate residual in ETH. The large corporate ETH treasuries now visible on-chain, one of which has staked over **5M ETH**, are deliberately not counted here: those coins were bought on the open market and are already inside the circulating float. If either Foundation balance falls between refreshes, that outflow enters the Foundation sell row at the next refresh.

## How ETH compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Against a capped, halving-model chain, Ethereum is the opposite design. Bitcoin's issuance is fixed in code, halves on a known block height and terminates at 21M coins; Ethereum has no cap at all, and its issuance is a live function of how much ETH is staked. Less predictable in principle, far gentler in practice: **+0.212%** over 90 days annualises to well under 1%, below what most capped chains ran in their early schedules. The trade is that Ethereum can never promise a terminal supply.

Against uncapped continuous-emission Layer 1s, Ethereum sits at the low end. Chains that set a fixed percentage inflation rate with a scheduled decay mint against total supply directly and typically print several percent a year before any fee burn. Ethereum mints against the square root of the staked pool instead, so doubling the stake raises issuance by only about 41%. That square root is the most underrated feature of Ethereum's monetary design, and it is why a staking ratio at an all-time high near **35%** has not produced a matching jump in the mint.

Against fee-burn chains, though, Ethereum is now the weak case. Exchange tokens that burn a share of revenue each quarter produce a burn that scales with the business; Ethereum's burn scales with L1 congestion, and Ethereum has spent three years successfully exporting congestion to rollups. The 2023-era framing of ETH as deflationary money rested on a base fee measured in tens of gwei; at **0.1 gwei** it no longer describes the asset.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, **EIP-8363** (also circulating as EIP-8361), the "Tapered Issuance Burn" draft filed **Aug 4 2026**: it would burn a rising share of validator rewards as the staking ratio climbs, reaching zero net consensus issuance at roughly 60.25M ETH staked. It missed the Hegotá proposed-for-inclusion list, and at the **Aug 6 2026** All Core Devs call the presenting author signalled they were considering withdrawing it, so nothing here reflects it — but adoption would be the largest change to ETH issuance since the Merge. Second, **Glamsterdam**, which forks on the Sepolia testnet on **Sep 28 2026** with a mainnet target still only stated as **Q4 2026**; the gas-limit path it opens would push the base fee, and the burn, lower still. Third, the **staking ratio**: with the exit queue at two validators and roughly 2.23M ETH waiting to enter on a 39-day queue, the mint rises from here. Fourth, the pace of **Ethereum Foundation tranches** — three 1,000-ETH releases in this window, on a tightening rhythm, and any acceleration lands straight in the Foundation sell row. Fifth, any sustained return of L1 congestion, the only thing that can make the burn matter again.

## Summary

Ethereum is mildly and predictably inflationary. Consensus-layer issuance of **257.0K ETH** per 90 days is the only mint, set by a square-root curve on an active balance of **42.39M ETH**, and it is offset by an EIP-1559 base-fee burn of just **3.9K ETH** — around one and a half percent of what is paid out, and still falling. Net supply moved **+0.212%** over the last 90 days and is projected at **+0.216%** for the next. The main risk sits in the design rather than the calendar: Ethereum's deflationary reputation depends on a burn its own scaling roadmap keeps shrinking, and with no hard cap and no buyback, nothing else stands between issuance and the market. The offsetting fact is that the issuance itself is small, square-root-damped, and — if the tapered-issuance draft ever ships — capable of being cut to zero by protocol rule rather than by promise.
