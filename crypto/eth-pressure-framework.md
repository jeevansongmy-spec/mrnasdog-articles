# ETH Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

ETH, the native coin of Ethereum, is mildly inflationary: Ethereum pays its validators in newly created ETH, and that issuance created **261,416 ETH** over the last 90 days while the EIP-1559 fee burn destroyed only **3,303 ETH**. The MrNasdog Pressure Framework therefore reads ETH at **+0.21% net** over 90 days, and the same for the next 90, against a supply-monitor reading of **+1.11%** — a gap of **0.90 percentage points** that traces to a one-day recount in the monitor's own supply feed, not to any new ETH. ETH has no supply cap, so issuance keeps running for as long as validators are paid, and its size grows with the amount of ETH staked.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 24 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **ETH at +0.21% net**: the sell side added **261,416 ETH** of new issuance and the buy side removed **3,303 ETH** through the fee burn, out of a circulating supply of **122.08M ETH**. The independent supply monitor reads **+1.11%**. The gap is **0.90 percentage points**, above the framework's half-point tolerance, so ETH ships with a **data-conflict flag**. The deep walk found the cause: the supply figure the monitor reads sat near **120.68M ETH** from mid-2025 until **Sep 2 2026** while the chain kept issuing, then jumped **1.33M ETH** in a single day on **Sep 3 2026** to catch up. That jump is a recount of ETH that already existed, most of it issued before this window, so the framework keeps its own figure. The forward column also reads **+0.21%**, because no fork or fee change is scheduled inside the next 90 days. The label for ETH is **mildly inflationary by validator issuance**: an uncapped proof-of-stake coin whose fee burn offsets only a small slice of what it pays its validators.

## Sell pressure: where new ETH comes from

Almost all of it comes from one place. Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **261,416 ETH**, about **2,905 ETH** a day. Every Ethereum validator is paid in newly created ETH for proposing blocks, voting on them and serving on sync committees, and the total paid out each epoch grows with the square root of all the ETH staked. The stake grew fast this window: **3.87M ETH** went into the Ethereum deposit contract in 90 days, and the active validator set now holds **43.46M ETH**. The issuance figure was measured, not assumed — Ethereum supply was read at both ends of the window, rose by **258,113 ETH**, and adding back the burn gives the new ETH created. The same number was checked against the protocol's own reward rule applied to the staked balance, and the two agree closely. Because more ETH keeps being staked, the rate per day is climbing, from about **2,820 ETH** a day in late June to about **2,950 ETH** a day now; the forward column still holds the trailing rate.

Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero**. ETH has no vesting schedule: the 2014 ETH sale and the founding allocations were spendable from the first Ethereum block in 2015, no unlock tracker lists any ETH left to release, and ETH circulating supply equals ETH total supply. Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is also **zero**, and this one needs care, because the Ethereum Foundation does sell ETH. Its main wallet fell from **9,774 ETH** to **5,774 ETH** across the window as it paid for its work. But every ETH the Foundation holds is already counted in circulating supply, so a Foundation sale moves coins from one holder in the float to another and adds no new ETH to the market count. Sell #4, long-term locked or bankruptcy supply, is **zero**: no estate distributes ETH on a schedule, and the listed companies and ETH exchange-traded funds that hold large amounts — one company alone reports about **5.98M ETH** — bought on the open market, so those coins were in the float already.

## Buy pressure: where new ETH goes

Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is the only buy row with a number: **3,302.7 ETH**. Under EIP-1559 the base fee of every Ethereum transaction is destroyed as it is paid, and under EIP-4844 so is the blob fee rollups pay to post their data. The burn was read block by block across all **645,520** blocks of the window: **3,298.2 ETH** of base fees and **4.5 ETH** of blob fees. That is about **37 ETH** a day, and an independent read of the same burn over the last 7 and 30 days lands within about one percent. The rate picked up at the end of the window, to about **53 ETH** a day over the last week, with no fork or fee change behind it, so the forward column holds the trailing rate. The burn is real, but it is small: new issuance was **79 times** larger. Ethereum blocks have run with a large gas limit and cheap blob space since the Fusaka upgrade and its two blob-capacity forks, so base fees stay low and little ETH is destroyed.

Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is **zero** — no contract or treasury buys ETH off the market. Buy #3, foundation buying, is **zero**: the Ethereum Foundation sells ETH and does not buy it back. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is **zero** even though ETH staking kept growing, because staked ETH stays inside the circulating count. A bigger stake removes nothing from the float; on Ethereum it does the opposite, since it is the stake that sets the size of Sell #1.

## Foundation and overhang

The one team-held ETH overhang worth naming is the Ethereum Foundation, with about **94,000 ETH** across its three main wallets. Its main wallet holds **5,774 ETH** after falling **4,000 ETH** this window, a Foundation multisig sent out **4,938 ETH**, and the Foundation keeps about **70,000 ETH** more in its own staked position under the treasury staking plan it announced on **Feb 24 2026**, with rewards going back to its treasury. It sells ETH in regular over-the-counter deals to fund research, grants and operations. Those balances are re-read on every refresh, but they do not open a Sell #3 row: ETH circulating supply equals ETH total supply, so there is no non-circulating bucket anywhere on Ethereum and every Foundation ETH is already part of the float. There is no Ethereum DAO treasury holding ETH, no bankruptcy residual and no locked allocation waiting to unlock. Exchange custody wallets, ETH exchange-traded funds and corporate treasuries are tracked as context only — those coins belong to depositors and shareholders. If a balance outside the float ever appears — for example a new locked allocation — and it falls between refreshes, that outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How ETH compares to other smart-contract Layer 1s

ETH belongs to the class of uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1s that pay validators in new coins and burn part of their fees. What sets ETH apart inside that class is how its issuance is sized. Many proof-of-stake chains set a yearly inflation rate on total supply; Ethereum instead pays a reward that grows with the square root of the ETH staked, so the more ETH is staked, the more ETH is issued, but each staker earns a smaller yield. That is why ETH issuance has been rising through 2026 as the stake grew, while the rule itself never changed. Against proof-of-work coins with a hard cap, the contrast is the cap: a capped chain issues a shrinking subsidy toward a fixed ceiling, while ETH issuance has no end date and no ceiling.

Against burn-heavy chains, the difference is scale. The EIP-1559 fee burn can outrun issuance only when Ethereum blocks are busy and base fees are high, and in this window they were neither: the burn removed about **1.3%** of what issuance added. Ethereum's own gas fees came to about **$11.7M** over the last 30 days, roughly **0.04%** of its market value in a year, because a large share of Ethereum activity now runs on rollups that pay small blob fees. That is a deliberate design choice — cheap blockspace for rollups — and it is the reason ETH currently reads as mildly inflationary rather than deflationary.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the staking queue: ETH is still entering the validator set, and every ETH added to the stake raises issuance, so the current rate of about **2,950 ETH** a day is already above the **2,905 ETH** a day booked forward. Second, the Glamsterdam upgrade: it activates on the Sepolia testnet on **Oct 6 2026** and on Hoodi around **Oct 27 2026**, while its mainnet date is still unscheduled; it changes no issuance parameter, but its gas repricing and a much higher gas limit would likely lower base fees and shrink the burn further. Third, EIP-8363, a draft published on **Aug 4 2026** that would burn a rising share of validator rewards as staking grows; it missed the cutoff for the next upgrade, so it cannot change this forecast, but if it is ever adopted it would turn part of Sell #1 into a burn. Fourth, the burn rate itself, which rose to about **53 ETH** a day in the last week of the window. Fifth, the Ethereum Foundation's balances, which change through sales that stay inside the float.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads ETH at **+0.21% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+0.21%** over the next 90: validator issuance of **261,416 ETH** against an EIP-1559 fee burn of **3,303 ETH**, with no vesting, no unlocks and no buyback. The structural mechanism is an uncapped proof-of-stake reward that grows with the square root of the **43.46M ETH** staked, offset only lightly by a burn that depends on how busy Ethereum blocks are. The key risk for the reading is direction: a larger stake raises issuance, and cheaper blockspace after the next upgrade would lower the burn, so both pull the same way. ETH has no supply cap; its supply grows until the burn catches up with issuance or the protocol changes how validators are paid.
