# ETH Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

ETH supply is roughly steady, edging up slowly: between Aug 18 and Nov 16 2026 Ethereum paid its validators **266,723 ETH** in new coins, while the fee burn destroyed **6,606 ETH**, so net supply grew **+0.21%** in 90 days, and the next 90 days look the same. ETH has no supply cap and no vesting schedule; new coins come only from validator pay, and the burn is the only built-in way coins leave. Other trackers show **+1.27%** for the same window because of an old gap in their supply figure, not because of new ETH.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

Over the 90 days to Nov 16 2026, Ethereum's supply grew by **260,117 ETH**, a net rise of **+0.21%**, and the next 90 days project the same **+0.21%**. Other trackers print **+1.27%**, a gap of **1.05 points**, so the coin page carries a warning note. The gap comes from a supply figure that stood still near 120.68M ETH until Sep 2 2026 and then jumped 1.33M ETH on Sep 3 2026 to catch up with the chain; that jump is a correction, not new coins, and it should leave the 90-day count around Dec 2 2026. On the chain itself, ETH is a mildly inflationary coin whose validator pay outruns the fee burn about forty to one.

## Sell pressure: where new ETH comes from

The first and only real source of new ETH is validator pay. Ethereum runs on proof of stake, and every few minutes the network creates fresh ETH to reward the validators who propose and check blocks. From Aug 18 to Nov 16 2026 that came to **266,723 ETH**, close to **2,960 ETH** a day. The pay is set by the protocol and grows with the amount staked, not by a calendar, so it does not halve or step down on a date. About 44.7M ETH is staked today, and on Nov 16 2026 another **910K ETH** was waiting in line to join, so the payout per day is more likely to creep up than fall in the next 90 days.

Vesting unlocks add **0**. The 2014 crowd sale and the launch allocation could move from the network's first day in July 2015, so there is no locked team or investor batch waiting to open, and no unlock calendar to track.

Foundation and unscheduled unlocks add **0**. The Ethereum Foundation does sell ETH to pay for its work, and it kept doing so this autumn: its main wallet sent out 2,000 ETH in the window, and wallets tied to it sold 2,785 ETH on Oct 26 2026 and more lots of 1,000 to 2,000 ETH in November. Every one of those coins was already counted as circulating, so the sales move ETH from one holder to another without adding a single new coin.

Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply adds **0**. No bankrupt firm or court is sitting on ETH for a later payout. The ETH stolen from Binance on Oct 29 2026 was already circulating before the theft, so the hack changed who holds those coins, not how many exist.

## Buy pressure: where new ETH goes

Programmatic buybacks remove **0**. No contract, treasury or company buys ETH back on the network's behalf; Ethereum has no fee switch that turns income into ETH purchases.

The protocol fee burn removed **6,606 ETH**. Since 2021 the base fee of every transaction is destroyed, and since 2024 so is the fee rollups pay to post their data in blobs. Across all 645,604 blocks from Aug 18 to Nov 16 2026, the base fee burned about 6,596 ETH and blobs added only about **10 ETH**, because blob space is still cheap. The total is lumpy: roughly **1,756 ETH**, over a quarter of the 90-day burn, went in under seven hours on the night of Oct 29 2026, when the Binance hack sent people rushing to move coins. On a normal day the burn now takes out only about 54 ETH, against close to 2,960 ETH of new validator pay.

Foundation buying removes **0**. The Foundation is a seller, not a buyer. Spot funds and listed treasury companies do buy ETH, but they buy coins that are already circulating, so their purchases, like their sales, leave the supply count unchanged.

New long-term locks remove **0**. Staked ETH still counts as circulating, so a bigger stake does not shrink supply. A proposal to burn part of validator pay, EIP-8363, was pulled from the next planned upgrade on Oct 1 2026 and has no new date.

## Foundation and overhang

The only team-linked holder is the Ethereum Foundation. On Nov 16 2026 it held about **93.37K ETH** across four known wallets: about 68.29K ETH in a multisig safe that collects staking rewards, about 20.18K ETH in a multisig it uses for DeFi, about 4.77K ETH in its long-standing main wallet and about 134 ETH in an older wallet. On top of that, it staked around 70K ETH earlier in 2026. The main wallet fell by 2,000 ETH over the 90 days, and the reward safe grew by about 428 ETH.

Because every ETH ever created is already counted as circulating, the Foundation's stack is not a hidden supply waiting to unlock; selling it changes the holder, not the count. We re-read these wallets at every rebuild, and if a balance ever appeared outside the circulating count and then fell, that outflow would enter the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How ETH compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Bitcoin is the clean contrast: it has a hard cap of 21M coins and a halving every four years, so its new supply shrinks on a known calendar. ETH has no cap and no halving. Its issuance depends on how much ETH is staked, rising with the square root of the stake, so it can go up or down with staking demand rather than following a fixed schedule.

Among proof-of-stake chains, Solana also has no cap, but its yearly inflation rate was set to start at 8% and step down by 15% each year toward a 1.5% floor, so its path is scheduled while ETH's is driven by the stake. Cardano pays stakers from a fixed reserve toward a 45B cap, so its new supply runs down over time. Avalanche caps supply at 720M and burns every transaction fee, a design closer to ETH's burn but with a ceiling ETH does not have.

What sets ETH apart is the burn. In busy years the burn has outrun validator pay and supply has fallen. Today, with most activity on rollups and blob space cheap, the burn covers only a small slice of new issuance, so ETH sits at the low end of uncapped proof-of-stake chains: a little over 0.2% net per 90 days, well under one percent a year.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**The staking line.** On Nov 16 2026 about 910K ETH was waiting to start staking. As it joins, validator pay rises a little, and a much bigger stake would lift issuance above today's rate.

**Glamsterdam.** The upgrade's Hoodi test-network fork was postponed on Oct 22 2026 and, as of Nov 6 2026, stays on hold while developers check how its new gas prices affect existing contracts, and mainnet has no date. None of its planned changes alter validator pay or the burn, but a busier chain after it would burn more.

**Around Dec 2 2026.** The Sep 3 2026 correction leaves other trackers' 90-day window around this date, after which their figure should fall back close to ours.

**The Foundation's selling.** Sales of 1,000 to 2,000 ETH kept coming through November 2026. They add no new supply, but they are worth watching as selling pressure on the market.

**The issuance debate.** A debate on validator pay was set for Nov 4 2026 at Devcon 8, a month after the burn proposal was pulled on Oct 1 2026. No change to issuance is scheduled; a dated proposal would be the first real sign.

## Summary

ETH supply grew **+0.21%** between Aug 18 and Nov 16 2026: validators were paid **266,723 ETH** in new coins and the fee burn destroyed **6,606 ETH**, over a quarter of it on the night of the Oct 29 2026 Binance hack. Ethereum has no supply cap, no vesting and no buyback, so validator pay is the only source of new ETH and the burn the only sink. The main risk to that reading is a larger stake, which lifts issuance, while a busier chain is the only thing that would lift the burn. The next 90 days look the same at about **+0.21%**, and the higher **+1.27%** shown elsewhere comes from a one-off catch-up in a supply figure, not from new ETH.
