# ETH Inflation Analysis · November 2026 · Mixed flows · supply roughly steady

ETH supply is close to flat and rising slowly. From Aug 27 to Nov 25 2026 Ethereum paid its validators **267,359 ETH** in new coins, while the fee burn destroyed **6,667 ETH**, so supply grew a net **+0.21%** in 90 days. ETH has no supply cap and no vesting; other trackers show **+1.31%** only because of an old supply figure that caught up with the chain on Sep 3 2026.

## ETH burn rate today

The page opens with a live "ETH burn rate today" box, read from the chain once a day: ETH burned in the last 24 hours, new ETH paid to validators each day (about **2,970 ETH**, the 90-day pace), and the net change. On an ordinary day the burn takes out only about 55 ETH, a small slice of new issuance, so ETH supply keeps rising a little; in the hours after the Oct 29 2026 Binance hack began, about 1,688 ETH burned in roughly five hours. For today's numbers, see [mrnasdog.com/research/eth/inflation](https://mrnasdog.com/research/eth/inflation).

## The verdict, in one paragraph

In the 90 days to Nov 25 2026, Ethereum's supply rose by **260,692 ETH**, a net **+0.21%** on a circulating supply of **122.25M ETH**, and the next 90 days should look about the same at **+0.21%**. Other supply trackers show **+1.31%** for the same 90 days, a gap of **1.10 percentage points**. That difference does not come from new ETH: the supply figure those trackers use stood still near 120.67M ETH until Sep 2 2026 and then jumped 1.33M ETH on Sep 3 2026 to match the chain, so the coin page carries a warning note until that jump leaves the 90-day window in early December 2026. The label that fits ETH today is **mildly inflationary by design**: a steady validator payout that the fee burn only dents.

## Sell pressure: where new ETH comes from

**Protocol inflation is 267,359 ETH.** Ethereum runs on proof of stake, and every epoch of about six and a half minutes the protocol creates new ETH for the validators who propose blocks and vote on them. From Aug 27 to Nov 25 2026 that came to **267,359 ETH**, about **2,970 ETH a day**. Ethereum issuance has no halving and no end date; the payout follows the square root of the total stake, so more stake means a larger total payout but a smaller yield per validator. About **44.75M ETH** is staked today, and on Nov 25 2026 another **698K ETH** sat in the queue waiting to start staking, so ETH issuance per day should edge up a little over the next 90 days.

**Vesting unlocks are zero.** ETH was sold in a public crowdsale in 2014, and the coins from that sale and from the launch grants could move from Ethereum's first block on Jul 30 2015. No locked team or investor batch is left to open.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks are zero.** The Ethereum Foundation does sell ETH to pay for research and grants. Its main wallet sent out 2,000 ETH between Aug 27 and Nov 25 2026, and a wallet that trackers link to the Foundation sold close to 9,000 ETH between Oct 26 and Nov 19 2026. Every one of those ETH was already part of the circulating supply, so a Foundation sale moves coins to a new owner without adding new ETH.

**Long-term locked or bankruptcy supply is zero.** No bankrupt lender or court holds ETH for a later payout. The ETH stolen in the Binance hack on Oct 29 2026 was already circulating before the theft, so the hack changed who holds the ETH, not how much ETH exists.

## Buy pressure: where new ETH goes

**Programmatic buyback is zero.** Ethereum has no buyback. No contract, foundation or treasury buys ETH on behalf of the network.

**The protocol fee burn removed 6,667 ETH.** Since the London upgrade in August 2021, the base fee on every Ethereum transaction is burned, and since March 2024 the fee that rollups pay to post their data as blobs is burned too. Across all **645,639 blocks** from Aug 27 to Nov 25 2026 the burn destroyed **6,667 ETH**; blobs made up only about 13 ETH of that, because blob space is cheap while rollups use less than it offers. A single evening carried a quarter of the burn: about **1,688 ETH** burned in roughly five hours after the Binance hack began on Oct 29 2026, when panicked users and the attacker crowded the chain and pushed the base fee up. On a normal day the ETH burn is closer to 55 ETH, against close to 3,000 ETH of new ETH issuance.

**Foundation buying is zero.** The Ethereum Foundation sells ETH and has no plan to buy any back. Spot ether funds and listed companies such as BitMine, which added 7,819 ETH in the week to Nov 23 2026, trade ETH that is already circulating, so their buying and selling does not change the supply count.

**New long-term lock is zero.** Staked ETH still counts as circulating supply, so even a fast-growing stake removes nothing from the float. A draft proposal to burn a rising share of validator pay as the stake grows has been debated since August 2026, but it is not part of any scheduled upgrade.

## Foundation and overhang

The one team-linked holder is the **Ethereum Foundation**. On Nov 25 2026 its four known wallets held about **93.44K ETH**: about 68.35K ETH in its main safe, which collects staking rewards, 20.18K ETH in its DeFi wallet, 4.77K ETH in its old main wallet and a small remainder elsewhere. It also has ETH staked under the treasury staking plan it began in February 2026. A separate wallet that trackers link to the Foundation, though the Foundation has not confirmed it, had close to 9,000 ETH left after its mid-November sales. All of this ETH is already in the circulating supply, so selling it never raises the count; we check these wallet balances again each time we update this page. If a Foundation balance ever turned out to sit outside the circulating supply and its balance fell between refreshes, that outflow would enter the Foundation unlock row at the next refresh.

The largest holders outside the Foundation are not team wallets: BitMine held 6,085,564 ETH on Nov 23 2026, close to 5% of all ETH, and spot ether funds, exchanges and the Binance hacker hold large balances too. Their trades matter for price, but none of them can create or destroy ETH.

## How ETH compares to other proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains

Ethereum belongs to the group of uncapped proof-of-stake Layer 1 chains that pay stakers in new coins and burn part of their fees. What sets ETH apart is the size of the stake: issuance follows the square root of the total stake, so as more ETH is staked the payout grows slowly, and at about 0.21% per 90 days, or a little under 0.9% a year, ETH issuance sits on the low side for this group. Several large proof-of-stake chains pay stakers several percent a year and burn far less of their fees, so their supply grows faster than Ethereum's.

Compared with Bitcoin, the mechanism is different rather than better or worse. Bitcoin has a hard cap of 21M coins and a fixed payout that halves every four years; Ethereum has no cap and a payout set by the stake, with an EIP-1559 fee burn that Bitcoin does not have. When the Ethereum main chain is busy, the burn can match or beat new issuance, as it did for stretches in 2022 and 2023; with most activity now on rollups and blob fees low, the ETH burn covers only about **2.5%** of new ETH, so the supply trend depends almost entirely on issuance.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

**The Glamsterdam test fork.** Ethereum core developers pencilled in the Hoodi test fork for the week of Dec 14 2026, with the go or no-go decision due at their next consensus call; mid-January 2027 is the earliest mainnet date without spec changes. Glamsterdam does not change validator pay or the burn rule, but a higher gas limit after it could lower the base fee and shrink the ETH burn further.

**The staking queue.** On Nov 25 2026 about 698K ETH was waiting to start staking. As it joins, Ethereum issuance rises slightly, by roughly 0.5% of the payout for every extra 1% of stake.

**The tracker catch-up around Dec 2 2026.** Once Sep 3 2026 leaves the 90-day window, other trackers should show roughly the same +0.21% as the chain, and the warning note on the coin page can go.

**Foundation and treasury-company sales.** The Ethereum Foundation and listed ETH holders such as BTCS sold ETH in November 2026 to cover costs and repay loans. These sales add no new ETH but are worth watching for price.

**The issuance debate.** The draft plan to burn part of validator pay is not scheduled for any upgrade. If developers ever put it in a fork, the ETH issuance row would shrink from that fork's date.

## Summary

ETH supply grew **+0.21%** between Aug 27 and Nov 25 2026: Ethereum validators were paid **267,359 ETH** in new coins and the EIP-1559 fee burn destroyed **6,667 ETH**, about 2.5% of that. Ethereum has no supply cap, no vesting and no buyback, so the ETH supply trend is set by staking issuance, which follows the square root of the 44.75M ETH stake. The main risk to the reading is a lower burn: if a bigger gas limit after Glamsterdam keeps fees cheap, almost all new ETH stays in circulation. Foundation sales, treasury-company trades and the Binance hack moved ETH between owners but did not change how much ETH exists.
