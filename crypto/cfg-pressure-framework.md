# CFG Inflation Analysis · August 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

Centrifuge's CFG is an Ethereum-native governance token whose issuance is written into a contract: about **3% a year**, minted weekly, compounding on the whole supply. The Pressure Framework reads **+3.35% net** supply growth over the last 90 days — roughly **12.74M CFG** reaching outside hands against a tradable base of **380.38M** — and projects **+2.52%** forward, with a buy side of exactly **zero** on all four rows: no buyback, no fee burn, no treasury accumulation, nothing newly locked. Our monitor reads **−34.13%** for the same window, a gap of about **37 percentage points** that is a counting change rather than a sell, so a ⚠ chip ships.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90 days to **Aug 23 2026** the framework reads Centrifuge at **+3.35%** net supply growth and projects **+2.52%** for the next 90 days. Our monitor reads **−34.13%**, a gap of about **37 percentage points**, which triggers the ⚠ chip. The deep walk settles it without ambiguity. The monitor's own day-by-day record drops from **577.5M** tracked supply on **Jun 8 2026** to **379.3M** on **Jun 9 2026** — a one-day step of roughly 198M tokens, with no burn of remotely that size anywhere on Ethereum — and has sat flat ever since. The chain says the opposite: total CFG supply rose from **632.1M** on **May 24 2026** to **700.3M** on **Aug 23 2026**. What changed was the definition, not the supply — the tracked count was re-based onto Centrifuge's own published released-supply figure of 54.6%, about **380.4M**, and that step is still inside this window, ageing out around **Sep 7 2026**. CFG is **structurally inflationary on the active float**: a hard-coded mint with no offsetting sink.

## Sell pressure: where new CFG comes from

**Protocol inflation** is the backbone of the CFG ledger at **8.28M CFG** over the window and about **5.12M** forward. Centrifuge runs a dedicated inflation minter contract on Ethereum whose settings were re-read directly from the chain for this build — a seven-day period and a per-period rate that compounds to **2.998% a year** on total supply — so the widely quoted "3% inflation" is not a policy Centrifuge chooses each quarter, it is literally compiled into the contract. Two mints landed inside the window, on **Jun 4 2026** and **Aug 3 2026**, and both reconcile to that rate at a whole number of weeks — thirteen and then eight — so the schedule and the chain agree exactly. Every newly minted CFG is paid into the Centrifuge Treasury first. That treasury is a full pass-through: over the same 90 days it received about 38.3M CFG and ended the period holding only **1.68M**, so the mint is booked as reaching the market rather than parked. A governance thread on **Jul 28 2026** argues for cutting the rate; it has not been put to a vote, so the emission schedule stands.

**Vesting unlocks** book at **zero**. Centrifuge publishes three vesting streams — the team's 12% allocation through **May 2030**, an incentive tranche of 100M CFG vesting linearly through **Apr 2029**, and an investor remainder running 22 months to **Mar 2028** — but none carries a dated cliff calendar with quanta, and the incentive tranche vests into the Centrifuge Treasury rather than onto the market, which makes it an internal transfer. Two unlock trackers were walked for this build and neither lists a CFG release inside the window. Where a schedule is unreadable but the escrow is not, the framework reads the escrow: Centrifuge's reserve wallet sent nothing at all to an outside address in these 90 days — its only payments went to Centrifuge's own treasury — so no vesting reached buyers.

**Foundation and unscheduled unlocks** add **4.45M CFG**: the portion of the outflow that came from balances Centrifuge already held rather than from the new mint. Releases had gone quiet through July and have now restarted — about **3.57M CFG on Aug 13 2026**, then 46,666 on **Aug 17 2026** and 10,416 on **Aug 18 2026**, after roughly 4.51M on **Jun 8 2026** and 2.50M on **Jun 30 2026**. **Long-term locked or bankruptcy** is **zero**: Centrifuge has no bankruptcy estate, and the one route that could have produced a supply backlog — converting legacy chain CFG and wrapped WCFG into the current token — closed permanently on **Apr 10 2026**, so no further legacy CFG can ever appear.

## Buy pressure: where new CFG goes

The buy side of the Centrifuge ledger is completely empty, and that is the structural point about CFG. **Programmatic buyback** is **zero**: the first attempt to route protocol revenue toward the token never reached the votes it needed, no replacement has been tabled, and no contract anywhere buys CFG. **Protocol fee burn** is **zero**: fees on Centrifuge's tokenized-asset products are paid in the assets themselves and in ETH, and none of them destroy CFG. **Foundation buy** is **zero** — the treasury is funded by minting, so it has no reason to buy on the open market, and no purchase was observed in any Centrifuge wallet during the window; those wallets ended the period smaller, not larger.

**New long-term lock** is also **zero**, despite an eye-catching **33.75M CFG** now sitting in a governance proxy contract. That CFG came out of Centrifuge's own reserve rather than the tradable float, so parking it removes nothing from the market, and the contract itself is a permissioned executor with no time lock and no release schedule — it can send the balance out at any moment, and this build confirmed it has never sent a token out at all. It is tracked as overhang, not credited as absorption. The small burns that appear on-chain belong to the closed migration helper and are one leg of a one-for-one port of tokens that already existed on the retired chain; because the framework does not book the port's mint leg, it does not book its burn leg either.

## Foundation and overhang

Three Centrifuge-controlled pools sit behind the tradable float and are refreshed from the chain on every rebuild. The reserve wallet holds about **128.07M CFG**, roughly a third of the entire tradable supply, and it grew during the window because a post-migration reconciliation mint of **59.81M CFG** landed there on **Jun 4 2026** — a one-for-one port of legacy supply that was created on Ethereum but never entered the float. The Centrifuge Treasury wallet, the destination of every inflation mint, holds only about **1.68M CFG** because it spends almost everything it receives. The governance proxy holds **33.75M CFG** under a permissioned executor with no lock. Together the three moved from about 108.1M to about 163.5M over the window while releasing **12.80M** to outside parties, and none of them has a published release schedule — which is precisely why they are enumerated rather than projected. If any of these balances falls to an outside address between refreshes, the outflow enters the Foundation row at the next refresh.

## How CFG compares to other real-world-asset tokens

The structural class CFG belongs to is governance tokens attached to real-world-asset credit protocols, and the comparison that matters is mechanism, not price. The strongest tokens in that class close the loop: protocol revenue is routed back into the token through a buyback or a burn, so growth in assets under management shows up as demand for the token. Centrifuge has built the revenue side — tokenized Treasury, S&P 500 and collateralised-loan products, with institutional partners arriving through 2026 — but has never connected it to CFG. Fees are earned in the assets and in ETH; nothing flows back.

Against fixed-supply and burn-driven designs the contrast is sharper still. A capped token with a fee burn shrinks its float as usage grows. CFG has no cap at all, and its issuance is the reverse of usage-linked: the inflation minter pays out the same 3% a year whether Centrifuge finances one billion dollars of assets or none. Compared with the chains whose emissions at least buy something — validator security, block production — CFG pays for nothing structural, because after the migration to Ethereum there are no collators to fund and no staking to reward. That is the substance of the complaint raised in the Centrifuge governance forum in **Jul 2026**: a governance-only token carrying an emission schedule designed for a chain that no longer exists. It is also the backdrop to the most consequential proposal on the table, which would retire the token question entirely rather than fix the rate.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First and largest, the token-to-equity proposal published on **Aug 18 2026**, which would let CFG holders convert one token into one tokenized share of Centrifuge Inc.; it is a comment-stage request that still needs board approval and an on-chain vote, but if it executes it removes converted CFG from circulation outright and rewrites this ledger. Second, whether the inflation thread of **Jul 28 2026** becomes a formal vote; a rate cut is the single largest change available to the sell side. Third, the reserve wallet holding **128.07M CFG**, whose drawdown pace sets the Foundation row entirely, now that releases restarted on **Aug 13 2026**. Fourth, the governance proxy holding **33.75M CFG**, which can move without notice and would land in the sell ledger if it did. Fifth, around **Sep 7 2026**, the one-day counting step of **Jun 9 2026** rolls out of the trailing 90-day window, at which point our monitor should stop reading a false −34% and converge back toward the framework.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads Centrifuge's CFG at **+3.35%** net supply growth over the 90 days to **Aug 23 2026** and projects **+2.52%** forward — structurally inflationary on the active float, with a buy side of exactly zero. The mechanism is unusually clean to describe: a contract mints about **3% a year** into the Centrifuge Treasury, the treasury spends it, and nothing anywhere buys or burns CFG back. The key risk is the overhang rather than the mint — **128.07M CFG** in a reserve wallet and **33.75M** in an unlocked governance proxy together exceed 40% of the tradable float and carry no published release schedule, and distributions restarted on **Aug 13 2026** after a quiet July. There is no cap and no ceiling: CFG supply grows indefinitely until governance changes the rate or, as the **Aug 18 2026** proposal contemplates, replaces the token with equity altogether.
