# CFG Inflation Analysis · September 2026 · Supply growing, projected to keep growing

CFG, the governance token of the real-world-asset tokenization platform Centrifuge, is issued by a live Ethereum contract that adds **2.998%** a year and is answered by nothing at all — no buyback, no burn, no lock. Over the 90 days to **Sep 7 2026** that minter created **3,166,749 CFG** in a single firing on **Aug 3 2026**, and the project's own wallets released a further **16,582,091 CFG** of supply they already held, of which **10,635,509 CFG** left in the first three days of September alone. That is **19.75M CFG** reaching the market against **zero** removed, so the MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CFG at **+5.19% net** over the last 90 days and **+5.71%** forward. The supply monitor reads **−34.14%** — a **39.33 percentage point** gap that is a bookkeeping restatement of the tracked float, not a burn.

## The verdict, in one paragraph

For the 90-day window ending **Sep 7 2026**, the Pressure Framework reads **CFG at +5.19% net**: **19,748,840 CFG** reached outside hands on the sell side and the buy side removed **zero**, on every one of its four rows. The supply monitor reads the realised 90-day change at **−34.14%**, and the gap of **39.33 percentage points** is large enough to ship a **data-conflict flag** on the CFG overview. The flag is not a disagreement about Centrifuge tokenomics — it is a dating problem. The tracked float for CFG was restated in one day on **Jun 9 2026**, from **577.5M** to **379.3M**, and has been pinned at **380,384,016 CFG** ever since; the monitor's 90-day window still spans that step. Read on the chain instead, CFG total supply went the other way over the same window, from **697,164,473.64** to **700,331,222.69**. Nothing was destroyed. The label for CFG is **persistently inflationary with an unanswered buy side**: a token that mints on a schedule and has no mechanism of any kind pointing the other way.

## Sell pressure: where new CFG comes from

Sell #1, protocol inflation, is **3,166,749 CFG**, and it is unusually easy to verify because the whole issuance engine is one small Ethereum contract. That contract accrues **0.0566665722%** of CFG total supply every **seven days** and is permissionless to trigger: anyone may poke it, at which point it compounds the accrued weeks and mints the entire backlog to the Centrifuge spending wallet in one transaction. Compounded, its rate is **2.998%** a year. Reading the contract's period and rate directly, then inverting its own arithmetic on the CFG mint that landed on **Aug 3 2026**, returns **8.000000** whole weeks of accrual — so neither the rate nor the mechanism is in doubt. Only **8** of the window's **12.86** weeks were actually paid, which is why the forward column books the contract schedule of **5,119,582 CFG** rather than the slower trailing rate: about five weeks of CFG issuance is already accrued and still owed.

Sell #3, foundation and unscheduled unlocks, is the larger line at **16,582,091 CFG**, and none of it is new coins — it is Centrifuge spending CFG it already held. Three wallets are read as one set: a reserve holding **116,067,866 CFG**, the spending wallet the minter pays into, and a governance executor holding **33,750,000 CFG** that has never sent a token out. Across the window that set fell from **168,078,032** to **151,495,941 CFG**, and after subtracting the new mint and a small pass-through the residual is exactly the Sell #3 figure, with the transfer log and the balance reads closing on each other to zero. The releases were lumpy and then accelerated: **2,500,000 CFG** on **Jun 30 2026**, **3,572,251** on **Aug 13 2026**, **1,367,838** on **Aug 27 2026**, then **4,044,339** on **Sep 2 2026** and **6,212,400** on **Sep 3 2026**. Sell #2, vesting unlocks, is **zero** — the Centrifuge incentive tranche vests in a straight line into the project treasury through **April 2029**, which is an internal wallet rather than the market, and the readable reserve sent every one of its **27,000,000 CFG** of window outflows to that same treasury. Whatever the treasury later spends is already counted in Sell #3. Sell #4 is **zero**: there is no estate, and the swap window for the retired Centrifuge chain's token is permanently shut, so no backlog of legacy CFG can appear behind the current supply.

## Buy pressure: where new CFG goes

Nowhere. All four buy rows are **zero**, and that is the defining fact of the CFG ledger. Buy #1, programmatic buyback, is zero because Centrifuge operates no buyback contract and no revenue share — product fees are taken in the assets being tokenized and in the network's own gas coin, never in CFG, so revenue growth never becomes a bid for the token. Buy #2, the protocol fee burn, is zero and was verified on both surfaces the framework requires: the two unspendable addresses have never held a single CFG, no transfer to either happened inside the window, and CFG total supply rose rather than fell. The CFG contract does carry a public burn function, so this is a choice Centrifuge has not made rather than something the code forbids. Buy #3, foundation buying, is zero — the treasury is funded by minting, not by purchasing, and every large flow this window ran outward. Buy #4, new long-term locks, is zero: the governance executor that received **16,875,000 CFG** in August has no timelock and no release schedule, so its balance is tracked as an overhang rather than credited as a lock, and there is no CFG staking contract on Ethereum to lock into.

## Foundation and overhang

The team-controlled CFG overhang is large and it is the number that matters most for the next reading. The reserve holds **116,067,866 CFG**, roughly **30.5%** of the CFG circulating count, and it is on-chain and readable, so it is re-read at every refresh rather than taken on trust. The spending wallet it feeds held **1,678,074 CFG** at the close of the window after passing **27,000,000 CFG** through in August and September. The governance executor holds **33,750,000 CFG**; it received **16,875,000 CFG** across **Aug 4 2026** and **Aug 5 2026** and has never made an outgoing transfer, so it is watched rather than booked. Behind all three sits the incentive tranche still vesting into the treasury through **April 2029**. Exchange custody wallets and unidentified large CFG holders are excluded by rule. If any of these balances falls between refreshes and the CFG does not end somewhere it cannot be sold from, the outflow enters Sell #3 at the next refresh.

## How CFG compares to other tokenization-protocol tokens

The tokenization sector splits cleanly on one mechanism question, and CFG lands on the harder side of it. Some real-world-asset protocols route platform revenue into open-market buying or into a burn, so growth in assets under management pulls against issuance; others separate the product economics from the token entirely, and the token's only supply mechanism is issuance. CFG is in the second group. Centrifuge fees are collected in the tokenized assets themselves and settled in the network's gas coin, so however large the platform grows, the CFG float feels none of it. The contrast with the burn-side coins the framework tracks is stark: a token with a fee burn or a reserve-funded destruction has a line pointing down every quarter, while CFG has four zeros in that column.

The second contrast is with capped and expiring-schedule tokens. A hard-capped coin issues toward a ceiling and then stops; a coin whose vesting calendar has run out simply has no sell rows left. CFG has neither shape. Its issuance comes from an uncapped contract with no end date and no maximum supply, and the reserve behind Sell #3 is deep enough at **116M CFG** to fund the current release pace for years. That is what separates a coin whose supply pressure fades from one whose supply pressure is a standing feature. The third contrast is governance, and it is the reason the next 90 days matter more than usual for CFG: Centrifuge has put a proposal to its holders to convert the token into equity, which is a structural answer to the same problem this page measures — a token that mints but never captures.

## What to watch in the next 90 days

First, the Centrifuge token-to-equity vote closes on **Sep 10 2026**. It proposes exchanging CFG for shares in the operating company at one share per token, it is unexecuted, and it changes no supply row today — but a passing vote would rewrite what the CFG ledger is measuring. Second, the release pace out of the Centrifuge reserve: **10,635,509 CFG** left across three days at the start of September, and whether that was a one-off deployment or a new run rate decides the forward column on its own. Third, the inflation minter: about **five weeks** of CFG accrual was unpaid at the window close, and the next poke will mint that backlog in one transaction. Fourth, the tracked float, pinned at **380,384,016 CFG** since **Jun 9 2026** — the data-conflict flag on this coin clears the moment that step ages out of the monitor's window or the classifier moves again. Fifth, any first appearance of a CFG buy mechanism: a fee switch, a burn, or a lock would be the first non-zero entry that column has ever carried.

## Summary

The MrNasdog Pressure Framework reads CFG at **+5.19% net** over the trailing 90 days and **+5.71%** over the next 90, on a buy side that is zero across all four rows. The structural mechanism is a permissionless Ethereum contract that mints **2.998%** of CFG supply a year into the Centrifuge treasury, plus a treasury that spends CFG it already holds faster than the minter creates it — **16,582,091 CFG** of released reserves against **3,166,749 CFG** of new issuance this window. The key risk is that the release pace is discretionary and accelerating, and the reserve behind it still holds **116,067,866 CFG**, about **30.5%** of the circulating count. There is no ceiling: CFG has no maximum supply and no expiry on its issuance contract, so the only thing that ends this reading is a governance decision — and one is on the table, closing **Sep 10 2026**.
